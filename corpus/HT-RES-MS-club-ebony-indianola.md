# Club Ebony and the people who kept the stage booked

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP19*

## 2. Story

Club Ebony’s low commercial building at 404 Hanna Avenue stands within an Indianola neighborhood shaped by the cotton industry and segregation. The historic district nomination records it as property 136, a contributing nightclub with a board-and-batten façade. The building provided a stage and gathering place within a Black entertainment circuit. The records identify musicians who played there, but putting on a show also meant deciding who to invite, finding patrons and keeping the doors open. [3][4]

B.B. King describes his own return to the club in a 1980 interview with John Jones. He names Johnny Jones and adds, “He used to do the booking.” That is King’s wording in the printed Mississippi Department of Archives and History transcript. By his account the booking work helped bring him home for performances after he started recording. The transcript preserves a particular job, at a particular local venue, in a musician’s own recollection. It leaves early staff rosters and many other relationships open. [5]

The museum identifies Mary Shepard as the operator who retired before King bought the club in 2008. King donated it to the museum in 2012. After repairs and renovation, the museum announced a June 2023 reopening. Deputy director Robert Terrell said the project returned the stage to its earlier position at the west end of the room and added a sound booth. The stage position is a restoration decision. The sound booth is new infrastructure that makes the building function as a venue now. [2][4]

## 3. Then and now

The historic district nomination documents a standing nightclub on the same site. The operator’s contemporary reopening record documents completed alterations and continued performance use. Today's event venue remains distinct from the B.B. King Museum’s main galleries, address and everyday admission window. [1–4]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The district nomination estimates the clubhouse date circa 1945, and the museum's Blues Sites page also associates activity with 1945. Visit Mississippi calls its establishment 1948. No original opening notice or lease was retrieved. Building construction, initial use and a formal opening may have different dates. [3][6][7]
- Current operator listings establish event use. The December anniversary notice mentions a club jam without stable door hours in its text. No day-to-day walk-in schedule has been verified. [1][2]
- Early staff and performer contracts, the initial construction and opening records, and Shepard’s own account remain open.
