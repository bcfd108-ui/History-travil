## 2. The story
**Mas Hashimoto**, interviewed by **Tom Ikeda** in Watsonville on **July 30, 2008**, recalls his mother's work at Poston in segment 23. She was an assistant cook, earning sixteen dollars a month in his recollection. The family still faced property-tax obligations outside confinement; his brothers sent money back. His short description is **“Money was hard to come by.”** [3]

He also remembers women helping one another learn sewing and make rooms more livable. This account makes maintenance, domestic work and mutual support visible without treating adaptation as evidence that incarceration was acceptable. His hourly-pay estimate is recollection, not a payroll calculation independently verified here. The transcript header and complete segment 23 were directly read; the entire interview and recording were not reviewed. [3]

A second directly read survivor source is **Joe Okimoto's June 4, 2025 Densho-published reflection**. He remembers being three when his family was removed, first confined at Santa Anita and then at Poston. His writing links childhood fear to his later concern for civil liberties. Densho explicitly publishes the piece as his personal opinion. This dossier uses the remembered family experience, not his contemporary legal or political assertions as independently verified facts. [4]

The historical question is not how successfully people “made the best of it.” It is what work families had to do while freedom and ordinary household control had been taken from them.
