# Biloxi’s Old Brick House and the kitchen remembered by a resident

*On Old Ground · Mississippi OP25 · Labeled research draft · NO-ANCHOR.*

## 2. Story
The Old Brick House, also known as the Rogers or Rodgers House and the former Biloxi Garden Center, records a way of arranging domestic work away from the principal rooms. The original nomination describes a long brick building with recessed doors, rear porch and the memory of an outside kitchen and dining room. Those later vanished when the street was built in the 1920s. A passage had joined the spaces. Someone walking a plate from the kitchen to the house would have used a different building and crossed a covered passage. [2, 3]

A resident of 1920–21 made that movement readable. “From the rear gallery downstairs a covered and floored passageway” led toward the kitchen, remembered Mrs. Richard Guice Sr. Her recollections survive here as an excerpt reproduced in the nomination out of the Biloxi Garden Center files. She also recalled the kitchen’s floor covering and iron fittings for pots and kettles. The early workers who cooked, served and maintained this property are still unnamed in the records read. [2]

The house presents an ownership problem. State records trace the land to a Spanish-period patent associated with Jean Baptiste Carquote and a sale by the family to William Rodgers in 1843. The inventory leaves open whether the Carquotte family built the house, while another state history puts construction at 1835–40. The inventory records ownership by John L. Henley from 1850 to 1872. An owner’s name, a land patent and a construction date refer to different events. [2–4]

The Garden Center began restoration in 1952 and acquired title in 1966. The nomination identifies the front gallery as rebuilt after Camille. The state records major damage in Katrina and a subsequent restoration. The city’s current renovation notice is another change in access and does not tell which of those earlier elements were repaired in the present campaign. [1–4]

## 3. Then and now
The early house faced water rather than the street added later. The original nomination and state inventory record the house, and the Garden Center account includes work after storms. A standing building and its modern public access are separate facts. The city lists a renovation closure and this draft offers remote interpretation. [1–4]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements
- The MDAH inventory estimates c. 1850 and allows earlier construction. The heritage-area history gives 1835–40. Read both as estimates. The Spanish-period land patent is earlier than the house and does not resolve the builder. [2–4]
- The historic nomination, property inventory and heritage-area materials print different street numbers. No number is used as a visitor locator during renovations. Original renumbering evidence is open. [1–4]
- The original Guice manuscript, early workers’ records and a dated renovation reopening remain unverified. Her recollection is a quotation from the printed nomination, not a transcription made here from the private file. [1, 2]
