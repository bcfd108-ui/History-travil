# Nanih Waiya and the return of the Mother Mound

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP14 · Labeled research draft · NO-ANCHOR within this program · Reflection only*

## 2. Story

The Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians writes about Nanih Waiya as a living place of significance and names it the “Mother Mound.” Its public cultural account explains why the return mattered without requiring a visitor to be on its grounds. The Tribe identifies the mound and the cave as distinct places. The account belongs to the Tribe, and readers can consult its published words directly. This dossier does not recast its ceremonial or oral traditions. [1, 2]

The late twentieth-century state park was one chapter in a much longer history. A 2007 Mississippi bill recorded that the state was disposing of some parks after a 2004 decision and declared that Nanih Waiya held sacred significance to the Choctaws. Senate Bill 2732 instructed the state wildlife and parks bodies to convey the park to the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians. The text vested authority to carry out the transfer in the state department's executive director. Legislative approval and the change in ownership were two acts. The official legislative history records approval on March 12, 2007, as chapter 310. The Tribe dates the return of the mound to August 8, 2008. [1, 3, 4]

Choctaw writer Roger D. Amos describes the date of the deed and identifies Miko Beasley Denson and 17 Tribal Council members in the later signing of a proclamation. His public account helps distinguish work by the Tribe’s elected leaders from the language of the state law. The original signed deed and proclamation were not inspected. The record that matters for the present is the Tribe's own account of continued control. [1, 5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** Nanih Waiya Mound and Village in Winston County is recorded separately from Nanih Waiya Cave Mound in the federal register. The public record for the former marks the address restricted. No archaeological pin, boundary reconstruction or route is supplied. [6, 7]

**Present public anchor.** Blank within this program. The Tribe’s public visitor statement identifies the mound as open and the cave as closed without approval. That distinction is preserved as operator evidence. An independent non-sensitive interpretation stop within the assigned scope was not verified. [1, 2]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- A similarly titled 2006 committee substitute, SB 2803, preceded 2007 SB 2732. The chapter 310 record verifies the latter's approval. The Tribe reports the return in 2008. Draft legislation, enactment and physical transfer cannot be collapsed into one date. [1, 3, 4, 8]
- Mound and cave have different register records and different current access statements. The private title chain between enactment and transfer could not be verified from an inspected deed. If later sources invoke other intermediaries, preserve the accounts separately until deed evidence resolves them. [1–7]
- The earlier sweep's provisional access shorthand needs to be read alongside the Tribe's published distinction. The federal listing is archival evidence. It provides no authority for this catalog to route readers to cultural ground. [1, 2, 6, 7]
