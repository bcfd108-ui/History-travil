# Cataldo Mission / Coeur d’Alene’s Old Mission State Park
Research draft for Dash QC • 2026-11-19 UTC • No field visit, outreach or app publication. Brief U249 read in full. Sources are linked beside claims; archival descriptions are not eyewitness accounts. Unverified locator fields are empty.

## 1. The story
Start with the people, not the date on the building. The Coeur d’Alene Tribe identifies itself as Schitsu’umsh, “Those who were found here” or “The discovered people.” Its own published history describes generations traveling the region’s lakes, rivers, forests and mountains. The mission is therefore a chapter within a continuing homeland, not the beginning of local history. https://www.cdatribe-nsn.gov/

A trail-history brochure published on the Tribe’s website recounts an invitation to Father DeSmet and his arrival in 1842. It relates that encounter to Circling Raven’s vision. Treat this as the Tribe’s published account, not a tourist legend to embellish. The same brochure dates establishment at Cataldo to 1848 and completion of the church to 1853. https://www.cdatribe-nsn.gov/lake/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2020/03/RouteOfTheCDAsFrontPage.pdf

Construction was not a lone priest’s achievement. The Library of Congress credits Ravalli, Brother Huybrechts and Native workmen, using a small repertoire of hand tools, rope and pulleys. Its HABS record dates initial work approximately 1848–1853; current Idaho Parks interpretation instead uses 1850–1853. Preserve that disagreement: preparatory/site work and church construction may be different events, but these records do not establish that reconciliation. https://www.loc.gov/resource/hhh.id0020.sheet/?sp=6 ; https://parksandrecreation.idaho.gov/state-park/coeur-dalenes-old-mission-state-park/

The building also occupied a network of exchange. Kootenai County records a Coeur d’Alene village named sq'wt'u here and says the later town took Father Joseph Cataldo’s name. Its local history follows a ferry connection to the Mullan Road and the arrival of the railway in 1889. This is a useful way to read the surroundings: Indigenous place, mission, road, river crossing and railway are overlapping histories, not interchangeable founding dates. https://www.kcgov.us/594/Cataldo

The National Register file describes a larger settlement of workshops, travelers’ accommodation, a mill and Indigenous dwellings. It also records substantial 1970s repairs: the surviving church is not an untouched 1853 object. https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/66000312_text

The encounter cannot responsibly end in a cheerful fusion-of-cultures sentence. A University of Idaho project identifies recordings of Felix Aripa, Cliff SiJohn and Father Thomas Connolly on the mission, and a separate SiJohn interview addressing missionary and Indian-agent influences. The page attributes the phrase “turning the culture inside out” to SiJohn. That short quotation is the published description, not a transcript newly heard by this researcher. The audiovisual testimony should be reviewed before adding longer first-person quotations. https://www.lib.uidaho.edu/digital/L3/ShowOneObjectSiteID50ObjectID591.html

## 2. Then / now
Then: a working settlement. Now: church, restored Parish House, cemetery and Sacred Encounters exhibition. Distinguish original fabric from restoration. https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/66000312_text ; https://parksandrecreation.idaho.gov/state-park/coeur-dalenes-old-mission-state-park/

For a repeatable image comparison, use the HABS measured drawings and photographs, not a generic internet image. The archive catalog supplies 13 photographs, 11 measured drawings and nine data pages, with survey number HABS ID-1. Select an actual drawing sheet and compare from a permitted visitor position; do not assume every archival view is publicly accessible today. https://www.loc.gov/resource/hhh.id0020.sheet/?sp=6

## 3. Physical remainder and access
**Suggested onsite progression:** begin in Sacred Encounters so the people and relationship precede the building; continue to the church exterior and interior where open; finish with the Parish House and a quiet, noncompetitive cemetery walk. The strongest story moment: compare the surviving church with the vanished working settlement. This is an interpretive recommendation, not a measured route or assurance that every interior is open. Site components: https://parksandrecreation.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/parks/coeurd-alenes-old-mission/Field-Trip-Packet-Coeur-dAlenes-Old-Mission-State-Park_2020_03042020.pdf ; settlement record: https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/66000312_text

The current park page lists 31732 S Mission Road, Cataldo; winter hours 10 a.m.–3 p.m., November–March, with major winter holidays closed; summer 9 a.m.–5 p.m., April–October. Posted vehicle entrance $7; exhibit $5/person or $10/family. These were checked November 19 UTC, not guaranteed for a later trip. Museum/restroom accessibility is listed; a fully accessible route through every historic interior is not verified. https://parksandrecreation.idaho.gov/state-park/coeur-dalenes-old-mission-state-park/

The 2020 park education packet describes timber construction and the cemetery’s lost grave houses. It explicitly says no remnants of those houses remain. Do not turn graves into collectible hunt objects. Look without rubbing, lifting, digging or handling historic surfaces; touch only objects explicitly offered for handling by staff. https://parksandrecreation.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/parks/coeurd-alenes-old-mission/Field-Trip-Packet-Coeur-dAlenes-Old-Mission-State-Park_2020_03042020.pdf

## 4. Honest gaps / publication checks
- Start date: HABS c. 1848 versus park 1850; completion agrees at 1853. Do not conceal the discrepancy. URLs above.
- Departure chronology: HABS ends Jesuit use here in 1877; Tribe-hosted brochure gives relocation to DeSmet in 1878. It also names development/pollution; older nomination supplies a different emphasis. Neither should be silently substituted for the other. https://www.loc.gov/resource/hhh.id0020.sheet/?sp=6 ; https://www.cdatribe-nsn.gov/lake/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2020/03/RouteOfTheCDAsFrontPage.pdf
- Dimensions: the education packet calls the mission about 90 feet high; nomination describes 90 by 40 feet and 30 feet high. Likely an error in the packet, but a checked drawing is needed before publishing corrected dimensions. https://parksandrecreation.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/parks/coeurd-alenes-old-mission/Field-Trip-Packet-Coeur-dAlenes-Old-Mission-State-Park_2020_03042020.pdf ; https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/66000312_text
- Library of Congress title says Shoshone County while the nomination identifies Kootenai. Retain each catalog’s original title; do not let a legacy heading determine current jurisdiction. Same HABS/NPS URLs above.
- No audio testimony was transcribed, tribal ceremonial material reproduced, or current religious-service schedule verified. Indigenous testimony is a required next-depth source, not decorative material.

## 5. Hunt material: observational candidates, not deployed challenges
1. Church front: from the authorized approach, find the timber-supported porch. Question: which visible base or surface today most clearly shows a repair, and what material/color distinguishes it from its neighbor? Photograph only if permitted. Answer depends on current fabric; do not supply an invented key. Basis: restoration record https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/66000312_text
2. Visitor-center exhibit: find the historical mission-settlement drawing. Question: which outbuilding is closest to the church on the displayed drawing, and what does its actual caption call it? Requires exhibit availability and field validation. Basis: park education packet URL above.
No timed cemetery challenges; no physical caches or object removal. Proposed questions are field-verification tasks, not guarantees that online imagery cannot reveal an answer.
