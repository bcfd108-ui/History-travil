# French Camp Historic Village and the house that moved

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP12 · Labeled research draft*

## 2. Story

The house visitors can enter tells two histories. The first belongs to a roadside business inside the Choctaw Nation. The second belongs to a building that was hauled into the village in 1975, when French Camp Academy staff and students took on its renovation. Between them lies the distinction that gives this stop its value. A shelter built for a different household can show how people made room for a traveler. The records have to tell us who actually lived and worked on either site. [1, 2, 4, 6]

The federal survey places Louis LeFleur on the Trace as a “trader and innkeeper” about 1812, with an 1813 issue of the *Washington Republican* cited for the description. Those four words come from the survey's printed account. The newspaper page itself was not inspected. NPS identifies the stand as a stop within Choctaw country and records LeFleur's marriage to a Choctaw woman and their son Greenwood, who became a Choctaw leader. Her name, the names of the people who provided food and beds, and the terms under which they worked require evidence of their own. [1, 4]

The modern shop occupies one side of a dogtrot, a house organized around a roofed central passage. French Camp Academy reports that Benjamin F. Ramsey bought the building in 1879 and that it passed into the Huffman family through inheritance. Academy staff and students helped renovate it after the move from two miles south. The opposite side is described as the village hospitality room. Students’ names and relocation records are still missing. [2]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** LeFleur's early nineteenth-century stand in the vicinity of today's French Camp. The Academy puts the relocated cabin over an approximate tavern vicinity. The NPS description uses a 900-foot northeast relationship whose starting point needs better documentation. The settlement and a moved house are separate historical subjects. [1, 2, 4]

**Present public anchor.** French Camp Historic Village visitor area, signed from the parkway near milepost 180.7. A drive-up or short walk starts from village visitor parking and stays within posted visitor areas. An Academy classroom, private guest lodging and any unsignposted historic ground are outside this visit. [1–3, 5]

## 5. Public progression

Start with the village orientation map in the visitor area. Follow the signed approach to the dogtrot. From the open public side, first look along its exterior, then look through the middle passage if the premises are open. The story lands at **The roof over the passage**, where two enclosed rooms and an open central space make visible what a surviving building can teach even after relocation. Use the exterior alone when the shop is closed and the visitor grounds are open. [1–3, 5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The town places establishment in 1810. The federal survey and NPS say about 1812. The 1813 newspaper citation supports operation by that time if the survey's reference is accurate. Its original page and any earlier contemporary license or ledger were unavailable for inspection. [1, 4, 8]
- The Academy and NPS descriptions do not supply a common surveyed origin for their approximate placements. Exact tavern footprint remains open. [1, 2]
- Academy records identifying the workers and students who moved and renovated the cabin were not retrieved. No room-by-room inventory of 1840s fabric was verified. [2]

## 7. Historical photo records

The federal survey and HAER record are leads. No frame of the original stand or independently cleared early frame showing this cabin before relocation was verified. Adopted frame, printed credit and Photo Point remain blank. [4, 6]

## 8. Optional observations

Historical Choctaw ground carries reflection only. No access-check or photo invitation is assigned to the historic stand. The public village boundary is for interpreting the relocated building. [1, 2, 5]
