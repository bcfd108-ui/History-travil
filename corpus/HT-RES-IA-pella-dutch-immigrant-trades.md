# Pella — Three wagons for the last leg

Iowa OP05 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**The fare paid for a wagon and the work that kept it going.** John (Jan) Hospers was a schoolteacher before emigrating. His 1849 diary records the work of making a household mobile: packing bedding, negotiating overseas passage and hiring transport across the Midwest. In the July 25 entry, after describing his wife's illness in Burlington, he says that they “left for Pella, at $20 per wagon”. Three covered wagons took the party west. The rest of the entry separates the drivers' daily allowance for food and feeding the horses from the wagon price. The quotation is from the 1912 English translation in an online transcription. The handwritten Dutch original was not read. [5, 6]

**His son was already earning a living on the new ground.** Henry Hospers arrived with the earlier group. The University of Iowa biography identifies reserve teaching, chain pulling and surveying among his early Pella roles. The Northwestern College finding aid then tracks work as surveyor, teacher, notary and editor. Set those trades alongside the father's account of negotiating coach and wagon fares. One man counted wagons; another helped measure the new settlement. The collections report provides a locator for papers and excerpts, with no archive visit in this itinerary. [5–7]

**The square has its own household record.** The public Scholte House on the north side of the square belonged to a different family, Hendrik and Maria. The nomination distinguishes the original house from additions. Pella Historical dates building work to 1847–48 and describes later family use and donation to the historical society. Its Historical Village, including the workshop called De Timmerman, interprets trades in a managed visitor setting. The present Vermeer Windmill is recent, built as a working Dutch-style mill and opened in 2002. The mill and workshop illustrate skills; neither is evidence that a named Hospers used that equipment. [2–4, 8, 9]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic places.** John Hospers’s diary records arrival on July 28, 1849 and lodging with P. Welle. Henry’s early work is associated with Pella. The dwellings or specific survey stakes of the Hospers family are unverified. The Scholte House is the other household’s surviving property. Use the operator’s current visitor address, 728 Washington Street. The federal nomination prints 739 Washington Street; scope and numbering history remain open. [1, 2, 5–8]

**Lawful public anchor.** Scholte House Museum and Gardens, 728 Washington Street. Drive up or make the short downtown approach from legal signed parking. Follow operator paths and signed public rooms during normal hours. The garden paths and house threshold differ. The Historical Village entrance at 714 East 1st Street is a separate public optional stop during its own open hours. Read remotely if closures or winter surfaces rule out both. [1–4]

## 5. A short visit

Begin on the museum side of the square, then read the 1849 fare entry from a public path or inside the open museum. If the Village is open, compare the trades represented there with survey and transport work documented in the Hospers sources. Land on **The last three wagons into Pella**, reading the July 28 arrival entry. Treat household deaths mentioned in the diary as quiet remembrance, separate from the trade observation. [1–7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

John's biography is in the translated diary introduction. Henry's published biography and the family finding aid place his arrival in 1847; the Iowa Legislature biographical page gives 1840. The 1912 editor independently distinguishes Henry’s 1847 arrival from John's 1849 diary. Retain the discrepancy. No original immigration ledger, survey field book or signed manuscript was retrieved. Operator 728 and register 739 Washington Street differ; use the operator for present arrival. The operator reported a previous winter pause followed by reopening March 23, 2026. The next full winter closure and reopening dates were not verified. [1, 5–8, 10, 11]
