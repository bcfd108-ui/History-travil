# St. Mary Basilica and the cathedral its congregation built on credit

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP04 · November 20, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

The public sidewalk on South Union Street gives a view of the church. The parish welcomes worshippers and describes visitor arrangements separately. Follow its current instructions for an interior visit. [1,2]

## 2. Story

On February 24, 1842, the cornerstone was laid for Bishop John Joseph Chanche’s new cathedral. Natchez builder James Hardie undertook construction. The congregation celebrated its first Mass inside on Christmas Day, 1843. Years later, in a letter seeking support, Bishop James Oliver Van de Velde described largely boarded window openings with only small portions glazed. People had been worshipping for over a decade in a building still awaiting much of what visitors recognize today. [3,4]

## 3. Then and now

Credit shaped the architecture. Unpaid tradespeople and suppliers sued, leaving a record of the work. The parish archives report that the building twice faced auction by 1849. In 1859 a contemporary newspaper credited Irish-born Peter Warner with the newly finished interior. Only in 1886, after the debt had been cleared, did Bishop Francis Janssens consecrate the cathedral. The first Mass, the finished interior and the consecration each mark a different event. [4,5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The Gothic Revival exterior belongs to the earliest campaign. Memorial windows and Stations of the Cross came in the late nineteenth century. Conrad Schmitt Studios installed Carrara marble furnishings in 1930 and returned for restoration after a 1998 storm. The church received its minor basilica designation in 1999. [5]

## 5. Public progression

Begin with the pointed exterior windows. If the church is receiving visitors, the colored glass seen from within offers a way to understand Van de Velde’s complaint about boards and the later congregation’s investment in memorial windows. Worship and private observances take precedence. [1,4,5]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

The parish names Baltimore architect Robert Cary Long Jr. An independent architectural history explains the evidence, including drawings listed in an 1850 sale catalogue and a related Baltimore church. Warner’s documented work on the later interior is a separate attribution. The sources name contractors more readily than their crews. Visit Natchez gives 107 South Union for visitors, while the architectural inventory gives 101. Both identify the same church. [2,3,5]
