# Jeffers Petroglyphs — The number grew as the record changed

*History Travel research draft · Minnesota OP06 · Independent Dash review pending.*

## 2 · Story — The number grew as the record changed

The Minnesota Historical Society describes about 5,000 sacred carvings at Jeffers. Archaeologist Gordon Lothson and colleagues identified about 2,000 on part of the exposure in 1971. That earlier count came from a specific survey, not from an accounting of every mark or from what a person should expect to see. In a later field school, Robert Clouse’s team recorded individual peck marks instead of tracing expected shapes. MNHS says its subsequent documentation and conservation involved Native American elders, conservators, archaeologists and staff. A figure familiar to a visitor can be much harder to record faithfully than to recognize at a glance. [2,4]

In Tom Sanders’s April 2014 report, Dakota elder Jerry Flute calls Jeffers “a special place, not just for visitors but also for Native Americans.” This is the elder’s statement as printed in the operator-hosted report. Neither authorship of a particular carving nor meaning of a particular figure follows from the count. The visitor’s task is to receive the public interpretation with care. The report, long-running field records and consultation history explain why the publicly described place cannot be reduced to a worksheet of symbols. [2,4–5]

The MNHS archaeology account records a 1966 acquisition from the Warren Jeffers estate and a subsequent public site. The 1970 federal nomination gave an earlier assessment of the age of the carvings. Later work changed how the markings were documented and what could be counted. The preservation story includes people deciding how to look, how to record and what ought to remain undisturbed. [4,6]

## 3 · Then / now

The carvings belong to a much longer history than the modern historic-site operation. The present public interpretive facility explains protection and research. The ordinary public interior, when reopened, is the sole destination proposed by this dossier. Cultural and ceremonial ground and rock-surface routing remain outside it. [1–5]

## 6 · Gaps and conflicts

- Lothson’s approximately 2,000 identified marks refer to part of the exposed face and the method then used. MNHS’s approximately 5,000 is a later estimate for the site. Do not add or average them. [2,4]
- The 1970 nomination describes a range from several thousand to several hundred years old. MNHS reports use over as long as 7,000 years, while the Office of the State Archaeologist says the earliest carving may date to perhaps 8,000 years ago. Treat these as source-specific estimates. [4,6–7]
- Flute’s attributed statement is available in Sanders’s report. The underlying recording or interview was not located, and a separate Nation-hosted edition of that statement was not verified. Current winter access is closed and the 2027 public opening date remains unverified in the operator schedule consulted. [1,5]
