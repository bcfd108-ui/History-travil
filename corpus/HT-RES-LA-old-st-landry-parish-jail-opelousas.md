# Old St. Landry Parish Jail (1939–40 PWA Courthouse & Jail), Opelousas — supplement correction and Art Deco courthouse square

## 1. Trip gate

**CRITICAL SUPPLEMENT CORRECTION:** The institutional sweep supplement listed "Old St. Landry Parish Jail, Opelousas" as a *"standing jail on the courthouse square... hanging-era fabric carried in Register/HMdb leads."* Primary verification against the **Opelousas Historic District** NRHP nomination (`89000477`), City of Opelousas records, and Living New Deal documentation proves **no separate nineteenth-century or standing hanging-era jail survives on Courthouse Square**: the early 1806/1811 log and brick jails, the 1847 courthouse, and the 1886 Victorian courthouse (which held a third-floor jail) were **all demolished in 1938**. It remains an **active parish courthouse** (not a walk-in jail museum); the modern St. Landry Parish medium-security jail stands elsewhere at `108 S. Market St.` [1, 2, 4, 5]

## 2. The story

Opelousas—settled as a French and Spanish post (`Poste des Opelousas`) in the eighteenth century and designated parish seat of St. Landry Parish in **1805–1807**—went through a rapid succession of courthouse-square jails before the present building was erected. The parish's earliest public jail was a **ca. 1806–1811** log and brick structure on the square, succeeded by brick courthouse buildings in **1820**, **1847**, and an ornate Victorian brick courthouse completed in **1886** (which housed a parish jail on its third floor). [1, 2, 3]

In **1938**, St. Landry Parish voters and officials cleared the square entirely, **demolishing the 1886 courthouse and its integrated jail** to make way for a modern Public Works Administration (**PWA**) project (`Docket LA-1126-F` / New Deal funding). Designed in **Art Deco / PWA Moderne** style by New Orleans architect **Theodore L. Perrier** and built by **A. J. Rife Construction Co.** between **1939 and 1940** (dedicated 1940), the three-story limestone-and-brick building functioned from opening day as the **St. Landry Parish Courthouse and Jail**, placing the courts and clerk offices on the lower floors and a **modern steel-celled parish jail on the third floor**. [1, 2, 3]

Over the main Court Street entrance and facade, Louisiana sculptor **Angela Gregory** carved monumental **limestone bas-relief panels** depicting figures of agriculture, justice, oil/industry, and parish heritage—one of the notable New Deal architectural sculpture commissions in Acadiana. [1, 2, 3]

The third-floor courthouse jail remained in active use for roughly four decades until St. Landry Parish opened a separate parish jail facility at **108 S. Market St.** (sources conflict between **1979** and **1984**—see §6). Courthouse Square itself remains a contributing centerpiece of the **Opelousas Historic District**, listed on the National Register in **1989** (`NRHP 89000477`). [1, 4, 5, 6]

## 4. What's still there

- **Public (Courthouse Square & exterior):** Complete **1939–40 PWA Art Deco courthouse and former jail building** exterior, including the **Angela Gregory** bas-relief sculptures, original bronze/aluminum entry doors, fluted cast-iron/metal lamp standards, live oaks, and square gazebo/monuments. [1, 2, 7]
- **Restricted / non-public:** Active courtrooms and the former **third-floor jail level** inside the courthouse (no public jail museum); active **108 S. Market St.** parish jail (excluded). [4, 5]
- **Gone:** All nineteenth-century standalone jails and the 1886 Victorian courthouse (razed 1938). [1, 2]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Arrive at Courthouse Square** (`118 S. Court St.`, bounded by Court, Landry, Bellevue, and Market streets in downtown Opelousas) and use public square street parking. [1, 2]
2. **Stand at the Court Street facade** to inspect the limestone Art Deco massing and **Angela Gregory's** sculpted bas-reliefs above the main entry and bays. [1, 2, 7]
3. **Walk the square's perimeter** beneath the live oaks and fluted lamp posts, noting that the third-floor recessed masonry band housed the parish jail from 1940 until the modern Market Street jail opened. [1, 3, 7]
4. **Optional weekday step:** Enter only the public ground-floor lobby during open courthouse hours if visiting public clerk/heritage displays; do not route into upper judicial rooms or toward `108 S. Market St.` [4, 5]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Supplement error corrected:** The sweep supplement assumed a surviving standalone nineteenth-century / hanging-era jail on the Opelousas Courthouse Square. NRHP `89000477`, City of Opelousas history, and Living New Deal confirm **all pre-1938 courthouse and jail buildings were demolished in 1938**; the only surviving historic jail fabric on the square is the **1939–40 third-floor jail wing** inside the active Art Deco courthouse. [1, 2, 3]
- **Year third-floor jail closed (`1979` vs. `1984`):** *St. Landry Now* states the separate St. Landry Parish Jail (`108 S. Market St.`) opened in **1979**, whereas a Louisiana incarceration-transparency parish profile dates the Market Street jail opening to **1984**. Both dates are printed; original parish Police Jury construction/transfer resolutions were not consulted directly. [4, 6]
- **PWA construction cost (`$280,000` vs. `$481,794`):** City of Opelousas heritage tour copy cites **$280,000**, while the Living New Deal entry cites **$481,794** (`$216,807` PWA grant + `$264,987` parish bond). Likely reflects original base contract vs. total project/furnishings cost; printed as an unresolved discrepancy. [2, 3]
- **Street numbering:** Listed variously as `118 S. Court St.` (parish/clerk address & geocoders) and `149 S. Court St.` (historic district inventory). Both refer to the Courthouse Square block. [1, 2, 9]
