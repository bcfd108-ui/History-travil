## 2 · Story — Waiting for the logs

The *Little Falls Herald* ran “Mills Have Late Start” on March 29, 1912. The report expected the Pine Tree sawmill to start around April 10. It said logs were insufficiently advanced to keep the sawmill running, contrasted the previous March 27 opening, and described the forecast difference as “a difference of a full pay to the employees.” The words come from the indexed text rendered for a digitized original page. The separate PDF visual fetch returned HTTP 403. April 10 was an expectation in the newspaper, and no follow-up issue confirming that start date was obtained. [4]

The workers’ lost time is the news here. The report described an overhaul and another coat of whitewash while the mill waited, and linked preparations by the boom company to the ice leaving the river. Two different jobs follow from that record: keeping a factory ready and delivering enough logs to feed the saws. The photograph of the falls and the dam explains why the water mattered. The delay in the newspaper explains why a powerful river could still leave a crew waiting. [4,7]

The county history distinguishes two companies. Pine Tree sawed timber and dimension lumber and held finished boards in piles to dry. Hennepin used water to power its process and pulp wood, first making pulp and later making paper. Its surviving site gives a traveler a landmark for a larger industrial conversion. The park is at the paper works, separate from the place where the 1912 sawmill employees waited. [2,5]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then:** Pine Tree’s large sawmill was north of the Broadway bridge on the west bank, according to the county history. A 1910 *Herald* item describes the demolition of a different, older mill used for storage after the large sawmill was built. Hennepin Paper’s water supply and canals served a separate plant. **Now:** city Mill Park preserves outdoor remnants at the former paper works, and the sawmill property is described by the county history as spanning later uses, including LeBourget Park and adjacent industrial property. This itinerary visits the paper ground and reads the sawmill across the wider history of the river. [1–3,5–6,8]

## 4 · Surviving features and access

- **Public exterior:** ordinary signed Mill Park path and outdoor interpretation when open. [1–3]
- **View from public ground:** retained stone mill fabric and the river from the authorized path, subject to siting and sightline. Stay outside the ruins and upstream infrastructure. [2–3,8]
- **Outside the visit:** old sawmill parcels north of Broadway, industrial works, dam structures, closed ruins and shoreline scrambling. [2,5,8]

## 6 · Gaps and conflicts

The county teaching history gives Hennepin Paper’s operation as 1890 through December 1998. The state byway study dates selection of Little Falls for the associated industrial operations to 1891. These are differently labeled events, and a contemporary Hennepin startup or incorporation record is still needed. The byway study dates partial construction of the hydro development to 1888 and completion to the 1910s, distinct from the county’s 1888 canal reference. The 1910 paper’s older demolition and the 1912 sawmill restart concern distinct facilities. No named employee payroll or actual 1912 reopening day was obtained. [4–8]
