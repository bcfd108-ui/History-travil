## 2. The story

Start with the artist's present tense. **Terran Last Gun**, born in Browning and an enrolled Piikani citizen, writes on his own website: **“Color and shape are the building blocks of my art practice and connect me to my Piikani heritage.”** This is a published first-person artist statement, not a recording heard for this research. His biography describes a contemporary practice based in Santa Fe, not permanent residence or a visitor studio in Browning. [3]

The statement changes how to approach the museum: not as a warehouse of a vanished people, but as a place to encounter historical and continuing artistic work. The IACB's online **Land / Sky** exhibition places Last Gun's own commentary beside named works, including **Old Rock**, ink and colored pencil on antique notebook ledger paper dated approximately 1908. The artwork carries a **2021** copyright date. The paper's age is not the work's creation date. [4]

Do not treat “Plains Indian” as a single identity. The museum describes collections from multiple Northern Plains peoples. [2] Last Gun's own biography distinguishes Montana's Amskapi Piikani/Blackfeet from the other constituent nations of the Blackfoot Confederacy, including Siksika, Kainai, and northern Piikani in Canada. [3] A particular artist's account is not a universal explanation of every Nation's art.

Nor is the federal institution's curatorial framing the same thing as an artist's voice. Keep the credit line, named Nation, and speaker visible. The story proposed here is the relationship between an old medium and a living person's choices, without decoding designs beyond what that person has publicly chosen to explain.

## 3. Then / now

The museum was founded in **1941**. Its institutional description combines historic arts and everyday objects with contemporary exhibitions. [2]

A separate Smithsonian collection record documents a **1941–1942 museum poster by Victor Pepion**, identified as Pikuni/Piegan, Blackfeet Nation. That object is held by the National Museum of the American Indian, following an IACB transfer in 2000; it is **not verified on display in Browning**. [5]

Today, the Browning museum's official exhibition page lists **America 250, July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027**. It also retains older shows, including Last Gun's Land/Sky and the 2024–25 acquisitions exhibition. An archive of exhibitions does not establish that those works are currently on the wall. [6]

This is a temporal comparison of named makers and public institutions, not a confirmed photographic re-creation or a claim that the museum's current display is unchanged since 1941.

## 4. What's still there

- **Public museum galleries:** the established visitor anchor; no back rooms, storage, staff-only spaces, or handling access implied. [1][2]
- **Current published exhibition period:** America 250 spans this research date. The announcement covers three IACB museums, and its illustrated Kiowa medallion is explicitly from the **Southern Plains Indian Museum**, not proof of a Browning object. [7]
- **Available online interpretation:** Last Gun's Land / Sky includes artist-authored commentary. It is a useful fallback if the related work is not displayed locally. [4]
- **Museum amenities:** the NPS partner listing identifies parking and restrooms, but a detailed current mobility-access route was not found. [8]
- **Excluded:** homes, cultural sites, community land, workshops, and routes to the places that inspire artists. Inspiration is not a visitor-access invitation.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Arrive during the operator's winter window. Confirm which galleries are open and follow the museum's photo restrictions before taking out a camera.
2. Read the institution's introduction, then choose a publicly displayed object with a named maker or Nation. Do not supply an identity when its label does not.
3. Compare a historical object's documented date with a contemporary work's date, if suitable works are available. Ask whether a date belongs to the artwork, its material, or its acquisition.
4. Read Last Gun's own statement, using the linked online exhibition if necessary. Do not promise his work is currently installed.
5. **End with the strongest story moment:** a living artist choosing how to carry history into new work. A ledger page can be old while the artistic act is new. Let that distinction replace the idea of Indigenous art as a finished chapter.

This is an editorial museum-reading sequence, not a booked tour, guaranteed object list, or artist encounter. If weather or a closure prevents admission, retain an online reading visit rather than substituting nearby cultural/private ground.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- State tourism data lists different winter hours, and its descriptive text also conflicts with its own summer schedule. The current DOI operator page takes precedence. [1][9]
- The 2024–25 winter exhibition is over; its brochure has inconsistent May end dates. Neither is evidence of a current 2026 winter installation. [6][10]
- America 250 is published as running now, not independently observed open. No complete Browning object checklist was verified. [7]
- Last Gun's Native-language name is spelled differently across institutional and artist pages; this draft uses his English name rather than silently harmonizing orthographies. [3][4]
- No original object, exhibition installation, or photo pixels were visually inspected. Text and catalog records were read.
- No curator was identified as speaking for the Blackfeet Nation; no Tribal-government endorsement is claimed.
- Current entrance accessibility, winter clearing, extra holiday closures, and personal recording policy require day-specific confirmation. No contact made.
