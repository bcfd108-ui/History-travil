## 2. The story

Beauregard Parish was carved out of Imperial Calcasieu Parish in **1912–1913**, with the lumber boomtown of **DeRidder** chosen as parish seat. Alongside a grand Beaux-Arts parish courthouse, the new police jury commissioned a remarkable **Collegiate / Late Gothic Revival** parish jail designed by the New Orleans architectural firm **Stevens & Nelson**, completed in **1914** (see §6 for conflicting `1915` contractor/cost claims). [1, 3]

Built in reinforced concrete finished in grey stucco, the jail took the form of a **four-story central tower/keep** ringed by **four protruding two-and-a-half-story cell wings** under a red clay-tile roof, complete with a **Tudor-arched wraparound porch**, an overhanging bay window, arched dormers, mock crenelations, and heavily barred multi-pane windows. Inside the central tower, a **free-standing steel spiral staircase** linked the upper detention levels, while a **subterranean concrete tunnel** connected the jail basement directly beneath the street to the neighboring courthouse so prisoners could be moved securely to trial. [1, 3]

The building's grim nickname—"Hanging Jail"—derives from **March 9, 1928**, when **Joe Genna** and **Molton Brasseaux** were hanged simultaneously inside the jail's central spiral-stair tower after being convicted of murdering DeRidder taxi driver **J. J. Brevelle**. According to parish tourist commission records, the pair were executed on a dual gallows rigged inside the central shaft—reported as the only double hanging in Beauregard Parish and one of the last legal hangings inside a Louisiana parish jail before executions shifted to Angola. [1, 2]

The building operated as the parish jail into the late twentieth century (1980s) before being preserved and listed on the **National Register of Historic Places on December 17, 1981** (`NRHP 81000711`). Today the restored interior is interpreted on Beauregard Tourist Commission daytime tours. [1, 3]

### Haunted history / lore

Local ghost-tour promotion and paranormal anecdotes circulate around the 1928 double hanging and spiral-stair tower, including claims of footsteps and cold spots; the Beauregard Tourist Commission itself focuses primarily on the documented 1914 architecture and 1928 execution record, and no unverified paranormal claims are adopted here as historical fact. [1, 2]

## 4. What's still there

- **Public (guided tour):** Central four-story tower and steel spiral staircase, surviving cell wings and barred cell doors, Tudor-arched porch, and historical exhibits interpreting the 1914 construction and March 9, 1928 execution. [1, 3]
- **Visible from public sidewalk:** Full exterior of the Gothic Revival stucco jail on Courthouse Square—red tile hipped/gabled roofs, mock crenelations, barred windows, and corner turret/bay details adjacent to the Beauregard Parish Courthouse. [3, 4]
- **Restricted / non-public:** Subterranean courthouse tunnel access (closed/non-walk-through except as guided) and active modern county courthouse/jail operations elsewhere.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Park near Courthouse Square in downtown DeRidder** (W. 1st St. & N. Pine St. / US-171) during weekday business hours. [1, 2]
2. **Walk the outer square sidewalks** first to read the Stevens & Nelson Gothic Revival silhouette—four-story tower, four projecting cell wings, red tile roof, and Tudor-arched porch—alongside the Beaux-Arts courthouse. [3, 4]
3. **Check in at the Beauregard Tourist Commission (Old Post Office across the street)** before **10:00 AM, 11:00 AM, 1:30 PM, or 3:00 PM** (Mon–Fri) to purchase admission and join the guided interior tour. [1, 2]
4. **Inside the central tower,** follow the spiral stair and cell wings around the 1928 hanging history before exiting back onto W. 1st Street. [1, 2]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Completion date & cost:** The **1981 NRHP nomination (`81000711`)** states the jail was **completed in 1914** by Stevens & Nelson. The Beauregard Tourist Commission's jail history page cites **1915** completion by the **Falls City Construction Co.** at a cost of **$168,000** (covering the courthouse/jail complex). Both dates are printed; original 1913–15 Beauregard Parish Police Jury minutes were not examined directly. [1, 3]
- **Street address spread:** Various sources place Courthouse Square at **`205 W. 1st St.`** (jail site / geocoder), **`313 W. First St.`** (Beauregard Tourist Commission courthouse listing), or Hwy. 171 / Pine St. Always check in at the **Old Post Office / Tourist Commission** across from the square rather than relying on street-number interpolation alone. [2, 3, 6]
- **Tunnel status today:** Whether the subterranean courthouse-to-jail tunnel remains physically open inside the tour route or sealed off for courthouse security was not verified from an operator floorplan.
