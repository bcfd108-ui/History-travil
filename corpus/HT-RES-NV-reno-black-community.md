## 2. The story
**Lonnie Feemster**, born and raised in Reno, describes housing discrimination from both personal experience and his work in real estate and the Reno-Sparks NAACP. In Sarah Russell's January 30, 2025 published interview article, he recalls a neighbor expressing hostility while he accompanied a Black client. He also connects exclusion from ownership with diminished opportunities to build and transfer family wealth. These are his attributed observations, not a current legal assessment of any property. No client identity or location is reproduced. [1]

**Adrienne Feemster**, in a published Our Town Reno interview about her grandmother **Dolores Feemster**, remembers community care and belonging. Her phrase **“to work from your heart outward”** describes the lesson she drew from her grandmother. She recounts Dolores opening opportunities for children excluded from existing organizations and helping young people feel supported. This is a granddaughter's firsthand perspective on family legacy, with some recollections of stories others told her, not Dolores's own recorded voice. [2]

Read together, the accounts complicate a map-centered history. Exclusion drew boundaries, but people made relationships and opportunities that a boundary alone cannot explain. That is this dossier's interpretive inference. It does not imply that resilience erased discrimination or that community members owed outsiders a hopeful ending.

This entry is specifically Reno community and housing history. It is not a retelling of Las Vegas resort segregation, and no Las Vegas interview has been substituted for a Reno voice. The speakers represent their own experiences, not every Black resident or organization.
