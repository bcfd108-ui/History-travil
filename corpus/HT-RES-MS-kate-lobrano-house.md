# Bay St. Louis through the moves of the Kate Lobrano House

*On Old Ground · Mississippi OP23 · Labeled research draft.*

## 2. Story
The little house across from the Hancock County Courthouse was moved and turned before it became a museum. The state’s 2024 Landmark report describes a grocery around 1900 on Frank and Kate Lobrano’s Main Street property. By the 1917 Sanborn map it appears to have moved toward the back of the parcel, facing Main Street. The report suggests use as a cook’s house in that phase but does not establish it. The combination of use, position and orientation is what gives this otherwise modest structure a readable history. [2, 3, 5]

Kate Maynard Lobrano planned a small rear parcel for her mother, Rebecca Jane Douglas Maynard. The state researchers reconstruct the next move by comparing maps: the house turned toward Cue Street and gained a wing by the 1924 edition. The original house and later gallery arrangement let several rooms open off the side porch. For a family, the circulation outside the rooms meant a different balance of privacy and weather than a single line of doors through the rooms. [2, 3]

The family’s 1988 donation put the cottage to a third use. The recorded quitclaim, reproduced in the Mississippi board packet, says the parcel would revert if “ever be vacated or not used by the Hancock County Historical Society”. The named grantors put an explicit condition on that transfer. The society moved in in 1990, enlarged the premises and developed public interpretation and collections. The deed is the grantors’ record of the transfer condition. The state report and the society describe the resulting uses. [1–4]

Katrina damaged the roof and walls. The designation report describes rebuilding with new exterior materials and distinguishes restoration from the late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century record. The porch, window pattern and doors give a visitor ways to read the earlier building even after those repairs. [2, 3]

## 3. Then and now
The surviving one-story house now contains a turn-of-the-century house museum and historical society operations. The former positions on the Lobrano parcel and the current building at 108 Cue Street are separate phases. The state identifies the house today as element 269 in the Old Bay St. Louis Historic District and separately as a Mississippi Landmark. The district reference applies to the larger historic district. [1–4, 6]

## 5. Public progression
Begin at the staffed museum entrance across from the courthouse side of Cue Street. Look along the gallery and note the doors that open onto it. In the exhibit rooms, follow the three places the building occupied in the family parcel as reconstructed by the state’s researchers. End at **The House That Turned**, with the Cue Street facade in view from the lawful museum approach. Its front orientation is evidence of the decision to make a different home out of an earlier building. [1–5]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements
- The society calls the cottage an 1896 building. The MDAH report says around 1900 and identifies an original grocery use. The report treats the rear-parcel cook’s-house interpretation as possible and arrives at the later orientation by maps. Neither account establishes an exact building date from a construction contract. [1–4]
- The 1988 gift, the instrument filed in January 1989 and the 1990 move-in are different milestones. Do not collapse them into one opening date. The society gives 1977 for its founding; the MDAH packet describes formation in 1974. Charter and organizational records could distinguish commencement and incorporation. [1–4]
- LOC’s 1917 map reader returned HTTP 403 and its digital JSON attempt returned HTTP 429. No sheet or map overlay was verified here. The narrative attributes the map interpretation to the state’s 2024 researchers. The legacy district nomination is accessible; the later district PDF endpoint yielded no readable document in the web reader. [2, 5–7]
