# Miami — the manager and the last feature
Research draft · Oklahoma OP05 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

The opening program belongs to April 18–19, 1929 in the Coleman Theatre’s archive. The organ had arrived February 21, the operator reports. A theatre program depended on people working behind a visible show: the ticket desk, the stage, sound and projection. The program is a physical record of the opening; its full internal page text failed in the online reader, so the dossier uses its catalog date and does not assign individual opening-night jobs. [2–3]

Another workday appears in a January 4, 1990 clipping, which names **Serita Turner** as the manager who described the last feature film before the shutdown. The contemporary headline says, “Coleman shows its last movie.” The paper’s report concerns the close of the old film operation. NPS documents public and private restoration work after the 1989 donation. The operator now advertises performances and makes clear that the original Wurlitzer again accompanies some films. The headline shows what a work stoppage could feel like on the day it happened. The marquee did light up again. [1,3–5]

The building’s surviving room layout and organ made the restoration more than a cosmetic facelift. The operator describes a ballroom left unfinished in 1929 and subsequently used for set construction and by Charles Banks Wilson for large works. His story supplies another named user of the building, without assuming any ordinary visit will enter that room. [3]

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic ground.** Original theatre at North Main and its historic workspaces. **Lawful physical anchor.** Signed public streetside approach and sidewalk at 103 N Main. Interior tour only when ordinary public availability is confirmed by the operator. [1,3,5]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public exterior — theatre façade and marquee from an open municipal sidewalk. Public interior when confirmed — ordinary tour areas. Stage, backstage, ballroom and organ operation follow operator programming. No demonstration promised. [1,3,5]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

Approach the public frontage. Read the opening-program date and the loss of the old film operation. If the operator confirms an ordinary tour, follow it. **Strongest story moment — The blank marquee.** Read the 1990 headline at the public frontage and compare it with the present calendar. [1–5]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

NPS describes the building's architectural detail and current operation, and the 1990 clipping uses the term Moorish while the operator styles the facade Spanish Colonial Mission. The interior release status and dates should follow the operator.
