## 2. The story

Oakley's institutional history dates back to **1894**, when the Mississippi Legislature appropriated $125,000 to acquire plantation tracts—including **2,700 acres at Oakley Plantation** in Hinds County—as state penal farms to replace private convict leasing (`Rankin`, `Oakley`, and `Belmont`). In its early prison-farm years, Oakley housed women prisoners and the state prison hospital (until women inmates transferred to Parchman); on **July 21, 1913**, a documented fire swept through the Oakley Prison Farm, killing **35 Black prisoners**—a tragic historical event noted plainly without sensationalism. [2, 3]

In **1925**, after overcrowding at two white prison camps, the state transferred 75 youths aged 14 to 21 to Oakley, beginning its shift toward juvenile confinement. Separately, the **Mississippi State Federation of Colored Women's Clubs (MSFCWC)** spent more than a decade campaigning for a dedicated educational reform school to keep African American youth off adult penal farms—opening the short-lived Margaret Murray Washington Home in Clinton in November 1929 (destroyed by fire a year later) and winning passage of **Senate Bill No. 239 in 1940**, which made the state responsible for maintaining a reformatory for Black youth at **Oakley Training School** alongside the segregated white Columbia Training School. [3, 4]

Both institutions were **desegregated around 1970** (with older boys assigned to Oakley and younger boys and girls initially assigned to Columbia), and following Columbia's 2008 closure, **Oakley Youth Development Center** remains Mississippi's sole state juvenile correctional campus (1,068-acre agricultural tract, 150-bed capacity). [1, 3, 4]

### Haunted history / lore

No haunted-place folklore is carried here. Documented fatalities (including the July 21, 1913 prison-farm fire) are reported strictly as historical fact without sensational framing. [2, 3]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Establishment date (`1894` vs. `1910` vs. `1925` vs. `1940`):** The sweep supplement cited **`est. 1910`**. Official MDOC records establish state purchase of Oakley Plantation in **1894**; secondary penal histories date the first juvenile camp conversion to **1925**; and *Mississippi Encyclopedia* documents **Senate Bill No. 239 (1940)** formally establishing **Oakley Training School** as a state reform school for Black youth. All primary milestones are preserved here rather than the unverified `1910` figure. [2, 3, 4]
- **1913 fire & prison-hospital burials:** Whether an institutional burial plot survives from the 1894–1925 Oakley prison-farm/hospital era or the July 21, 1913 fire was not verified from Hinds County or MDAH records this run.
