## 2. The story
**Original interpretive angle:** gaining entry to a profession and feeling accepted by colleagues are not the same achievement. A history of work becomes more useful when it allows both accomplishment and difficulty to remain visible.

**Named firsthand worker:** Wendy Kamm, interviewed by **Kathy Root on November 30, 1984**, in the University of Montana's Smokejumpers 1984 Reunion Oral History Project. The university identifies her as one of the first three women smokejumpers at Missoula in 1982. Archive identifier: **OH_133_053**. [2]

The transcript was downloaded and its relevant passage read. Printed page 5, PDF page 6 including the cover, was rendered and visually checked. Root asks how men received the three women. Kamm answers: **“It was mixed. Some were pleased, and some weren't at all pleased.”** She also recalls closeness among people from the same rookie class. [3]

This is Kamm's attributed memory, not a statement about every male colleague or a judgment about today's workforce. The fuller interview includes her account of harassment. Its presence prevents a simple celebratory narrative from standing in for what she said. It does not establish an independently adjudicated finding against a named individual. [2][3]

**Original visitor narration:** A milestone can fit on a plaque. Belonging usually cannot. Listen for the difference between being hired and being able to become a colleague, a friend and a person known for more than being first. History can honor the achievement without asking the worker to remember only the good parts.

This question is about workplace culture, not an invitation to evaluate a visitor's strength or reproduce recruitment tests. No operational firefighting, aviation, equipment, dispatch or training details from the transcript are repeated. A published historical account is not a safe-practice manual.

The narrative remains deliberately personal. We have not reconstructed Kamm's complete employment file, family circumstances, wages or career chronology. Nor have we collected a representative sample of women's experiences. Her testimony earns its place because it is hers, not because it can stand for everyone.
