## 2. Story
**Ave Maria Grotto, Cullman, Alabama · Research draft · Fred**

A person walking through this former quarry sees two sorts of work. The first is the tiny buildings made by Brother Joseph Zoetl. The second is the work needed to make a path and plant a hillside around them. Zoetl came to the new Benedictine abbey in 1892 from Bavaria. His regular work included shoveling coal in its power house. The abbey dates his first miniatures to about 1912. While his subjects included buildings he had seen, including ones in Landshut and at the abbey, printed descriptions and pictures gave him many more places to work from. [3–4]

Nomination writer Linda Bayer describes components made first at a table, using “simple hand tools,” including pliers and brushes. Tiles, small figurines, glass and objects people discarded went into surfaces cast or modeled with cement. A ring or a reflector had a new job once it became a fragment of a wall. Bayer also names Brother Patrick O'Neill, who landscaped the quarry in 1932–34. His planting and paths let the visitor look into a model district without visually overwhelming its scale. The abbey opened the relocated display in 1934. The institution dates Zoetl's final work, a miniature of Lourdes Basilica, to 1958. [3–4]

## 3. Then and now
Early models stood near the monastery gardens. The former quarry became their permanent setting, with terracing and planted slopes. The abbey's present guide locates works in the ticketed outdoor display. Individual surfaces may have undergone maintenance. The form can identify an original composition without assigning a date to every fragment now visible. [3–5]

## 4. Surviving features and access
- **Authorized exterior** — Landscaped display path, model groups, retaining walls and cavern-like Ave Maria Grotto, viewed from the visitor path.
- **Public service interior when open** — Admissions and gift shop only.
- **Outside this itinerary** — Monastic buildings, work areas, cemetery and planted slopes. Stay on the marked exhibit walk. [1, 3–5]

## 5. Public trip progression
Start at the signed admissions entrance. On the exhibit path look at the low retaining wall and the level at which the models were meant to be seen. End on the signed path in front of the main grotto at **The quarry becomes an exhibit**, where the excavated slope and the constructed grotto can be understood together. [3–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
- NPS lists 1932 and 1934 as significant years. The nomination attributes quarry landscaping to 1932–34 and the abbey dates the opening to 1934. Those dates apply to different stages. The abbey's approximate 1912 date is for Zoetl's earlier work. [3–4, 6]
- The operator's ordinary Sunday opening reads 11:30. Its 2026 event page lists noon Sundays during its separate Christmas program. Use the applicable dated schedule and check with the operator. [1–2]
- The official register uses Zoetl; artist archive listings also use Zoettl. Filed under the site, with both spellings available for search. No letter by Zoetl or maintenance inventory assigning dates to individual fragments was verified. [3, 6–7]
