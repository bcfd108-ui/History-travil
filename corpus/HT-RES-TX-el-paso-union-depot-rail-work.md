# El Paso Union Depot — the March notice that removed a transfer
Research draft · Texas OP03 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### A new station changed the paperwork

In February 1906, general passenger agent V. R. Stiles advised connecting agents of a change effective March 1. The Houston Daily Post reprinted the notice, including “Acknowledge receipt on inclosed card.” The circular told offices to stop requiring a local transfer on through tickets and to stop including a 50-cent transfer charge in through rates using the specified lines. One shared address simplified a trip across multiple railroads. The instructions changed records and prices at other stations before passengers entered this one [3].

### The doorway joined a business street

The paper expected lines entering the city to share the station. Its editorial assertion describes the plan, without measuring subsequent traffic. Burnham’s depot presented a public face to a district of hotels and commerce. Walter H. Horne’s c. 1916 postcard shows rails to one side of a city street with commercial buildings and a streetcar. It is a contextual view of the station district, visibly distinct from Byrd Williams Jr.’s direct depot photograph [1, 3–6].

### A railway building can still be a station

Amtrak lists a station building and waiting room at the historic address. Seen from the street, the prominent tower and large openings mark the continuing building. The 1906 notice gives that frontage a particular function: the passenger no longer had to purchase the separate through transfer that the circular withdrew [1, 3–5].

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic place.** El Paso Union Passenger Station at West San Francisco Avenue and the adjoining railroad facilities [1, 4].

**Lawful physical anchor.** Public street sidewalk facing the passenger frontage, 700 West San Francisco Avenue. Use ordinary downtown legal parking and a short signed public sidewalk approach. Avoid trackside, platforms, driveways and controlled doors. Access to the station interior follows Amtrak service and instructions and is outside this tourism plan [1–2].

1. From public sidewalk view, locate the tower in relation to the station’s street frontage [1, 4–5].
2. Read the district postcard as evidence of the approaches, leaving its higher vantage unresolved [6].
3. **Strongest story moment — Fifty cents came off the ticket.** Read Stiles’s instructions from the public frontage. The circular sends the expense change out to the same rail network now meeting at your feet [1, 3].

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The NPS Register index prints 1905 as a significant year. The 1906 circular gives its own service-effective date, March 1, and Amtrak describes completion in 1906 [1, 3–4]. Do not use the index label as a proven day of opening. The newspaper masthead and page image were read, with notice wording cross-checked in the dated page transcription. Direct Sun Metro Union Depot visitor URL did not return a readable page [7]. A general city sightseeing interior schedule is unverified.
