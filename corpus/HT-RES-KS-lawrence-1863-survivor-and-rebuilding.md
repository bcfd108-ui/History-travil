# Lawrence in 1863 — the survivor’s words and the rebuilt block

Kansas OP04 · Draft research for Dash · November 19, 2026 Pacific

## 2. The story

### A letter from a town trying to account for itself

August 21, 1863 marks the attack and killings in Lawrence. The Kansas Historical Society preserves an excerpt of Mary Savage’s letter to Jane Simpson, dated November 29. In the source’s published excerpt she describes the downtown afterward as “a heap of ashes.” The words are a survivor’s experience written months later. The same account tells of her defending her husband and of women putting out fires. Recovery begins in this record at the scale of people protecting one another. [3,4]

George Edwin Young wrote to his father on August 23, two days after the attack. KU’s finding aid identifies Young as a traveler lodging at the Johnson House en route to hired work. The letter was sent from Minneola and survives unsigned with a typed annotated transcription. The finding aid and KU’s facsimile notice were read. Young’s trip gives a way of seeing Lawrence as a place where travelers and workers were caught alongside residents. The visit does not recreate his route, lodging or refuge. At the Eldridge House, Captain Alexander Banks’s recollection, published by the city’s visitor bureau, records another person’s experience of the day. The two hotels remain distinct. [2,5,6]

### Reading a replacement building

The present Eldridge House Hotel is at the corner of Seventh and Massachusetts. Its nomination dates the main exterior to work built in two stages, 1925–26 and 1926–28. It replaced the hotel of about 1866, which rose after the destruction of an earlier building in 1863. The nomination says one low portion appears to date from the 1866 hotel. That attribution is qualified and is not a claim about what can be seen or approached. The rebuilt block and successive hotel images let a traveler read loss and decisions to rebuild without calling the brick exterior a survivor of August 1863. [2,7–9]

## 3. Then / now

The historical anchor in this dossier is the Eldridge House block. Mary Savage and George Young are documents of the wider city, and Young’s lodging was the Johnson House, elsewhere. The lawful visit is the public sidewalk at Seventh and Massachusetts looking at the current Eldridge frontage from outside, using signed metered downtown parking for a short walk. No hotel interior, service area, private lot, grave or private home is included. [1–3,5,7]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The city bureau and survivor records date the attack August 21, 1863. The current hotel’s nomination calls the earlier hotel's destruction “spring, 1863” in one narrative line. Preserve that inconsistency and use the dated event record. [2,5,7]
- Kansas Historical Society writes of 150 men dead or dying by noon. NPS gives 150 total estimated casualties. Explore Lawrence gives about 200 male residents killed. These scopes and definitions differ, and no individual reconciliation was established. [2,4,10]
