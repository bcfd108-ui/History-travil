## 2. The story
**Mark Johnson's Denver organizing recollections** appear in Chava Willig Levy's 1988 *A People's History of the Independent Living Movement*, published by the University of Kansas Research and Training Center on Independent Living. Levy explicitly thanks named contributors, including Johnson, for willingly offering their accounts. The Denver section connects Johnson's growing involvement to inaccessible transit and the Atlantis community. This is published participant evidence mediated by an author, not a newly obtained interview or a formal release inspected here. [7]

The important issue is not whether someone can overcome an obstacle through extraordinary effort. It is whether the ordinary city is available to them at all. A home without usable transportation does not deliver equal participation. That framing keeps agency with disabled people rather than treating them as beneficiaries of someone else's kindness.

A 2026 NPR/CPR report includes interviews with participants and organizers, including **Barry Rosenberg** and **Bobby Simpson**. Simpson describes the historical action as consequential in his own terms: **“It changed lives.”** The report also quotes Brian Macleod's earlier recollection of people recognizing their capacity to effect change. These are attributed public accounts; no private health history, care details, home address or operational protest method is carried into this dossier. [8]

The selected sources distinguish organizing, independent living and public access as connected concerns. They do not support crediting one person or one demonstration with the whole national movement. No present-day legal rights advice or tactical instructions are offered.

## 3. Then / now
- **1978:** Denver's transit-access campaign is the historical focus documented by the local library and participant reporting. [8,9]
- **1988:** Levy's account records movement voices before the ADA's passage; it is not retrospective proof of every later policy outcome. [7]
- **2014:** DPL announces receipt of the **Laura Hershey papers, WH2274**, and **Wade and Molly Blank papers, WH2283**. The latter is a named archival lead, not proof that every folder is open today. [9]
- **2026:** public reporting revisits the movement, while Atlantis presents an institutionally curated history. Publication dates and remembered event dates remain separate. [5,8]

The endpoint is not “access was solved.” It is an understanding that design, services and equal participation must be checked in practice, including for this proposed history visit.
