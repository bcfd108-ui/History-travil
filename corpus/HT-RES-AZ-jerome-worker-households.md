## 2. The story

**Named documented household/community worker: Mary (Shea) Lyons.** Sharlot Hall Museum's institutional oral-history catalog identifies her December 8, 1977 interview with **Cecelia Clack**, audio **113A, Side 1**, acquisition **ACC#2021.068**, duration **20:19**. Listed subjects include living in Jerome during **1925–1953** and teaching in its schools. The catalog marks it digitized, without a transcript, and warns that the recording ends abruptly. The catalog record was read; the audio was **not heard**. [3]

A second, distinct interview, October 31, 1974, with **Virginia Rice**, is cataloged as audio **1421–1422**, **ACC#88.015**, duration **1:37:19**. It covers family history, schools, library and social life, among other subjects. It is not combined with the 1977 interview or treated as the same recording. [3]

**Original interpretive voice:** A mining town is still a town when the account leaves the mine gate. Families need learning, books, care and company. A teacher's record changes the scale of the story: from what the ground yielded to what people built around a working life. The household is not decorative background to industry. It is part of the community that made sustained work possible.

This interpretation is deliberately modest. Catalog subjects validate the research direction; they do not supply anecdotes, exact classroom practices, quotations, wages or family experiences. No sentence is placed in Lyons's mouth.
