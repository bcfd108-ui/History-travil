# Shattuck — the wheels that had to work
Research draft · Oklahoma OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

Daniel Halladay’s 1854 patent says, “the pump T draws the Water from a Well.” Its longer description explains that rotating sails and a governor change the angle presented to the wind. The patent is an original public technical record. It explains a working principle without identifying any one of Shattuck’s installed display mills as the patent example. [3]

Public-radio reporter Rachel Hopkin interviewed longtime Shattuck volunteer Doug Schoenhals. He names Phillis Ballew, who wanted to put up a windmill like her grandfather’s. Marvin Stinson restored it and lent additional mills, and volunteers assembled to build a park. Schoenhals recalled, “At our first official meeting we had nine people there.” Lorenz’s interview describes why she kept taking part. These are named witnesses to preservation work and different evidence from the patent. The museum’s original Eclipse restoration stays indoors for protection according to the 2023 reporting. [2,4]

## 3 · Then / now

The historic wells, farms and mills that supplied water were distributed across the region. The museum is a curated collection with a present public park anchor. Use signed off-road parking and outdoor exhibit paths only. Observe moving equipment from established visitor space. [1–2,4]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public exterior — signed outdoor paths and visible windmills. The dugout is reconstructed, and the historic farmhouse was moved from south of town. Public interior of the mercantile or protected display depends on operator access. No private well or working farm visit. [1–2]
