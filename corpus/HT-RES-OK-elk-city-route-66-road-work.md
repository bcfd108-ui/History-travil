# Elk City — the work under the new highway
Research draft · Oklahoma OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

On September 4, 1958, the *Elk City Journal* reported a planned start to East Elk City paving. It named Jess Reagan, superintendent for Peter Kiewit Sons, and distinguished work on the stabilized base from underpass, grading and drainage work by other firms. Its headline reads “Paving Start Due On ’66 Elk East.” The report relayed what the superintendent expected as work progressed. It does not establish that the date was met. [2]

Oklahoma SHPO’s roadbed study quotes the 1926 state highway designation and explains the changing paving standards and successive alignments. Compare that study with the September article. Highway work did not amount to one moment of painting a new number. Drainage and grade had to be ready before paving, and firms handled different portions. The paper says the underpass east of town had just been completed. The workers’ names and exact modern survival of the reported component sites remain open. [2–3]

The city’s Museum Complex interprets the road. Its restored settings and replicas serve as interpretation. The historic construction zone is distinct and offers no independent verified visitor footing in this record. [1,3]

## 3 · Then / now

Historic East Elk City roadwork is identified by the newspaper’s project description, with no current alignment match established sufficiently for visitor routing. Public physical anchor is the Elk City Museum Complex signed parking and front entrance, short walk. [1–3]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior — city museum galleries. Public exterior — designated visitor grounds only. Specific 1958 drainage, roadbed and underpass fabric are unverified. No roadside field stop. [1–3]

The report’s planned and projected dates are expectations. Opening notice, final acceptance and the identity of the other construction workers remain unverified.
