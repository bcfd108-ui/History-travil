# Marianna — The relief boat came in

**HT-RES-AR-marianna-st-francis-levee-workers · AR OP06 with OP07 source correction · Labeled research draft · November 20, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate
Use the open public sidewalk at Court Square Park near Poplar and Main in daylight and passable weather. Use marked crossings and lawful parking. Posted closures govern in every season. This outdoor stop excludes courthouse interiors, levees, works yards, shore and rescue landings. [1, 2, 8, 9]

## 2. Story
An April 1897 newspaper reported the relief steamer Ora Lee’s return to Marianna from an expedition up the St. Francis River. The dispatch counted 160 people and 200 head of cattle aboard. Those are a correspondent’s reported figures, unconfirmed by a manifest. The combination shows how a rescue could take on the work of keeping a farm household alive after the water fell. [3]

Thirty years later Captain Joseph V. De La Hunt recounted the U.S. Steamer Augustine’s flood work. The original typescript records about 2,000 livestock and 1,500 people delivered to Marianna, a relief headquarters under Colonel Robinson. It separately records transfer of two barges to the Harry Lee, also bound for Marianna. These are a participant’s approximate counts for that work, drawn from the original typescript. They belong to 1927 and are separate from the 1897 newspaper figures. [4, 7]

The boats had different jobs. De La Hunt named levee board member C. E. Yancey, who directed the steamer toward its field headquarters. He also named Joe Smith. In places where the steamer could not go, Smith used his gas boat to take the barges out and bring them back. The captain wrote that the crew had to “go out into the field for half mile or more at times.” He also credited the Marianna committee and residents with the relief work. The account names a boat operator, a levee official and a relief organizer. Their names identify roles without supplying a verified public landing. [7, PDF p. 2]

## 3. Then and now
The 1897 dispatch records an arrival at Marianna with no landing address. The district nomination dates civic improvements around the square to the following decade, including street and sidewalk work and a Civic Improvement League project in the park. The modern courthouse address locates the civic reading stop. It leaves the water landing unresolved. [1–3]

## 4. Surviving features and access
- City Square Park public sidewalk: civic exterior. Give court business, memorial users and pedestrians room. [1, 2]
- Courthouse: exterior context only. The working court is an address reference. [1]
- Historic landing, levees and operating works: documentary only. No inferred public access. [3, 4]

## 5. Visit progression
Park in a lawful marked public space if available and walk to the square-side public sidewalk. Read the rescue figures there and pause on **the smaller boat that could reach the fields**. The square gives the story a town anchor without sending visitors to flood-control ground. This is a quiet-witness stop. [1–3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
The 1897 page image and original 1927 typescript were inspected during OP07. The page title identifies J. V. De La Hunt as author. The 1927 pilot list appears to type J. J. De La Hunt. Original manifests, a second independent tally, named 1897 crew, levee labor payroll and precise landing locations remain open. The 1927 recovered document confirms Marianna’s direct relief role, which its abbreviated finding description did not establish. The state courts address and district nomination building address differ (15 and 16 East Chestnut). Use Poplar and Main for the square. [1–4, 7–9]
