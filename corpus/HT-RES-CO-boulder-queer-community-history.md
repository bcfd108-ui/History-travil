## 2. The story
**Mardi Moore**, an adult community organizer, speaks in Caitlin Rockett's January 29, 2015 *Boulder Weekly* profile about making local LGBTQ support reach more of Boulder County. Her account describes organizational work, not just public reputation: deciding whose needs receive programming, funding and attention. A concise excerpt is **“get resources assigned to all the alphabet.”** [3]

This is a published interview containing her directly attributed words. It is not an inferred identity from a photograph or association. The full article was read, but intimate family detail, private addresses, traumatic episodes and unrelated locations are deliberately not reproduced. No raw recording or signed release was inspected. Publication establishes a public account, not blanket consent to commercial reuse or a new interview. [3]

A second firsthand perspective concerns the work of preserving community memory. **Emily Howard**, who processed the Boulder Gay Liberation collection, explains in the university's 2023 published account why dance flyers mattered: **“It's nice to see the joy and celebration present there as well.”** This is Howard's account of archival work, not a claim to have participated in the 1970s organization. [4]

Together these accounts suggest an interpretive question: what practical labor lets a community become visible on its own terms? The proposed answer follows resources and records rather than turning someone else's disclosure or suffering into entertainment.

## 3. Then / now
