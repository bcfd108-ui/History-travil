## 2. Story
Luke Frederick started at the cash register in 1989. Speaking to Red River Radio as store president in 2014, he described the inventory as stretching “from water pipe to a Waterford Crystal.” The remark gives the old general store a modern problem. An independent shop had to stock the fittings somebody needed that afternoon and also offer goods the larger retailers could not match. His interview describes a working shop making decisions about its future, and his family account connects it to the Kaffies’ earlier trade. [2]

HABS identifies Harris and Adolph Kaffie as the founders of H. Kaffie and Brother and places the firm’s beginnings in 1863. The operator’s family history connects the enterprise to earlier travel selling and dates the present Front Street lot and building transaction to 1893. The surviving two-story shop belongs to the later stage of the business. HABS documents a parapet with a central sunburst and describes ground-floor alterations in the 1930s. Read those levels together from the sidewalk: the ornamental upper front tells you what the owners wanted a durable commercial address to say, while the windows show that the store could change how it sold. [3–4]

The story continued through people who worked there. Luke’s account in a second interview identifies his grandfather John Titus Frederick as a young floor sweeper hired in 1920 and describes furnishings he later made. HABS traces the transfer to J. Titus Frederick through conveyance book 301, folio 522; the deed itself was not retrieved. The register, bins and elevator mentioned in interviews are working-store objects under the owner’s control. Ask staff whether any is currently visible. An actual purchase is optional. [2–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
The retailer and HABS date the business to 1863 and the current store to a later period. The family account identifies an 1893 transaction, a television interview labels the building 1892, and HABS describes late nineteenth-century fabric. Original deed, building permit and nineteenth-century stock book were not read; keep exact construction year open.
