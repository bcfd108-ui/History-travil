## 2. The story
**Named historical workers:** a 2023 research article coauthored by museum professional Steven D. Sroka identifies **G. Ernest Untermann**, museum director, and **Billie Untermann**, staff scientist, in a dated historical photograph. This supplies named evidence of museum work rather than treating a display as something that simply appeared. [4]

**Primary voice, mediated quotation:** the article reproduces part of G. Ernest Untermann's 1959 *Curator* account, *A replica of Diplodocus*. His short statement **“We still have the molds in Vernal”** reveals a practical stewardship concern. The 1959 original was not independently retrieved; the quotation is read through the later research paper, not presented as an interview personally heard. [4][5]

**Original interpretive angle:** visitors often look through a museum object to the ancient animal it represents. This entry asks them to look once at the people who made that act of looking possible. Museum history includes naming, interpretation, care and decisions about what can be shown. Those are human stories, not just stages in a specimen's journey.

Billie's professional title should remain visible rather than reducing her role to a family relationship. At the same time, the retrieved photograph and article are not her own spoken account. Do not write invented first-person dialogue for her or turn a documented partnership into an unsupported division of labor.

The focus is historical museum-making and public understanding, not a how-to guide to preparation, casting, excavation or display construction. No dangerous method from the historical record is reproduced.

## 3. Then / now
The research paper distinguishes the historical concrete display from a later replacement and reports a move to the new Field House location in **2004**. A historical exhibit photograph therefore cannot be assumed to depict the current gallery or its current object. [4]
