# Eudora Welty House & Garden, a writer’s desk and her mother’s planting book

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP09 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Start at the visitor center at 1109 Pinehurst Street, Jackson. The historic house is next door at 1119. House tours are guided and ticketed. The garden has independent free visiting hours. [1]

## 2. Story

Eudora Welty was sixteen when her family moved to Pinehurst Street in 1925. Her upstairs bedroom at the front of the house also became her writing room. The setting survives, but a desk cannot show what it took to make a story. The Mississippi archives preserve typescript pages of ‘A Worn Path’ with editorial queries, Welty’s replies and handwritten revisions. The story appeared in A Curtain of Green in 1941. Read a revised page alongside the account of her writing room and the house becomes a place of daily work. [2,3]

## 3. Then and now

Outside, another worker kept another kind of record. Chestina Andrews Welty designed the garden in 1925. Mother and daughter planted, weeded and tended it together. Chestina’s surviving papers include a garden journal for 1937–41 with later entries in 1943 and 1945, a notebook, sketches and negatives. She recorded the weather and flower arrangements as well as planting plans. Her papers make it possible to identify what she was trying to grow in a given season. [4,5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The garden visitors see is a restoration. Conversations with Eudora, photographs and Chestina’s records helped guide it. The borders divide the grounds into outdoor rooms, with camellias among the plants that carry color into winter. Susan Haltom’s account of restoring the site documents the work after the family’s own gardening years. The surviving design and the survival of each individual plant are different claims. [5,6]

## 5. Public progression

On a house tour, begin at the visitor center; a short film precedes the guided visit. A person with time only for the garden can enter during free hours, Tuesday–Friday 8:30 a.m.–4 p.m. or Saturday 12:30–4 p.m. The house lists four weekday tour starts and two Saturday afternoons. Available places vary. [1,5]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

No claim is made that a specific paragraph was written on a specific date at the displayed desk. Nor is each present specimen identifiable as an original family planting. The manuscripts preserve revision; the journals and photographs document the garden at particular times. [3–6]
