## 2. The story
Start with families, not the military buildings. **Agatha Bart** and **Pauline Flett** appear in the Colville *Tribal Tribune*'s October 14, 2010 report, “Fort Spokane Healing Ceremony,” on printed page 6. Bart shared her mother's removal to the school from a Colville perspective. Flett spoke about her Spokane mother's punishment for speaking her language. These are published descendant accounts reported at a September 10 gathering, not invented voices for unnamed children in an archival photograph. [6]

Flett's forward-looking phrase, **“together relearn our languages, our histories, and our cultures,”** belongs to her reported remarks. It is not permission for an outside writer to declare healing complete, tell other families how to grieve, or prescribe a ceremony. The article identifies the gathering as organized by Colville and Spokane Tribal members. [6]

A separate published interview with **Sulustu (Barry Moses)**, a Spokane Tribal member, describes his reading of school and agency correspondence about resistance to enrollment and coercion. His account cautions against equating officials' claims of willing attendance with every family's experience. The underlying correspondence was not retrieved here, so this is his reported account of research, not independent collation of those letters. [7]

NPS documents the institution's assimilation purpose and forced separation, but its interpretation is kept distinct from Tribal families' testimony. Its article credits Tribal assistance with the exhibit and uses archival institutional photographs; neither makes the agency the speaker for every family. [8]

The story is of family separation, coercion and the continued transmission and renewal of language and culture. It is not a nostalgic school tour or a lesson in how children were “civilized.”

## 3. Then / now
**Then:** A military installation was repurposed for a government boarding school and later medical uses. Institutional functions and dates overlap in the record. Children and families experienced this landscape differently from the officials administering it. [8–9]

**Now:** NPS manages the public historic site and a seasonal museum. The March 10, 2026 visitor-center page says exhibits were developed with assistance from the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation and Spokane Tribe of Indians; it also says education programs are unavailable. That interpretive partnership is real, but it is not a current invitation to a Tribal gathering. [10]

Modern museum spaces are not unchanged school rooms. Interpretation should acknowledge what was removed, restored or repurposed rather than stage a visitor inside an imagined child's experience.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Reflection framework, not a route:
1. Begin remotely with the named family accounts and the closed-museum status.
2. Only if a public exterior is actually open and safe, use its designated approach; otherwise remain with the archival reading.
3. Distinguish the institution's records from the descendants' accounts. Ask whose choices and voices are visible in each.
4. Read the limited published testimony without reenacting punishment, separation or discipline.
5. **Strongest ending:** Attend to continued language and cultural life, while leaving decisions about remembrance and healing with the families and Tribes themselves.

No prescribed prayer, song, imitation ceremony or emotional role-play. No requirement that a visitor photograph the site to complete the reflection. Visitors may stop reading or leave.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **School dates:** NPS's historical article gives 1900–1907; the federal boarding-school profile gives an end as late as 1914 and discusses different closure/medical-use dates. These are not silently reconciled into one unqualified closing year. [8–9]
- **Important attribution trap:** The NPS article's vivid laundry recollection is **Irene Stewart's account of an Arizona boarding school**, not a Fort Spokane pupil's testimony. It is not reassigned to the photographed girls or to Spokane/Colville families. [8]
- **Descendant evidence:** The 2010 report preserves family accounts but is not a complete transcript or recording. Its publication date is 2010 despite a later file-host timestamp. No child's name or genealogy added beyond the text. [6]
- **Photographs:** An institutional image cannot establish consent, feelings, identity or the entire experience of those shown.
- **Winter opening:** July reopening language and an expired construction date are stale indicators. Off-season closure is explicit; exterior weather/parking access remains unverified. [1]
- **Fire evidence:** The August closure report does not settle November access. [5]
- **Burial evidence:** No finding, count, location or absence of graves is established here. Reports from other schools must not be transferred to this site.
