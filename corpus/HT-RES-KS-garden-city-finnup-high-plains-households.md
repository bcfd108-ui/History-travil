## 2. The story
The Little Finnup House nomination traces George Finnup's working life back to his father's lumberyard and general store in 1884. The same nomination credits him with Frederick Finnup Park as a memorial to his father. That is a documented connection between a local family's commercial life and present public ground. [2]

A second voice shows how differently a household could experience the High Plains. Former Garden City legislator Dave Heinemann told Rex Buchanan in 2021 about ditch irrigation: a manager he remembered as Mr. Mangan announced a thirty-six-hour turn. Out at night, Heinemann listened for the horse's hooves to “slosh, slosh, slosh,” telling him where the water had reached. This is firsthand recollection, not a current allotment rule. The transcript does not identify his work as Finnup employment or put that field in today's park. [3]

**Strongest story moment:** The horse’s sound marked where the water had reached during one family’s allotted turn. This stays in the reading until an on-ground link is documented. [3]
