# Des Moines Center Street — The neighborhood that knew whose son you were

*Iowa OP02 research draft for Dash.*

## 2. Story

### A neighborhood that recognized its children

Richard Duncan moved to Center Street at five. In Iowa Public Radio’s printed account of its October 2026 interview, he remembers music from clubs, people formally dressed, and a business district with Black owners and patrons. “Everyone knew one another,” he said. In the same account he remembers the disappearance of that togetherness. Read these as Duncan’s recollections, published in his own words inside the broadcaster’s account. [4]

The state historical marker places the neighborhood between 15th Street and Keosauqua Way. It names businesses, practitioners, the Crocker YMCA and Willkie House alongside homes. The Iowa Department of Education’s published neighborhood example, hosted by ERIC, describes Bystander advertising and the relationship between Buxton’s mine closures and some Black families’ movement to Des Moines. That is an account of a broader migration. It supplies no evidence that the particular Buxton Brown household moved here. [1,5]

### Highway construction and clearance

The state marker attributes displacement to 1960s urban renewal for I-235 and public services. Iowa Public Radio distinguishes freeway construction, described as beginning in the 1950s, from the Oak Ridge urban renewal project. The highway fact sheet gives 1957 planning, a first opening on December 14, 1961 and completion on October 30, 1968. These are different stages and scopes. A construction or opening date cannot date the acquisition of Duncan’s family home. [1,4,6]

Madison DeShay-Duncan describes what below-value compensation and renewed debt meant for property-owning families and businesses in the interview. The observation is her explanation of a mechanism, not an appraisal of any one address. Individual damages require parcel evidence. One such case survives in a published Iowa Supreme Court opinion. In September 1967 the city condemned a business building owned by Rozenting and Mary Hardaway under Oak Ridge Urban Renewal. The court records $40,000 from the sheriff’s jury, the owners’ $75,000 claim and a $30,000 district court verdict. Those are three stages of a single disputed case, with no implication that any figure was a neighborhood average or the amount ultimately paid. [4,8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicting evidence

- The marker, broadcaster and highway fact sheet emphasize different construction and renewal dates. The Hardaway opinion documents one condemnation and its litigation. The original acquisition card, final payment record and Duncan household’s tract were not retrieved. [1,4,6,8]
- A study describes the November 4, 2002 Return to Center Street Oral History Project. Its original recordings or transcripts were not located in the available sources. Duncan’s short passage comes from the 2026 broadcaster text. Full audio and documentary were not used as verified transcripts. [4,7]
