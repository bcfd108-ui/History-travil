# Anchuca, the frame house behind a merchant’s brick front

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP08 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Day visitors enter at Anchuca, 1010 First East Street, Vicksburg. The operator posts self-guided tours Monday–Saturday, 9:30 a.m.–4 p.m., beginning with a short film. [1]

## 2. Story

Stand in front of Anchuca and the house seems to belong to a single design. Four full-height columns frame a small balcony above the central entrance. Move through the house on the visitor tour and the materials change. An older frame block survives behind the brick front. The operator attributes an early cottage to J. W. Mauldin and the enlargement to Jane and Victor Wilson. Victor’s nearby businesses sold coal and ice. Keeping either commodity in supply depended on the trade that connected the river landing to customers in town. [1,2,3]

## 3. Then and now

The Wilsons acquired one parcel in 1849 and an adjoining one in 1855, according to the identified Warren County deed entries. Those dates mark property transactions. They give no contract date for a bricklayer, carpenter or plasterer. The operator reports the Wilson household endured the 1863 siege and lost two children in its aftermath. Joseph Emory Davis later lived in the house and died there in September 1870. [2,3]

## 4. Surviving features and access

James Butters photographed the north elevation for the Historic American Buildings Survey in July 1936. Steps divide the front planting; a narrow central balcony sits behind the columns. By the operator’s account, public inspections began in 1955. Today the inn sells day admission independently of room reservations. [1,2,4]

## 5. Public progression

On the ordinary visit, compare the balcony visible from outside with the change from brick to wood. The most revealing part of the building is the seam between two construction campaigns. Guest rooms and service areas follow staff boundaries. [1,2]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

The Wilsons are identifiable. The workers who built and maintained this particular house have not been individually verified. No direct speech transcript or contemporary eyewitness record was found for Jefferson Davis addressing neighbors from the balcony in 1869. The operator presents that episode as local tradition. The 1830s date for the rear is approximate. [2,3]
