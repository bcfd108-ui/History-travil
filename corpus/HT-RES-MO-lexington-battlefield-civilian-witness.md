# Lexington — Two houses in the record

Missouri OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review · Quiet witness only

## 2. Story

Oliver Anderson’s brick house had been a family residence before it became a wartime hospital. In his Sept. 21, 1861 report, Sterling Price, commander of the Missouri State Guard, described it as being used “as a hospital, and upon which a white flag was flying.” He also accused federal forces of firing from it and later killing three men who had surrendered. Those are Price’s allegations in a report to the Missouri governor. The quoted words are from the printed *Official Records* image, Series I, vol. III, p. 187. The state operator’s hospital identification establishes a shared point without settling those allegations. [2–4]

On Sept. 23 the federal commander in the Western Department, John C. Frémont, wrote to the Secretary of War, “Our loss 39 killed, 120 wounded.” This short report, in the same printed volume on p. 185, was sent from headquarters and promised fuller detail. His morning telegram emphasized failed relief and loss of the water supply. The two reports describe a surrender from the positions of different commands and with different access to the evidence. Their figures disagree. [4]

Another household appears in an 1874 Senate claims report. Thomas B. Wallace petitioned for compensation for the loss of his dwelling and its contents. The report says that Wallace and his family assisted with the wounded, that the dwelling was burned on Union orders, and that there was “no time being given for their removal.” The quoted phrase comes from the committee’s printed retelling about the contents. The committee identified witnesses including Union major R. P. Van Horn and Lexington citizen John F. Tyler. Its report says an earlier bill passed both houses and was vetoed. The underlying petition and sworn testimony were not obtained. This is the record of a claim and its advocacy, and the historic loss site is separate from the surviving Anderson House. [5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historical locations.** The surviving Anderson house, the wartime hospital use and Wallace’s destroyed dwelling are separate parts of the siege record. The HABS survey dates construction of Anderson House to 1853 and records later restoration. A place where an 1861 event occurred is not automatically a confident point or an accessible approach. [2, 4–7]

**Lawful physical anchor.** At Battle of Lexington State Historic Site, 1101 Delaware, use the signed drive-up Visitor Center and its open walk to the designated public approach to the Anderson House exterior. The reservation-dependent interior is outside this progression. Remain on open state park grounds and paths. Do not enter archaeological ground, closed tour space or a purported Wallace property. ## 5. A short visit

Start at the Visitor Center, then take the open designated walk to the Anderson House exterior if conditions permit. Finish with **A hospital and a house that could not be saved**. Read a short line from Price, a line from Frémont and the Senate’s account of the Wallace family. Keep the attention on the people inside those houses and the limits of what the surviving reports can tell. During ice or a closure, conclude inside the open Visitor Center or remotely. [1–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

Price’s report separates the Sept. 13 skirmish and the Sept. 18–20 final attack. The Wallace claims report calls Sept. 12 the first day of the fighting at the house and Sept. 13 the date of its destruction. The NPS battle overview places initial skirmishing south of town Sept. 13. Its June–October header is the campaign range, wider than Missouri State Parks’ three-day battle description. Preserve the earlier disagreement with the petition record. [2, 4–5, 8]

Frémont reported 39 killed and 120 wounded on his side and estimated 1,400 killed and wounded for Price’s side. Price later in his report gives his own losses as 25 killed and 72 wounded and separately claims about 3,500 prisoners. NPS gives an estimated casualty table of 3,500 U.S. and 100 Confederate. The sources record different figures and categories. Do not add those figures or turn a count of prisoners into a death count. Price’s allegation of killings after surrender remains uncorroborated in this return. Lack of a claim record for a family member also leaves civilian experience incomplete. [4–5, 8]
