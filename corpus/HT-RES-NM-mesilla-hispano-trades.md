# Mesilla trades: women behind the counter, families behind the story

Qualified documentary entry, not approved for publication or route activation. Public museum interpretation only; no working restaurant, private shop, home or kitchen visit. Historic founder firsthand voice remains a gap.

## 1. Trip gate

**Independent public anchor.** Taylor-Mesilla Historic Site, operated by New Mexico Historic Sites, publishes **2350 Calle Principal, Mesilla**. Its Reynolds Store Visitor Center presents *Mesilla: A Place of Growth and Change*. This is a state interpretation venue, distinct from the family's operating businesses discussed below. No restaurant visit is part of this dossier. [1]

**Published gate, incomplete winter schedule.** The operator lists closure Monday and Tuesday and guided tours Friday and Saturday at **10 a.m. and 2 p.m.** General timed admission is **$10 adults**, with listed exemptions; guided tours cost **$15 adults/$5 children 16 and younger**. The reviewed pages do not provide a complete opening/closing span or verified Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's exceptions. A recurring tour listing is not a confirmed booking. Keep date-specific winter access pending. [1][2]

**Accessibility.** The site has no dedicated parking. Its accessibility page identifies the designated accessible entry through the zaguán between visitor center and gift shop, reachable from town sidewalks. Visitor-center restrooms are accessible; some residence rooms are look-in only. ASL interpretation is not presently offered. Accommodation contact is **575-202-1638**; no contact was made. [3]

**Fallback.** Read the published family interview digitally if the public center is closed. Do not substitute a historical residence or current business as an impromptu research destination.

## 2. The story

**A merchant's wife was a merchant.** The state's history describes **Rafaela Barela** managing the store deeded to her during the Civil War, with her son Mariano participating in the business. It later identifies **Perla Alidib**, a refugee from the Mexican Revolution, operating a mercantile and converting residential space into rental apartments after inheriting the property. These are institutional historical accounts, not either woman's own testimony. [4]

**A named family's own working memory.** Sylvia Quintanilla's *La Posta de Mesilla: A Legacy of Hospitality*, in *The Historic Mesilla Valley*, **February 2024, printed pages 18, 20 and 22**, was directly read. **Jerean Camuñez Hutchinson**, great-niece of founder Katy Griggs Camuñez, recalls her own family's involvement after acquiring the business in 1996. Her daughter **Elena Hutchinson** describes learning the business alongside her mother: **“Learning from her and being able to show that in my work is special.”** This is a published interview quotation, not audio heard or an archival transcript. [5]

**Historic connection, carefully bounded.** NMSU's collection announcement traces the Griggs family through **Eugenia Ascarate**, from a prominent Mexican family, and identifies **Josefina Chavez's** recipes in the family's restaurant history. That supports a bicultural family-business context, not a universal definition of Hispano identity or an inferred self-description by every descendant. [6]

**Original interpretation.** The useful story is not a claim to the oldest building or the first dish. It is the transfer of practical knowledge between women and generations. A public museum can make that labor visible without turning a current workplace into a spectacle.

## 3. Then / now

**Different buildings, related subject.** The Barela and Reynolds storefronts belong to the state historic site. La Posta's family history belongs to another property and an operating business. The family interview is contextual reading, not testimony that its speakers worked in the museum's storefronts. No combined historic footprint is asserted.

**From household resource to collection.** NMSU reported receiving Griggs family papers in February 2018, including correspondence, family histories, photographs and Spanish and Mexican documents. The announcement establishes a collection lead. Individual documents, archival restrictions and a current reading-room appointment were not examined. [6]

**Not frozen in a founding year.** La Posta's own history describes Katy's mother cooking while Katy handled customers and the register. It is a retrospective family-business narrative, not an eyewitness deposition or a wage record. Its claims about culinary invention and famous visitors are not adopted. [7]

**Before departure:** use the museum's current admission information and resolve the intended day's hours. If that cannot be established, retain this as digital reading rather than an activated stop. Prefer daylight for any necessary approach; no night access is implied.

**At the authorized visitor center:** begin with the town-history interpretation. Read the merchant-family context as the institution presents it, keeping visitor areas and staff-only areas separate. This dossier does not require an interior residence tour.

**One layer deeper:** distinguish a building owner, a cook, a host and a later descendant. Each occupies a different evidentiary position. Do not assign remembered tasks to unnamed employees or claim everyone's experience was identical.

**Strongest ending:** finish with Elena's brief account of learning beside her mother. The emotional payoff is skilled work becoming family continuity, not an invitation to find the family at work. This is an editorial pairing of sources, not a claim that her words play in the museum.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

**Primary-voice limit:** no directly examined nineteenth-century Barela or Alidib testimony, nor a founder-era Katy interview, was recovered. The direct voice concerns later family work; earlier stories are inherited memory or institutional history. This dossier is qualified, not a fully closed historic-worker evidence unit.

**Conflicting opening scene:** the 2024 interview gives six original tables; the business's history gives four. Preserve that discrepancy instead of selecting the more picturesque version. [5][7]
