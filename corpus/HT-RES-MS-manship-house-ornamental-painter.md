# Manship House and a painter’s work at the Old Capitol

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP09 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Manship House Museum is closed. A verified public place to read this story is its owner’s workplace, the Old Capitol Museum at 100 South State Street, Jackson, open Tuesday–Saturday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. It is a different building. The surviving dining-room finish is in the closed house. [1–3]

## 2. Story

Charles Henry Manship learned the trade of ornamental painting and came to Jackson during its 1830s building boom. He advertised house and sign painting with James Waugh. State construction created opportunities for tradespeople able to make inexpensive wood resemble costly materials. The state museum records his work on the State House, today’s Old Capitol. The Society of Architectural Historians documents a later commission there as well: his firm painted the interior in 1870–71, working with architect Joseph Willis. [1–3]

## 3. Then and now

At home, Manship could show what a practiced hand could do. In about 1857 he built a Gothic Revival cottage for his family. The federal building survey compared its west front with figure 128 in Andrew Jackson Downing’s pattern book. Manship adapted the design to a center hall, a rear dining room and a separate kitchen. He painted the dining-room surfaces to resemble oak. The capacity to imitate material, rather than the purchase of a roomful of expensive oak panels, is what makes the room exceptional. His wife was Adaline Daley Manship. Four generations of the family retained the property before the state acquired it in 1975. [1,3–5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The cottage and its decorative work survive. The original detached kitchen does not. The museum describes finding the exterior colors under later paint. Furnishings are in storage during closure. The Old Capitol is itself a restored building. Its significance here is that Manship worked there. No particular surface in the visiting galleries has been verified as his surviving handiwork. [1–3,5]

## 5. Public progression

At the public Old Capitol, consider the craftsmen who had to finish a statehouse after its builders supplied the structure. Then consult the federal survey’s description of Manship’s own dining room to see what documentary evidence can establish about a specific surface. [3,5]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

The 1857 cottage, its interpretation to 1888, and the family’s 1975 conveyance are separate dates. The museum has not supplied a reopening date. The tour does not extend to the house grounds, stored furnishings or a roadside stop. [1,4,5]
