# Little Rock Central High School National Historic Site
HT-RES-AR-little-rock-central-high-students · Arkansas OP02 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

In September 1957, the plan to desegregate Central High School ceased to be an abstraction for the young people who had to cross the street. Elizabeth Eckford arrived separately from the others on September 4. The National Park Service quotes her later recollection, “I tried to see a friendly face somewhere in the mob.” She was looking for someone who would help. The Arkansas National Guard blocked entry that day. The episode belongs to the space around the school as much as to the doors. It matters where Eckford stood, who could see her and how little protection a public street offered. [3, 4]

In a 1979 Blackside interview, Ernest Green remembered the students who gathered at Daisy Bates’s home before approaching Central from one side, while Eckford was at the other. In the transcript he recalls that the groups did not know where one another were. His recollection of the two blocks makes the scale of the school a part of the story. He was elsewhere when the mob surrounded her and described piecing together her experience afterward. That makes Eckford’s words evidence of her own experience, and Green’s an account of what the group could and could not see. [4, 5]

The students entered under federal protection on September 25 after an earlier September 23 entry ended when they were removed for their safety. Green was the only senior among the Nine. In the same interview, he remembered former schoolmates at Horace Mann who “invited me to all the activities.” He also remembered uncertainty about his physics grade, and a stadium that felt enormous as he prepared to cross the stage in May. These are small details with historical weight. He had to finish a senior year under intense public attention and still relied on relationships outside Central. The NPS chronology places the graduation at Quigley Stadium, distinct from the school steps at the visitor anchor. [3, 5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** Central’s historic entrance on Park Street, the street where Eckford encountered the crowd, and the school Green attended belong to the 1957–58 account. The campus remains an operating school. The NPS visitor center and commemorative garden are present-day interpretive sites and the anchor for this visit. [1–4, 6]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive to the NPS visitor parking at 2120 W. Daisy L. Gatson Bates Drive. Take the short signed approach to the visitor center or the open garden. Read the campus from the public side. The visit excludes school property, steps, student entrances, the closed service station and any exact historical crowd or camera position. [1, 2]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The NPS operating-hours page closes the school to public use. Its visitor-center article describes exterior access to the steps and pool and mentions ranger-led interior visits. This dossier uses only the separate visitor center and garden because their published operating status agrees. [1, 2]
- NPS articles count ten young people attempting entry on September 4 and identify nine who ultimately attended. Those figures describe different groups. Its timeline separately distinguishes September 23 and September 25. [3, 4]
- Green’s first account is the transcript of the 1979 Blackside sessions. The standalone download at the public mirror returned HTTP 406 in the shell, while the web reader retrieved the full text, including the graduation and Horace Mann passages. A different WashU interview record loaded its player shell without transcript text in the retrieved output. The Eckford project page loaded only its web application shell in direct retrieval, so the quotation here is specifically the NPS’s printed excerpt, not a transcription made from her tape. [4, 5, 7, 8]
- The year 1957 is established for the gallery photograph, but its caption’s association with the September 4 exclusion conflicts with what the frame appears to show. Event attribution and the then/now match remain held. [9]
