## 2. The story
**Original interpretation:** A mining district needed more than miners. It needed measurements, legal claims, lodging, repair, transport and a place where those services became visible. Hailey's newspaper was part of that working system: it carried commerce as well as news.

The **November 27, 1882 Wood River Times**, volume I, number 163, names **T. E. Picotte** as publisher and gives its office at Main and Croy's southeast corner. Its period phrase **“Published Every Evening, Sundays Excepted”** captures a daily information service. On the same page, **Frank P. Cavanah** advertises mineral surveying and mining engineering, **Carrie Bache** a lodging house, and Cliff & McKay mining supplies and tinsmithing. Pacific Iron Works advertises hoisting and pumping equipment. These are advertisements and masthead claims, not independent proof that machinery was installed locally or that every service was delivered. [7]

**Evidence qualification:** the LOC-hosted page's indexed text was read in detail, but direct LOC page/PDF retrieval returned 403 and the download was HTML, not a readable scan. The wording is therefore held as indexed transcription pending image collation. It is not a visually verified newspaper facsimile. No invented compositor, wage or miner's testimony has been supplied.

The city's 2007 walking-tour record identifies the Wood River Times location as a **site**, distinct from its inventory of surviving buildings. It also names **Ed Fleming**, a freighter associated with the museum entrance's historic ox yoke. That latter identification is a dated local-history account, not this pass's observation that the yoke is still present. [8]

**Tribal history:** the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes' own published account describes regional ancestral lands, seasonal food gathering, fishing and contemporary sovereignty. It is used as regional context, not a Hailey-specific oral tradition or a visitor license for cultural places. No sacred-site coordinates or fabricated Indigenous narration. [9]
