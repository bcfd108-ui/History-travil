## 2. The story

By an act of the **1847 Louisiana Legislature**, the state established **"The Insane Asylum of the State of Louisiana"** to relieve the overcrowded Insane Department of New Orleans' Charity Hospital and county/parish jails. The Board of Administrators purchased a **250-acre tract called "Flowery Hill"** roughly one to two miles east of Jackson across a small stream and appointed architect **C. N. Gibbens** (Charles Gibbons) with explicit instructions **"not to design something that would look like a prison."** [2, 4]

On **July 5, 1847**, contractor **Robert Perry** signed the construction agreement (with Centenary College President **David Shattuck** standing security). Weather delays pushed the West Wing opening past its December 1847 target to the **fall of 1848**, when the first patients transferred from Charity Hospital in New Orleans and local jails (130 admissions in 1848–1850 under an initial staff of twelve). The **East Wing** finished in **1849**, and the **Center Building** opened in **1854**, though the planned 100-foot rear kitchen wing was never built due to legislative funding shortfalls. [2, 4]

The finished Center Building became the **largest Greek Revival structure in Louisiana** and holds the state's **only three-story Greek Revival temple portico**: six giant masonry columns with cast-iron Ionic capitals supporting a terra-cotta-ornamented pediment, ingeniously concealing **four and a half stories** behind the entablature and rooftop cupola. Crucially, its two long flanking wings featured three-story **Tuscan colonnades** acting as open-air circulation galleries—an adaptation of Gulf Coast plantation gallery architecture to institutional life, housing segregated male and female nonpaying wards while the Center Building held administrative offices, a chapel, and private apartments for paying patients. [2, 4]

Resident population climbed past **1,000 patients by 1898**. Renamed **East Louisiana State Hospital** in **1922**, the campus expanded with twentieth-century modern hospital buildings encroaching around the rear and flanks of the 1847–54 Center Building, which was later elevated by the Louisiana State Historic Preservation Office to **national architectural significance** on the National Register of Historic Places. [2, 3, 4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Architect spelling (`C. N. Gibbens` vs. `Charles Gibbons`):** The NRHP nomination gives **`C. N. Gibbens`**, whereas Karen Kingsley (*Buildings of Louisiana* / *64 Parishes*) identifies him as probably **`Charles Gibbons`**, designer of Grace Episcopal Church in nearby St. Francisville. Both forms are noted. [2, 4]
- **NRHP registration number:** The retrieved NPGallery asset (`64025e50-0cc9-4c0f-aa08-487514a713ad`) preserves the full typed form, `UTM 15/670800/3412260`, and SHPO national-significance addendum by Robert B. DeBlieux, but the scanned header's numeric NRIS ID was not extracted cleanly from the OCR text (`see §10`). [2]
- **Institutional cemeteries:** Any nineteenth- or twentieth-century patient burial grounds on the 250-acre Flowery Hill estate were not documented in the architectural NRHP file or LDH directory checked this run.
