# Bentonsport — The ferryman and the mill wall

Iowa OP06 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**The directory names workers a picturesque view leaves out.** The 1878 county history’s contemporary directory records “Greef, C. E., ferryman, Bentonsport.” The same directory lists J. Greef as a merchant, W. Greyson as a carpenter, H. Fulton as railroad engineer and W. S. Fulton as a section hand. Those terse labels preserve occupations amid changing ways of moving goods. The names are read from the county history’s digitized transcription. Initials have not been expanded. The later walking guide separately identifies Julius and Herman Greef in the store and bank partnership. This relationship gives the merchant story context with no assumption that every Greef in the directory was the same person. [4–7]

**Water powered businesses on the same bank where people crossed.** The county history dates the Green brothers’ paper mill to 1852 and its closure to 1874, when machinery reportedly went to Blue Rapids, Kansas. It puts a new flour mill built by James A. Brown in 1854 and calls its brick building four stories high, with three runs of buhrs. The present walking guide labels a garden within Brown's five-story mill foundation. The two height descriptions remain visible in this record. The rose garden and masonry allow a public visitor to read the industrial bank even without building interior access. Historic mill work, bank work and ferry work all depended on the river in distinct ways. [4, 5, 8]

**The bridge changed the crossing.** The Iowa DOT bridge record describes the structure and later alteration. The local guide refers to its opening in 1883 and presents pedestrian and bicycle use. Today's county closure notice controls actual access. The history is of a crossing; the draft visit stays on one bank. [1, 3, 4]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic place.** The historic district includes riverfront, older stores, private houses and the bridge. The historic landing and work buildings occupied more ground than the space open to visitors today. The district map is evidence for property relationships, not permission to enter them. [4, 6, 8]

**Lawful public anchor.** Bentonsport Park, 22098 Hawk Drive, in Van Buren County. Use the north-bank public park entrance and designated open River Park paths. Take a short walk from lawful parking to the public mill-wall and garden area if open and passable. Stay above the river edge. No bridge crossing or approach through a barrier, private businesses, guest rooms or residences. [1–4]

## 5. A short visit

Begin at the county park and read the 1878 names at an open public path. Look at the mill wall from permitted ground and then at the bridge without crossing it. Land on **Where the ferryman earned his living**, with a clear distinction between the documented occupation and the unverified exact working position of C. E. Greef. [1–6]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The county bridge restriction supersedes the generic walking guide’s pedestrian access for the center span. Do not infer a reopening date. Direct retrieval of the MyCountyParks full Bentonsport page returned HTTP 503, while indexed county park text carried its published site address and general hours. The county Conservation front page was readable and supplied the current closure. The Internet Archive web stream could not be opened in the web retriever, but a direct download returned its OCR transcription of the 1878 book. Exact historic ferry landing, payroll and merchant ledgers are still missing. The four-story versus five-story mill descriptions are unresolved. The Greef papers are a finding aid lead, with specific letters and household records unexamined. [1–8]
