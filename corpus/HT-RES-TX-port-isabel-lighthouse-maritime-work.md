# Port Isabel — Keeping a light through the weather
Research draft · Texas OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

**The lantern leaked “in every direction.”** The U.S. Coast Guard history carries these words from the 1879 condition account. Rain made it impossible to keep the lens and lamps dry. By 1881 a new iron lantern was on the tower. A light on the chart depended on someone being able to get inside and keep the equipment working through bad weather. [2]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then — light, fuel and title.** The National Register nomination gives the start of work as February 1852 and names Stephen Powers as keeper in October 1853. It records an 1854 arrangement of 15 lamps and 21 reflectors and a third-order Fresnel lens in 1857. The Coast Guard puts the interruption of service in May 1888 after officials found the United States had no title to the station land. It records a sale, a return to service in 1895 and final discontinuance in 1905. Weather, equipment and paperwork about the ground beneath it all affected whether this light could guide anyone. [2–3]

**Now — one old tower and two modern interpretations.** The operator describes the keeper’s cottage as a reconstruction and the lens now on display as a modern reproduction. [1,4]

## 4 · What’s still there

- **Exterior only on open days.** Historic brick tower, viewed from signed public lower paths. [1,3]
- **Public interior when open.** Tower entry is ticketed and weather dependent. Children must be at least five and accompanied under the posted age rule. The cottage visitor center has separate hours. [1]
- **Not accessible.** Any closed tower area and any off-path ground. The original lighting apparatus is unverified as a surviving feature in place. [1,4]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Historic location.** The tower occupies the historic light-station site. The reconstructed cottage supplies adjacent interpretation. [3–4]
2. **Lawful anchor.** Use the signed Port Isabel Lighthouse State Historic Site lower grounds from Queen Isabella Boulevard. If the tower is open and the party meets the operator’s rules, the reproduction lens is an optional interior stop. If weather closes the whole site, read remotely. [1]
3. **Strongest story moment — A dry light in a storm.** From a public lower path, look at how exposed the lantern stands above the town. Read the keeper-era condition words there. [1–4]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The Coast Guard table says first lit in 1853. Its narrative dates completion to 1852 and is less precise about first exhibition. The nomination records 1852 work and identifies a keeper in October 1853. The actual first exhibition day is unresolved. The nomination gives 82 feet. The operator gives 72 feet. Neither measurement datum was verified. Direct inspection of the 1879 station report remains open. [1–3]
