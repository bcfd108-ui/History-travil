# Rock Creek Station — the work between riders

Nebraska OP05 · Draft research for Dash · November 19, 2026

## 2. The story

### A place to cross, a place to keep stock

The state archaeological account treats Rock Creek as two road ranches on opposing sides of a crossing. Between them were functions a traveler could need in short order: a crossing, dry space, supplies, a repair and possibly a post office. In 1859 David McCanles bought the west ranch from Newton Glenn, according to McCanles’s son William Monroe. The son recalled a house, a well and a toll bridge on the developing east side and remembered that the mail and stage business was already on the west side when his father bought it. The road ranch dates to 1857 in the operator and NPS accounts. The Pony Express company’s use came later. [1–4,9]

### The household behind a fast handoff

“We furnished the hay for both of the ranches on Rock Creek.” William Monroe’s later published recollection is specific about that labor. His account identifies the Wellmans at the station, Dock Brink as a stock tender and James Butler Hickok as an assistant stock tender. He recalls accompanying Wellman to Brownville for supplies. His account of the ranch places animal care and freight procurement beside the relay rider. The NPS historic resource study identifies an east-side relay building and cites the Leavenworth & Pike’s Peak company’s scheduled stage stop and an Overland Mail contract. The documents establish that Rock Creek belonged to the service network. A stage schedule and a Pony Express exchange tell different parts of its use. [3,4]

### The case and the popular story

The state archive publishes a transcription of the complaint made by Leroy McCanles on July 13, 1861 and the court cost bill. The cost bill records a charge that “was not Sustained.” William Monroe’s published account, based on his recollection of witnessing the incident, says he was with his father at the east ranch. The NPS study reports three men dead. The later national popular account associated with George Ward Nichols portrayed a many-against-one fight, a version reproduced and examined in the state historical journal. A complaint records an allegation, a cost bill records disposition, and a witnessed memory published years later has its own limits. Read this passage indoors or remotely. [3–6]

## 3. Then / now

A crossing and ranch workplaces once served traffic on the Oregon–California corridor. Nebraska’s operator describes archaeological work preceding reconstruction of buildings and other features. The state archaeological account also reports buried wood found during 2021 work and gives the possibility that it relates to wagon travel. The finds preserve a research question. Today's bridge, buildings and public trails provide ways to study the crossing while respecting original ground. [1,2]

## 5. Public trip-shaped progression

1. Start at the state historical park public approach. Check accepted permit and path conditions, and use the visitor center only if staffed for a confirmed appointment or open in season. [1]
2. Use the signed public path to compare the two reconstructed ranch groups. From an open maintained path, read the well, toll and hay entries against the crossing. Skip steep or icy approaches. [1–4]
3. **Strongest story moment — The sentence about hay.** At the public visitor center or an open designated path, read the son’s single sentence about supplying two ranches. A short exchange of horses depended on two workplaces and a seasonal supply chain. Read the 1861 court material as a separate reflection in the visitor center or at home. [3–5]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The 1857 date applies to the early road ranch in the park and NPS accounts. The son’s 1859 purchase and east-side building work are dates for a change of ownership and expansion. His chronology is published recollection. [1–4]
- The son describes a rental of the east station and a dispute over slow payments. The state court introduction says the company agreed to purchase it from McCanles. The actual lease, sale contract and payment ledger were not retrieved. The complaint and cost bill were read in the Society’s transcription. [4,5]
- NPS reports three deaths. The son’s narrative and the many-assailants account discussed in the state journal give differing accounts of the confrontation. The trial cost record cannot settle the sequence of events or witness positions. [3–6]
- The similarly named NPGallery listing 82004860 is for Rock Creek Stage Station in Wyoming. A Nebraska listing number for this park was not confirmed. [7]

## 7. Historical photo record

The state archaeological page publishes an exhibit image with a caption identifying it as a photo of the East Ranch in 1860. The linked page supplies no photographer, original item identifier or image provenance establishing the medium, so the caption is retained as an exhibit lead. Later portraits and archaeological images cannot establish a camera station on the crossing or witness ground. No Photo Point. [2,6,8]

## Connected ground — The Pony Express Eastern Divisions, 1860–1861

Rock Creek's two road ranches stood on the Pony Express route between Hollenberg Station in Kansas and Fort Kearny, where furnishing hay for both ranches was part of the work that kept the relay moving.
