# Boley — Who had the authority to sell a lot

## 2. The story

**A name printed as authority.** The September 14, 1905 Boley Progress carried a Fort Smith and Western Townsite Company advertisement identifying “T M HAYNES Local Manager.” It told readers to contact Haynes for purchases and warned that other purported representatives were unauthorized. The printed advertisement is direct evidence of how a new town made a stranger’s first transaction legible. Its surviving repository OCR is imperfect; only the clear name/title fragment is quoted. [4]

**Land, enterprise and a second generation of work.** The town describes its founding in 1903 on land allotted to Abigail Barnett. The nomination gives her name as Abigail Barnett McCormick and traces the land through her father. Those descriptions refer to the same founding story, but allotment, inheritance and the subsequent townsite’s title transfers require deed verification. Haynes’s later 1909 building housed a mercantile business below a telephone office and lodging. The state survey separately indexes a 1910 newspaper feature calling Haynes the town’s founder. That title is the newspaper’s framing, while the earlier ad gives his contemporaneous office. [1–5]

**Two banks and several addresses.** The register describes the Boley Bank and Trust Company building and Farmers and Merchants Bank building separately. Public interpretation should not transfer the later bank’s history to the earlier storefront or assume present use from the nomination’s inventory. The visible district records how sales, credit and services grew into adjacent businesses. [2,3]

## 3. Then / now

Historic location: Boley’s townsite and commercial district; the Haynes Building dates to 1909, four years after the ad. Lawful anchor: public Pecan Street sidewalk in the downtown district where visibly open and continuous, with the town’s own Pecan Street contact location as orientation. Public frontage is the limit. Exact Haynes office of 1905, current private uses and accessible sidewalk transitions remain unverified. [1–4,6]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicts

Town, nomination, state survey and period paper use different language for allotment, inheritance and founding. No original deed or full chain of title was retrieved. Current ownership and public interior access remain unverified. The exact 1910 December feature was cataloged via the state survey but the original page was not read. [1–5]
