# HT-RES-NJ-paterson-great-falls — Paterson: the falls that powered a planned industrial city
Division NJ seed-catalog • Research draft • Checked November 25, 2026

In 1791 Alexander Hamilton's Society for Establishing Useful Manufactures picked the Great Falls of the Passaic River as the engine of a planned industrial city. Paterson was laid out the next year beside the 77-foot falls, and an engineered raceway system carried the river's power from mill to mill. The falling water came first; the city was built around it.

Great Falls of the Passaic River, Paterson, Passaic County, New Jersey

**Public access:** The falls are the centerpiece of Paterson Great Falls National Historical Park. Start at Overlook Park, 72 McBride Avenue Extension, and stay on designated overlooks and open pathways. The park is open dawn to dusk; obey temporary closures. Confirm hours and conditions with the National Park Service before visiting.

## The story

Hamilton argued that American manufacturing needed a national center, powered by water and built to a plan. The Society for Establishing Useful Manufactures was organized in 1791, and in 1792 the new city of Paterson was established at the Great Falls, where the Passaic River drops over basalt cliffs.

A network of raceways — engineered waterways built and modified from 1791 into the 1840s — distributed the falls' energy to mills along its course. Generations of immigrant workers staffed the factories, and Paterson grew into a manufacturing center for locomotives, textiles, and silk. In 1914 a hydroelectric powerhouse was built at the falls, continuing the site's original use of waterpower into the electric age. The district is preserved today as a national historical park, where the falls, raceways, and mill landscape can still be read together.

## Then / now

- 1791: Society for Establishing Useful Manufactures organized.
- 1792: Paterson established at the Great Falls.
- 1791–1848: industrial raceway network constructed and modified.
- 1914: hydroelectric powerhouse constructed at the falls.
- Today: Paterson Great Falls National Historical Park; the falls and raceway landscape remain.

## What's still there

The falls themselves, dropping between dark basalt cliffs, with the river and bridges above. Overlook Park gives the principal public view. Surviving raceways and mill buildings carry the industrial story beyond the overlook.

## The stop, step by step

Reach Overlook Park at 72 McBride Avenue Extension. From the overlook, find the waterfall descending between the dark basalt cliffs. Read the falls against the raceway landscape: the water you are watching is the power source the city was planned around. Follow open park pathways as posted and return via the park entrance.
