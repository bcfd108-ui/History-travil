# Dubuque Ice Harbor — A river channel had to be made and remade

*Iowa OP01 research draft · Dash review pending.*

## 2. Story

### A city rearranges its landing

The federal building survey treats Ice Harbor as a major public undertaking. The harbor and earlier river improvements made it possible to bring commercial traffic into the city, and Second Street connected the riverfront to the city grid. The survey records nearby industrial and commercial structures as part of the harbor story. Its 1882 date concerns the surveyed harbor resource. It cannot date a vessel moved there for museum display a century later. [5]

### The engineer who gave a ship its voice

The Corps launched the William M. Black in 1934 to work on the Missouri River. A large suction head gathered water and sediment at the bottom. A separate engine set turned the paddlewheels that propelled the vessel. The federal nomination counts 60 people aboard, with 12 in navigation and 48 in the dredge, steward and other functions. It documents cabins and bunks that made working and sleeping part of one shift system. In 1949 Assistant Engineer Paul Thompson built the dredge a new whistle. The nomination describes its intended sound as “chord, with the tones coming and blending just right.” That wording is quoted as transmitted by the federal nomination. [3,6]

### The cost of keeping one channel open

On the Missouri in 1937, Lowell L. Standley wrote to the government after the dredge’s wake sank his tied rowboat. Six months later he asked, “Can you tell me what the Dept. is going to do about it.” The nomination reproduces the agency’s denial and cites the paper record. The disagreement puts a riverbank owner beside a powerful public navigation project. The surviving ship also carries the line from its November 21, 1973 logbook, reproduced in the nomination: “last boiler cooled down.” The Corps’ vessel became a museum object in a city on another river. [6]

## 4. Surviving features

- **Public exterior.** City Riverwalk and marked overlooks, conditional on weather and notices. The marina and its docks have separate seasonal use. [1]
- **Public interior during posted hours.** River Museum ordinary ticketed visitor areas. [2,4]
- **Exterior interpretation only where open.** Operator identifies a paddlewheel from the Black in front of the Mississippi River Center. No claim that the ship is visible from every public walkway. [3–4]
- **Not accessible this winter.** Black boarding is closed. The tender Tavern is not offered for boarding. Stay clear of dock approaches. [3]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. From the city Riverwalk, read where Dubuque’s town streets came down to the harbor and what that meant for freight. Use the marked public overlooks only if conditions allow. [1,5]
2. At the museum’s Mississippi River Center, compare the harbor’s purpose with the dredge’s work to maintain another river. Read about the meals, cabins and shifts that made the machine run. [2–4,6]
3. **Story landing — The last boiler cooled down.** Where the public entrance approach is open, read the 1973 log line at the operator-identified paddlewheel by the Center. Indoor fallback within the open public museum or remote reading. [2–4,6]

## 6. Gaps and conflicting records

- The River Museum dates the launch to September 1934. The KCRG report also gives September 1934 in narration, while its quoted interpretation uses 1935 and says retirement in 1972 and arrival in 1975. The nomination places decommissioning and the last boiler cooling in 1973, records a September 1979 deed and gives June 1, 1980 for public opening. It does not independently establish a 1975 arrival. NPGallery gives April 12, 1982 for the register asset, distinct from the later landmark designation described by the operator. Preserve the milestone differences. [3,6–8]
- The general activity page describes June–October boarding. The vessel’s own dated page says Memorial Day 2027 through October, with weather restrictions after Labor Day. Use the vessel-specific posting. [3,9]
- Standley’s letter, the steam log and crew figures were read in the nomination, not in the separately cited originals. Original payroll and log pages and a contemporary Dubuque dock-worker record were not retrieved. Thompson is a documented dredge engineer. The draft does not identify him as an Ice Harbor employee. [5–6]
- Library of Congress Ice Harbor metadata and the survey identification were recovered in search. The item open returned HTTP 403 in the earlier check and the image collection endpoint remained inaccessible. No federal survey frame assigned. [5]
