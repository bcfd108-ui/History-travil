# Troy Hospital — Dr. Meyers' house hospital on Main Street

## 1. Trip gate

The Troy Hospital is a **privately owned single-family home**. Its National Register listing (ref. 11000524, 2011) records it as private property in current use as a single dwelling, and the 2015 photograph reviewed below shows a lived-in house with a garden fence. **There is no interior access, tour, or museum.** [1, 4]

## 2. The story

Troy grew in a timbered gulch east of Moscow — first called Huffs Gulch, incorporated in 1892 as **Vollmer** for businessman John P. Vollmer, and renamed **Troy** by town vote in 1897. Rail access, the Troy Lumber and Manufacturing Company (founded 1904), and farm trade drove strong growth between 1905 and 1916. [1]

In **1905** merchant **Oscar Larson** (born Minnesota 1860; co-founder of the Vollmer Milling and Mercantile Company; later president of the First Bank of Troy, 1911–14) built a large two-story, nine-room Queen Anne–influenced house at South Main and Sixth, close to the commercial core. Larson died in 1924. [1]

In **1926** **Dr. Charles Meyers** bought the house from the Trout family, who owned it by then, and turned it into Troy's hospital. The nomination reconstructs the layout: on the first floor, his office, a surgery, a two-bed ward, a kitchen and a bathroom; upstairs, another two-bed ward, a single patient room, and a nurse's room. His sister **Julia Meyers** was his first nurse; **Hildure Pearson, Hannah Sundell and Carrie Shostrum** also nursed there, and local women cooked for patients and staff and heated water to sterilize instruments. [1]

The work combined house calls to outlying farms (especially for births) with surgery in town. Oral tradition remembers "Tonsil Tuesday," when the smell of ether hung over town; the *Troy Weekly News* reported Dr. Meyers removing the tonsils of five children on a Tuesday in mid-August 1932 and seven the next month, along with gall-bladder and appendix operations, hospital births, and other admissions. The paper printed patients' names; this draft does not repeat them. For nearly two decades this small house was the main source of medical care in Troy and the surrounding ridges. [1]

Dr. Meyers built his own house on the adjoining lot and married his wife Gunda in Sweden; they had two sons. In **1942** he sold the hospital to **J. B. Nelson** and announced it would close on **July 12, 1942**; Nelson converted it to a photography studio, and the Meyerses moved to Seattle. Later owners used it as a business and as a home. The owners at the time of the 2010 nomination, who had made it their residence, asked Troy residents to vote on period-appropriate repainting colors. [1]

## 4. What's still there

- **Visible from the public right-of-way:** the two-story cross-gabled house with shingled upper walls, brick-patterned asphalt siding below (2010), red paint with white trim, standing-seam metal roof, front-gabled south dormer and hipped north dormer, leaded upper lights in the front windows, and the 1936 enclosed entry; the matching front-gabled garage. [1, 4]
- **Not accessible:** interior, yard, garage, carport. The flat-roofed carport is noncontributing. [1]
- **Adjacent:** Troy Downtown Historic District (separately listed; Suzanne Julin nomination, 2009) begins at the business district just northwest. Its boundary relative to this lot was not verified this run. [1]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Construction/conversion dates:** the prior state block described this as a "1913 wooden hospital." The NRHP registration form gives **1905** construction as a residence and **1926** conversion; 1913 is not supported by the nomination and is withdrawn. [1]
- **Name spelling:** the form uses both "Meyers" and "Meyer" ("lost their tonsils to Dr. Meyer"); "Meyers" is used here. The alternate name on the form is "Dr. Meyers' Hospital"; an earlier June 7, 2008 draft nomination by Lovell, Peterson and Wall is cited but not seen. [1]
- **Listing date:** NPS evaluation sheet shows receipt July 1, 2011, pending list July 22, 2011, 45th day Aug. 16, 2011; the exact entry date was not legible in the OCR. Left blank in §10.
- **Current owner/use 2026:** not re-verified; the 2015 photograph shows residential use. No owner names are printed here.
- **Primary newspapers:** *Troy Weekly News* originals are held by the Latah County Historical Society, Moscow; not consulted directly. LOC Chronicling America returned a Cloudflare challenge from the research box this run.
- **Dr. Meyers' first name, training, and dates:** only "Dr. Charles Meyers" (arrived 1926, left 1942) is given; licensing and life dates not researched.
- **Photograph sign text:** an oval sign on the fence in the 2015 photograph bears the house number and a "circa" date that is not legible at the inspected resolution.
