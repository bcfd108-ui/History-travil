## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** A child remembers Christmas as abundance. The memory also reveals the hours of work that made that abundance possible.

**Directly read firsthand source:** GRHC's public *Christmas* compilation includes a signed **email message from Mary Ebach**, headed as memories of growing up near **Rugby, North Dakota**. On printed page **15**, she recalls being one of twelve family members, limited purchased gifts, and the cookies and meals her mother prepared. Her phrase **“the special cookies baked by our mother”** makes the household labor visible. This is her published written recollection, not a newly conducted oral interview. [3]

**Second local memory:** on the same page, **Bonnie (Aichele) Schimke** places her childhood Christmas in **Tuttle, North Dakota**, tentatively **1949 or 1950**. Her account likewise remembers home-prepared food and her parents' effort to create an occasion for a child. The uncertainty is hers and is retained; no exact year is selected. [3]

**Interpretive synthesis:** These are not simply food lists. They show how family care becomes tradition through repeated work. Warm childhood memory and the burden of preparation can coexist. Neither narrator gives a time-use survey, and the draft does not invent hours worked or assign every task solely to women.

**Collection context:** NDSU says the GRHC preserves Germans-from-Russia history in North Dakota and the Northern Plains, with specified regional groups and their descendants among its collecting focus. Inclusion in the collection is evidence of curatorial context, not permission to infer every narrator's religion, citizenship or precise migration ancestry. [4]
