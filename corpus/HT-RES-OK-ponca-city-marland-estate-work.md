# Ponca City — the hands that finished the Marland mansion

## 2 · The story

### From oil payroll to building trades

E.W. Marland began building the hilltop mansion in 1925. The operator places his successful 101 Ranch well in June 1911 and puts the opening of the mansion in 1928. Those two dates mark a change of work: drilling turned oil into a fortune, and a succession of stoneworkers, woodworkers, metalworkers and decorators turned part of that fortune into a house. National Register documentation identifies John Duncan Forsyth as the architect who recruited artists and artisans for the job. Its description places the light-colored stone quarry near the adjoining Arkansas River. [3–4]

### The door under the car shelter

Forsyth wrote a set of notes on the residence. In Denise Browning’s 1978 article, he credits the entrance’s “Florentine wrought ironwork” to William H. Jackson of New York. His wording survives in the article’s quotation. The nomination calls the supplier the William H. Jackson Company. Together the records identify the firm and the architect’s language. They leave the individual ironworkers’ names unrecorded. [4–5]

Browning reads the plan alongside the building. Passengers could step out beneath the porte cochere. Carved stone corbels supported the wooden beams. She assigns the likenesses of Marland’s dogs to sculptor Pellegrini and describes the entrance hall ceiling as the work of Vincent Mangliotti. The same account distinguishes polished limestone, hand-laid tile and leaded glass in adjoining spaces. Each demanded different tools and a different sequence of work. Even the ceiling that looks like another material was paint, made to resemble a gold mosaic. [5]

### The expense of keeping it

The park-like estate once included stables, water features and recreation grounds. The register narrative follows the property after Marland lost control of his oil company, through the sale to the Carmelite Fathers in 1941, changes under the Felician order, and the city’s acquisition. Its description already records a filled pool and part of the original estate replaced by a subdivision. The property a guest can see is the surviving work of a much larger commission. [4]

## 3 · Then / now

- **Historic location.** The mansion and the wider project associated with its original gates, studio, landscape, stables and service buildings off Monument Road. The listing describes changes to the institutional buildings and former grounds. It is a historical boundary record. [4]
- **Lawful physical anchor.** Marland Mansion & Estate, 901 Monument Road. Drive to the signed visitor arrival and walk to the staffed tour entrance. Use only public tour rooms and operator-designated paths during the visit. No independent approach to any historic private stables, residence or post-estate parcel. [1–2,4]
- **What survives.** The historic stone building remains. Tour rooms carry surviving details, while changes described in the nomination include a covered passage and institutional additions. [2,4–5]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Start on the signed approach. Look at stone as a construction material and read the nomination’s brief description of the walls and roof. [4]
2. Follow the standard mansion tour through whatever entrance and interior rooms are open. Read Browning’s account of the materials separately as you pass from outside into the finished rooms. [1,5]
3. **Strongest story moment — The worker at the threshold.** At the public tour’s entrance hall or the permitted outside approach, connect the ironwork credit in Forsyth’s notes with the piece of the house you can see. If the door or corbels are screened off, finish with the passage in the tour’s publicly accessible arrival area. [4–5]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The register narrative estimates about **$2.5 million** spent from 1925–28. Visit Ponca City prints **$5.5 million** and omits an accounting breakdown. The scope of the two figures is unresolved. [4,6]
- City and mansion operator list Monday–Saturday only. Visit Ponca City also prints Sunday 1–5 and conflicting address and admission details. Use the operator’s 901 Monument Road, $12 regular adult admission and posted closure calendar. [1–2,6]
- The nomination calls the mansion three stories. Visit Ponca City describes four floors. A floor-by-floor comparison of the published counting conventions remains open. [4,6]
- Forsyth’s original notes and original 1926 plan were cited by Browning and were not retrieved. The quoted words are from her published transcription. Other craft attribution and spelling remain subject to original-plan comparison. The Library of Congress HABS item page returned HTTP 403 in the direct open. The catalog identifies drawings, which are useful as evidence of design rather than as period photographs. [5,7]
