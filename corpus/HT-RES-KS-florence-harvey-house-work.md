# Florence Harvey House — The work on both sides of the pass-through

## 2. The story

**Before the train came through.** In 1878 Fred Harvey began operating the hotel for the rail trade. The hotel appears as the Clifton in local and registration accounts. Its rooms supported longer stays, while the meal operation followed passing trains. The 1879 enlargement and the size of the hotel described in local histories concern a complex larger than the surviving building. [2–4]

**Matilda Thomas remembers the dishes.** Florence Historical Society’s published local history identifies Thomas as a teenager who worked there in 1880. She remembered Harvey telling her “don't throw the dishes so hard or you'll break them.” These words are her reported recollection of his speech, printed in a later local account. The source also names John Hess as head pastry cook and Mary Kusick as a housekeeper. The warning to one worker conveys the pressure of service more directly than a promotional menu. The original interview record has not been established. [2]

**Who could reach the tub.** The same local account identifies Phebe Amelia Lofgren as having helped her mother wash dishes. It describes an inverted dishpan used as a step and reports different wages for child and mother. The historical society's retelling says Lofgren received 75 cents per week and her mother $16–17 a month. No payroll confirms either figure here. The account places preparation, cleaning and family income on the other side of the dining-room door, where the passenger generally did not look. [2]

**The rooms moved, too.** The nomination reports the last train meal on March 31, 1900 and the transfer of operations to Newton. Portions of the complex had different fates. The section preserved by Florence’s historical society was moved across the tracks and continued in residential and boarding-house use. The surviving remnant belongs to the history without standing at the original rail-side site. [2–4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The two association pages print 221 Marion and 204 West Third. The nomination places the moved building at the southwest corner. No separate building is established by the differently described addresses. [1–3]
- The old hotel, the 1879 expansion and the moved remnant are different fabric. The nomination describes the surviving piece as said to be the original part. The construction sequence, full original footprint and current interior display are unverified beyond the published record. [2,3]
- No original employee register, payroll, interview transcript or ordinary open-hours grid was retrieved. The reported Thomas and Lofgren accounts are attributed to the society’s later published history. [1,2]

## Connected ground — The Harvey Houses on the Santa Fe Line

This surviving section of the Clifton Hotel was the Fred Harvey Company's first house with sleeping rooms, taken under contract on January 1, 1878 — the second year of a system that went on to run eating houses and hotels along the Santa Fe at La Junta, Clovis, Winslow, and dozens of other stops.
