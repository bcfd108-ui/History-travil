# Abilene: the people who connected the calls
KS OP03 draft for Dash • November 19, 2026 Pacific • exterior sidewalk anchor only

## 1. Trip gate
**Daylight public sidewalk exterior only, United Building, 300 North Cedar Street, corner of NW Third Street, Abilene, Kansas.** The local visitors bureau documents the building as a downtown walk-by. Stay on open municipal sidewalk; skip in ice, closure or inadequate standing space. No business entry, tour, purchase or photograph is needed. [1,2]

## 2. The story
**When a call needed a worker.** After the 1904 flood, Cleyson L. Brown acquired the former Brady Building. Local Bicentennial plaque documentation identifies the address as his company's switching center. The National Register nomination records the 1902 Brown Telephone Company charter, 1911 United name and 1927 expansion. An independent telephone business grew inside an older downtown building and eventually occupied a much longer stretch of the street. [2,3]

**A company rule followed workers home.** The nomination quotes the March 1918 company newsletter, *The Voice*: “Save the other tenth.” It describes a 1922 direction that employees save 10 percent or find employment elsewhere. Named savings committee secretary David J. Eisenhower enters the record through this work. This is primary management language transcribed in the nomination, not an employee endorsement or proof of every worker's experience. [3]

**Work and housing.** Dickinson County Historical Society identifies the separately located Lebold Mansion as the late-1920s Girls Club, housing female United Companies employees. The historical housing link matters, but it does not establish that every resident switched calls or provide the names or firsthand recollections of residents. The house is now private. [4,5]

## 3. Then / now
The nomination describes early photographs showing the original Brown Block corner and records a substantial 1927 northern addition. The bureau identifies the exterior as a current downtown walk-by. Read the facade as a changed workplace, not as an unchanged 1904 exchange, and do not infer that switchboards or historic interiors survive there. [1,3]

## 4. What's still there
- **Public exterior view:** United Building from open city sidewalk along North Cedar/NW Third. Use lawful downtown parking and public crossings. Stay clear of traffic, doors and private frontage. No access to the building interior, rear lot or roof is asserted. [1,3]
- **Historic linked property, no customer routing:** The privately occupied Lebold Mansion is not part of the sidewalk stop. Don't visit or photograph the residence on the strength of this entry. [4,5]
- **Separate museum, not the switching center:** Dickinson County Heritage Center, 412 S. Campbell St., has a Museum of Independent Telephony. Operator-listed November–March hours are Tue–Fri 10 a.m.–5 p.m., closed Sat–Mon; confirm date-specific holiday hours. Its admission is separate and not needed for this entry. [6]

A historic road or district pin is not a public entrance.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Make one brief daylight sidewalk stop. From a clear public standing point read the corner-building and expansion story, then the short wage-saving account: the phone company connected callers and also reached into workers' household budgets. Read the Girls Club paragraph without leaving the public anchor. No route to either the private residence or an interior. [1,3,4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
The plaque account describes an absorption in 1911; the nomination records the 1911 renaming and Brown's sale of controlling stock to Missouri and Kansas Telephone in 1914. Those claims are not identical; use only the attested 1911 name change as common chronology. No named switchboard worker statement, original read of the 1918 newsletter, or present-day doorway/sidewalk field inspection. The two latter failures do not justify a fabricated worker voice or a hunt. [2,3]

## 7. Photo record
