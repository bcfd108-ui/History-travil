# Columbia Training School Site (Marion County BusinessPlex), Columbia — 1918–2008 industrial training school

## 1. Trip gate

**NO-ANCHOR RESEARCH READING — NO VISITOR ROUTING.** The former **Columbia Training School** (`1730 State Highway 44, Columbia, MS 39429`) closed as a state juvenile facility in **2008** following federal findings, and its ~3,100-acre campus was transferred (~2013) to Marion County as the **Marion County BusinessPlex**—a multi-tenant county business, light-industrial, and local sports/recreation park. Although the sole surviving 1918 campus landmark—the **Superintendent's Home** (`NRHP 95000757`, listed July 14, 1995)—was documented in 1995 as vacant/unrestored, there is **no public history museum, interpretive center, or historic tour program** on site, and surrounding BusinessPlex buildings house active private/county tenants. **No visitor pin or campus tour route is issued.** [1, 2, 3]

## 2. The story

Proposed by State Representative **J. E. Norwood** on **January 13, 1916**, and signed into law by Governor **Theodore G. Bilbo** on **March 28, 1916**, the **Mississippi Industrial and Training Institute** (later **Columbia Training School**) was created as Mississippi's first state juvenile reformatory—aimed at housing and training delinquent, destitute, and abandoned white boys and girls apart from adult prisons (while Black youth were later sent to Oakley Training School until desegregation around 1970). [1, 3]

To win the state campus over rival bids from Smith, Jones, and Forrest counties, the **Marion County Board of Trade** and local citizens donated **3,100–3,300 acres** of farm and timber land northeast of Columbia along Highway 44 plus **$50,000** (paired with a $25,000 state appropriation). Approved by the state board on **May 14, 1917**, the campus erected an administration building, two cottages, and the **Superintendent's Home** (`1918`, designed by McComb architect **Xavier A. Kramer** and built by **Dobbs & Wetmore**), enrolling its **first students on August 21, 1918**. [1, 3]

Most of the original 1918 institutional buildings were demolished in the **1960s and 1970s** and replaced with newer dormitories and classrooms—leaving Kramer's two-story white-stucco Craftsman **Superintendent's Home** (`NRHP 95000757`) as the **only major original 1918 building surviving**. Following federal Department of Justice investigations and reform-mandated closures, the state permanently **closed Columbia Training School in 2008**, sitting the property idle until Marion County took control around 2013 to redevelop it into the **Marion County BusinessPlex**. [1, 2, 3]

### Haunted history / lore

No haunted-place folklore is carried here. All history rests on the 1995 NRHP nomination (`95000757`), Marion County/Mississippi Power BusinessPlex announcements, and local press records. [1, 2, 3]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Donated acreage (`3,100` vs. `3,300` acres):** The 1995 NRHP nomination (`95000757`) records **3,100 acres** donated by Marion County citizens, whereas *The Columbian-Progress* (Nov. 7, 2018) cites **3,300 acres**. Both figures are printed. [1, 3]
- **Current physical condition of the 1918 Superintendent's Home:** Listed as vacant in the 1995 NRHP form and showing weathered porch overhangs in a September 2012 Commons photograph; whether it underwent stabilization after the 2013 BusinessPlex transfer was not confirmed in 2018–2026 county notices. [1, 3, 4]
