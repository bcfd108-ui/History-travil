## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** the story of a peach does not end at a beautiful harvest photograph. It includes the people who worked, raised families and argued for a decent place to rest afterward.

The Palisade Historical Society publishes **Salvador Rivas's recollections**, compiled from interviews **October 2024–January 2025** and his writings. He describes moving to Palisade in the early 1980s and working as an orchard foreman for **Mike Martin**. The publication names **Karalyn Dorn** as principal interviewer and translator/summarizer of his Spanish writings. [2]

Rivas's perspective holds appreciation and criticism together. He remembers community help while also describing workers' poorer housing and disagreement over improving it. His first-person account identifies him as both foreman and an immigrant worker who benefited from community meals. [2]

His short published voice is: **“Honest people mean a lot to me.”** [2] That statement places mutual respect beside the economic story. It is quoted from the English publication, not from audio heard during this research.

**Original interpretation:** a foreman can occupy more than one position at once. He can organize work, value relationships with growers and recognize that workers' conditions differ from owners'. Keeping those tensions visible produces a more useful history than either a cheerful harvest postcard or an account in which the worker has no judgment of his own.

## 3. Then / now

The historical society explicitly warns that its oral-history summaries are recollections, not independently fact-checked findings. [3] That warning is part of the evidence, not an inconvenience to remove. Rivas is a primary witness to his remembered experiences; his account is not a comprehensive labor survey or proof that every employer behaved alike.

Palisade's museum offers public agricultural-history interpretation. [1] Grand Junction's Museum of the West offers a broader regional setting. [4] Neither is the orchard where this account took place. No currently operating business, service organization or household is made a visitor destination by appearing in a historical narrative.

The regional filename does not relocate the testimony: this is specifically a Palisade account within the Grand Valley. Do not label its events as occurring inside Grand Junction without additional evidence.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

Proposed institutional reading experience, not an activated travel route:

1. **Confirm the museum entrance and day's gate.** Do not improvise around construction or enter neighboring property.
2. **Start with the work question.** What does the public interpretation say about who made the harvest possible? Do not assign an unidentified object to a particular worker.
3. **Read a short part of the published account.** Identify whether the passage is Rivas's writing, an interviewer summary or an editor's context.
4. **Compare gratitude with unequal conditions.** Both can exist in one recollection. Neither should be deleted to make the story simpler.
5. **End with the strongest story moment:** consider the worker as a community member and author, not merely labor behind a product. Reflect on what a museum can preserve without making someone's workplace or home public.

If visiting the Grand Junction alternative, the same questions can guide public interpretation, but the specific source may need to be read on one's own device. No object match, exhibit installation, seating, duration or walking distance has been verified. A digital-only session is complete in itself.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The document dates its interviews to 2024–2025; its retrospective work chronology is not a contemporaneous payroll record. No employment ledger, housing record or meeting minutes were inspected. The original Spanish writing and English summary were not compared line by line. The publication's editor sometimes adds historical context beyond Rivas's direct experience. [2]

A Jeff Johnson orchard memoir was located in the society's index but returned a verification/loading page when opened. It is not used as read evidence. [3]
