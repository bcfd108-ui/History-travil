## 2. The story
Begin with **Ron Wright's own published recollection**, not with a diagram of the violence. On the Mountain Meadows Monument Foundation's board page, Wright recalls learning in 1958, during a family trip, that relatives John and William Prewitt had been killed. His father told him and his sister. The short firsthand phrase is **“my dad told my sister and me.”** His account links family knowledge to later advocacy for historical recognition and truthful interpretation. This is a descendant-family recollection published by the organization, not an 1857 eyewitness statement or independently verified genealogy. [4]

**Nicole Fancher**, on the same page, describes taking over newsletter work from her grandfather Burr Fancher in 2017 and continuing the work of her grandfather and uncle Scott. The point is not a board roster: keeping records and explaining them is intergenerational work. [4]

The descendant organization MMMD explicitly places families' wishes and remembrance at the center of its work. That published position matters when a travel product decides what not to turn into a hunt. Its statements do not establish that one organization speaks for every family. [5]

The historical foundation is unambiguous at this level: about 120 emigrants traveling toward California were killed in September 1857, with 17 young children surviving. The operator's account places responsibility on local Mormon militiamen and dates the culminating massacre to September 11 after the violence began September 7. This dossier omits operational detail. [1]

**Institutional acknowledgment must be attributed carefully.** On September 11, 2007, Henry B. Eyring spoke on behalf of the First Presidency of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The inspected text expresses **“profound regret”**, identifies local Church leaders and members as responsible, and separately acknowledges the unjust burden of principal blame placed on Paiute people. This is the institution's own acknowledgment, not a descendant's acceptance of it. [3]

The strongest reflection is the distance between making an acknowledgment and having it received. Reporter Jessica Ravitz recorded **Priscilla Dickson**, identified as a Tackett-family descendant, asking for a direct apology; **Patty Norris** said it fell short of a direct apology while also saying she felt it was an apology and appreciated the effort. These are contemporary published reactions mediated by a reporter, not transcripts of interviews independently heard here. [6]

In the Utah Historical Society’s collection of monument photographs, the names occupy more than one image. Box 1, folder 1 lists two images of people killed, an inscription, then a separate list of survivors. The order matters. A child missing from the list of the dead still had a life to be accounted for. The Foundation describes fifteen surviving children taken east together in 1859 and two boys kept behind as witnesses and returned later. Its account places the larger group’s arrival in Carrollton, Arkansas, on September 25. Return to relatives took two years for children separated in September 1857. [10,11]

For the final reading put the finding aid’s spare words, “List of survivors,” beside the family compilation. Leave time after the names. The finding aid was read directly. The individual prints and the original relatives’ depositions were not examined. [8,11]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Count:** operator/MMMD use about 120 or 120; the Foundation's main historical narrative uses 121. Retain “about 120” in summary, with exact counts attributed rather than falsely reconciled. [1,5,10]
- **A concrete transcription/display problem:** the Foundation's newer victims page labels Alexander Fancher's entry “Lives Lost: 11,” but lists nine names and two survivors. Its earlier losses compilation explicitly distinguishes 11 family members, nine killed, two survivors. This is flagged, not silently corrected in the source. No total is recalculated from the inconsistent page. [7–8]
- **Apology terminology:** Eyring's text says regret; Ravitz reports that Church historian Richard Turley understood it as an apology and that some descendants did not hear the direct apology they wanted. No blanket claim of reconciliation or unanimous satisfaction is made. [3,6]
- **Paiute voice:** institutional acknowledgment of misassigned blame is not a Paiute-authored account. Ravitz also quotes **Lora Tom**, identified as a Paiute representative, describing the harm of inherited blame and the failure to seek Paiute accounts. That is a named published voice mediated by a reporter, not an inspected Tribal-authored statement; that gap stays explicit. Do not convert the Church's characterization into a consensus on participation.
- **Present access:** no winter calendar or separate non-burial public cultural entrance verified. Archive-only remains the release gate.
- No current board appointment, full genealogy, deposition authenticity, current signage wording or exhibit installation is independently certified by this dossier.

Age thresholds also differ. The Church’s published account says the seventeen surviving children were six and younger. The Foundation says seven and younger. The state archive describes those killed as over seven and identifies seventeen young survivors. These summaries do not establish each child’s age. [10,12,13]
