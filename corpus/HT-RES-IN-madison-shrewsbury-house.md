## 2. The story
Captain Charles L. Shrewsbury, a Virginia native and Ohio River shipping merchant and packer, commissioned Madison architect Francis J. Costigan to design a residence. Construction occurred 1846–1849. The result is a refined Greek Revival / classical brick house with a one-story garden portico supported by fluted columns with corn-stalk capitals, a low hip roof with denticulated cornice and attic windows, and elegant interior double parlors separated by Corinthian columns. A freestanding spiral staircase rises through the central hall to the attic. Shrewsbury, who later served as mayor of Madison (1870–1872), lived here with his family and entertained prominent guests.

Later owners included the Wyatt, Walker, and Windle families. The Walkers opened the house for public inspection in the 1930s. Historic Madison, Inc. acquired the property in the 1990s. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places and is recognized as one of Costigan's principal surviving works (alongside the Lanier House and Costigan's own residence). Interiors retain significant original and early features, including plasterwork and fireplaces.

## 3. Then / now
- 1846–1849: designed by Francis Costigan and built for Captain Shrewsbury.
- 1849–1872: Shrewsbury family residence; Shrewsbury serves as mayor late in the period.
- early 20th century: Walker family renovations and public openings.
- 1990s: acquired by Historic Madison, Inc.
- Today: preserved historic house in the Madison Historic District; exterior and major interior spaces intact.

## 4. What's still there
The brick house with its garden portico, corn-stalk capitals, hip roof, and classical detailing remains on West First Street. The spiral staircase and parlor column screens are documented interior features. The surrounding Madison Historic District contains other 19th-century buildings, including Costigan designs and the Ohio River setting.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Arrive in historic Madison and park near West First Street. Walk the sidewalk for exterior views of the portico and massing. If a tour is available, enter to see the central hall, spiral stair, and double parlors. Optional combination with the nearby Lanier State Historic Site and a walk of the riverfront historic district. Return via First Street or Main Street.
