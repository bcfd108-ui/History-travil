## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** a grocery-store sign can look like ordinary lettering. A family member remembers whose hand painted it, and suddenly the business becomes a record of childhood work.

TCCC publishes a first-person recollection by **David Lee**, son of the owners of **Alan's Market** in Barrio Hollywood, described as founded in the 1950s. He remembers helping his parents, whitewashing the building and painting its lettering. His short voice: **“the imperfect black lettering is my doing.”** He also remembers timing stocking chores to enjoy the walk-in refrigerator's cooling during humid weather. [2]

This is a named family worker's published recollection, not an anonymous illustration of immigrant enterprise. The quotation was read on the community institution's webpage; no original interview audio, recording date or transcript accession was established.

**Original interpretation:** the detail matters because family labor can disappear behind the word “owner.” Someone stocks, cleans, paints and learns how a working day feels. Looking for those acts in a historical account makes a family business legible without pretending every child experienced it in the same way. The remembered lettering is not claimed to survive today.

## 3. Then / now

The historical subject is a family market; the current destination is a cultural institution preserving community memory. They are different places with different access permissions. No storefront survival, continuity of business ownership or public admission is inferred.

A separate University of Arizona oral-history spotlight identifies **Lucy Estella Lim** discussing Chinese-Mexican family identity and growing up in a family-owned market during the 1950s and 1960s. The institutional page links a **3:29** audio short. Its description was read, but the audio was not heard in this pass; no quotation or additional life detail is attributed to Lim. [3]

That second source is a research lead, not a way to merge distinct families into one generalized story. A future deeper version could place different recollections beside each other while preserving each narrator's own identity and chronology.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Before travel:** verify the opening day and any holiday or event conflict. Retain the digital reading option.
2. **Enter only the public exhibit:** orient to the institution's current arrangements rather than following an invented room map.
3. **Read one family account slowly:** distinguish who owned a business from who describes doing its work. Check whether the displayed material identifies its narrator and source date.
4. **Bring in Lee's short published memory:** use it as a lens, not a promise that a specific physical panel is present.
5. **End at the strongest moment:** reconsider the supposedly imperfect lettering as someone's contribution to a family livelihood. The point is not to find the old wall; it is to notice the person whose effort a general history can overlook.

This sequence is proposed, not field-tested. No driving circuit, historic-store walk or exterior photo-recreation route is supplied. If the interpretation turns to exclusion or neighborhood loss, pause for reflection rather than turning that material into a challenge.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The community webpage does not date Lee's quoted recollection. Its historical founding period is not the date of his account. Do not assign him an exact childhood age or year from the market's approximate founding decade. [2]

The Arizona Historical Society explains that many locations in the Soleng Tom photo collection were identified using a **1940 city directory**, unless otherwise noted. That is a cataloging method, not proof of a photograph's precise exposure date or present building survival. The collection was copied in **1986** from an album Tom loaned; copying date and historical image date must remain separate. [4]

Neither the full Lim interview nor a comprehensive family ledger was examined. The center's advertised history boards were not physically inventoried. No historical store census is calculated from the selected examples, and no account is treated as representative of every Chinese or Chinese-Mexican household.

Winter holiday access and accessibility details remain open. No person or institution was contacted to resolve these gaps.
