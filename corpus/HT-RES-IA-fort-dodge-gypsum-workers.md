# Fort Dodge — The plaster above the stairs

Iowa OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**Before the mill came the kitchen stove.** In his later memoir as excerpted in the Historic American Buildings Survey, Webb Vincent recalled getting started with George Ringland in 1870. They tried heating gypsum outdoors and “we cooked gypsum in kitchen utensils on our kitchen stoves.” He dated their first mill to 1872 and remembered the panic of 1873 as part of an eight-year struggle to make a profit. These are an owner's recalled experiments and a timeline from his perspective. The downtown district nomination records the first stucco shipment on March 18, 1873, after the mill was built. The construction and shipment dates refer to separate events. The operations depended on grinding, transport and heat, with a rail connection carrying a finished product beyond Fort Dodge. [5, 6]

**A wall could be a sales demonstration.** Vincent recalled that the new stucco-based first plaster coat was used in repairing a bank ceiling and on the third floor of his home. He said that visitors climbed two flights of stairs to see it. HABS connects the surviving house to the industrial story with an 1879 deed transferring the property from Adeline E. M. Swain to Vincent for $4,000, and Anne Vincent's later interview about finishing the interior. A successful test in an owner's house tells little about what quarry hands and mill crews were paid, how shifts were divided or who carried and tended the material. Their names and regular work remain separate research questions. [5, 6]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic places.** Gypsum Hollow was the early industrial ground described by Vincent and the nomination. The HABS-surveyed Swain-Vincent House at 824 Third Avenue South gave the product an off-site testing and demonstration setting. The recollection is material evidence for use in the house at the time. The survival of that specific plaster layer after alterations remains unverified. [5–7]

**Lawful public anchor.** The publicly passable Third Avenue South sidewalk by the Vincent House frontage, Fort Dodge. Drive up and take a short approach from lawful parking. Remain on the city right-of-way, with no entry to the yard, private walks or interior. Skip the stop if a clear public sidewalk or safe footing is absent. The city identifies sidewalks as part of the usual public right-of-way. The operator's own venue site establishes the house and separately scheduled access. [1, 2, 5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

HABS and the National Register nomination give 1871 for the house. The current venue gives 1872. HABS lists a January 1871 lot deed and reports Anne Vincent's recollection of later interior work. A dated construction or inspection document beyond those reports was not obtained. The 1872 first-mill date is an independent event. The privately printed memoir was read only as quoted in HABS, and its original was not retrieved. The Iowa Geological Survey report at publications.iowa.gov returned HTTP 400 because the PDF was too large (26,318,862 bytes). Early payroll, quarry crew accounts and present-day plaster inspection remain open. [1, 5–7, 9]
