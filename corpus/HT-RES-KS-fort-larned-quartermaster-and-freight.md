# Fort Larned: the warehouse behind the wagon train
Draft for Dash QC • checked November 20, 2026 • no publication or hunt activation

## 1. Trip gate
**Visitable anchor:** signed Fort Larned visitor parking off KS 156, then the designated park walk to the visitor center and open warehouse. Stay in public circulation areas. [8–9]

**Public NPS visitor-center and historic-building stop, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Central; closed November 26, December 25 and January 1; snow/ice can interrupt access.** The parking gate locks at 5 p.m.; that is not an extra half-hour of visiting time. [1] No History Travel fee, booking, task or photograph is owed. NPS currently lists free admission. [2]

## 2. The story
**A new building could still be too small.** In October 1867, quartermaster inspector Marshall I. Ludington praised the newly built storehouse as “a substantial and suitable one.” The NPS Historic Structures Report reproduces his October 18 letter to Easton, citing Q.M.C.C.F., Box 533. Its next account describes the following year's overcrowded grain storage and threatened spoilage. This is a primary quotation mediated through an NPS study, not an original manuscript inspected here. [3,4]

**Someone had to receive, record and issue it.** Today's NPS interpretation separates quartermaster supplies from food and ammunition. Clothing, bedding, tools and animal feed occupied the warehouse; officers and clerks managed accountability, while the issue room connected storage with the people needing it. That is the human work to picture, rather than an anonymous line of picturesque wagons. [5]

**An inventory could also record dispossession.** NPS publishes the text of the April 18, 1867 order associated with Hancock's destruction of a Cheyenne-Lakota village. It names Captain G. W. Bradley, expedition quartermaster, as recipient of seized property and directs an inventory. This is evidence of the Army's actions and language, not a Tribal account or validation of the order's accusations. Read it quietly here, not as a destination to locate or reenact. [6]

## 3. Then / now
The storehouse later became a civilian barn, with raised walls and a hayloft; the historical study describes substantial alteration rather than an untouched 1867 room. [3] The current NPS warehouse page presents an interpreted sandstone interior. [5] NPS also identifies an original 1840s Conestoga wagon in the Quartermaster Storage Building, but does **not** establish that this particular wagon hauled freight to Larned during military occupation. An authentic period object is not automatically a site-used object. [7]

## 4. What's still there
**Lawful physical anchor:** Fort Larned National Historic Site visitor center and Quartermaster Warehouse, approached through the signed public park entrance/visitor parking off KS 156, six miles west of Larned. The operator prints 1767 KS Hwy 156, Larned, KS 67550. [8]

- Visitor center: public visitor facility during opening hours; begin with the day's room/route information. [2,8]
- Quartermaster Warehouse: public interpretive building when open; NPS lists wheelchair-accessible amenities, not a guarantee that every approach or threshold will suit every visitor. [5]
- Boundary: use signed visitor circulation and only open exhibit areas. Closed furnished rooms require staff accompaniment; gated service roads are not visitor approaches. No riverbank, quarry, historic wagon-yard or vanished teamsters' quarters route is supplied. [8,9]

Historic location: the quartermaster operation occupied the military post. The historic illustration also depicts vanished outbuildings; their drawn footprints are not present-day visitor entrances. A historic road or district pin is not a public entrance. [8]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
**One short interpretive visit, not an activated route.** Start at the visitor center to orient to the public buildings. If open, visit the warehouse and read the difference between stored goods and the issue room. The wagon is an optional exhibit, subject to its current display. Finish by reading the inspection and storage accounts together, followed by quiet reflection on the seized-property record. No reenactment, collecting or scored activity. [3,5–9]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
Current NPS gives the warehouse as 158 by 48 feet; the historical report gives 158 by 40. Do not harmonize these measurements without evidence. [3,5] The compendium allows grounds until 5, but the visitor-hours page says grounds closure begins at 4:30; use the earlier visitor window. [1,9] The original Ludington letter, grain complaint and a named freight delivery ledger remain unread; no particular load, civilian teamster or shipment is invented. NPSHistory is a third-party host of an NPS study, not the park operator. The dedicated directions page timed out twice; the official brochure supplies approach context. Tribal-authored treatment and a current matched photograph remain gaps, not reasons to invent them.
