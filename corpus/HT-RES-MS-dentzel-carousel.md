# Highland Park Dentzel Carousel and the shelter that kept it working

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP13 · Labeled research draft*

## 2. Story

The carousel at Highland Park was already a product of a Philadelphia workshop when it became part of Meridian's city park in 1909. The person who put his name on the ride spoke through its own painted decoration. The landmark nomination transcribes a panel reading “G. A. DENTZEL BUILDER,” followed by an advertisement for the company's carousel and a Germantown address. The inspected 2017 photograph shows the lettering partly behind a spoke. The longer transcription is from the nomination. It places the maker's claim where a visitor can see the commercial identity and the work of the carvers and painters together. [2, 3, 7]

Meridian bought a compact model. The federal nomination recorded a roughly 30-foot stationary carousel, two animals abreast, with horses, deer, antelope, giraffes, a lion, a tiger and two chariots. The revolving platform brought the figures past the people waiting. They were fixed to it, so the motion an operator had to manage was the floor turning rather than each animal rising and falling. The survey counted 28 carved animals and described three levels of painted scenery. Several painters' and carvers' names remain open. [2–4]

Meridian also built a shelter from company plans. In the nomination's account, the city park opened at the end of a streetcar line. A clerestory put light above the ring and a floor mosaic marked the space underneath. The building has served as an operating enclosure across more than a century of daily care and periodic repair. The city reopened it on November 18, 2026 after roofing, window and mechanical building work. Parks Director Thomas Adams described Phase One as “about protecting the building,” with a second phase for the ride itself dependent on grant funds. The quote is from his statement reported November 16. [2, 5, 6]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The 1909 Highland Park installation in Meridian survives at its historic park position. The carousel was made in Philadelphia before Meridian acquired it. The building and mechanism each have a separate work history. [1–4]

**Present public anchor.** Highland Park Dentzel Carousel and Shelter Building, 1802 Carousel Drive. Drive to signed park visitor parking and use the normal public approach. Interior viewing and riding follow staff permission, posted seasonal hours and rules. [1, 5, 6]

## 5. Public progression

Walk the signed entrance approach and look up at the clerestory. Inside, stay on the public side of the rail and compare the silhouettes of the outside row with the upper paintings. The story lands at **The maker’s sign above the turning floor**, where a shop's painted claim sits over the shaped wood, paint and machine it sold. When staffed entry is unavailable, read the silhouette of the building from the ordinary public approach and use the dated interior photograph remotely. [1, 2, 5–7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- Meridian gives 1896 and says the ride was manufactured for the 1904 St. Louis fair. The landmark study prefers 1892–1899, allows an earlier range and documents 1909 installation. A Dentzel invoice, serial ledger or fair concession record with a match to this machine was not retrieved. [1–4]
- The city history page gives 1977 for National Register and October 1986 for landmark status. The state property record distinguishes the 1977 survey, Highland Park listing February 28, 1979, and the carousel landmark February 27, 1987. NPS lists the carousel's federal package with February 27, 1987. [1–4]
- The state travel listing retained a renovation closure from 2025 when checked. The November 18, 2026 reports establish the reopening event. The city's recurring hours are the working schedule, subject to direct seasonal confirmation and future restoration. [1, 5, 6, 8]
- Original workshop payroll, the city purchase voucher and conservation in-progress records for individual painted panels were not retrieved. The 2026 completion refers to work on the shelter. [2, 5, 6]
