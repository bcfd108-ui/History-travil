# Piggott — The work behind the writing room

**HT-RES-AR-piggott-hemingway-pfeiffer-print-and-farm-work · AR OP06 · Labeled research draft · November 20, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate
A-State’s visitor page checked Nov. 20, 2026 lists hourly guided tours Monday–Saturday, first at 9 a.m. and last at 3 p.m. The museum closes at 4 p.m. and when the Piggott School District closes for weather. Check holiday hours before driving. Regular physical admission is $20, with reduced, student and free categories in the operator’s schedule. Start at the Museum Store west of the barn. [1]

## 2. Story
In a letter dated January 5 [1932], Hemingway addressed his mother-in-law Mary Pfeiffer and recalled “the wonderful time I had in Piggott.” The published transcription opens with a greeting to the family. In this particular document Piggott was a household he corresponded with. The letter provides no inventory of which pages were written there. [3]

Pauline Pfeiffer had a journalism education and worked at Vogue in Paris. A-State describes her editing and criticism of Hemingway’s work. That is a different record of work from the pages bearing his name. The preservation nomination also records Paul Pfeiffer’s bottomland holdings and his involvement in the Piggott Custom Gin Company. Together the sources connect editorial experience, a family’s economic resources and the physical room a writer used. They leave the workers on the surrounding farms largely unnamed. [2, 4]

## 3. Then and now
The preservation nomination records the Pfeiffer house and carriage house as separate structures. A-State restored the property to a 1930s appearance after acquiring it in 1997 and opened the museum in 1999. What visitors see reflects that interpretation and restoration. The letter dates a remembered visit. It does not date the restoration’s fittings. [2–4]

## 4. Surviving features and access
- House and Barn Studio: staffed tour only. The operator puts the store west of the studio and the general parking lot farther west, below the slope. [1]
- Surrounding agricultural parcels: documentary context. Keep them outside the visit.

## 5. Visit progression
Start at the Museum Store, follow the guide through the house, then finish in the Barn Studio. Read the opening of the letter where the guide finishes the tour. The story lands in **the walk from the family house to the writing room**, a physical way to read the connection that the letter alone leaves implicit. [1–3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
The nomination says 1926 for the first Paris encounter and attributes much of one novel to work in Piggott in winter 1928. A-State places the meeting in 1925 and describes the Piggott work as portions of the novel and several stories. The sources differ in date and strength. Check letters and manuscript collation before fixing composition details. The letter was read in a published transcription excerpt. The manuscript image and full text were not inspected here. No named farm worker or farm payroll was verified. [2–5]
