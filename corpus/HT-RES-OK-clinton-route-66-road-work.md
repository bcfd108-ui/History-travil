## 2. The story
**One-minute reading.** Ralph Kobel’s roadside business was not simply a gas pump. A traveler could have a vehicle repaired, eat and sleep at the same stop. The highway that made that work possible moved away from it while Clinton itself remained a Route 66 city. Read the local business story with the road agency’s actual relocation chronology rather than treating “the Mother Road” as one line on the map. [C5–C7]

**Five-minute reading.** The federal nomination ties the Y to the land transactions: Albert P. Sights developed the Neptune Park subdivision and set aside highway easement; Sights sold the lot to D. L. Kelly and Ralph Kobel in 1935. Kobel became sole owner in 1937; the nomination points to Custer County deed book 56, p. 587 and book 60, p. 583. The underlying deed images were not independently retrieved. The account connects a known service-station operator to the corner instead of assuming a nameless building grew there. It also records a WPA extension of city water south in 1937 and development of municipal Neptune Park on donated land the year before. Infrastructure and service work, not just souvenir culture, made the location function. [C5, sec. 8, pp. 11–12]

By the end of WWII the nomination finds the station and repairs, offices, a café and tourist courts operating as a cluster. An undated archival photo held by the museum records actual uniformed attendants and other employees; the image does not identify them, and the nomination’s printed telephone-directory evidence points to business services, not the staff roster. When the local alignment stopped running in front of the Y in 1956, the 1957 telephone directory still listed the café and lodging. The nomination records Glenna Kobel’s 1968 sale. The change was a process, not a single “bypass closed it” moment. [C5, sec. 8, pp. 12–13]

**Strongest story moment:** At the museum, follow the stages of one ordinary traveler’s stop: auto work, meal and overnight rest. Then read the same named business against the 1956 local loss of frontage and the state’s 1959 relocation entry. The public artifacts tell what made a road service economy spring up; the paperwork tells why workers at a perfectly usable corner no longer saw the traffic.

**Deep cut.** ODOT lists Oklahoma’s original designation through Clinton Dec. 7, 1926, a 1935 Choctaw/10th routing action and a 1941 eastbound city route, followed by an Aug. 3, 1959 relocation in Clinton and vicinity. The SHPO roadbed record places the Gary Boulevard designation in 1959. Keep the federal nomination's earlier 1956 account about no longer passing the Y as a distinct claim, not a silent replacement for ODOT's 1959 commission action. NPS also documents relief construction at McLain Rogers Park from 1934 to 1937, including the east gate directly on old Route 66 and the 1941 Highway Patrol Building. The park shows what the road demanded from a city even outside private retail. [C3, C5–C7]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
1. **Interpretation stop:** Start at the museum during confirmed hours; contrast a journey’s service, food and overnight work with the exhibit's national story.
2. **Optional view stop:** Make a separate local drive to the city park if ordinary parking, signs and a safe pedestrian route are apparent; view the gate from within public park space. No roadside stopping or inferred crossing, and no promise of nighttime neon.
3. **Archive finish:** Read the Y account and ODOT chronology while parked legally or at home. If the museum is closed, consult the online sources rather than route to private property.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- Nomination dates Kobel’s construction only tentatively to 1937; two museum photos suggest an earlier similar structure. Property transfer dates are firmer than construction dates. Keep the uncertainty.
- Do not conflate 1956 movement away from the Y (nomination), 1959 commission relocation (ODOT) or 1970 I-40 opening as reported in the nomination. More primary road diagrams would be needed for a versioned through-route.
- Named people here are landowner, service operator and seller. No clerk, cook, mechanic or motel worker names have been verified from the undated photo. No reconstructed testimony attributed to anyone.
- Neither present permissions nor ordinary parking at the Y are verified. Exact park gate/view accessibility, crosswalk, image capture and asset field condition are not verified. No address for the Y is printed as a visitor destination and no coordinate guessed.
