# Crossett — Jasper Burns walked to the work

**HT-RES-AR-crossett-mill-and-forest-workers · AR OP06 · Labeled research draft · November 20, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate
Crossett’s parks page describes Centennial Park across Main Street from City Hall, which the city lists at 201 Main St. Use the park’s open public area during daylight subject to posted hours, closures and weather. No mill, rail corridor, school grounds or experimental forest visit is part of this stop. [1, 2]

## 2. Story
Work on the experimental forest began during a Depression relief effort. In his 1980 Forest Service account, researcher R. R. Reynolds reproduces a September 29, 1933 note: “Began looking over timberland for a proposed Experimental Forest.” By December the project included building roads by hand. Reynolds names Ike Rawls, a former company log scaler assigned timekeeping, scaling and tools. The jobs were different forms of measuring a day’s work in the woods. [3]

Reynolds also names Jasper Burns, a Black worker whose knee was injured at a mill. He remembers a group from Stillion walking about eight miles along the Rock Island line to reach Crossett before being driven to the job. The return repeated the walk. Reynolds gives ten working hours, $1.25 per day and a four-day workweek. He says Burns later became a permanent research staff member. These particulars are one supervisor’s retrospective account. A relief payroll or testimony from Burns’s household would give an independent measure of the schedule and its costs. [3]

## 3. Then and now
The raw materials, transport and wages of a forestry town show up in Reynolds’s account of a filling-station pickup, hand tools and a long walk after a mill closed. The city’s present Centennial Park is a town reading place with a surviving Rose Inn fireplace. The park has no demonstrated role in the 1933 pickup. The municipal auditorium nomination records a separate, later relationship between company resources and town civic life. [1, 3, 4]

## 4. Surviving features and access

## 5. Visit progression
Go directly to the park side of Main Street. Read Reynolds’s diary line, then his account of the Stillion workers. End with **the walk after the shift**, when Burns still had miles to cover after the crew’s ride back into town. Keep the historic route off the itinerary. [1, 3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
The diary wording is Reynolds’s 1980 transcription of his earlier note. The original notebook was not inspected. The researcher’s account is direct testimony of a supervisor, with no independent payroll, newspaper corroboration or Burns family testimony located in this batch. Road-site ownership and access remain outside the draft. Exact pickup location and park hours are unverified. [1, 3]
