## 2. The story

Long before state medical use, the **Greenwell Springs** bluff beside the Amite River gained nineteenth-century fame as a mineral-water resort (`Greenwell Springs Hotel` era). In the **1920s** (commonly cited around 1926–1928), the State of Louisiana established **Greenwell Springs Tuberculosis Hospital / Sanatorium** on the quiet wooded pine tract along the Amite River (`23260 Greenwell Springs Rd.`), providing fresh-air sanatorium wards, residential staff quarters, and utility buildings away from urban centers. [1, 6]

As tuberculosis treatment shifted away from long-term inpatient sanatoriums, the campus transitioned in the **1980s** into a state **psychiatric and behavioral-health hospital** under the Louisiana Department of Health and Hospitals (**DHH**). In **March 2012**, amid state hospital closures and budget cuts under Governor Bobby Jindal, Greenwell Springs Hospital closed permanently. [1, 2]

After brief plans for BREC historic park reuse, East Baton Rouge / Central authorities transferred the `23260 Greenwell Springs Rd.` parcel for a new Central school campus; **demolition began in summer 2015**, leaving virtually nothing of the sanatorium complex standing by March 2016 aside from an isolated smokestack reported during clearing. [3, 4]
