## 2. The story
The revealing object is an ordinary school paper. **Alberta Henry**, recalled in a University of Utah digital exhibition through an excerpt from her oral history, described making copies of students' work before they submitted it, because missing assignments were being used to explain their difficulties. The short published instruction is **“give me your test paper before you hand it in.”** The exhibition attributes this to **Interviews with African Americans in Utah, Alberta Henry, Interview 1**, item **893658**. The excerpt was read; the underlying repository returned HTTP 418, and no full audio or transcript is claimed. [4–5]

That concrete action changes the scale of civil-rights history. It was not only a public speech or a law. It could be someone ensuring that a young person's work could not disappear from the record. This is an interpretive inference from Henry's account, not a claim about any present school or teacher.

The library exhibition describes Henry's advocacy for Black teachers and counselors and a student recognition organization that became the **Rainbow Honor Society**. Her work linked access, recognition and practical support. Keep that community-building dimension alongside the account of exclusion. [4]

The repository finding aid identifies her papers as records of NAACP leadership, educational work and community organizations. It names an **Alberta Henry Education Foundation** folder in **ACCN 2069, Box 2, Folder 1**, dated 1969–1998. That is a lead to actual organizational records, not proof that the folder's contents have been read or that its date range dates the foundation's beginning. [6]

## 3. Then / now
- **1949:** the University exhibition places Henry's move to Salt Lake City in this year, describing the limits imposed on professional employment for Black women. [4]
- **1960s onward:** retrospective sources describe scholarship organizing and educational advocacy. The institutional founding year is not collapsed into one date; see the discrepancies below. [6–7]
- **1980–92:** the state archive's published biography identifies Henry's presidency of the Salt Lake City NAACP. [7]
- **Present:** her own recollection, institutional papers and retrospective interpretation survive in different forms. None should be mislabeled as a current display at the city library.

A building open to everyone today does not establish how welcome people were elsewhere in the historical city. Conversely, a history of exclusion does not turn every current institution into the setting of that history. The separation is essential to this stop.

## 4. What's still there
**Public reading space:** the Main Library's ordinary public interior is the only proposed physical anchor. The operator describes a rooftop terrace with weather/program restrictions; it is excluded here rather than treated as a winter viewpoint. [2]

**Research holdings elsewhere:** the Alberta Henry papers are held by University of Utah Special Collections, not the City Library. The finding aid encourages advance notice, requires on-site use and warns of restrictions on portions of the collection. No archival visit or retrieval request has been made. [6]

**Named documentary record:** the state archive's Henry biography reproduces descriptions of her 1975 Council on Black Affairs and 1976 State House Fellowships oaths, both in **Series 17170**. These are historical service records, not evidence of a present office or invitation to enter a government workspace. [7]

**Excluded:** private residences, churches during worship or other operations, schools, former business interiors, student records, restricted archives and photographs of people currently using services. There is no city walking circuit in this file.

**Permanent rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
1. **Before leaving:** check the Main Library's holiday and weather gate. If closed, use the same online sources elsewhere; do not shift to a school or church door.
2. **In the public reading area:** identify who is speaking and who is publishing. Henry's quoted recollection and a curator's summary are not the same evidence type.
3. **Read the small action:** consider why making a copy of schoolwork could matter when an institution's account and a student's account disagreed.
4. **Add the community layer:** distinguish scholarships, representation and recognition from a story told only as individual endurance.
5. **End on the evidence:** the strongest moment is realizing that preserving a record can itself be protective work. Ask what becomes invisible when only the official summary survives.

This is quiet reflection, not an adversarial exercise with current staff. Do not ask a visitor to accuse a school, perform discrimination, recite slurs or present personal hardship for verification. No timed challenge or proof-of-visit photo.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Direct source boundary:** Henry's firsthand words were inspected as a quoted excerpt in the repository's digital exhibit. Full item 893658 could not be retrieved, so interview date, interviewer and exact surrounding context remain unchecked. [4–5]
- **Education dates:** the University exhibit gives Henry's bachelor's degree as 1982; the Utah State Archives biography and manuscript finding aid give 1980. No degree date is adopted as settled. [4,6–7]
- **Foundation dates:** the manuscript finding aid says 1965; the library guide describes a 1960 student group, 1966 foundation and December 1967 charter. These may represent different organizational stages, but that reconciliation is not proven. [6,8]
- **Jurisdiction wording:** sources vary between Salt Lake City and Salt Lake County school district wording. This draft does not silently normalize the employer's institutional name across records. [4,6–7]
- **Venue evidence:** no current Black-community exhibit or Henry installation at Main Library was verified. Historical event listings elsewhere are not reused as current invitations.
- Operator access pages were search-indexed but resisted full retrieval; the state directory independently confirms the regular public gate, not special-day conditions.
- This single case is a focused entry, not a comprehensive history or a voice representing every Black Utahn.
