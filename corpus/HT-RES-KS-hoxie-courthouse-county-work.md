# Hoxie — The mail carrier who became county clerk

## 2. The story

**A county seat and a county office.** A county history recounts the 1886 election that moved the county seat from Kenneth to Hoxie. State historical records identify the current courthouse as the 1917–1918 building. The election created a place to conduct the county’s business. The brick and stone building visitors see belongs to a later generation of that work. The early frame courthouse in the published historical photograph is a separate structure. [1,4–6]

**Seven years on the road.** William L. Davidson was born at Kenneth in 1883. William E. Connelley’s near-contemporary 1919 history reports that he returned to Hoxie in 1906, entered hardware work and spent seven years as “a rural mail carrier.” The same biography says he was elected county clerk in 1916 and gives January 13, 1919 as the end of the term. The account is a modern transcription of the printed biography. It gives an officeholder working across the courthouse building period. It does not identify Davidson as the project manager or show where he sat on a given date. [4,7]

**Two generations of recordkeeping.** Connelley names William’s father Samuel P. Davidson as a probate judge, newspaper editor and Hoxie postmaster. The county records indexed by the state include microfilmed probate dockets. This is a separate office and a separate paper trail from his son’s elected clerkship. The state archive’s finding aid supplies provenance and dates, but the docket pages and county commission minutes were not available in the retrieved item. [7,8]

**Who put up the building.** The courthouse survey credits Thomas W. Williamson & Company and contractor George Cope and Sons. Local retrospective history repeats the attribution. The courthouse’s raised base, two-story columns on the east and differentiated elevations show the importance the county assigned to the building. The design and construction attributions rest on the later records cited here. No signed contract, bond, payment roll or minutes were inspected. [4–6]

## 3. Then / now

**Historic location.** The 1917–1918 courthouse on its Hoxie block. The town’s public offices moved at an earlier date. Kenneth and the old frame building are historic context. Their exact positions and visitation are outside this stop. [1,4–7]

**Lawful physical anchor.** The ordinary public Ninth Street frontage outside the address printed by the district court. Approach by legal street parking and a short walk on public sidewalk where open and continuous. Stop at the public right-of-way. The county directory and court still place working offices here. Neither source advertises a public historic tour, and the exact accessible short-walk alignment and winter maintenance are unverified. [1–3,5]

The courthouse continues as an office and courthouse. The biographies, office directory and surviving elevations let the traveler compare the long continuity of public work with changes in who performed it and where. A modern courtroom layout or the site of Davidson’s desk has not been reconstructed. [3–5,7]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- State archive administrative history gives 1873 for establishment, while the courthouse survey says the county was created in 1867 and organized in 1880. These accounts use different terms. The enabling and organization instruments were not retrieved. The 1886 transfer and 1917–1918 standing building are separate dated events. [4,5,8]
- Direct 1917–1918 commission minutes, bond, building contract, contractor’s accounts and named worker payroll were not located in the retrieved online sources. Connelley’s original printed page was read through KSGenWeb’s transcription, not a facsimile. Davidson’s exact role on the new build remains unverified. [5,7,8]
- The November 26 closure is an office-calendar entry. Holiday or restricted grounds, precise public standing point, snow clearance and step-free continuity remain for a field check. [1–3]
