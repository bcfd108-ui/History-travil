# Keokuk — Three ways through the rapids

Iowa OP03 · Research draft for Dash

## 2. Story

**Get a tow past shallow water.** The Des Moines Rapids were a practical barrier to navigation. The Historic American Engineering Record sets out the history of successive methods for passing them. Work on the federal canal ran from 1867 to 1877. Montgomery Meigs later oversaw its improvement work, dated 1883–1889 in the report. Look at the river as a place that held a tow back long before it sent electric power down a transmission line. [3]

**A power project obliged to keep navigation going.** In its Feb. 8, 1913 account, Commercial West reprinted a World's Work story about promoters seeking an engineer and finding that “there was no lack of applicants for the job.” They chose Hugh L. Cooper. The passage is a contemporary printed version available through FRASER’s indexed PDF text. It captures how promoters described their choice of engineer. It gives no count or pay scale for laborers. An Iowa-side replacement lock and dry dock accompanied the dam and powerhouse. HAER dates the main power and navigation work to 1910–1913 and treats the dry dock as its own 1912–1914 construction sequence. [3, 4]

**The next lock brought another workforce.** The reported 1952–1957 conversion to a 1,200-foot chamber was another construction campaign. HAER's contract history names George H. Holiday for painting, Clifton Dunlon for plastering and Economy Builders for earthwork and concrete. The names are in an engineering study based on institutional records. Individual day wages, workplace accounts and the contribution of crews below the contract names remain open. [3]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The canal, early power dam and lock, the dry dock and the later working chamber belong to overlapping but different locations and phases by the Mississippi at Keokuk and Hamilton. The modern lock is operational. Federal river-mile and gage records belong to infrastructure, not to the visitor deck. [3, 5, 6]

**Lawful public anchor.** City Gateway Parks public observation area in the First and Second Street area, Keokuk, a drive-up and short walk only along posted municipal access. The local tourism bureau identifies an old bridge observation deck near Lucas and North First. Use the signed public entrance only if open. The city lists its observation deck as a reservable public facility. Keep out of reserved, fenced, road, rail and operational space. If public access is ambiguous or walking surfaces unsafe, omit the stop. [1, 2]

## 4. Surviving features and access

- **Public exterior** — the signed city observation area, subject to closures and reservations. Lower approaches and seasonal maintenance were not verified. [1, 2]
- **View only** — powerhouse, dam line and lock landscape. Operating apparatus, federal work areas and the bank below are viewed only from municipal public space. Corps documentation supplies engineering context. [2, 3, 5]
- **Source or access held** — the lock office and working facility, dry-dock ground, riverboat boarding, and the occasionally advertised inland museum option are outside this routing. A current firsthand museum schedule was unavailable. [2, 3, 5]

## 5. A short visit

At the signed public deck, start with the river itself. Read the three phases in the HAER account from the public rail if conditions permit. Follow the length of the powerhouse with your eyes, then read the 1950s names. Land on **Three ways through the rapids**, the shift from federal canal to early twentieth-century low lock to today's long chamber. The lock need not be operating within view for the three-phase story to work. [1–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The city’s construction notice attaches to Hubinger Landing Bank Street Campground. It does not establish a closure at Gateway Parks. The signed visitor approach, reservation and ice status still govern the actual visit. The tourism bureau deck address and the city’s First/Second Street area describe the approach with different granularity. Their parcel-level correspondence was not established. [1, 2]
- HAER gives river mile 364.5 for its complex. The Corps live gage identifies 364.2 for its instrument. The reported locations have different scopes and neither gives a visitor coordinate. [3, 6]
