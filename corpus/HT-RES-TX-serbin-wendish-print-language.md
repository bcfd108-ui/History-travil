# Serbin — carrying a language into a new school
Research draft · Texas OP05 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### Migration required written promises

In 1854 a Wendish Lutheran group came to Texas with Johann Kilian, part of a migration whose organizers articulated their intentions before they reached Serbin. The Texas Wendish Heritage Society prints a translation of Kilian’s call, which placed its aim “to emigrate to North America” alongside a plan to establish a congregation and community. It specifies a first-year salary in Taler and divides payments around departure. The terms place a human obligation inside the story of a ship crossing. The society locates the original in the congregation’s archive. The reproduction read for this dossier is a translation [2–3].

### A pupil remembered learning on both sides of the Atlantic

Teacher L. O. Kasper’s school account reproduces a greeting from former pupil John Teinert: “I attended Pastor Kilian’s classes already in Germany.” Teinert remembered resuming instruction in Texas and completing it in 1856. Kasper’s account places the new school in 1856, notes subsequent teachers and names the people he could still identify from early classes. The letter excerpt survives here through a teacher’s printed account and its later online reproduction. It describes continuity in a single pupil’s life, without establishing that every class used the same language [4].

### Print made the language portable

The museum identifies Kilian’s translations into Wendish, his writings and printed works in both German and Sorbian among its holdings. Those objects show how teaching and correspondence continued beyond one gathering. Ask staff which related items are currently on ordinary public display. The online museum inventory is a research lead, and no particular volume or page is promised in the gallery [1–2, 5].
