# Carlsbad CCC workers: who kept their names in the story

Division NM • OP04 • Local research draft • Checked 19 November 2026

Visitor-center-only interpretive reference. Named firsthand source is a later park historian remembering recognition work, not a CCC enrollee's inspected testimony. Enrollee-voice gap remains open. No cave, trail, road itinerary, employee-property or work-area access; no upload or contact.

## 1. Trip gate

**Official public visitor center.** NPS publishes visitor-center hours **9 a.m.–5 p.m.** and closure on **Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day**. Its operating-hours page explicitly extends those holiday closures to other park facilities. Do not treat the visitor center as available on those dates. [1]

**Printed public address.** NPS basic information identifies **727 Carlsbad Caverns Highway, Carlsbad, NM 88220** as the visitor-center address. The frequently repeated **3225 National Parks Highway** is labeled a mailing address, not this visitor entrance. No driving directions are supplied. [2]

**Separate permissions.** This entry covers only public visitor-center interpretation during an actual opening. Cavern admission, tours and other activities have separate requirements and are not included or authorized by this dossier. No ticket was purchased and no tour requested.

**Winter and accessibility.** NPS notes that winter can bring snow and ice; its accessibility page confirms accessible visitor-center services and parking. Same-day conditions, facility interruptions and a particular accommodation were not independently verified. Telephone **575-236-1309** is published by NPS. [1][2][3]

## 2. The story

**Bob Hoff's own recollection.** In a March 8, 2009 post, former Carlsbad Caverns park historian **Bob Hoff** recalls the dedication of a CCC exhibit on **May 17, 2000**. He credits interpreter **Dave Hutson's** interviewing and interpretive work as a major impetus. This is Hoff's firsthand account of later commemoration, not a worker's account of the 1930s. [4]

His short written voice is **“Dave also conducted some interviews”**. The phrase matters because remembering labor required another kind of labor: asking, listening and preserving. Hoff also thanks **Avelina Childress** for supplying photographs. [4]

**Original interpretation.** A finished visitor landscape can hide the people who made it. This story turns attention toward the work of restoring names and experiences to the public record. The recognition was not automatic; people had to gather evidence and decide that labor belonged in the interpretation.

**No borrowed voice.** No unidentified CCC worker is given invented dialogue. Hoff's account is not presented as the opinion of every enrollee, nor as an official statement of current park policy.

## 3. Then / now

**Official historical frame.** The NPS *Building the Future* panel dates **Camp NP-1-N to 1938–1942**, connects it to Depression-era employment and gives **$30 monthly pay**. It establishes the park's institutional account of the program, not an individual's verified pay record. [5]

**A different Carlsbad record.** UNT catalogs an interview with **Albert B. Thorn**, conducted by **Ronald E. Marcello on July 25, 1990**, covering CCC service at Lake Arthur and Carlsbad in **Company 2842**. The catalog is inspected; interview page access did not succeed. No quotation or narrative detail is extracted from an unread transcript. [6]

**Do not merge camps.** The Carlsbad geographic connection does not establish that Thorn served in the park camp discussed by Hoff or in the NPS panel. That identification remains unproved. Neither company number nor camp designation should be silently substituted for the other.

## 4. What's still there

**Public visitor center:** the independently established current interpretive anchor, subject to operating conditions. [1][2]

**Digital NPS panel:** an accessible public documentary resource about the CCC. Its presence online does not prove its exact current physical display location. [5]

**Historical recognition record:** Hoff's published recollection of the 2000 dedication. The continued presence or arrangement of that particular exhibit has not been verified. [4]

**Archive lead:** UNT's named interview record, which is not a Carlsbad walk-in archive appointment. [6]

**Excluded:** caves, trail systems, former camp footprints, staff housing, service roads, utility installations, maintenance buildings and any operational area. Historical association is not access authorization.

**Permanent rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

**Before departure: establish the two timelines.** Read the NPS panel for the Depression-era program, then Hoff for the later effort to recognize it. Keep building work and the history of remembering that work distinct.

**At the public center: use what is actually presented.** Stay within open visitor areas. Current labels and staff direction govern the visit; do not assume the 2000 exhibit remains in its earlier form.

**Compare record types.** An institutional panel supplies a broad account. A historian's recollection reveals how evidence was gathered. A catalog points toward a worker interview but does not substitute for hearing or reading it.

**End with the strongest story moment.** Consider the chain from worker to interviewer to preserved public history. The endpoint is recognition of people, not a search for where they worked. No field route is needed to make the historical connection.

**If closed: remain digital.** The official panel and published recollection support a complete reference session. Do not improvise a stop at a gate, campground, staff building or nearby historical footprint.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

**Enrollee account still missing.** A named CCC worker interview was located, but its content was not successfully opened. This draft therefore does not meet a stronger standard requiring directly examined CCC-enrollee testimony. Its inspected primary source is Hoff's later park-worker recollection. [4][6]

**Publication and event dates differ legitimately.** Hoff's post is from 2009 and describes a 2000 dedication. Neither date is the CCC camp's operating period. [4][5]

**Schedule ambiguity elsewhere.** NPS's basic-information page has generalized park-hours text that is not the same as visitor-center hours. The specific operating-hours page is used for this stop. Check current alerts before relying on an actual visit; no live condition clearance was obtained. [1][2]

**Unverified details stay out.** No exact household remittance, individual work assignment, reconstructed wage budget, surviving structure attribution or physical CCC exhibit location is asserted. No operational construction details are reproduced.
