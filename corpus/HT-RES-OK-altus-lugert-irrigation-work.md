# Altus–Lugert — moving water after the dam
Research draft · Oklahoma OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

The Bureau of Reclamation places the authorization for irrigation and flood control in 1938. The lower reaches stayed a works problem after the dam was built. A main canal carried water from the reservoir, crossed the North Fork through a concrete siphon and later divided into the larger system. The Bureau describes a 270-mile network including the main canal. Those figures describe the system as a whole. A gated or private work site is outside the visit. [2–3]

The Senate’s 1947 report is titled “Changing the name of the Lugert-Altus irrigation project in the State of Oklahoma to the W.C. Austin project.” The federal item supplies an administrative milestone, separate from the first water or the end of construction. An earlier local dam and the wider federal effort appear in the historical account. The Museum of the Western Prairie holds the project photograph collection. Photographer R. M. Reynolds appears in its item record for a 1946 dam exposure. His dated work gives the construction story a named observer. [4–7]

## 3 · Then / now

Historic work runs across the reservoir, dam, canals and irrigated farmland. The quiet reading includes the older Lugert setting, with no submerged-town approach. The only public anchor is the Museum of the Western Prairie ordinary visitor entrance, drive-up and short walk. Confirm the open exhibit on arrival. [1–3,5–7]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior — museum galleries. Documents and project images can be read remotely. Extant dam and irrigation works are physical history with operating boundaries. No dam access, water entry or active canal frontage. [1–3,6]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

Read the museum’s irrigation account, trace the path from reservoir to siphon and compare the naming report with the project chronology. **Strongest story moment — Water had to cross water.** Stop in the open museum and read the concrete siphon description as a piece of work that stands between storage and delivery. [1–4,6]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

No named beneficiary’s original farm diary or work record recovered. The earlier local structure and the 1940s federal one are separate. The date of authorization, dam image and Senate naming report do not establish the dates every farm began receiving water. Exhibited object inventory unverified. [2–7]
