## 2. The story
**Hiroshi Kashiwagi**, interviewed by **Chizu Omori and Emiko Omori on October 1, 1992**, recalled resisting registration after his rights had been taken away. In segment 3 he distinguishes refusing to register from the label subsequently applied to him. He also remembers his mother's overriding concern: **“her real concern was to keep us all together.”** The published segment transcript was read, not merely a catalog summary; the recording itself was not played. [4]

The choice of a short family-centered passage is deliberate. It resists reducing an incarcerated person to a questionnaire category. His explanation combines protest, uncertainty and family dependence. It does not establish that every family made the same choices or that an interviewer’s question should become a visitor's loyalty test.

The repository explains that this interview was made for the Omori sisters' documentary *Rabbit in the Moon*, rather than as a comprehensive life history. A focused interview has its own purpose and silences. The record also identifies Kashiwagi's later writing and theatrical work; incarceration is not the entirety of a person's life. [5]

The interpretive landing is the distance between an institutional label and a family's reasons. That is an editorial reading of the testimony, not an invitation to debate which incarcerated people deserved their rights. Do not ask visitors to answer the historical questionnaire, role-play a confinement decision or judge family members against one another.
