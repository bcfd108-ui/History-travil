# Eureka Springs railroad workers and historic depot

## 2. Story

The trip brought nearly eight hundred people into a steep Ozark town, according to the 1899 Fort Smith Elevator item abstracted by local historians Dorothy Doville and Mary Lou Jacobsen. Thirteen coaches ran in two sections. The report preserves the names of two men in charge: “J. P. Collier was conductor of one section, P. J. McNamara of the other.” That is the wording of the published microfilm abstract, with a word joined across a print line. It gives a named human scale to a feat often recorded only in passenger totals. The original newspaper issue and its full date still need direct verification. [4]

Once passengers arrived, the railway’s work turned into someone else’s shift. The same newspaper account singled out hotel rates, electric cars and a concert. It treats the excursion as a success. Read it as a local report about visitors’ experience, without assuming it describes a hotel worker’s pay or even names who served the visitors. The National Register narrative places the first passenger-depot celebration in January 1883, when the line from Seligman was completed. By 1899 the visiting group could arrive as a mass excursion. By 1913 the railroad was building a new stone depot north of town. [4–6]

A separate local recollection adds an occupational path that the excursion report cannot. In Cora Pinkley Call’s account of the Ross family, Dixie A. Ross helped lay the last tie of the line from Seligman and later became a conductor. The book recounts that he quit during a major strike while sympathizing with the union. The PDF could be read only as web-indexed text after direct retrieval returned HTTP 503. Treat it as a retrospective community account with payroll and strike records open. The worker voices so far are preserved through a newspaper and a local author, with no direct employee testimony in hand. [7]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The 1899 train came to the earlier railway town. The surviving native-stone 1913 depot at 299 N. Main is a later structure. The everyday visitor anchor at the restored 1889 Calif House, 95 S. Main, gives a public interior at a different point along Main Street. [1, 2, 4–6]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive up to the Eureka Springs Historical Museum in the Calif House and use the short posted entrance approach. Small visitor parking is limited to museum use. Use permitted public parking and the open walk if needed. Keep this draft visit on the museum approach and staffed floors; the rail yard, platform, road shoulders near the depot and private lots are excluded. [1, 2]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The 1979 boundary increase places the stone depot in 1913. The earlier first passenger depot and the 1899 Woodmen excursion are separate evidence; the more recent district material and essay differ on what date best describes the railway’s service decline. The nomination emphasizes the forty-year regional line, while the architectural essay describes an early-1920s end to the local passenger era. Those scopes cannot establish a final train date for this depot. [5, 6, 8]
- The rail operator’s lunch page gives a dated October 31, 2026 end. The excursion page provides a season start and month-by-month availability, and the older landing page still shows 2020. None supplies a verified ordinary November depot entrance. The chamber’s museum page lists a different schedule from the museum itself. Use the museum’s first-party schedule. [1–3, 9, 10]
- Full Elevator issue and original local book file were not retrieved. The original payroll and a named hotel employee’s account are open. The abstract’s page 33 does establish both conductors’ names. [4, 7]
