# Normal Hill: the clock tower Lewiston built twice

History Travel home-ring draft for Dash QC · Checked Nov. 18, 2026 Pacific · Not published; no field photography or cleared image pair.

## 1. The story

Start with the distinction visitors can actually see: the tower's masonry survived the fire that gutted Lewiston State Normal School, but the silhouette did not. The spire they see now is a reconstruction. The place is not less authentic for that. It gives a visitor a way to read loss, repair, and memory in one building. [A, B]

The Idaho Legislature established the Normal School in 1893 to train teachers. The campus historian traces its first ten acres to half of Lewiston's city-owned Plaza Park, and reproduces an excerpt of a 1900 fire-insurance map. Today those old street names do not line up word-for-word with the current signs. The historian crosswalks Cleveland to today's Tenth Avenue and describes the early campus roughly between Fifth and Sixth Streets and Seventh and Ninth Avenues. A map swipe here can teach a visitor how the city itself changed, not just how one tower changed. [C, D, E]

The college's facilities plan dates the first building to 1895. Its account places a large east addition in 1905; the college's 2014 fire history calls the lost wing the 1906 wing. Neither source makes clear whether one date describes completion versus opening. After the 1917 fire, the facilities plan says the 1918 rebuild reused the tower and front and rear walls but did not rebuild the east wing or spire. The retrospective also credits firefighters with saving the west wing then under construction and describes the loss of records from 1896 to 1917. That gives the absence a human scale: a school could resume teaching, but it could not replace all the proof of who had studied there. The college subsequently called on descendants to help recover independently held documents. [A, F]

The restored cupola and clock came with the 1993 centennial. The college added a flag to the tower in 2018 and presented it as returning something lost in 1917. Read that flag, the clock and the masonry as features from different periods, not evidence that the entire prefire building remained intact. The building today is called Reid Centennial Hall; the campus map also identifies a different building called Administration, with the Silverthorne Theatre. This distinction matters when an archive labels a photograph simply “Administration Building.” [A, B, G, H]

## 2. Then / now and photo evidence

- **Then:** the December 1900 Sanborn map is a primary, publicly reusable period source for the early campus footprint. LC State's historian reproduces and identifies the relevant sheet-one detail. Link to the Library of Congress catalog and the institutional transcription for QC. This is map evidence, not a matched-ground photograph. [D, E]
- **Historic photo candidates:** the 1910 University of Idaho yearbook includes a captioned Lewiston State Normal School panorama showing a pointed tower, a prefire lead. Confirm printed page/scan frame and reuse status before embedding. A separate c.1925 University of Idaho photo is cataloged as Administration Building, archive item cities337 / 5-007-3c; it is not proven to be Reid, and its education-use statement does not clear general app reuse. The collection's coordinates identify a catalog location, not a photographer's surveyed stance. [I, J]
- **Now:** LC State's 2024 Tree Campus submission has a labeled contemporary exterior of Reid's clock tower. Its caption uses 1893 for construction, conflicting with the facilities plan; do not carry the caption's construction date forward. Use this photo as identity and feature evidence only, not as a cleared or aligned slider frame. [A, K]
- **Stand Here candidate:** identify Reid from the current visitor map first. Compare the 1910 tower base to the modern facade, then check a second fixed landmark such as a surviving masonry return or street edge before estimating any bearing. The clock, spire and flag alone do not prove alignment because they were changed. Candidate position is a permitted exterior walkway facing Reid. Exact viewpoint, bearing, both images' reuse status, and a surveyed public pin are unverified; keep overlay/navigation off. [A, G, I, K]

## 3. Runnable visitor stop

Use LC State's official campus directions and select **Reid Centennial Hall (RCH)**, not the separate Admin building. Main campus: 500 8th Avenue, Lewiston, Idaho. Admissions' directions identify a visitor lot beside Reid and say a permit is required. Use posted campus rules, an approved visitor permit or separately lawful parking; do not assume a free spot. Use sidewalks and public approaches during daylight, do not enter classrooms or residence areas, and stop if access is restricted. The archive is closed-stack and requests advance arrangements; no archive or interior tower tour is part of this entry. No exact barrier-free exterior photo approach checked. [C, G, H, L]

Stop progression, 15–25 minutes suggested: find Reid on the map; read the fire account where the clock tower is in view; compare the cupola and flag with the old silhouette; finish by looking at the campus footprint on the 1900 map. The strongest moment is looking at the tower and knowing which part is a survivor and which part was deliberately brought back. If public approach is blocked, the period map and modern labeled image still tell the story remotely, without a pretend exact pin.

## 4. Candidate physical find

## 5. Honest gaps

- Founding 1893, facilities construction 1895, and different dates for the east addition are separate facts or source discrepancies, not one flattened anniversary.
- LC State's facilities plan says the tower was salvaged; anniversary wording says the fire destroyed the tower. Describe surviving tower fabric and loss of its old top; do not claim an engineering reconciliation or precisely which stones survived.
- Institutional archives and records show the building and lost institutional records; firsthand prefire student voices were not inspected. No invented quote.
- Site-level NRHP identifier, photographer position, public fixture coordinates, current alignment, and public photo reuse permissions not independently confirmed. Keeping those fields empty is deliberate.
