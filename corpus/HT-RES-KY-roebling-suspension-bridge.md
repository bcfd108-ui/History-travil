## 2. Story
**John A. Roebling Suspension Bridge, Covington approach · Research draft · Fred**

The bridge grew from commercial pressure on both sides of the Ohio. In the HAER history, Lexington interests wanted a more dependable connection with Cincinnati, while ferry and steamboat interests opposed it. John A. Roebling's design needed political approval, financing, quarry and masonry work and cables. The towers had begun to rise before the financial panic of 1857. Work resumed, was interrupted again and recovered during the Civil War. HAER notes that the “scarcity of skilled workmen” slowed progress. Factory workers in Trenton supplied wire. Other workers brought the cable and masonry together here. The engineering survey records details of that work but gives no complete individual crew roster. [3–5]

The bridge admitted the public in December 1866, with additional work and the formal completion associated with 1867. This dates the crossing and its construction phases separately. It connected Covington and Cincinnati directly while changing how travel was priced and organized. The two towers and long suspended deck survived successive changes. The structural composition visible from Kentucky recalls a job that outlasted both financing trouble and wartime interruption. [3–5]

## 3. Then and now
The historic bridge is an operating crossing. The Kentucky and Ohio engineering survey files treat it as one structure. This entry concerns its Kentucky public approach and does not create another bridge record for Ohio. Modern traffic, sidewalk work and maintenance affect today's traveler. [1–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
- HAER OH-28 gives December 1, 1866 for initial public opening, and 1867 for completion. The National Register significant year is 1867. A modern LOC photographer-supplied note includes both an erroneous 1876 opening and a second 1867 opening claim. Preserve the distinction supported by HAER, and do not use that catalog note to date the bridge. [3–6]
- HAER's Kentucky datasheet abbreviates the engineer as John E. Roebling. The federal register metadata and engineering account support John A. Roebling. [3–5]
- KYTC's Roadshow lists “Tuesday, Nov. 20” and the November 18 reprint says Tuesday, November 24. Both describe east-side closure until completion. Do not infer a reopening from either predicted date. The posted west-side access and physical signs govern. [1–2]
- Individual construction payroll and independent precision coordinates for the lawful approach remain open.
