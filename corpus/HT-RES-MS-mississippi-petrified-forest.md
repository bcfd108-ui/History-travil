# Mississippi Petrified Forest and the river's buried logjam

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP12 · Labeled research draft*

## 2. Story

The stone logs near Flora took their shape as trees. Their place in the ground records a second life as river debris. In the geological survey's reconstruction, retreating seas left streams crossing this part of what is now Mississippi in the early Oligocene. As a river cut its banks, trees fell and accumulated. The abandoned channel acquired a cover of sediment. Mineral-bearing groundwater later moved through it and silica replaced the woody tissue. The logs are evidence of a river carrying, jamming and burying timber. [4–6]

Richard R. Priddy's 1960 county geology bulletin gives that general account a local place in the rock. Figure 32 documents the fossil timber where gullies cut the basal Forest Hill beds. His team recorded a landscape still being opened by erosion, years before the public loop gave travelers a fixed way to view it. The state's later field guide describes the deposit in two words, “ancient logjam.” Its suggested ages and environmental reconstruction are scientific interpretations of the strata. [4, 5]

Interest in making the site visitable predates its current operator. Priddy mentions earlier development efforts and highway-map labeling. The geological survey reports that Bob Schabilion bought the tract from Judge J. S. Black on October 22, 1962 and opened it to visitors April 1, 1963. Names of the first trail builders and museum preparators are still unverified. The physical work of showing fossils while leaving the deposit in place is the next human layer to document. [4, 5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** Fossil-bearing Forest Hill strata near Flora. Some pieces along a modern walking loop may have been collected or arranged. The site’s geology gives a visitor language for comparing the exposed beds and interpreted specimens, while exact recovery positions require trail or collection records. [2, 4, 5]

**Present public anchor.** Mississippi Petrified Forest admission entrance, 124 Forest Park Road, Flora. Drive up to operator parking, check in at the visitor counter and follow the signed trail within the managed property. The museum is a staffed public interior during operator hours. [1–3]

## 5. Public progression

Begin with the operator's trail information and make the self-guided loop in daylight. Pause at the signed views where stone grain and layering can be read from the paving. Use the museum to compare the local logs with the wider sample of fossil material on display. The story lands at **Wood grain in stone**, where a visible log's former living structure and the minerals that retained its form can be read together. Allow roughly 30 minutes for the loop before museum time, based on the operator's estimate. [1–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- NPS dates the National Natural Landmark designation to April 1965. The geological guide describes a June 4, 1966 declaration by the Secretary of the Interior. Federal listing and the later reported recognition are recorded separately while the signed original designation and event program remain unlocated. [4, 7]
- Flora’s town page gives Christmas Day as the closure. The operator explicitly adds Thanksgiving and December 24 and 26. Follow the operator. It lists 9–6 for April 1 through Labor Day and 9–5 for winter, with last trail entry one hour before close. Confirm around the seasonal changeover. [1, 3]
- The 1937 *National Geographic* article by J. R. Hildebrand is cited in the geological guide. The original issue was not retrieved, so its wording and imagery are not used. [4]
- Exact origins of trail displays, the builders' records, slopes and posted cross-slope or door clearances were not verified. Paving alone cannot establish wheelchair suitability. [2, 4, 5]
