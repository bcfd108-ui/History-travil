# Natchez City Cemetery and the five workers beneath the Turning Angel

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP04 · November 20, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Living municipal cemetery. Enter by its ordinary public entrance during gate hours and use the signed public walks. Funerals and families have priority. Reflection only. [1]

## 2. Story

Five workers are named on a monument paid for by the Natchez Drug Company: Carrie O. Murray, Inez Netterville, Luella D. Booth, Mary E. Worthy and Ada White. The city records that five headstones bear the same death date, March 14, 1908. Reading their names restores something the name ‘Turning Angel’ leaves out: all five went to work that morning. [2,3]

## 3. Then and now

The explosion took place downtown at Main and Union, in a former Masonic building that John H. Chambliss acquired in 1904. A dispatch printed six days later described people caught in the chemical room and named the carpenter Urey Hotchkiss. The Historic Natchez Foundation counts eleven deaths in the disaster. The five graves record those commemorated together. A count confined to one workroom and a count for the entire building have different denominators. [4,5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The company’s stone angel stands with the workers’ headstones in the city cemetery. This is a burial ground with living family connections. The Shelter House is the cemetery office and the public point for directions. The factory and cemetery are distinct places. [1,2]

## 5. Public progression

The individual markers and their shared date carry the story. A city cemetery publication identifies S. Quinn Boothe as the father of one of the workers. His surname appears there with a final ‘e’. The monument transcription gives Luella’s surname as Booth. This record preserves both spellings. [3,6]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

Local lore says the angel seems to turn when seen in passing light. The city records the telling. The inscription and contemporary account document the loss of life. The visit belongs in daylight. Surviving accounts reviewed here do not establish each woman’s job or age. [2–5]
