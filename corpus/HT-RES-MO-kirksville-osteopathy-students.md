# Kirksville — A school small enough to move

## 2. Story

On February 15, 1893, the American School of Osteopathy issued a diploma to William Smith. The original diploma survives in a facsimile published in the museum’s illustrated school history. Smith was already medically trained before coming to Kirksville and taught students himself. His early diploma preserves a time when the faculty, students and advocates of a new school could be the same people and when a printed credential carried their claim to a profession out of town. [3]

The school had a second voice by May 1894. Its new *Journal of Osteopathy* reported the cohort’s examinations and congratulated students on the “splendid grades attained.” This is the contemporary school publication’s account, a voice that wanted its readers to believe in the work. Its reporting shows examinations and named graduates becoming public evidence of an institution’s existence. A certificate and a printed journal made claims that a small room by itself could never carry. [3, 4]

Nettie Bolles appears by name in the 1894 diploma list in the museum history. That history also identifies her with the school's journal and later recalls her departure for Colorado. Read her place alongside Smith’s. The story becomes one of specific people learning, teaching, publishing and leaving Kirksville with the school’s name attached to their work. Earlier medical theories and outcomes belong to the period record. The visit reads the institution's history. [3, 4]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The museum dates the first school building’s teaching use to 1892–1894. Heritage Hall holds the relocated early school and, separately, a historic Virginia cabin associated with A. T. Still. The museum describes the buildings’ move in 1994. The cabin’s original geography and the Kirksville school’s founding experience are different. This dossier leaves original parcel coordinates and an original-footprint public approach open. [2, 3]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive up to the A.T. Still University Museum of Osteopathic Medicine at 800 W. Jefferson St., Kirksville, and use the short signed museum approach and ordinary visitor entrance. Continue to Heritage Hall only where museum staff and signs allow. Patient, teaching and archive areas are outside the visit. [1, 2]
