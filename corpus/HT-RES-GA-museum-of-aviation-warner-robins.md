## 2. Story
**Museum of Aviation, Warner Robins · Research draft · Fred**

Putting an aircraft on display takes a different set of hands from the ones that flew it. Air Force museum historians Bill Head and Diane Truluck trace the institution to a history program and an early collection of papers, photographs and objects associated with World War I pilot Guy Orlando Stone. Their report calls Stone's material “the first collection at the Robins AFB Museum of Aviation.” An official 1975 caption puts Stone with Maj. Gen. William R. Hayes. Rather than treating one Air Force collection as inevitable, the report gives it a donor, curators, organizers and a first storage problem. [2]

The report's 1984 photographic captions document work on the museum site before later building phases. They name Maj. Gen. Cornelius Nugteren working with volunteers and show the successive jobs of clearing, laying foundations and putting up the first buildings. Peggy Young appears as a director guiding visitors in later years. Jim Balletto and Bill Paul appear in an exhibit-development caption alongside Robert Lee Scott and artist Butler Brown. The named volunteers, museum workers and donors give this institution a history beyond the objects inside it. [2]

The museum is part of a larger wartime town story, but it is its own lawful destination. An oral-history catalog from Houston County's project identifies Gene and Betty Sullivan. The archive summarizes Gene recalling changing geography, a school and wartime encounters. An exhibit visitor can use that oral history remotely to connect a museum campus to people's everyday experience around the depot, without treating a library or the base as a stop. [1–4]

## 3. Then and now
Early indoor collection space and the later planned campus are different phases. The current public museum has exhibits in purpose-built or adapted galleries and hangars. The state WWII trail identifies the Scott Exhibit Hangar and its Tuskegee Airmen material. The public museum campus is distinct from the operating installation it interprets. [1–4]
