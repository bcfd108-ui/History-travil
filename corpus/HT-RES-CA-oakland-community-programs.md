## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** before a student could learn, someone had to arrive early enough to make breakfast. The history of community service is also a history of time given before a person's own work or classes began.

**Billy X Jennings**, interviewed by **Pendarvis Harshaw** for KQED in 2019, recalled helping with the Oakland breakfast program for roughly three hours before his **9 a.m. Laney College class**. His measure of the work was direct: **“I was actually doing something to change the conditions.”** [4] This is named participant testimony published in a reported interview, not a reconstructed scene. It does not establish every volunteer's schedule.

**Ericka C. Huggins**, interviewed by **David Cline in Oakland on June 30, 2016**, recalled deeper involvement in northern California's community survival programs, including free medical clinics and an ambulance program. She described Party members' service as unpaid and collective. [5, printed p.20] Her retrospective account links caregiving and organizing, but it does not prove that every program she discusses operated at an Oakland address: the interview also explicitly discusses Los Angeles and New Haven. Relevant transcript passages were read; the recording was not played.

**Original interpretation:** a slogan can fit on a poster. Feeding children reliably requires repetitive work that a poster cannot show. This stop asks visitors to notice the people, donated time and practical care behind the public image, without endorsing every organizational claim or flattening a complicated history into a celebration.

## 3. Then / now

AAMLO's October 2026 institutional account places the Oakland breakfast program in **1969** and describes the provision of meals before school. It separately dates the federal School Breakfast Program's pilot to **1966** and permanent authorization to **1975**. [6] These are not the same program. The earlier federal pilot prevents the simplistic claim that the Panthers invented federal school breakfast; the precise extent of later influence needs fuller policy evidence.

The current public connection is unusually specific: OPL advertises **Oakland Star Special Collection Exhibition: From the Archives The Black Panther Party Original Newspapers 1967–1971**, in AAMLO's Museum Gallery, **October 24–December 31, 2026**. Its description expressly includes the Free Breakfast Program, public-health education, housing and survival programs. [7] This is verified operator advertising, not an independent inspection of installed cases. No particular newspaper issue, photograph, Huggins recording or Jennings account is promised on display.

Party newspapers are contemporary organizational publications with their own purposes and perspective. The archive preserving them and the present museum presenting them are not thereby endorsing every assertion. Read publication date, authorship and evidence separately.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Before leaving:** check the building date and exhibition period. Prefer an arrival with time to read rather than the last minutes before closing. Daylight is a planning preference, not an invented outdoor admission restriction.
2. **Enter the present institution:** orient to the permitted public gallery. Do not treat a research-room door or staff area as part of the tour.
3. **Read one service claim carefully:** if an appropriate newspaper is actually displayed, distinguish what it says happened from what later scholarship or another record corroborates. Do not require a particular issue to be present.
4. **Bring in the worker's day:** read the short Jennings excerpt, or use the linked account remotely if personal-device reading is unsuitable. The point is the work before class, not collecting a named-person sighting.
5. **End with the strongest story moment:** pause over the difference between advocating that children should eat and repeatedly being there to feed them. Ask what kinds of labor disappear when history is remembered only through famous leaders. Keep the ending quiet and unscored.

This is one institutional visit with a narrative arc, not a multi-stop historic-site route. If the exhibition is unavailable, use the sources remotely; do not improvise a hunt for former program buildings.
