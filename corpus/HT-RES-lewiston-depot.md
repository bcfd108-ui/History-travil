## Selection and original story
Choose the **Lewiston Depot at 13th and Main**, rather than another mill overview. It has an identified circa-1910 primary photograph, a National Register nomination with map and photographs, and a visible architectural story that does not require entry into a railway yard. Current exterior condition and safe viewing spot still need field verification. This is a new central-Lewiston rail/arrival entry, not a claim to have checked every live app entry for duplication.

**Original interpretive lead:** Before arrival meant a parking lot, a town could introduce itself through a waiting room. Lewiston built a depot whose tall central block and long lower wings made an ordinary journey feel civic. The most interesting contrast is not simply steam versus cars. It is the building organizing different kinds of movement: passengers arriving from the street, baggage moving beside them, trains behind, and information handled upstairs. This is an interpretation of documented functions, not an eyewitness quotation. [D1]

### Evidence spine
The 1973 nomination records Northern Pacific’s 1908 design and completion in 1909, following purchase of the lot in 1906 and removal of a flour mill. It explains the Camas Prairie Railroad as a cooperative resolution of competing railroad interests. The brick building combined waiting spaces, baggage/storage wings, a trackside ticket-office bay and an upstairs telegraph office. The separate men’s smoking and women’s retiring rooms reveal how passenger facilities encoded social expectations. These are documented historical functions, not a claim the rooms retain their original use today. [D1, PDF pp.2–3]

A University of Idaho image supplies something different: visual evidence near the building’s beginning. The catalog dates it **about 1910**, although the structured date field reads 1910. In the image inspected for this dossier, the open-sided left wing, prominent central roofline, utility poles, rough street edge and stacks of brick are visible. Bricks in a photograph do not by themselves prove what construction job was underway or who placed them. Keep “about” in the caption. [D2]

The nomination’s photographic packet provides a later comparison: the view associated with its 1972 photograph form looks northwest and shows the depot in an urban streetscape. Another photograph in the same PDF shows early vehicles and horse-drawn conveyances; **do not label that separate historical image 1972** merely because the adjacent form has that date. An archive packet can contain several temporal layers. [D1, PDF pp.7–9]

### Public fallback if the depot view cannot be safely confirmed
Locomotive Park, **2102 Main Street**, is independently listed by the City of Lewiston with picnic tables, public art and restrooms. It can be a separate public park pause, not a substitute photo of the depot or proof of depot access. The local visitor bureau identifies displayed Engine 92 as a logging locomotive built in 1924, purchased by the logging company in 1942, and later acquired by the city for $1. These are tourism-history claims, not yet checked against builder records. Stay on public paths; do not infer permission to climb from an old promotional reference to ringing its bell. [D5–D6]

A 2023 city capital plan describes a **proposed** Locomotive Park-to-levee connection across tracks. A funded/listed proposal is not evidence of a completed authorized crossing. Do not route such a link without current confirmation. [D7]
