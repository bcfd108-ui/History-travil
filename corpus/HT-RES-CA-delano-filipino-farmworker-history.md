## 2. The story
**Philip Vera Cruz's 1971 oral history**, published by the **Welga Archive, Bulosan Center for Filipino Studies**, describes learning about organizing while working near Delano. The transcription identifies the questioner as **Valledor**; this record does not establish a full interviewer name. Vera Cruz recalls signing union-related papers without being certain what action they committed him to, hearing news unevenly, and seeking a meeting. His account gives the process a worker's scale rather than a seamless leadership narrative. [4]

His short phrase **“I'm just trying to start from the beginning”** captures the interview's back-and-forth. The narrator corrects the questioner's proposed sequence; later, he remembers a meeting discussion emphasizing a contract rather than only a wage increase. This directly read transcript is firsthand recollection, not a contemporaneous attendance register or complete recording review. [4]

The story lands on the labor of becoming informed and deciding to participate. A strike may appear as a single date in a timeline, but a person at work did not necessarily experience it as one clear announcement. This is an interpretation of the source, not a claim that all Filipino workers knew or chose the same thing.

The Delano nexus is explicit. Do not substitute a Salinas photograph merely because it depicts Filipino farmworkers. Community history deserves place-specific evidence, and Filipino organizing should not disappear into a generic account centered only on better-known leaders.
