# Aline — the room that counted as proof
Research draft · Oklahoma OP05 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

Marshal McCully crossed into the Cherokee Outlet in the 1893 opening. OHS describes a dugout first and then a sod dwelling in 1894. B. B. Chapman’s 1967 article reproduces his homestead testimony: “I built my house in March and established actual residence thereon, April 10, 1894.” His proof lists a house, barn, well, windmill, orchard and fenced acres. George W. Bockoven and James A. Dawson were witnesses. The words are McCully’s testimony as reproduced by Chapman. The original file has yet to be checked. [2–3]

The evidence describes the work in the walls. OHS records a team and sod plow, cut strips, a shovel, poles for rafters and clay plaster. The nomination records a cedar ridge pole and a log partition that supports it. In 1895 the dirt floor received wooden flooring. Chapman dates Marshal’s marriage to Pearle Bowen to 1907, and describes a subsequent move into a frame residence, with the sod house used for storage. The household’s record runs from investment in a first residence to preservation of a building that served another purpose. [2–4]

The Cherokee Nation describes its earlier grazing income and the pressure preceding sale of the Outlet. The household’s federal proof records what McCully had made. The Nation’s history establishes whose lands the federal entry system was transferring. The effects of that transfer enter the visit as quiet reflection. [3,5]

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic ground.** The sod house and homestead. The nomination groups the surviving house, protective structure and historical fabric; exact parcel boundaries beyond the visitor facility remain open. **Lawful physical anchor.** Sod House Museum ordinary signed entrance, drive-up and short walk. The enclosing building protects the original sod dwelling. Follow staff for any door or interior route. [1–4]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior — museum exhibits and protected viewing of the sod walls. Historic room interiors — view as staff allows. Exterior — ordinary approach only. The former dugout, crop ground, well and orchard location have no visitor pin in this draft. [1–4]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

Begin with the museum’s exhibit on the dugout and sod construction. Walk to the permitted view of the original walls and note the protective shell. **Strongest story moment — The room that counted as proof.** Read McCully’s dated residency declaration beside the protected wall. The evidence describes a home and also the property a settler had to document. [2–4]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

Family affidavit reproduced by Chapman, with no independent file scan retrieved. The proof’s 12 × 24 description gives a whole house dimension. Chapman’s approximate 10 × 12 room sizes describe interiors. The nomination does not establish exact locations for all other homestead improvements. Staff-dependent access supersedes older bare-hours listings. [1,3–4]
