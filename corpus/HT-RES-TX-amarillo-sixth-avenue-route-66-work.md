# Amarillo’s Nat — the dance floor over the pool
Research draft · Texas OP02 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### A swim in June 1926

Amarillo’s June 6, 1926 Sunday News-Globe ran an advertisement urging a “Dip in the / THE NAT” in the digitized newspaper’s OCR line sequence. The clean, clearly isolated wording “THE NAT” is the publication’s printed name as transcribed by UNT. The appeal was summer heat and city dust. The advertisement dates the venue’s swim-era identity to the same year when the surviving chronology places its conversion [4].

### The map route reached a building that was already here

Guy Carlander designed the 1922 natatorium. The state marker and building operator describe an open-air pool initially and a 1923 roof for year-round use. The operator describes J. D. Tucker commissioning subflooring and maple over a timber structure in 1926. Its later street-facing show front gave those coming along Route 66 a conspicuous entrance while the uses behind it changed [1–2, 5–6].

### One address, two businesses underfoot

The pool sold relief from the heat. The ballroom sold time together, with live music and dining, and the state marker remembers a peak staff of 40. That retrospective count is the marker’s report, with no payroll checked. The operator describes a present retail marketplace for local dealers. The same address has made money by selling admission, social life and then merchandise [1, 5].

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic place.** Natatorium, later ballroom, in the U.S. Route 66 Sixth Street Historic District. The marker and operator both use 2705 SW Sixth Avenue; the state survey files the same building under South Georgia Street [1, 5–7].

**Lawful physical anchor.** Sixth Avenue public sidewalk at the 2705 frontage. Drive-up via lawful street parking and a short walk. Use the municipal sidewalk and designated crossings. Interior is operator-controlled; no permission to explore lower levels or closed areas [1, 3].

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The city arts-project PDF summarizes the 1922 Nat as an indoor pool [3]. The state marker, the building operator and the Handbook of Texas date the original open-air pool to 1922 and its cover to 1923 [2, 5, 8]. Do not substitute those two events for one another. The exact date when swimming ceased in 1926 and the ballroom opened is unverified. The two street addresses identify different frontages of the same building in the cited records [1, 7].
