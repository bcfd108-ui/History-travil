## 2. The story
**Named firsthand household voice:** UNLV preserves **Edna Jackson-Ferguson's narrative oral history of April 15, 1975**, collection **OH-00571**, digital transcript **OH_00571_transcript**. In her recollection of early camp life, she describes contributing to her husband's administrative work: **“I helped with the book work and reports”**. This is her own retrospective statement in the published transcript, not an invented reconstruction of domestic labor. [3]

Her account includes food, household arrangements, isolation and everyday uncertainty. Its early camp experiences precede the better-known construction period; do not place everything she recalls in Depression-era Boulder City. UNLV notes that this legacy transcript received limited editing without the narrator's participation. Unintelligible passages remain unresolved. [3]

**Original interpretive angle:** public works are often remembered through a finished structure and the people on its payroll. A household account changes the frame. It shows how sustaining daily life and helping with paperwork can sit alongside a formally recognized job without appearing in the same employment record. The question is not whether one kind of work matters more, but what the usual picture leaves out.

The account does not establish that Edna was a salaried employee or that every worker's household had the same experience. Do not convert her contribution into an unsupported job title. Her words provide a specific view, not a universal portrait or proof that working conditions were acceptable.

Historical hardship is not a reenactment invitation. The source's travel, survival and workplace descriptions are not turned into instructions or routes. Her personal claims of being first are not adopted as a historical conclusion about earlier inhabitants.
