## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** a welcoming table is something people make, repeatedly. Behind “family style” are hours of preparation, shared household tasks and a language in which guests can feel at home.

**Scott Ygoa**, identified as the Star Hotel & Bar's owner in PBS Reno's *FoodNotes* segment dated **September 1, 2023**, gives a firsthand account of his Elko upbringing and hospitality work. In the published transcript he remembers Basque spoken around family meals, cooking from scratch, and everyone's participation. His concise formulation is **“time went into meals”**. [5]

This is a named operator's household recollection, not an anonymous claim that Basque hospitality happened effortlessly. It is also a contemporary account looking backward, not a recorded interview with the earliest boardinghouse cooks. Transcript text was read; the video and audio were not played.

**Original interpretation:** an abundant meal can hide the work that made abundance possible. This entry asks visitors to notice that work without treating a present-day family or restaurant staff as a living exhibit. It is distinct from the Buffalo sheep/radio dossier: the center here is domestic and hospitality labor, language around a table and the transition from boarding to restaurant service.

## 3. Then / now

Gloria Pilar Totoricagüena's **2004** essay published by **Eusko Ikaskuntza** places Elko's boardinghouses in the growth of the Basque community. It identifies **Pedro and Matilde Jauregui** with the Star's construction in **1910**, and separately discusses other hotel families. It names **Ana Mari Arbillaga** in connection with cooking at the Nevada Dinner House after the Nevada Hotel's 1985 sale. [6] These are community-published historical attributions, not direct quotations from those workers.

In the 2023 interview, Ygoa describes the Star as a restaurant and recalls boarders remaining until several years earlier. [5] Do not turn that relative date into a precise final-boarding year, or a 2023 ownership statement into verified 2026 business status. No current restaurant access or hours were checked because no restaurant visit is being proposed.

The museum is a separate institution, not a relocated boardinghouse. Its regional history role supplies context, while the oral account supplies the particular family perspective. State tourism mentions Basque cultural artifacts there, but no specific kitchen object, named-worker display or transcript was verified onsite. [2,3]
