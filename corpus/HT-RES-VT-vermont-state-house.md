# HT-RES-VT-vermont-state-house — Montpelier: the capitol that rose from the ashes
Division VT seed-catalog • Research draft • Checked November 25, 2026

Vermont's present State House opened in 1859, two years after fire destroyed the capitol that stood before it. The legislature rebuilt on the same ground, kept the earlier building's Greek Revival design language, and raised a granite capitol under a gilded dome. It is not a museum piece: the building still does the job it was built for.

Vermont State House, State Street, Montpelier, Washington County, Vermont

**Public access:** The State House stands on State Street in Montpelier and is viewed from public sidewalks and the authorized grounds. Interior visitation is optional and subject to operating hours and security requirements. Confirm visiting information with the State of Vermont before going inside.

## The story

Montpelier was designated Vermont's permanent seat of government in 1805, and the first State House rose in 1808. A larger second capitol replaced it in 1836. In 1857, fire destroyed most of that second building.

The state rebuilt. The present State House, opened in 1859, retained elements of the earlier design — the granite exterior, the columned portico — at a larger scale, crowned by the gilded dome that remains the building's signature. The reconstruction was not smooth: it involved architectural disagreements, a legislative investigation, and changes to the building's engineering, documented in correspondence preserved by the University of Vermont. The legislature returned in October 1859. The agricultural statue atop the dome was replaced in 1938; the figure there today is the successor, not the original.

Unlike many historic capitols converted entirely to museums, this one continues to serve as Vermont's working seat of government.

## What's still there

The granite capitol itself: columned entrance, gilded dome, and the statue above it, facing State Street with the wooded hill behind. The building's exterior is the record — fire, rebuilding, and continuity of government in one view.

## The stop, step by step

Stand on State Street facing the State House. Find the granite columned entrance beneath the gilded dome. Read the building from the street: portico, dome, statue. If you go inside, follow the State's posted visiting hours and security requirements.

## Honest gaps & disagreements

This entry rests on the State's official State House materials and the University of Vermont's reconstruction archive; the engineering changes made during rebuilding are documented in that correspondence and are summarized here, not adjudicated. The dome statue's replacement history is stated at the level the official record supports.
