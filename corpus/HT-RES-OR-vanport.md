## 2. The story
**Regina S. Flowers** recalled a community of classmates and activities, not merely a place waiting to flood. Interviewed by Jenna Jula with daughter Jeigh Hopkins on April 14, 2022, she described arriving from Oklahoma as a child after her father, **Fred Johnson Sr.**, sought shipyard work. Her mother, **Emily Grace Gadsby Johnson**, led Girl Scouts. Flowers remembered neighborhood outdoor movies and meeting her future husband at school. [5–6]

Her distinction matters: **“Segregation came with the housing.”** She recalled integrated schooling without describing all after-school life as integrated. The published transcript also preserves her memory of class photographs her mother retrieved from the flood. These recollections center Black family life and agency rather than reducing residents to anonymous victims. They are retrospective testimony, not a 1948 interview. [6, pp. 2–5, 9–11]

**Ed Washington's** separately published PSU interview record identifies his family's move from Birmingham to Vanport in 1944, the loss of home and possessions on May 30, 1948, and his later civil-rights work. Its biography is editorial context; it is not presented here as a verbatim statement from Washington. His accompanying transcript contains conspicuous transcription errors, so it is not used for exact quotations. [7–8]

Vanport Mosaic's community-produced oral-history work offers a further resident-led pathway, including Washington's film. The collection description and film record were read; the embedded six-minute film was not independently watched in this pass. No unseen words are assigned to him. [9–10]

The flood was a disaster, but displacement was also shaped by decisions about who could rent or buy elsewhere. Michael McGregor's historical essay connects discriminatory housing practices with the constrained choices facing Black families after the flood. Emergency shelter did not automatically produce fair access to a permanent home. [11]

## 3. Then / now
**Then:** Wartime housing supported shipbuilding workers and families. Carl Abbott's account dates federal approval to August 18, 1942, first tenants to December 12, and destruction to May 30, 1948. His historical geography describes the main development west of Denver Avenue, while separately noting East Vanport. [12]

**Now:** Today's recreational landscapes are not a preserved neighborhood street grid. The park is a lawful contemporary place to reflect, not evidence that a visitor stands where Flowers lived. Vanport Mosaic's exhibit relates the former city to several modern uses and notes that it was not rebuilt. That broad contextual description is not permission to enter each facility. [13]

Keep the historical extent, present park property and exact family addresses distinct. No historical address has been geocoded or turned into a destination. The reading should move from a lived neighborhood to displacement and then to residents' continuing work of remembrance, not from a disaster spectacle to a souvenir.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Modern anchor versus city footprint:** The operator uses a broad Vanport association; Abbott locates the main historical site west of Denver Avenue. East Delta Park is chosen for independently documented public access, not certified exact overlap. [1,12]
- **Population:** Wartime peak and the smaller 1948 population are different measures. McGregor uses both 18,000 and 18,500 displaced residents; no artificial precision or conversion to a house count. [11]
- **Casualties:** Abbott gives fifteen deaths; Vanport Mosaic says at least fifteen. This dossier does not turn that into a verified complete roll, reproduce unsupported larger totals, or dismiss a family's grief through a numeric contest. [12–13]
- **Chronology inconsistency:** McGregor's phrase about an embankment serving the newly developed complex for eight years should not be used to override the documented 1942–1948 housing chronology. [11–12]
- **Oral-history limits:** Flowers's remembered childhood ages are not used to infer a precise arrival date. The transcript records corrections between mother and daughter; a visitor narrative must not silently choose an invented version. [6]
- **Integration:** A resident's positive school memories do not establish equal housing opportunity or universal integration. [6,11]
- **Winter access:** Published hours are not a real-time weather clearance; exact safe seating, restrooms, marker location and an accessible path remain unverified.
