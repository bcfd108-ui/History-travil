# Post — A company town and the price of a public gathering place
Research draft · Texas OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

**“Lease on the land at $1 per year.”** The December 30, 1921 Lynn County News printed these words in a report datelined Post City, December 22. The Double-U company offered a twenty-year lease for a community tabernacle, with a purchase option at a fixed price within five years. The article names the commercial club and churches as backers and reports contributions from throughout the county. It imagines political rallies and poultry shows under one roof. This is a contemporary report on a project under construction. It leaves open whether the purchase option was exercised and when the completed venue opened. [2]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then — streets, farms and a lease.** The City of Post dates the founding to 1907, the arrival of rail to 1910 and incorporation under the shorter name to 1914. It describes development of farm holdings and houses and a program of planting shade trees. The C. W. Post archives at Texas Tech index town and development plans. The 1921 newspaper shows the company still controlling land needed for civic life seven years after incorporation. **Now — a downtown public street.** East Main is a place to read and see the present civic town. The newspaper’s tabernacle has no parcel, building or address established here. [1–4]

## 4 · What’s still there

- **View from public ground.** Present East Main street frontage from ordinary sidewalks. Building ages and surviving company-period structures remain unassigned here. [1,5]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The original lease, board minutes, cost ledger and proof of construction or later use were not retrieved. The town plans were located at finding-aid level. Parcel sheets were not opened. The Texas Tech Dan Cockrum oral-history record indexed a 1983 interview but supplied no audio or transcript in the item read. Museum hours remain an open operator check. [2–4,6–7]
