## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** Being visible is not the same as being heard. Bismarck's Black residents belonged to the city's everyday history, not just to the moments when white neighbors noticed them.

**Named community voice, with the source chain intact:** Kathie Ryckman Anderson's *Era Bell Thompson: A North Dakota Daughter*, published by the State Historical Society, reproduces Thompson's autobiographical account of entering Bismarck school. The short phrase **“a sudden wide-eyed hush fell over them”** is Thompson's recollection as quoted by Anderson, not a recording heard here or an original memoir page independently read. [3]

Anderson also describes Thompson's Bismarck school athletics and writing, including her own humorous newspaper, *Snap*, and graduation in **1924**. These establish local participation, not just a story of exclusion. Her memoir's scenes in Mandan, Fargo and Grand Forks must not be relocated to Bismarck. [3]

**Another local strand:** the state's educational history identifies **W. H. W. Comer**, a Black Bismarck barber and property owner, and **Virginia Comer**, who continued managing property after his death. This is institutional historical synthesis, not their firsthand testimony. [4]

**Original interpretation:** Place authored work, business and community participation beside discrimination. Achievement does not prove that prejudice was absent, and injury need not erase a person's full life. No single resident is made to speak for all Black Bismarck families.

## 3. Then / now

The independent archive preserves records through which a fuller city history can be recovered. Its presence does not certify a themed public trail or a surviving place connected to either family.

The comparison is between who made the city's history and how that history is represented. Visitors can read a named author's words without entering the places where her family lived or asking current residents to explain the past.

This entry deliberately stops short of a comprehensive community history. The available material supports an opening centered on a writer and a business household; it does not establish the voices of every generation, working class or present-day resident.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The original *American Daughter* edition was not directly inspected. The quoted passage is a clearly attributed excerpt reproduced in a historical article. No direct-interview claim is made. [3]
- Anderson's notes identify a **September 16, 1975** Thompson interview by **Larry Sprunk** in Bismarck. That is a finding lead, not an audio item heard or accession number verified here. [3]
- Original contemporary newspaper records and the Comers' own writings remain a primary-evidence gap. The educational page's racial identification is the source, not an inference from names or photographs. [4]
