# Huntsville — A letter from the Woodland Home years
Research draft · Texas OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

**‘My dearest love.’** The Southwestern University catalog transcribes this address from Margaret Lea Houston’s letter sent from Huntsville to Sam Houston in Washington, D.C., cataloged under March 16, 1848. The letter records illnesses, news of children and relatives, and her hopes for her husband’s salvation. On the inspected scan, old tape crosses lines of handwriting and gaps interrupt the page. The quote is the archive’s transcription. The letter puts family care beside a career that took Houston away from home. The sources consulted do not locate her writing desk. [3]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then — the household and the people who kept it going.** The museum places the Houston family at Woodland from 1847 to 1859 and says four of the couple’s eight children were born in the house. The university archives account assigns construction to 1848 and identifies Joshua Houston among the people enslaved in the household. The latter description is a later institutional account. The sources consulted do not yield Joshua’s own account of his life at Woodland. **Now — the original-site house in a larger public museum complex.** The public map and federal register identify it separately from the wider exhibits now on the grounds. [2,4–5]

## 4 · What’s still there

- **Exterior only.** Woodland Home from signed paths. Follow on-site barriers. [1–2,5]
- **Public interior.** General museum galleries during opening. Indoor access to Woodland Home itself, including the room depicted in the 1996 survey image, is unconfirmed. [1–2,6]
- **Not accessible.** No visitor destination is assigned to a working area attributed to enslaved people or to a room associated with the letter. [2–4]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Historic location.** The original-site Woodland Home on museum grounds. The letter is located to Huntsville by its catalog. [2–3]
2. **Lawful anchor.** Start at the public museum entrance and use its map to reach the signed Woodland Home path in an open period. Posted barriers remain the boundary. [1–2]
3. **Strongest story moment — A household at two addresses.** Read the cataloged salutation from the public view. The letter’s addresses put home and public office far apart. Read the archive’s account of Joshua Houston here as quiet witness. [3–4]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The museum’s 1847–1859 span and four births are [2]. The university archive says construction in 1848, about eight years of Houston family occupancy and multiple owners between 1853 and 1878 [4]. These accounts differ. The archives also describe the restored house being returned to its original property, an important distinction within the museum’s original-site framing. NPS records 1847 and 1859 as significant years [5]. The NPS download [7] returned a one-page placeholder. The signed submission was not obtained there. The letter’s title and accession give March 16, 1848 while the creation-date field displays 18XX. [2–5,7]
