# McRaven and the brickmaker’s house

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP08 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Guided history tour from the staffed entrance at 1445 Harrison Street, Vicksburg. Buy or confirm the ordinary history tour with the operator. [1]

## 2. Story

The earliest part of McRaven is the hardest to date. The tour presents an early kitchen and upper room as a house built in 1797 by Andrew Glass. The land records tell a narrower story. Warren County Deed Book J, page 479, records William Bobb’s purchase of the lot in 1837. Book U, page 598, places the enlarged property in the hands of his brother John H. Bobb in 1844. Ownership is easier to date than either the brickwork or the person who laid it. The original deed pages have not been independently inspected for this draft. [1,2]

## 3. Then and now

John and his brother supplied brick to a growing river city. Local historian Evan Winschel links the brickyard to the Balfour House and other surviving buildings. The operators attribute a substantial enlargement of this house to John in 1849. On May 18, 1864, during the Union occupation, John was killed after an altercation with soldiers. The city’s transcription of his gravestone fixes the date and gives Philadelphia as his birthplace. Accounts of the killing are more detailed than the inscription. No verified roll of brickyard workers was recovered. [1,3,4]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The small rear rooms and the taller formal rooms with a two-story gallery survive within a single house. The front stair and larger parlor give a sense of what the later household wanted visitors to see. The 45-minute history tour is the verified public visit. [1]

## 5. Public progression

Follow the guide from one building section to the next. The change in room size makes the argument for more than one building campaign without requiring an exact date for every wall. [1]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

The operator credits Sheriff Stephen Howard with an 1836 addition. The title references place William Bobb on the parcel in 1837 and leave the earlier builder unresolved. The operator also tells stories of Mary Elizabeth Howard’s ghost. Those are part of the modern tour tradition. The documentary sources cited here establish no haunting. [1,2]

## 7. Historical photograph
