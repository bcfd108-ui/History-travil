## 2. The story

Railroad history often begins with the arrival of a locomotive. This stop begins with the person who had to get a worker out of a rooming house and back to work. In **Marie Prescott's 15 February 1988 oral history**, she remembered **“there was always a young man who was the caller, it was his job to call the railroad men.”** The 22-word excerpt is from transcript page 6, SOHS **Oral History 378C**, interviewed by Sue Waldron and transcribed/edited by Marjorie Edens. The transcript was read; audio was not listened to. [3]

Prescott is a named **community witness**, not a locomotive worker. She explicitly said her father and brother did not work for the railroad. Her memories connect lodging with irregular demands on labor, and describe her mother's care of a railroad lodger's room. This puts domestic work beside paid railway employment without claiming she knew every department's procedures. Her recollections were recorded long after childhood and contain uncertainties; they are not a current operating manual. [3]

The National Park Service's district history independently explains how housing and traveler services grew around the depot. It dates the railroad's northern arrival to early **1884**, with the through connection to California three years later. It identifies homes for brakemen, firemen and conductors alongside other trades, and explains the later reduction in Ashland's importance after Southern Pacific shifted its principal route east in **1927**. [4]

Together these sources change the scale of the story. A railway was not only machinery moving through a town. It needed a town's beds, meals, repairs and households. A route decision could therefore reach beyond railway payrolls into the businesses and family work that supported them. That broader reading is an inference from the testimony and district history, not a measured economic-impact estimate.

## 3. Then / now

Then, railroad employment helped organize the surrounding neighborhood and its daily rhythm. Now, a municipal park offers a lawful place to consider that relationship. Its railway-themed design is modern interpretation, not proof that a particular bench or play structure occupies a historic work station. [1]

A nearby historic district is a documentary category, not a single public attraction. The NPS account's recognition of surviving character does not open homes, businesses or industrial land. Neither this dossier nor an archival image establishes an authorized path through the old working landscape. [4]

The useful comparison is **community support work versus the visible train**. The park provides a place to read, pause and ask what an image of a locomotive leaves out. It does not reproduce Prescott's viewpoint or the exact location where a caller performed his work.

## 4. What's still there

The city confirms the public park and commemorative sculpture. Their present condition and interpretive wording were not inspected onsite. No claim is made that an actual historic locomotive, original tool or worker's monument is on display. [1]

A separate historic-building record illustrates why location needs care: the National Register file for **Ashland Depot Hotel, South Wing**, **91000047**, includes a supplementary ruling dated **19 February 1991** directing that all references to the building's move be dated **1990**. It lists private ownership at nomination. This record is evidence of preservation and relocation, not proof of current ownership, public admission or an intact original railway setting. The property is not added as a stop. [5]

The surviving evidence used here is principally the recorded community account, municipal visitor information and historic documentation. No archaeological or industrial remnant is offered as something to find.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Before arrival:** Read the caller passage and its provenance. Decide whether a short outdoor interpretive stop fits weather and current park signs. Do not plan around train movements.
2. **Within the public park:** Start with the modern railway commemoration, if accessible. Identify it as interpretation, not an original workplace object. Remain within public visitor space.
3. **At a public sitting place:** Contrast the familiar image of a train with lodging and household labor. Do not point out a private house as Prescott's, a boarding house or a worker's home without separate verified permission and evidence.
4. **End with the strongest story moment:** The person who wakes a worker, and the person who prepares his room, rarely appear in the heroic railway image. Ask: **Who kept railway workers ready between trains, and whose work was least likely to be recorded?**

This is a proposed reading sequence, not a guided program or confirmed sequence of onsite plaques. If the sculpture or sitting area is unavailable, do not seek a substitute vantage across railway boundaries.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Source geography matters:** Search results included a railroad mural in Ashland, Wisconsin, and other Ashland depots. None is evidence for Ashland, Oregon.
- **Arrival dates are distinct events:** The northern arrival in 1884 and through connection in 1887 should not be flattened into contradictory dates for one occurrence. [4]
- **Transcript reliability:** Prescott and Waldron discuss uncertainty over changes to the depot. The transcript's pagination/order also becomes irregular later. The quoted page 6 is identifiable; no uncertain later event date is adopted. [3]
- **Relocation:** The 1990 correction in the register is retained rather than repeating an uncorrected move date. [5]
- **Winter gate:** The city search and opened page differed. Neither establishes a guaranteed Railroad Park restroom opening or exact daily park hours. The operator remains verified; the amenity gate remains open. [2]
- **Scope:** This is a community-labor story, not a full account of every railroad workforce. No generalized Indigenous narrative is invented without a Tribal-authored source. No technical operating advice, current work schedule or train timetable is supplied.
- **No substitute venue assumed:** SOHS research-page captures differed on Saturday hours; a Medford archive visit has not been appended to this Ashland stop.
