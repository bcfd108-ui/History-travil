# Graball Landing and the signs people kept putting back

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP20*

## 2. Story

When Emmett Till was killed in August 1955, the recovery of his body was one part of a larger record connecting Mississippi and Chicago. The federal monument proclamation names Graball Landing as the area associated with the recovery and cautions that channel changes and erosion make precise identification difficult. “Near” is the operative word here. The current memorial is evidence of remembrance in a particular community. A federal boundary and a marker do not fix an exact river recovery point. [1–3]

The public sign has its own history. In 2008 the local memorial commission erected a sign at Graball Landing. It was removed, and later signs were damaged or shot. The proclamation records the dedication of the current one on October 19, 2019, and recognizes the history of defacement and replacement as significant in its own right. Each version was made by people trying to keep a child’s name and a fact in public view. The sequence documents repeated decisions to maintain a memorial even when its earlier material failed to survive or could no longer remain in place. [3]

In 2021, as one damaged predecessor was being publicly interpreted at the Smithsonian, Patrick Weems of the Emmett Till Interpretive Center asked, “Why is it so difficult to honor a 14-year-old child?” Those words are from a Smithsonian-published interview about the display. They describe the memorial work from the viewpoint of someone involved in it. This file follows that work, while the Sumner courthouse dossier carries the trial and testimony. [3][4]
