# El Dorado Armstrong lease oil story and Oil Heritage Park
HT-RES-AR-el-dorado-oil-field-workers · Arkansas OP05 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

On January 11, 1921, the Oklahoma Leader reported that a “test well on the Armstrong lease” had come in. The story names it for Mitchell and Donham. Read the tiny dispatch in the third column of the printed page. It reports excitement in town and distinguishes the discovery from nearby wells then understood as yielding gas and some oil. Its declaration that it was Arkansas’s first real well compresses a complicated history into newspaper language. It offers a view of how quickly the news moved across state lines. [3, p. 2]

El Dorado resident E. Boyd Alderson Sr. remembered the boom from the scale of his family’s work. In his January 26, 2009, Pryor Center interview, he described the streams as “covered with oil.” He recalled drawing water from a well for cows and horses because the creeks could no longer provide it. He also remembered Busey gathering subscriptions and named laundry proprietor Wong Hing among those involved. This is a named local voice and a later recollection. It favors no exact pollution boundary, identifies no drilling crew and does not itself confirm a subscription record or cash return. [4, pp. 32–33]

The state geological history calls the discovery the January 10, 1921 well completed by Busey and Mitchell on the Armstrong lease, after gas production began in 1920. The McWilliams house nomination explains how a boom became a town-building event, including the demand for transportation, housing and commercial space. Richard Mason recalled studying the boom and pointed to the name No. 1 Armstrong, describing special trains after the news. His account speaks to how a later local researcher encountered the record. A public park’s sculpture and plaques give a place to read these different scales of evidence, from a single well and an interstate wire story to the work of watering animals. [1, 2, 5–7]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The 1921 well was on the Armstrong lease outside the downtown interpretive stop. Modern Oil Heritage Park presents the broader oil-town story. ## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The Oklahoma Leader dispatch calls it the Mitchell–Donham test well. The state geological history credits Busey and Mitchell, while the nomination and later accounts use Busey Well or No. 1 Armstrong. The naming, operating and financing roles should stay distinct until lease or drilling records are read. The newspaper’s claim of a first real well differs in scope from the geology bulletin’s chronology of earlier gas. [3–7]
- The McWilliams nomination places the well two miles west of town. The 2020 preservation plan describes it approximately one mile southwest. No attempt was made to reconcile these by guessing a point. [6, 8]
- The 2009 interview is a recollection of childhood and local accounts. No surface or water measurements or original investment, payroll, land or well completion records were retrieved. [4, 5]
- The Main Street and state tourism pages confirm a public interpretive stop. They do not publish a general hours or seasonal schedule. Use the listed visitor contact and posted status. The managing entity is held. [1, 2]
