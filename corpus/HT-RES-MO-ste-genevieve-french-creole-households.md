# Ste. Geneviève — Pélagie’s household on the census page

Missouri OP02 · Draft for independent Dash review · Quiet witness

## 2. Story

**A legal document changed two lives.** On June 11, 1832, Félicité Bauvais executed a document freeing Pélagie and her first child Félix. The historic structure report transcribes the assurance as “the same as if they had never been slaves.” Its figure DH-12 reproduces the actual signed page, which was inspected. The figure shows the dated instrument and Bauvais signature. The quoted English wording is the report’s rendering. The 1836 county register later enrolled Pélagie under Vital. That was another legal encounter, separate from the emancipation. [5]

**The head of the household.** Pélagie appears separately from Benjamin Amoureux in the 1840 and 1850 census discussion in the report. The 1850 household includes Félix, Francis, Mary, Amable and Joseph. Félix alone is listed with an occupation, laborer. A blank occupation cannot measure the work done by the other members of the household. The report traces three suits Pélagie filed after mistreatment in the 1840s. It records that she lost them. In one instance a man she previously sued then served on a jury in a later case involving her. [5]

**A home with a prior history.** The report dates Benjamin’s $700 purchase from Auguste Bauvais to 1852. In 1860 the census presents Benjamin as head and a farmer in a shared household with Pélagie, children and a grandchild. Census entries are snapshots and the household’s inferred move date comes from comparison with the deed, not a census taken in 1852. The family’s relationship was older than its move into this building. [5–6]

## 3. Then and now

The historical location is the Bauvais-Amoureux House near Le Grand Champ. Its French colonial construction sets upright timbers into earth. The survey catalog dates initial construction to 1793. The 1832 deed of emancipation and the earlier census households concern the family's history, and their original locations are not assigned to this house. [5–7]

The lawful anchor is the **Bauvais-Amoureux House NPS visitor parking and tour entry**, 327 St. Marys Road. Use the posted gravel lot and roughly 50-foot lawn approach only while open. At the Welcome Center obtain a free same-day house ticket when offered. NPS lists seven steps at the main entrance and flags the house as not wheelchair accessible. The center provides a staffed alternative when weather or capacity closes house access. [1–4]

## 5. A short visit

Orient at the Welcome Center, then drive to the marked house lot if open. On the short lawful approach take in the gallery and the long roof. Inside, if a guided place is available, listen for the time when Pélagie and Benjamin moved here. Finish with **The name at the head of the page**, reading the report’s 1850 household beside the emancipation document and the 1860 shared household. In flood, standing water, ice or closure, use the Welcome Center if accessible or the report remotely. [1–6]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The report prints inconsistent racial labels and surnames across period records. The dossier retains the distinction between the county's Vital entry and the Amoureux family name without treating the recorded race labels as Pélagie’s self-identification. The ages summarized for 1836 and 1850 imply different birth estimates. The actual census sheets and separate case records were not inspected. The retrieved HSR provided a readable image of the signed emancipation instrument. The roof seen in the HABS photograph predates later restoration. [5–8]
