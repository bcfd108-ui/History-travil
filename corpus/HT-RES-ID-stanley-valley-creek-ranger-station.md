# Stanley · Valley Creek Ranger Station

History Travel · Idaho · Draft for Dash QC · Desk review 18 November 2026

## 2. The story

At 2 Museum Drive, a Forest Service house and office share a roof. In the district where Bonnie Stilwill became a clerk, a federal appointment could change the arrangements of an entire family. “I did not finish high school, but I took the civil service test and I passed,” she recalled. Asked whether the job was in Challis, she corrected the interviewer: it was in Stanley. She worked two years. In the same interview she remembers her family’s two-bedroom mobile home and her daughter being the only eighth grader in her last year there. Her earlier recollection of children sleeping above a garage belongs to Cape Horn. It would be easy to transplant that vivid detail into the wrong building. [2, 4]

There was more than one Valley Creek station. The federal administrative history dates removal of the earlier building to 1933 and breaks the replacement compound into individual jobs. A telephone office/garage predates the main house, a shop followed, then a ranger dwelling in 1932–33, a barn in 1933, a woodshed-cellar in 1933–34 and an oil house in 1935–36. The shop and oil house were subsequently moved. The operator credits ranger Merle Markle and his wife with the 1933 complex. The Forest Service attributes later work to Civilian Conservation Corps crews. These accounts give a family, a work crew and several building seasons their places in the compound. [2, 5]

Harold Wadley arrived with his wife Gwen in the winter of 1960–61 and lived at the station as a ranger. SIHA preserves his recorded account. In 1972 the creation of the Sawtooth National Recreation Area marked the transition to a different station upriver. In 1982 a Forest Service special-use permit put the old station into the hands of the association as a museum. The permit changed who could come through the door. A recent restoration added a weather shelter near the place where local observation began in 1916. It holds instruments for daily highs and lows and for fire weather. The operator dates the installation to spring 2026. [2, 3]

## 3. Then / now

The station ran a district and housed people who worked in it. Today the office and residence hold exhibits. The old cold cellar’s exterior restoration was completed in 2011 and the interior exhibit reconstructs how it could have worked. The exhibit in the attached tool shed opened in 2021. The record preserves a distinction between an original building and objects placed inside to interpret it. [2, 5, 6]

## 4. What’s still there

- **Public interior, seasonal** — original house/office and the designated cold-cellar and tool-shed exhibits. Staff control which rooms are open. [1, 2]
- **Exterior only, in season** — restored weather-instrument shelter. The exhibit stands near the early observing site. [2]
- **Not accessible as an exhibit** — original garage, identified by the operator as office/storage space. The former clerk’s cabin is gone. [2, 7]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. In season, begin outside the house. Compare the front porch gable with the 1976 Forest Service photograph. In winter use the photograph and the association’s oral histories from home. [1, 3, 6]
2. Visit the tool shed, cold cellar and weather shelter in turn. They answer three practical questions about a working district — where tools went, where food kept, and where someone had to look at a thermometer day after day. [2]
3. **Strongest story moment** — finish at the house with page one of Stilwill’s interview. Read the short exchange about which town she worked in. An interviewer guessed wrong, and the person who did the job put Stanley back on the record. [4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

SIHA’s walking tour dates the earlier, relocated front section at Danner’s Cabins to 1908. The register nomination and federal history date the earlier station to 1909. They agree on removal or relocation in 1933. The sources also differ on the age of the oldest remaining structure. The administrative inventory lists the telephone office/garage in 1929, yet reports a 1931 earliest date from the eligibility assessment. Treat each date with its named building and source. The federal history says the listing used the name “Stanley Ranger Station.” The museum is distinct from the modern ranger facility. [2, 5, 6, 8, 9]

The general museum page says daily opening June 13. The explicitly labeled 2026 calendar says June 12. Both close the regular season in September. The Wadley video catalogue has no transcript, so no words here are attributed to him verbatim. [1, 2, 3]

The roadside state marker dates the move to the replacement ranger station to 1971. SIHA dates the end of work here to 1972 and the museum permit to 1982. The accounts may describe different steps. No dated move order was found. State marker 406 and HMdb database entry 182177 are separate identifiers. [2, 10]
