# Arkansas Post National Memorial, Arkansas Trading House story
HT-RES-AR-arkansas-post-river-settlement · Arkansas OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

John B. Treat needed somewhere dry. “The goods brought with me are in good order,” he reported to Superintendent Davy on October 6, 1805. This is a short quotation from the National Park Service structural history’s transcription of his letter, which names the Arkansas Trading House letterbook as its source. Treat initially stored the shipment at the garrison. He described inspecting two roughly 30-by-15-foot houses with rooms raised six feet. He preferred the one nearer the garrison, even though it cost $150 a year rather than $130. The lower space was cool enough for skins. The chosen property had room for garden and pasture and more separation from nearby combustible buildings. [3, printed pp. 22–24; 4]

The federal trading house was called a factory, a place where a factor conducted trade. The surviving letterbook guide says Treat reached the Post on September 4, 1805, after a journey of about three months from Pittsburgh. His October letter shows how travel, housing and storage shaped the work after arrival. A November letter, also transcribed by NPS, describes multiple resident commercial competitors and periodic traders arriving by way of the Wabash with Detroit goods. One clerk’s salary or shipment alone would underestimate the different kinds of work that brought a trading place into being. [3, 4]

NPS historic interpretation explains that the name Arkansas Post was used for settlements that shifted over time with flood and military changes. Treat’s letter belongs to the early U.S. trading operation. The meaning of Arkansas Post is longer than that institution and includes Quapaw history and earlier French and Spanish sites. The visit uses the public museum’s settlement interpretation and leaves village, treaty and battlefield ground outside this walk. [3–5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** Treat’s 1805 description places his preferred house close to a garrison. It does not give a modern parcel or surviving wall. The current memorial preserves and interprets parts of a later settlement landscape. The museum and the designated paved approach are the anchors for reading his account of freight and storage. [2, 3, 5, 7]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Arkansas Post National Memorial visitor center, 1741 Old Post Road, Gillett. Drive via AR 169 and use the signed public parking and short approach. A short out-and-back on the open paved townsite approach is optional. Turn back at any closure or wherever the path extends toward the shore, battlefield or restricted ground. [1, 2, 6, 7]

## 5. Public progression

Begin in the visitor center. Use Treat’s account to read how a river shipment went from the garrison to commercial storage. Then, if open and dry, step onto the paved townsite path and read the same account from the public approach before returning. **Strongest story moment · Where the goods could stay dry.** A cool space below the rooms, open ground and reduced exposure to fire help explain why the factor chose the higher-rent house. This is a quiet reading stop. [1–7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The NPS events and activities page describes daily film availability in its overview. The specific hours page confines access to Thu–Mon and lists 2026–27 closure dates for both visitor center and grounds. Use the more specific schedule. [1, 7]
- Treat’s quoted letter survives here as the structural history’s printed transcription. The microfilm M142 introduction was read separately. A manuscript frame for the October 6 letter and a securely matched plan or artifact for Treat’s rented house were not obtained. [3, 4]
- The National Register nomination says the fort renamed Fort Madison in 1804 stood on ground later taken by the river. It also presents a material disagreement over excavated trenches on the peninsula. Preston Holder interpreted them as parts of colonial fortifications. John Walthall interpreted them as compound fences. The trenches give no secure locator for Treat’s rented house and are outside this visit. [9, 10]
- The various historic locations called Arkansas Post span different periods. Do not project a village, garrison, house or military event onto the present visitor stop based on the shared name. [3, 5]
