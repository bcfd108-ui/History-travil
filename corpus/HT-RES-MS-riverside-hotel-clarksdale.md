# Riverside Hotel, from a Black hospital to a place musicians could stay

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP20*

## 2. Story

Before the Riverside Hotel, the building housed the G. T. Thomas Afro-American Hospital. Black patients had limited access to care in a segregated system. The family’s interpretation dates construction to 1916. NPS records that Bessie Smith died at the hospital after a 1937 automobile crash. The event belongs to the hospital history, and later stories about the accident and admission need their own evidence before they are repeated. The people who staffed its wards and the legal and admissions arrangements remain underdocumented in the documents read here. [2–4]

Z. L. Ratliff Hill adapted the building for lodging in 1943, according to the family and NPS. The Riverside opened as a hotel in 1944. She purchased the property in 1957. Her granddaughter Zelena “Zee” Ratliff remembers, “My grandmother had been operating since 1944.” This is family testimony printed on the operator’s history page. The distinction between opening and ownership matters. Hill was managing a business years before she held the deed. A family-run hotel could give Black musicians a place to stay when Jim Crow reduced their choices. The people who washed sheets, answered doors, kept rooms and knew the travelers helped make a touring circuit work. [2–4]

Frank “Rat” Ratliff carried on the hotel after his mother’s death. The current family account describes continued work by Joyce, Zelena and other relatives. The hotel ceased operating as lodging after an April 2020 storm in the family account. The newer public museum opening and tourism notice concern interpretation and restoration. They do not establish that guests can again stay overnight. The direct tour contact is the source for planning an inside visit now. [1–3][5]

## 3. Then and now

The historic building had medical and lodging uses in succession. The surviving structure and family-held exhibits now support a guided blues and civil-rights history program. The current museum use is distinct from either an operating hospital or a confirmed lodging business. Individual guest-room or medical fabric should be identified by a guide rather than inferred from furnishings. [1–5]
