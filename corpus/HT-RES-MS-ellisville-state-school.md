## 2. The story

Founded by the Mississippi Legislature in **1920** (originally titled the **Mississippi School and Colony for the Feeble-Minded**, reflecting the clinical terminology of the Progressive Era), **Ellisville State School** opened south of Ellisville along the US Highway 11 corridor (`Section 8`, Jones County) to house, educate, and train children and adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities apart from state psychiatric hospitals. [1, 3]

Over the mid-twentieth century, the institution developed into a sprawling self-contained campus arranged around curved tree-shaded loops and ponds west of US-11 (clearly mapped on the 1964 USGS `Ellisville` quadrangle). In **1972**, as tuberculosis waned, Ellisville State School briefly assumed stewardship of the former **Mississippi State Tuberculosis Sanatorium** in Simpson County (which later became **Boswell Regional Center** in 1976). [1, 4]

Today, administered by the **DMH Bureau of Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities**, Ellisville State School operates both its main **240-resident Ellisville campus** (`1101 Hwy 11 S.`) and community-living programs across 32 counties, emphasizing least-restrictive-environment training and vocational support. [1, 2]
