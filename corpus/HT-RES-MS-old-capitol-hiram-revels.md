# Old Capitol Museum and the room that elected Hiram Revels

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP09 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Public museum at 100 South State Street, Jackson. The state posts Tuesday–Saturday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Reservations are preferred. The historic House chamber is the setting for this entry. [1,2]

## 2. Story

On January 20, 1870, Mississippi legislators assembled in the Old House Chamber and elected Hiram Rhodes Revels to the United States Senate. The room matters. Popular election of senators came decades later. It was legislators, including a new generation of Black officeholders, who made the choice. Revels later explained that the Black members had discussed backing one of their own number. He was an African Methodist Episcopal minister, a former Union chaplain, and an alderman from Natchez before his election to Mississippi’s state senate. [2–4]

## 3. Then and now

The paper that carried the result out of this building survives. Provisional governor Adelbert Ames certified the January 20 election on January 25. A notation records when the credentials reached the U.S. Senate on February 23. Opponents challenged whether Revels met its citizenship requirement; supporters rejected an argument that depended on denying his earlier citizenship. The Senate voted 48 to 8 to seat him. He took the oath on February 25 in Washington, D.C. The vote in Jackson and the oath in Washington were different events, over a month apart. [3,4]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The 1839 capitol is now a museum. Its chambers also witnessed Mississippi’s secession in 1861 and the drafting of later constitutions. The museum’s account of the 150th anniversary of Revels’s election identifies the Old House Chamber as the room where the state vote occurred. [1,2]

## 5. Public progression

Start with the representative chamber. Picture state senators and representatives assembling there, then read the dated credentials to follow what happened next. A visitor does not need to locate a particular seat to understand the sequence. [2,3]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

No seat assignment for Revels or original ballot sheet was verified. The original credentials are in federal custody. An earlier dossier uses this museum’s first-floor statehood exhibit as a substitute for the closed Jefferson College campus. This entry concerns the legislative event that actually took place in this building. [1–3]
