# A machinist’s Greenville and the work of living through the 1927 flood

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP20*

## 2. Story

At the Greenville Boiler and Machine Works, Percy McCraney had a job before and after water entered the city in 1927. In an oral history recorded in the 1970s he says Black residents “caught tough times around Greenville here.” He had come to Greenville in 1919, according to his interview. The interview contains descriptions of displacement and unequal treatment, including the difficulty some residents faced getting food. It is a named resident’s account and includes both what McCraney said happened around him and what he said did or did not happen personally. That distinction matters in reading memory of a disaster. [2]

The state’s historical account of the flood describes the large-scale inundation and the relief arrangements, along with unequal conditions for Black residents. The photographs preserve a smaller view of the same kind of disruption. At Main and Poplar, a 1927 silver print records submerged vehicles, storefronts and utility poles receding along the business street. The photograph shows the physical state of an intersection. It leaves the conditions of work, movement and food distribution to be read through records such as McCraney’s testimony. [2–4]

The current museum in the restored Miller Building puts local artifacts, period photography and newspaper clippings together. The civic listing describes a broader history of Greenville from the late nineteenth century into the 1970s, with the flood among the chapters. The separate Flood Museum also interprets that disaster under different admission arrangements. The Miller Building galleries offer a place to read the town’s record without visiting an old riverfront or disaster site. [1]

## 3. Then and now

The 1927 downtown in the historical photograph is a documented business district under water. The present anchor is the Greenville History Museum’s Miller Building, which the civic listing identifies as restored. The exact date of this building, its earlier tenants and its individual condition during the flood did not verify in this pass. The historic flooded intersection is outside the progression. [1][4]

## 7. Historical photo records

Candidate held. Library of Congress Prints and Photographs Division, item 2002707514, digital ID cph.3b23045, LC-USZ62-75847; creator uncredited in record, April 24, 1927. The inspected photograph shows almost wheel-deep water around an automobile and a flooded street lined with poles and storefronts. The catalog identifies Main and Poplar in Greenville, looking north. It is a real Greenville view, separate from the modern museum. LOC lists no known restrictions. Original publication and an independently sufficient public-domain basis have not been confirmed for this particular print, so the image remains unadopted. No Photo Point. [4][5]
