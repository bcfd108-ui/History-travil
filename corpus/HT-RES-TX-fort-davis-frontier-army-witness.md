# Fort Davis — the fort kept accounts as well as a garrison
Research draft · Texas OP04 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### The fort ran on work

The NPS dates the return of the post’s U.S. Army garrison to July 1, 1867, when four troops of the Ninth Cavalry under Wesley Merritt arrived to establish it again. The agency describes labor that was rarely captured in later heroic shorthand. Men cut roads and Company I of the Twenty-Fifth Infantry completed 91.5 miles of telegraph line west from the post to Eagle Springs under George Andrews. Communications, construction and keeping animals and supplies available were parts of military power. The historic park study locates a July 1880 post return in its bibliography, where the reader can pursue the personnel snapshot independently [3–4].

### A quartermaster’s work became a legal case

The park places Henry Ossian Flipper’s Fort Davis arrival on November 29, 1880. A graduate of West Point in 1877, he served here as quartermaster and acting commissary. His daily responsibilities involved money, property and records. When commissary funds were missing, failure to report the shortage promptly and subsequent accounting became the center of his prosecution. He was acquitted of embezzlement and convicted of conduct unbecoming an officer and a gentleman. The park’s account and its administrative history trace the events; the finding of guilt on one charge should never be written as proof of theft [5–6].

### What the paperwork meant to him

The fort history quotes Flipper as later writing that “the trap was cunningly laid and I was sacrificed.” This is his recollection as quoted in that study, whose notes identify supporting published sources. The original memoir page is still unverified. The park records the later military review and the presidential pardon of February 19, 1999. The paperwork that marked his career became the object of a later review on different terms [5–7].

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic place.** Fort Davis military post, an army installation with quarters, barracks and serviced ground [3, 8–9].

**Lawful physical anchor.** NPS Fort Davis Visitor Center from the signed public entrance and parking area. Use a short walk toward the designated parade-ground overview where open. Enter only signed and open furnished buildings. Sensitive remains, closed rooms, military hazard areas and any offsite campaign, burial or tribal ground are outside this visit [1–2, 8].

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Orient inside the public visitor center. Use the NPS layout and ask which signed buildings are open [1–2, 8].
2. From the open parade-ground overview read the names of regiments and compare where garrison labor and officers’ quarters fit into the post [3–4, 8].
3. **Strongest story moment — The books that outlasted the fort.** At the public overlook or the open visitor center, read Flipper’s short recollection and the modern park account of the court finding and pardon. Allow a quiet pause, then leave by the same signed visitor approach [5–7].

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

NPS gives July 1877 for Flipper’s assignment following graduation; the older chapter says he graduated in 1876. Use the current NPS chronology for the visit, preserving the older date here [5–6]. The July 1880 post return is located through a written archival citation; image and original entry were not inspected [4]. Direct LOC Officers’ Row survey photo returned HTTP 403 [10].
