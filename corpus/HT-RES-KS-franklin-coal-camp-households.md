# Franklin — Mary Skubitz’s ledger and the miners’ hall

## 2. The story

**Coal work reached the family table.** The 1921 strike stopped earnings for mining households. Wives and other relatives met at Franklin on December 11 and marched over the following days. The gathering was a local meeting. The ranks that grew on the roads over subsequent days were a larger phenomenon. Mary Youvan Skubitz was among the organizers. The ledger pages she wrote in December survive at Pittsburg State University. The university’s online record is a finding aid and does not reproduce the ten handwritten pages in this retrieval. [2–5]

**Two records, two directions of attention.** A December 15, 1921 news page preserved by Texas Tech called Franklin “heretofore headquarters” of the protesters. Its report follows what officials knew, including a sheriff's report and the arrival of state troops. In the journal passage reproduced by Benjamin Goossen, Skubitz wrote, “There was absolutely no fear in these women’s hearts.” She was describing the women around her from within the movement. Her words and the wire reporter’s language describe overlapping events from different positions. The paper’s nickname for the marchers was an external label. [3,4,6]

**What the numbers count.** A museum trustee tells KMUW the march began with about 500 and reports that Skubitz put participation at 6,000 by December 13. A University of Nebraska reference gives a broader 2,000–6,000 range for the march across several days. Duke’s record gives 3,000 with higher reports. The meeting, the first day's line and a multi-day peak are distinct. None of these records is a verified full roll. [5,7,8]

## 3. Then / now

**Historic location.** The museum states an early union hall stood on its current site and identifies it as the march's starting location. The surviving public museum occupies the later community-center site. This interpretation does not identify original hall walls within today's building. Mining sites and march roads are separate locations. [2,5]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Miners Hall Museum admissions approach at 701 S Broadway. Use the designated short public approach and staff-accessible interior during posted hours. The current dragline work is a separate project and no public access there was established. [1,2]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Begin inside where staff admit visitors and establish the difference between the surviving museum and the old hall site. [1,2]
2. Read the 1921 news account alongside the manuscript locator. Follow the names and dates without projecting one reporter’s vantage across every town. [3,4,6]
3. **Strongest story moment — Ten pages of ledger paper.** Read the short journal passage and consider that the same sort of paper used to put a number on an obligation also carried a worker household’s account of collective action. Make space for quiet reading. [3,4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- December 11 meeting and December 12 start of marching are separate dated events. Participation estimates differ across the records above. [4,5,7,8]
- The PSU download supplies the finding aid, not an identified facsimile of Skubitz’s journal pages. The quotation is mediated by Goossen’s reproduction. Holiday exception hours and exact accessibility were not established from the operator page. [1,3,4]
