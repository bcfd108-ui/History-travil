# Medgar and Myrlie Evers’s house after the office closed

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP09 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

National Park Service ranger visit at 2332 Margaret Walker Alexander Drive, Jackson. Published house hours are Tuesday–Saturday, 9 a.m.–noon and 1–4 p.m. Myrlie’s Garden has outdoor interpretation and separate sunrise-to-sunset hours. Reflection only. [1]

## 2. Story

When work officially ended at the NAACP field office, the telephone calls and meetings often followed Medgar and Myrlie Evers home. Myrlie described the change as simply moving headquarters for the evening. She ran the Jackson field office, worked on speeches and helped organize the support on which visiting civil-rights workers depended. Medgar traveled Mississippi as the NAACP’s field secretary. They were raising three children. Their home gave organizers somewhere to meet and people under threat somewhere to stay. [2–4]

## 3. Then and now

They bought the one-story house in 1956, on what was then Guynes Street, in the Elraine subdivision, a development for Black middle-class families. The living, dining and kitchen areas were arranged for ordinary family life. Threats against their work shaped that life. On June 12, 1963, Medgar was assassinated outside after returning home. Myrlie continued working for civil rights and pursued justice for the killing. Her firsthand interview is also a record of the marriage and the work they had shared before that night. [2–4]

## 4. Surviving features and access

The family left Mississippi, but Myrlie retained the house and donated it to Tougaloo College in 1993. The college and family helped preserve it before its transfer to the National Park Service. The ranch house now stands in a residential neighborhood. The park provides an accessible ramp and warns that the rooms are small. The garden provides seating and interpretive exhibits for a visitor who prefers to remain outdoors. [1,2]

## 5. Public progression

Begin with the work Myrlie carried between office and home. In the rooms a ranger opens, consider where a family could make space for the movement in an evening already occupied by supper and children. The garden offers an equally valid place to read that record. [1,2]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

The park distinguishes house capacity from larger group arrangements. A visitor with particular access needs can check with staff about the room widths. The house history documents original ownership and later preservation; a surviving floor plan alone cannot establish which furnishings belonged to the family. [1,2]
