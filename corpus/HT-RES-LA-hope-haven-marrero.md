# Hope Haven Center (Hope Haven and Madonna Manor), Marrero — 1925–1941 Spanish Colonial Revival orphanage campus

**Research draft · Labeled THEME SWEEP (Family 2 — Orphanages & institutional care · Updated to public-park trail ANCHORED; historic buildings closed/fenced for parish stabilization) · Marrero, Jefferson Parish, Louisiana · November 21, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate

**SITE STATUS & ANCHOR UPDATE:** Following decades of closure and clergy-abuse litigation against the Archdiocese of New Orleans, **Jefferson Parish completed the $3.8 million purchase of the 15-acre Hope Haven campus** (`1101 Barataria Blvd., Marrero, LA 70072`) in **July 2025** (after leasing it since 2018). [1]

The **outdoor grounds** now serve as the **open public Hope Haven Fitness Trail and Park** along the central live-oak allee (confirmed by *Times-Picayune* July 2025 reporting and June 2026 site photography). However, the **1925–1941 Spanish Colonial Revival buildings** themselves remain **fenced, gutted/closed, and strictly off-limits to entry** during multi-million-dollar parish stabilization (`$5M` stabilization underway; natatorium, education/recreation reuse, and a **granite monument memorializing clergy-abuse survivors** planned). [1, 2]

**Anchor ruling: ANCHORED (public Hope Haven Fitness Trail & Park oak allee + Barataria Blvd. exterior views ONLY; fenced/closed historic buildings strictly excluded from entry).**

## 2. The story

Constructed along both sides of **Barataria Boulevard** in Marrero (`1925–1941`), **Hope Haven** (west side) and **Madonna Manor** (east side) together form the **largest ensemble of Spanish Colonial Revival architecture in Louisiana**, unified by white stucco walls, red clay-tile roofs, round-arched arcades, spiral columns, and ornate towers (`NRHP 99001358`, 1999). [2]

Father **Peter M. H. Wynhoven** of St. Joseph's Church in Gretna founded **Hope Haven** in **1925** for teenage boys (14 and older) of all faiths, starting with architect **William R. Burk's** three-story Administration Building (`1925`), followed by the **Julian Saenger Gymnasium** (`1930`) and the two-story **Vocational School** (`1931`, framed with carved relief tools—axes, pliers, planes, hammers—symbolizing trades taught to ~125 resident boys). Across Barataria Boulevard, **Madonna Manor** opened in **1932** under the Sisters of Notre Dame for younger boys and girls (housing ~100 boys and 40 girls by 1940), designed by **Jack J. H. Kessels** for **Diboll & Owen** with a circular **Star of David stained-glass window** honoring Jewish community donations, alongside Kessels's **School Building** (`1932`, for Andrew S. Montz) and the richly sculpted **St. John Bosco Chapel** (`1941`, Diboll-Kessels & Associates; exterior restored 2014). [2]

Behind the monumental architecture, the institution accumulated a painful documented history: in recent decades, hundreds of lawsuits against the **Archdiocese of New Orleans** alleged widespread physical and sexual abuse of children by clergy and religious staff at Hope Haven and Madonna Manor. As part of its bankruptcy settlement to compensate abuse survivors, the Archdiocese sold the 15-acre Hope Haven tract to **Jefferson Parish** (`$3.8M`, finalized **July 2025**), whose master plan retains the historic core for public health/recreation reuse alongside a planned **granite monument honoring survivors of abuse**. [1]

## 4. What's still there

- **Public (park trail & street exterior):** Paved **Hope Haven Fitness Trail and Park** beneath the historic live-oak allee, plus clear exterior views from Barataria Boulevard and park paths of the white-stucco, red-tile Spanish Colonial Revival arcades, towers, vocational-school tool reliefs, and St. John Bosco Chapel facade. [1, 2, 3]
- **Closed / off-limits:** Interiors and fenced construction zones of the 1925–1941 buildings during stabilization and redevelopment. [1]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Park at the Hope Haven Fitness Trail and Park lot** along **Barataria Boulevard** (`1101 Barataria Blvd.`, Marrero) during daylight park hours. [1]
2. **Walk the paved live-oak allee trail**, remaining strictly outside all fenced historic building stabilization perimeters. [1, 3]
3. **Read the architecture from public park paths and Barataria Blvd. sidewalks:** Burk's 1925 Administration tower, the 1931 Vocational School's carved trade-tool reliefs, Madonna Manor's Star of David rose window across the boulevard, and St. John Bosco Chapel's stone sculpture. [2]
4. **Hold dignified awareness** of the documented abuse-survivor history behind the Archdiocese bankruptcy sale and Jefferson Parish's planned survivor monument before departing. [1]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Madonna Manor ownership split:** While Jefferson Parish finalized the `$3.8M` purchase of the 15-acre Hope Haven tract in July 2025 (`nola.com`), `SAH Archipedia` notes community-care tenants operated in portions of the broader complex; exact parcel split across the east/west sides of Barataria Blvd. was not verified from Jefferson Parish tax-assessor plats. [1, 2]
- **Exact construction completion dates:** Planned natatorium, building stabilization, and survivor monument remain in phased funding/construction after July 2025. [1]
