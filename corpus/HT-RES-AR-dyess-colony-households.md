# Dyess — What came with twenty acres
HT-RES-AR-dyess-colony-households · Arkansas OP01 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

In the winter of 1935 Ray and Carrie Rivers Cash moved their family from Cleveland County, Arkansas, into the new agricultural colony at Dyess. A-State’s NEH narrative places them among households entering a planned community whose farmsteads spread out from a central settlement. The undertaking brought a dwelling, farm work and a set of obligations together. The center and the farmstead were two working pieces of the same project. [2–4]

In Bob Elfstrom’s 1969 film, Johnny Cash talks about the housing as he passes through Dyess. “They all had 20 acres of land to begin with in 1935,” he says in the excerpt transcribed by PBS. He lists a barn, chicken house, smokehouse and mule, and describes how the land was divided between neighboring houses. The account makes a livelihood legible in things a person could have walked past every day: where meat was cured, where animals were kept, and whose labor linked the house with the land. It is a later voice, printed from a film, and the broad figure belongs to Cash’s memory. [5]

Cash calls the houses he passes sharecropper houses. A-State situates the colony in a federal agricultural resettlement and eventual purchase program. Dyess and the ordinary tenancy Cash invokes bring overlapping working conditions into view, together with a different institutional arrangement. The general program sources leave the exact terms of the Cash family’s agreement and the timing of title transfer open. [3–5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The historic community center and the Cash family’s farmstead are separate points within the colony landscape. The house has been restored to represent its earlier period. The family’s lot and the location of individual outbuildings need a parcel plan before exact placement. [1–4]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Historic Dyess Colony Visitors Center, 110 Center Drive, drive-up with a short walk to the signed entrance. Continue next door to the Administration Building if open, and reach the separate boyhood home only with the operator’s guided route. Private residential drives and farm lots are outside the visit. ## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The public visit and restoration history are verified. The signed Cash resettlement agreement, lot map, precise acreage at move-in and working outbuilding plan were not retrieved during this pass. The operator interprets a resettlement colony and the film transcript preserves Cash’s use of the word sharecropping. Keep their different scopes visible. [3–5]
