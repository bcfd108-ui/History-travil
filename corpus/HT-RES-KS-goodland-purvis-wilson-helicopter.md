## 2. The story
**William J. Purvis and Charles A. Wilson** named themselves as Goodland inventors in U.S. Patent **1,028,781, Flying-machine**. The actual specification gives March 18, 1910 filing, November 2, 1911 renewal and June 4, 1912 grant. The patent records a design and claim, not a successful flight or priority over every other inventor. [2]

The City's museum account identifies them as Rock Island Railroad machinists who worked on the project outside their railway shifts. The account describes local stock purchases and John Keeran's involvement; a second City account identifies Keeran as treasurer of the Goodland Aviation Company and credits his daughter Betty with donating a replica. These are retrospective accounts, not independently read railway payroll or subscription books. [3–4]

**Strongest story moment:** Their day jobs did not make the flying machine inevitable. Shop experience, hours outside paid work and local money helped carry an uncertain design as far as a patent.
