# Albion State Normal School and the teachers it sent out across Idaho

**Research draft · Labeled THEME SWEEP (Family 2/4 — Institutional campus with a separate seasonal-lore layer) · Albion, Cassia County, Idaho · November 20, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate

The former Albion State Normal School campus is **privately owned** (Albion Campus Retreat, owners Troy and Heather Mortensen since a June 2007 public auction). It is not a public park and there is no open walk-in campus. Lawful public contact with this ground takes three forms only: [4, 5, 6, 7]

- **Public exterior from the public right-of-way.** The 1980 National Register district sits at the west end of Albion, southwest of State Highway 77 (North Street), and its building faces and open quadrangles are visible from the public street. Stay on the public road and sidewalk; the campus lawns and buildings are private property. [1, 4]
- **Albion museum (Swanger Hall area), seasonal.** The *Official 2024 Southern Idaho Visitor Guide* lists a campus museum of Normal School memorabilia (and the Diamondfield Jack trial) **open Saturdays mid-May through mid-September, or by appointment**. 2026 hours were **not** confirmed from an operator page this run; confirm before travel. [8]
- **Operator-ticketed or rented access.** Albion Campus Retreat rents Miller Hall, the President's Cottage and "Comish" (Cornish) Hall to booked groups; each October the owners run the ticketed **Haunted Mansions of Albion** attraction inside several campus buildings (Thu–Sat evenings per its site). These are commercial entries on the owner's terms, not history tours. [5, 6, 7]

**Anchor ruling: ANCHORED (public exterior from the public right-of-way on Highway 77 / North Street, plus a seasonal museum to be re-confirmed).** Interior access only through the operator.

## 2. The story

Albion State Normal School was Idaho's southern teacher-training school, created in the same 1893 legislative session that created its northern counterpart at Lewiston. Idaho State University's archival description dates the founding act to **March 7, 1893**, and records that **Josiah E. Miller** deeded five acres to the State on April 14, 1893. Cassia County citizens subscribed **$3,000** and built the first structure — the two-story lava-rock building now called **Swanger Hall** — in 1893–94, largely with volunteer labor and local stone. The school opened on **September 11, 1894**, under President F. A. Swanger. [1, 2, 3]

Within two years the state added a larger brick-and-sandstone administration building, designed by Boise architect **W. S. Campbell** (1897 per the district nomination), using the rock building as its rear wing; for about a decade that pair housed the entire school. Over the next three decades Idaho's leading architects built out a two-quadrangle campus: **J. E. Tourtellotte** (Miller Hall men's dormitory, 1901; Axline Gymnasium and armory, contract 1909, built by R. C. Alloway of Twin Falls for $25,000 of locally quarried sandstone and brick fired on site); **Wayland & Fennell** (Hansen Hall women's dormitory, 1905; the Training or "model" School, 1907, built by Ernest White; Bocock Memorial Hall, 1927, a $83,000 public-works administration/library/650-seat auditorium built by Bird Findlayson of Pocatello; the President's Residence, 1930, $10,000); **Tourtellotte & Hummel** (Hannah Cornish Hall, 1918, with 1926 wings of unconfirmed authorship); a $50,000 central heating plant (1925); and **Burton E. Morse** of Twin Falls (McMurray Hall men's dormitory, 1928–29). [1, 2]

The work of the place was training rural teachers. The district nomination quotes the school's own premise that "teachers must be educated and trained to teach," and records that most graduates went on to teach in Idaho schools. The school stayed small and chronically underfunded. In 1947 it was renamed **Southern Idaho College of Education** and authorized to grant four-year degrees. ISU's archival note says that, "over the strong objections of the citizens of Albion," the school was closed in **1951** and its responsibilities transferred to Idaho State College in Pocatello; Idaho Public Television's documentary records the final commencement in spring 1951 and an oral-history account that the legislature withdrew funding rather than voting a formal closure. (The 1980 nomination gives a different closing year; see §6.) ISU later named its College of Education building **Albion Hall** (2006) in the school's memory. [1, 3, 9]

The state kept the property until a Church of Christ lease brought **Magic Valley Christian College** to the campus (lease 1957; college open through 1969). The City of Albion then took ownership, and by the time of the 1978 and 1980 Register nominations several buildings stood vacant and deteriorating, Swanger Hall proper (the 1897 administration building) had burned in **1947**, and Hansen Hall was being demolished as the district form was written. The city sold the campus at public auction on **June 2, 2007**; the Mortensens rehabilitated Miller Hall as group lodging. [1, 2, 4, 5]

### LORE LAYER — "Haunted Mansions of Albion" (labeled; not stated as fact)

*Everything in this sub-section is reported claim or commercial entertainment, attributed to its source. None of it is presented as historical fact.*

- **Commercial haunt (fact of the event, not of ghosts):** For well over a decade (KIVI-TV: "every fall for the past 18 years"; report date not captured) the owners have staged a ticketed October haunted attraction in several campus buildings, marketed as the largest indoor/historic haunt in the Northwest. Its own site currently says "four historic college buildings"; a KIVI-TV report says five buildings and more than 80 actors. [6, 10]
- **Owner-reported paranormal accounts:** The *Times-News* (Twin Falls) quoted owner Troy Mortensen describing spirits "passing through" — running footsteps in an empty building, the smell of dough in an unused kitchen, a long-dead woman moving through dark rooms — and said the first "ghost report" came right after the 2007 purchase. These are the owners' accounts; no historical record reviewed identifies any person said to appear. [7, 11]
- **Television:** The *Times-News* (via *Idaho State Journal*, Aug. 24, 2017) reported that the Travel Channel's *Ghost Adventures* filmed on campus in July 2017. A broadcast is not evidence of a haunting. [7]
- **Reflection posture:** No death, disaster, or institutional abuse at the Normal School was found in the primary records reviewed. The lore attaches to the buildings' age and emptiness, not to a documented tragedy; it should not be dressed up as one.

## 4. What's still there

- **Visible from the public street (Highway 77 / North Street):** the campus edge, the 1925 heating plant with its tall stack (built into the slope near the east entrance at highway level), and building faces across the quadrangles. Exact sight lines from the right-of-way to each building were not field-verified this run. [1]
- **Standing district buildings recorded in 1980** (current condition of each not verified this run): Swanger Hall rock building (1893–94); Miller Hall (1901, remodeled 1938, rehabilitated post-2007); Training School (1907); Axline Gymnasium (1909–c.1914); Cornish Hall (1918/1926); Central Heating Plant (1925); Bocock Memorial Hall (1927); McMurray Hall (1928–29); President's Residence (1930). [1, 5]
- **Gone:** the 1897 Campbell administration building ("Swanger Hall proper," burned 1947) and Hansen Hall (1905, demolished as of the 1980 nomination). [1]
- **Private / excluded:** all campus grounds and building interiors outside booked or ticketed operator access.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Approach on State Highway 77 into Albion's west end** (about 42.4132, −113.5837; see §10). Park only in legal public street parking. [1]
2. **From the public right-of-way, read the campus plan:** two quadrangles framed by brick and sandstone buildings, with the 1925 heating plant and stack at the highway-level east entrance. [1]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Street address directional (unresolved):** The queue and the Haunted Mansions site footer give **437 E. North St.**; Albion Campus Retreat's site and the haunt's 2024 visitor-guide advertisement give **437 W. North Street**. ArcGIS returns a PointAddress match only for **437 W North St** (42.413241, −113.583700), consistent with the nomination's "west end of the town"; the "E" form geocodes by interpolation about 0.9 km east, off campus. This draft uses W. North St.; the E form is printed, not adopted. [4, 6, 8, 12]
- **Closing year:** The 1980 NRHP nomination says the legislature closed the school in **1949** and the state maintained the property to 1957. ISU's archival description, the LC name authority, and Idaho Public Television all give **1951** (last commencement spring 1951). This draft follows 1951 and prints the 1949 figure. [1, 3, 9]
- **Opening enrollment:** ISU archives — **26** students; other secondary accounts — 23. [3]
- **Magic Valley Christian College dates:** NRHP — leased 1957, opened the following year; *Times-News* (2017 history piece) — reopened 1959; both agree on closure in 1969. City ownership "since 1971" (NRHP) vs. 1970 (secondary). [1]
- **First-building completion:** Swanger Hall nomination dates the rock building 1893–94 and the brick administration building "within two years"; the district form dates the latter 1897. [1, 2]
- **Cornish vs. "Comish" Hall:** The nomination names **Hannah Cornish Hall**; the current operator spells it "Comish Hall." Spelling not resolved against a primary naming record.
- **Axline Gymnasium completion:** contract let 1909 (nomination); some secondary accounts say finished 1914. Completion date not confirmed from a primary record.
- **Museum status 2026:** Only the 2024 visitor guide was found; no 2025–2026 operator page with hours. Printed as a gap.
- **Newspapers not retrieved directly:** The nomination cites the *Idaho Daily Statesman*, *Capital News* (1895–1909) and *Twin Falls News* (1928–29). LOC Chronicling America returned a Cloudflare "Just a moment…" challenge from the research box this run, so quotations here come from the nomination's transcriptions, not from the page images.
