# The Battle of Tupelo and the names behind the rear guard

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP15 · Labeled research draft · Reflection only*

## 2. Story

In July 1864 Andrew J. Smith's Union expedition moved into northern Mississippi and met a Confederate force near Harrisburg, close to today's Tupelo. One part of the story is easy to miss if the campaign is read only through generals' names. In his July 25 report, Union Col. Edward Bouton described a brigade of three regiments of United States Colored Infantry and a battery of United States Colored Artillery. He names James C. Foster of the 59th, F. A. Kendrick of the 61st and J. B. Jones of the 68th as officers responsible for their units. NPS explains the place of those regiments in covering movement and helping the Union column establish its position. [4–6]

In the same printed volume, Confederate Lt. Gen. Stephen D. Lee reported to Braxton Bragg on July 14. “Could not force his position,” Lee wrote in the available transcription, then called the fight a draw. That dispatch was written before the later accounting and represents a participant's judgment about the morning. The NPS battle entry assesses the wider action as a Union victory, despite Smith leaving Tupelo afterward. The words “draw” and “victory” cover different windows and criteria. For the men in Bouton's units, the record should also ask after casualties, medical care and the experience of service beyond the commander’s report. [4–8]

The nearby one-acre memorial records a later decision about public commemoration. William E. Cox's 1974 federal photograph gives the monument a particular date in that memorial history. Its inscribed title includes Tupelo and Harrisburg. Neither the monument nor the acre it stands on represents everything that happened across the larger landscape. A traveler can get the operator's explanation of both names at its separate visitor center. [1–3, 9–11]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The 1864 battle was fought across the Harrisburg/Tupelo landscape. The present one-acre federally maintained monument site and the later parkway visitor center are different properties. [1, 4, 9, 11]

**Present public anchor.** Natchez Trace Parkway Visitor Center, 2680 Natchez Trace Parkway, Tupelo. Use its signed public parking and staffed information area. It is the NPS designated contact for Tupelo National Battlefield six miles away. [1–3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- Lee describes a draw in his day-of dispatch. NPS labels the combined action a Union victory. The register image records the eventual memorial’s own statement of victory. Preserve each author's scope and time. [4, 7, 10]
- MDAH dates the wider action July 13–15, while the later monument prints July 14–15. The NPS narrative includes the preceding movement and the major attack on July 14. Do not compress these different time windows. [4, 9, 10]
- The federal listing date is October 15, 1966. The MDAH database also records 1974 preparation and apparent documentation in 1976. The Cox form carries a 1976 receipt stamp. [9–11]
- Ohio State's adjacent pages 0301 and 0320 could not be opened in the web reader. Bouton's opening page and the separately hosted excerpt of Lee remained available. Rank-and-file letters and individual casualty records were not retrieved. [5, 7, 8, 12]
