# The Magnolias and the rooms behind Aberdeen’s portico

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP17*

## 2. Story

At 732 West Commerce Street, the Sykes household occupied an 1850 timber-frame house behind an unusually wide two-story gallery. William Alfred Sykes was a physician and planter. The state inventory and the original West Commerce Street district nomination identify the house and its lot. The nomination’s surveyor called out the upper balcony’s “sheaves of wheat” balustrade. It is a small observation beside the scale of the front, but it identifies the work required to make a repeated ornamental pattern at the door. The surviving record names the owner much more readily than it names the people who shaped the pieces or did the work inside the house. [2–5]

The nomination recorded a detached kitchen and well house as separate contributing features and an additional outbuilding as noncontributing. The kitchen and water supply help reconstruct a household divided among several structures. That is a firmer basis for explaining how the property functioned than identifying a particular cook or claiming that each surviving outbuilding had an unchanging use. Who performed the labor, their legal status, and who built the house remain open questions in this file. The original schedule or property-level document needed to name anyone was not retrieved. [4][5]

James Butters arrived with a HABS camera in 1936, when the record called the property the W. B. Walker House. He took an oblique front view and made separate numbered photographs of the stair hall and living room. The house later came under municipal ownership. The operator credits Clarence Day with purchasing it in 1984 and giving it to the city. Read the name on Butters’s survey beside the name now used for tours. These names chart successive moments in ownership and preservation, and the survey makes an earlier condition of the surviving building visible. [2][4][6][7]

## 3. Then and now

The house’s lot is still the historical one. The city and state use The Magnolias or Dr. William Alfred Sykes House. The Library of Congress uses W. B. Walker House for the same 1936 subject. The original district nomination describes a house and surviving dependencies at the time of its survey. Their current individual interior and lawn access remains unverified. [2–7]

## 4. Surviving features and access

- **Operated interior.** The operator advertises scheduled tours. Its direct contact page requires calling for an appointment. This dossier supplies no unconfirmed public opening. [1][2]
- **Historical house and outbuildings.** The HABS and district records document the gallery, stair hall, living room, kitchen and well house. Use their records for remote reading. [5–7]
- **Exterior boundary unresolved.** Municipal ownership alone does not establish a public lawn, driveway or a signed exterior stopping place. [3][4]

## 5. Public progression and strongest story moment

Research reading. Compare the 1936 HABS front elevation with the nomination’s description of the side structures. **Strongest story moment — The house beyond the front stairs.** The front recorded the owner’s public address. The separate kitchen and well put the work that sustained the household back into the picture. [5–7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The operator’s about page says daily tours. Its contact page says call for an appointment. The Aberdeen civic listing says Monday–Friday or by appointment. The more specific instruction on the house’s own contact page controls planning unless staff confirm a scheduled ordinary opening. [1–3]
- The exact architects, builders, workers and residents tied to the detached kitchen remain unverified. The 1850 federal schedule was not retrieved at this address. Use the files read for design and ownership only. [4][5][8]
- The browser-oriented LOC photo page was inaccessible on the first request. The LOC item’s JSON and exact image file were recovered directly; this kept the repository credit and frame linked. The 1960s Sam Collier photograph remains held because a public-domain basis was not verified. [6][9][10]
