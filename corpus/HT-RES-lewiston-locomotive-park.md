# Locomotive Park: the logging engine that stayed in Lewiston

History Travel home-ring draft for Dash QC · Checked November 18, 2026 Pacific · Public park locator documented; match-point and hunt not checked onsite.

## 1. The story

Since retirement, a working machine has become a landmark on Main Street. The visitor bureau identifies it as Potlatch Forests Engine 92, the company's last logging locomotive in use. It dates the engine to 1924 and Potlatch's acquisition to 1942. This is one of the valley's best first stops because the machine is still there, while much of the system that made its work possible must be read in photographs, timetables and traces on the ground. [A, B, C]

The small engine number tells a bigger story. An archival picture indexed by the University of Idaho identifies a Heisler 92 near a shop, still serviceable. A specialist photo collection pairs an archival photograph cataloged as a builder's view of Ohio Match No. 1 with a working-life view captioned PFI 92 at Headquarters, Idaho. These tie the machine to different jobs and places; the builder image is an attribution, not an independently checked factory register. The later park display tells of its retirement, not of where those historic camera stands were. [C, D]

Rather than treat the rails as a decorative backdrop, explain the job: getting logs from the woods into a mill system. Potlatch's December 1941 company publication reports timber moving in the region via both waterways and rail flatcars. It is a contemporary employer source, not neutral worker testimony and not proof that Engine 92 hauled the shipment described. The period employee timetable similarly documents operating rules at East Lewiston and the mill crossing; it illustrates the larger transportation system, not the logging engine's assignment. [E, F]

Engine 92 is still here, but there is an honest ambiguity in how it got into public hands. The visitor bureau says the city bought it for $1 after retirement. A rail historian says it was donated in 1963. The city park listing does not settle either wording. Before converting either version into a definitive plaque caption, find the acquisition record. [A, B, G]

## 2. Then / now and photo asset plan

- **Then:** University of Idaho, Hal Riegger Papers, catalog item **riegger70**, *Train Engine Standing Near Shop*. Indexed description explicitly says Heisler 92. The full item and image could not be independently opened in this pass. Do not infer exact date, place, rights or photographer position. [C]
- **Alternate historic leads:** privately hosted archival photographs of Ohio Match No. 1 and Potlatch 92. The host lists the Headquarters picture's date and photographer as unknown. Verify engine identification and photo rights independently; do not embed those scans merely because they can be viewed. [D]
- **Now:** park display at 2102 Main Street. The city verifies the park address, and the local visitor bureau identifies the exhibit. No dated field frame was supplied. Take a current photo from an authorized public path with the locomotive number, boiler, cab and a wheel assembly visible if unobstructed. [A, B]
- **Correct comparison type:** same **object**, different **place**. An archive-to-present slider can compare the machine from similar framing, with both locations labeled. Do not offer “Stand Here” as if the shop photograph was taken in the park. Exact historical bearing, photo point, date and rights remain blank.

## 3. Visit sequence and strongest moment

Start at the City of Lewiston's **Locomotive Park, 2102 Main Street**. Use signs and lawful public parking, and approach on the designated park path. City-listed amenities include restrooms; current opening or accessibility at a given hour was not field-checked. Allow 10–20 minutes as a planning estimate. Seasonal displays and setup can change paths and sightlines. Regional tourism lists the 2026 Winter Spirit opening ceremony for November 21 at 5 p.m. Pacific; the suggested field photo is a daylight visit, not a direction to shoot during an evening crowd. [A, H]

First read the number, then compare the display's surroundings with the archived working-life setting. The story lands when you see that the engine itself survived even though the worksite in the old photograph is somewhere else. If access is restricted or seasonal barriers obscure the object, skip the photo and keep the source link for later. Do not climb the engine, manipulate its equipment, enter the railway corridor, or assume a proposed connection to the levee exists. [A, I]

## 4. Candidate onsite observation

From an approved public path, record which side exposes the engine number and a visible wheel assembly in the same frame. How is the engine separated from the path today? The answer is a current observation, not a number obtained from a database. Keep scoring and coordinate navigation disabled until the approach, visibility and answer are checked in daylight. No collecting, contact or climbing.
