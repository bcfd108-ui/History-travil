# Jacksonport State Park, White River port
HT-RES-AR-jacksonport-steamboat-workers · Arkansas OP03 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

A steamboat was unloading freight at Jacksonport in early June 1861 when military authority interrupted a civilian run. In an affidavit summarized by an 1872 Senate claims report, former owner Abner Baird placed the Admiral on the Memphis–Jacksonport carrying trade after selling it to Ezekiel Lewis. The committee’s record says it was “unloading freight at the latter place” when Colonel Board demanded the boat. In that account Lewis refused and left; Baird subsequently took charge under orders. The brief phrase about freight survives in federal print as the committee’s summary of sworn evidence. The original affidavit was not separately retrieved. [2]

The report also preserves evidence from James McCain, who had served as chief engineer. He knew the boat by his work on it. The committee describes him bringing it into federal possession in April 1862 and serving with his original crew for another month. Minerva Lewis, the owner’s widow and estate administrator, pursued compensation. The report distinguishes the base sale amount, the debts assumed and several valuations. It says whether Lewis paid those assumed debts did not appear in the evidence. The competing sums measure different things, and an affidavit prepared for the claim cannot identify the people working the 1861 landing. [2]

Today the distinctive courthouse draws the eye from the park. It was completed in 1872, later than the unloading at the center of this account, and it tells a separate story about the civic work made possible by river commerce. The visitor center is the place to read the packet that makes a captain, a widow and an engineer visible. For the people carrying the freight, the strongest surviving detail in the packet remains the fact that the steamer was in mid-unload. [1–4]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The historical landing was in Jacksonport on the White River. No exact berth is established for the Admiral. The later courthouse and the modern visitor center are identified public anchors for context. [1–4]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Jacksonport State Park visitor center, 111 Avenue St., Newport mailing address. Drive to the signed visitor area and use the short open approach. If the courthouse is open, follow staff directions via designated park paths. Stay back from the riverbank, ramp, campground-only areas and low paths where high water or weather affects access. [1]

## 5. Public progression

Start at the visitor gallery, or use the signed open approach for a daylight exterior reading. Compare the postwar courthouse with the 1861 claim. **Strongest story moment · The freight was still coming off.** Slow down there with the report’s one short clause, and consider the work that went unfinished or unrecorded. Treat wartime compulsion as quiet witness. [1–4]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The claim petition gives May or June 1861. Baird’s account places the interruption in early June; his account also describes a release and a later impressment, while the petition summarizes continuous Confederate use through April 1862. The report does not supply an exact day or berth. It presents conflicting sale/valuation figures and different levels of detail on the loss. [2]
- The park overview gives 1852 for county-seat status. Its historical courthouse booklet places the move to Jacksonport in July 1853, and the state encyclopedia describes it as the 1854 choice. The surviving courthouse date of 1872 is separate. [1, 3, 7]
- A park bird-list brochure gives 205 Avenue St. and references an earlier exhibit; the current public location listing gives 111 Avenue St. Use the latter for the visitor center. No exact 1861 cargo manifest or image of the Admiral at Jacksonport was established. [1, 2, 5]
