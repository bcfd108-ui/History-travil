# Big Spring near Van Buren — The stone was cut by hand

Missouri OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

The park was already a place for visitors when Civilian Conservation Corps Company 1710 arrived in June 1933. Missouri had established Big Spring State Park in 1924. The company pitched an early camp in Spring Hollow and worked within an existing pattern of access, water and visitor facilities. Flooding appears in the older archive photographs and threatened access. Work on paths, walls and bridges changed how people could get through and use the park. Company 1710 stayed for more than four years. Companies 734 and 1740 also worked at Big Spring. [1, 6–8]

A reproduced 1933 plan identifies the work of landscape architect Robert Jones on a bridge over Big Spring Branch. The NPS study identifies Leo Anderson as the stonemason who directed work on the quarried stone. Its archive caption says Anderson “oversaw CCC enrollees and directed stone cutting and dressing.” That is the report’s exact identification, distinct from an enrollee’s speech. In the photograph, the blocks and men occupy the same work area, but the frame alone cannot assign a name to a face. Pair that scene with the timber photograph directly above it. Both make the hours of preparation apparent before a finished architectural feature enters the record. [6]

The companies’ labor went well beyond a lodge. The NPS plan and photographs record site drainage, grading, stonework, crossings and the reworking of an already-used landscape. A proposal’s drawing records an intended solution. A construction photograph records a particular stage. The public grounds show the accumulated results of early state work, CCC work and later federal management. The state transferred Big Spring and two other parks to NPS in 1969. [1, 6–8]

## 3. Then and now

**Historical location.** CCC work is distributed across the district near the Current River, around Big Spring Branch, former camp areas and lodge precinct. A quarry or early tent-camp photo is not a visitor locator. The NPS register district extends beyond today's short public approach. [6–7]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive to the signed NPS Big Spring parking area on the posted state route 103 approach south of Van Buren. The NPS describes a short accessible walkway from parking toward the spring. Use that short designated public approach when open. The Entrance Station is a separate optional drive-up indoor starting point when staffed. Keep to posted public surfaces, excluding construction grounds, old camp locations, riverbank and off-path stonework. [1–5]

## 5. A short visit

Check conditions and stop at the Entrance Station if staffed. From the designated Big Spring lot, use the open public accessible walkway. Finish with **The stone yard behind the visitor path**, reading the reproduced record of stone cutting and bridge design while taking in the spring corridor from the public path. If the walk is closed or icy, use the staffed station or the remote record. Leave the lodge turnoff out of the progression while the project area remains closed. [1–6]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The Big Spring visitor page still contains promotional restaurant text followed by an explicit lodge and cabin closure notice. The restoration update distinguishes building work from a public opening and describes a future concession. Use the explicit restrictions. The fall/winter page describes general year-round outdoor access without overriding a posted safety or project closure. [1–5]

The NPS survey records Company 1710’s long assignment and shorter separate company work. A company photograph and other photos in a state collection do not prove that all pictured enrollees belonged to the same company. The Smithsonian item-level viewer for Company 1710 returned HTTP 403 in the web reader. Its catalog establishes a lead, and originals of the camp enrollment or individual correspondence were not obtained. An item-level date and photographer for the Anderson captioned image are still required for adoption. [6, 9–11]

## 7. Historical photo records

**Candidate held.** NPS, Ozark National Scenic Riverways Archives, Cultural Landscape Report figure 2-27, printed p. 2-30. The actual reproduction shows men in a clearing among dressed and irregular stones, with trees and standing work surfaces behind. The caption associates the work with Leo Anderson. The print’s original accession, maker, reuse basis, and precise position are unverified. Credit available for research reference only: *Ozark National Scenic Riverways Archives, Cultural Landscape Report figure 2-27, photographer unknown, undated.* Figure 2-26 on the same page was also inspected.
