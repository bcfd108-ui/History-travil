# Maramec Spring — The ironworks began in a daybook

Missouri OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

In Ford Hughes’s printed account of examining a Massey and James daybook dated September 5, 1826, one entry reads “cutting out Potosi Road.” The line is quoted through Hughes’s transcription. He reports a $10 labor amount. He also reports a purchase of shoes by George Waggoner, cloth purchases by Edward Wilson and George Cole, and a payment recorded to Abel and James Benton for corn. The same book that accounts for a road tells what people bought and what the company needed brought in. These names establish recorded transactions. They establish neither company employment for each customer nor where each person lived. [4]

This was an ironworks with a settlement around it. The company needed ore, wood, charcoal, labor, provisions and haulage. Hughes describes boat shipments transferring to wagons or mule trains, and people exchanging produce or labor for supplies. The trade lets the story begin before the furnace could cast anything. The state historical society’s records group includes separate ironworks accounts and correspondence, and it catalogs ironworks scrip separately. The finding aids establish the existence of those records. They supply no entitlement to read a payment as cash or a wage until the actual entry can be checked. [4–6]

The 1977 *Bittersweet* account describes early partners Thomas James and Samuel Massey and the local materials, including the spring and its flow. Its account gives context for what a working furnace needed. Its narrative claims about individual workers and practices remain later local history. The official Register treats the works as a district with significance in industry, commerce and settlement. The park now exhibits material recovered from the works and models of the buildings where the processes took place. [2, 7–8]

## 3. Then and now

**Historical location.** The works occupied the spring-valley district south of St. James. The name Maramec is retained for the park and the official listed district. The river appears as Meramec in modern sources and in period usage. Some working surfaces, channels and structures survive as ruins. Other industrial spaces are known through records and models. The 1826 daybook gives a trade connection to Potosi, without placing a specific worker’s house or the road work at a visitor waypoint. [2, 4, 7–9]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Driver entrance and public parking at Maramec Spring Park, reached via Missouri Route 8. Use the Reception Building and the park’s signed paths, including the visitor approach toward the Maramec Museum and the visible ruins wherever open. The experience is a drive-up and short walk inside the park. Stay on the posted walking surface and outside marked ruin, water, construction and service boundaries. [1–3]

## 5. A short visit

Start at the Reception Building and consult the operator about an open short walking line. Add the museum when open and use its site model to see how buildings related to the water. Continue on the signed visitor path to the visible furnace ruins in passable weather. Finish with **The road enters the ledger**, reading Hughes’s short road-work entry alongside the corn and shoes. The remains show what was built. The daybook shows the people and supplies the works had to bring together. If ice or a closure affects the path, keep the reading inside the open Reception Building or remote. [1–4]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The Register highlights 1826 and 1876. Hughes’s September 1826 record concerns early business at the camp and the start of the undertaking. Those dates should not be treated as furnace commissioning or a worker’s last shift without the dated operating books. The original waste-book page and the named customers’ residences were not retrieved. C0001 and C1515 are catalog records. The Missouri geology raw PDF returned HTTP 503 with 85 bytes, and one NPGallery nomination download resolved to a one-page placeholder. The Missouri State Parks copy returned HTML instead of PDF bytes. The surviving nomination evidence in this pass is the federal listing metadata. The dated original scrip and payroll entries remain open. [4–10, 12–13]
