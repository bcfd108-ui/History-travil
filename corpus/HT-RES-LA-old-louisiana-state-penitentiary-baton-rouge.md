# Old Louisiana State Penitentiary Site (Warden's House / Prison Store), Baton Rouge — 1835–1925 urban penitentiary and surviving 1838–39 brick office

**Research draft · Labeled THEME SWEEP (Family 1 — Prisons, penitentiaries, jails · Updated to street-exterior ANCHORED at 703 Laurel St.) · Baton Rouge, East Baton Rouge Parish, Louisiana · November 21, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate

**SITE STATUS & ANCHOR UPDATE:** While the main walled **Old Louisiana State Penitentiary** compound in downtown Baton Rouge (`317' × 714'` bounded by Laurel, 7th, Florida, and 9th streets) was sold and demolished after 1906–1925, **one original structure survives across the street**: the **1838–1839 Warden's House / Louisiana State Prison Store** at **703 Laurel Street** (`701–05 Laurel St.`, corner of N. 7th St.). [1, 2]

The Warden's House was adaptively restored in **1966** as private commercial/architectural offices and remains **privately occupied—no public museum interior access** exists. However, the building stands flush along the public downtown sidewalk with its exterior historic marker visible from the right-of-way. [1]

**Anchor ruling: ANCHORED (public sidewalk exterior & exterior plaque only at the surviving 1838–39 Warden's House, 703 Laurel St.; interior private offices and redeveloped former prison yard blocks excluded).**

## 2. The story

Authorized by the Louisiana Legislature in **1834** and opened in **1835** on what were then the eastern outskirts of Baton Rouge, the **Old Louisiana State Penitentiary** was Louisiana's first state prison—a walled brick compound spanning roughly **317 by 714 feet** across the blocks now bounded by **Laurel, 7th, Florida, and 9th streets** (`1835–1917` primary operations, with final city processing continuing into `1925`). [1, 2, 3]

During the antebellum and post–Civil War decades, Baton Rouge penitentiary inmates manufactured bricks and textiles inside the walls and were also hired or leased out onto nearby plantations and public works until Federal occupation freed prisoners during the Civil War; post-war **convict leasing** under private contractors (`1869–1901`) eventually shifted state inmates to Major S. L. James's operations and then to **Angola** after 1901. [1, 3]

By **Act 105 of the 1837 Louisiana Legislature**, the state purchased the lot across 7th Street (`703 Laurel St.`) and appropriated **$15,000** to erect a **two-story brick Prison Store and clerk's/warden's residence**—constructed **1838–1839** using **prison-made soft mud bricks** laid in running bond with soldier-course lintels, native **heart-cypress** framing, decorative brick gable parapets, and an attached two-story east wing. [1]

After **Act 189 of 1906** authorized selling the main Baton Rouge penitentiary grounds to finance construction at the Angola state farm, the walled prison across the street was demolished (`1917–1925`), while the Warden's House continued briefly for prisoner intake/processing until **1925**. It later served commercially and as a WWII **USO lodge** under American Legion Nicholson Post #38 before architects **John J. Desmond and Lewis E. Miremont** acquired and restored it in **1966** (later recorded by the Historic American Buildings Survey as **HABS LA-1140** and listed on the **National Register of Historic Places** in **1974**). [1, 2]

## 4. What's still there

- **Visible from public sidewalk (`703 Laurel St.`):** Complete exterior of the **1838–1839 Classical Revival Warden's House / Prison Store**—prisoner-made red/orange brick walls, corbelled brick cornice, decorative gable parapets with semicircular attic fanlight, paired transomed double store doors, and exterior historical plaque. [1, 4]
- **Private / non-public:** Interior exposed heart-cypress framing and private office rooms; east courtyard behind shortened brick wall. [1]
- **Gone:** Main `317' × 714'` prison walls, cellblocks, and factories across N. 7th St. (demolished early 20th century). [1, 3]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Walk along Laurel Street to the corner of N. 7th Street (`703 Laurel St.`)** in downtown Baton Rouge on the public city sidewalk. [1]
2. **Inspect the 1838–39 prison-brick facade and historic plaque** from the sidewalk, noting the twin double-door **Prison Store** entrance (`west bays`) and the separate **Warden's/Clerk's** paneled residence door (`east bay`). [1, 4]
3. **Look southeast across N. 7th Street toward Laurel/Florida** to orient the vanished `317' × 714'` walled penitentiary footprint before departing (do not enter private offices at 703 Laurel St.). [1]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Closure year (`1917` vs. `1925`):** USACE Baton Rouge history notes the Baton Rouge prison operated **until 1917**; the 1974 NRHP nomination notes the complex was vacated/moved by **1925** after intermediate prisoner processing at the Warden's House. Both milestones fit a phased 1917–1925 wind-down after Act 189 of 1906. [1, 3]
- **NRHP reference number:** The scanned 1974 NRHP nomination (`NPGallery d50f15f5-7d32-4a5b-b6f0-5332a564e7af`) carries nomination signature date April 19, 1974, without a typed 8-digit NRIS code on the scanned page. [1]
- **Historic archaeology:** `Hard Labor: History and archaeology at the Old Louisiana State Penitentiary, Baton Rouge, Louisiana` exists in Wikimedia Commons PDF archives but was not fully parsed this run.
