## 2. The story
**One-minute hook:** A community’s history can be carried through a daughter interviewing her mother, not just through a demographic chart or an exhibit label.

**Named firsthand family account:** *A Hmong Mother’s Journey to Fresno*, published by Community Alliance on 2 March 2015, credits interview and translation to Chonny Vang and identifies the speaker as her mother, Zoua Vang. The publication says it reprints the kNOw Youth Media interview with permission. Zoua recalls arriving in Fresno in 1994 and prioritizing her children’s schooling despite having been denied educational opportunities herself. Her short statement, “Education is the key to a better life,” captures her own emphasis. This is a translated, edited published family testimony, not a claim about every Hmong household or a literal unedited recording. [5]

The account contains painful personal experiences. This dossier does not reproduce intimate details, locations, finances or family allegations as visitor material. Reading respectfully does not require extracting everything a narrator disclosed.

**A different local voice:** StoryCorps catalogs Lisa Herrick and Lee Herrick’s conversation recorded 1 March 2020, duration 35:21, identifier **mby019713**. Its description concerns their creative lives in Fresno and Lisa’s Hmong-American identity. The published subject log places her discussion of ancestry at 05:41 and writing at 26:17. These are repository-provided navigation cues, not timestamps verified by listening in this pass. No verbatim statement is invented for either participant. [6]

**Interpretive synthesis:** Neither a single family’s migration nor a single person’s search for family history is a complete history of the diaspora. Together these sources support a narrower, stronger story: people in Fresno have actively recorded, translated and shaped how family experience is carried forward. The visitor’s job is to recognize authorship and limits, not demand a representative story from every person encountered.
