# Mason City — The house the drawing became

Iowa OP03 · Research draft for Dash

## 2. Story

**A published plan and a doctor's house.** Frank Lloyd Wright’s magazine project bore the title “A Fireproof House for $5,000”. The words are the design’s original title as transmitted in the National Register research, and the title describes Wright’s published concept. The Stockmans commissioned a home and there were material changes between the design on paper and the house built for them in 1908. The federal nomination describes frame construction and stucco. A visitor can see the compact outline and the way the roof, window bands and projecting elements sit against it. Those surviving features help tell the story of translating a drawing into a particular house. The title supplies neither a local building cost nor a fire-resistance finding. [2–5]

**A succession of owners.** Dr. George and Eleanor Stockman are the clients identified by the operator. The nomination documents a 1919 sale to O'Lear at $11,000 and associates a later owner, George Laird, with the mayoralty in 1922. The sale figure belongs to the transfer, not to the 1908 building contract. When the site was later threatened with redevelopment, preservation depended on moving the building itself. The surviving rooms and their appearance at the present site reflect that second piece of work as well. [2–4]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** 311 First Street SE, the street address still displayed in the federal listing. The old address is part of the property record and is excluded from this visit. [2–4]

**Lawful public anchor.** Architectural Interpretive Center, 520 First Street NE, for scheduled public tours. The house is next door at 530 First Street NE. For exterior-only viewing use the First Street NE public sidewalk, with no route into garden or porch outside posted access. The operator says that the house moved here in 1989. [1, 2]

## 4. Surviving features and access

- **Public exterior view** — low roof, grouped upper windows and projecting porch structure from the street side when the sidewalk is open and passable. [1–3, 6]
- **Ticketed public interior** — the operator limits tours to eight. Winter hours are separate from its private appointment option. Interior photography is prohibited. Use only tour-authorized rooms. [1]
- **Source only** — historic parcel and archival plans. [3–5]

## 5. A short visit

Compare Wright's design title with the actual silhouette from the street. On a booked public tour, let the operator take the visit through the house’s room arrangement. Land on **The drawing became a wooden house**, looking again at the roof and the wall finish from the public exterior. During inclement weather or outside the schedule, read the nomination remotely. [1, 3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- Operator says 1989 for the move. SAH Archipedia gives 1988. The accessible records do not identify a separate planning, relocation and reopening date sequence to explain the difference. [2, 5]
- The nomination places the magazine design in April 1907. SAH Archipedia gives April 1906. The architectural press reprint also identifies April 1907. Wright’s complete magazine original, construction ledger and contractor's contract were not read. [3, 5, 7]
- The federal address predates the move. Use the operator address for the present house. [1, 4]
