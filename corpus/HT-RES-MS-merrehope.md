# Merrehope and the women who made the house public

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP13 · Labeled research draft*

## 2. Story

In the early 1970s, architectural historian Dawn Maddox traced the place now called Merrehope through deeds, interviews and fabric. The 1863 warranty deed calls one building “the dwelling formerly built by W. H. Jackson.” That is a short quotation from Maddox's transcription in the nomination. A later deed associated the earlier house with Confederate General Joseph E. Johnston's temporary presence in Meridian. A document that places a particular building on this property is a starting point for the story. Matching that building to timber still standing is a different question. [3, 4]

John and Eliza Gary held the property by the late 1860s. They sold it to J. C. Lloyd in 1881. Maddox associated the main block with Gary and questioned whether the rear attachment was the earlier Jackson house. The operator identifies an “Original Cottage” in its display, while the National Register author points to a competing description in W. G. Croom's 1882 history that suggests a building on the *site* of Lloyd's home may have been replaced. Under S. H. Floyd the house acquired its early twentieth-century appearance. In several rooms, a visitor can read successive decisions in the same doorway, window or wall surface. [3–6]

By the twentieth century the building housed a number of tenants. The operator names Otto Tibbetts as the owner who made eight apartments after 1945 and identifies a parlor changed to living quarters and a library used as common space. The nomination describes conversion across 1914–1968, and identifies a coalition of nine women's clubs with 201 members buying the building in 1968. It credits donated labor, expertise and supplies from residents, schools, businesses and local government. Maddox interviewed Foundation president Mrs. R. M. Hamill in 1971. The respondent's own words are not available in the published nomination, but the named interview and the preservation work documented there place the women inside the history of this building. Today the operator's founding account remembers five women discussing a place where their clubs could meet. That is institutional memory with no contemporaneous conversation record. [2, 3, 5, 7]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The McLemore–Jackson, Gary, Lloyd and Floyd property at what was 905 31st Avenue. The original dwelling, later villa and remodeled house share a property history. The identity of the surviving rear fabric is disputed. [3, 4, 6]

**Present public anchor.** Merrehope house museum, 905 Martin Luther King Jr. Drive. Drive to the signed visitor approach and use the staffed ticket entrance and docent route during posted hours. The exterior may be seen from the street's open public approach when the museum is closed. Private events, unused rooms and neighboring properties are outside the boundary. [1–4]

## 5. Public progression

Begin with the porch elevation and look at how it meets the projecting bays. Take the advertised guided interior visit if open. Ask staff to identify the room formerly divided into apartments and the part of the route interpreted as the early cottage. End at **The ruby glass at the threshold**, where a recognizable piece of surviving fabric closes a story of owners, tenants and women who organized to keep the house in use. During the holiday event, decoration and the experience of a traditional architecture tour may differ. [1–3, 5, 6]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The state inventory gives circa 1858 for the first house. The 1971 nomination gives an 1859 deed and house context, with a directly quoted 1863 reference. The 1988 district inventory also gives 1858. The deed images were not retrieved independently. [3, 4, 6]
- The operator identifies the cottage as original. The 1971 survey uses the word “debatable” for that physical attribution, and Croom's wording as reproduced by Maddox can indicate replacement. An original Croom directory scan and building-fabric dating evidence were unavailable. [3, 5]
- Maddox calls the present arrangement extensively remodeled in 1904. The state gives an 1881 Italianate phase and uses a c.1904 remodel, while the district entry puts Gary's expansion in the context of 1868. Purchase, building campaign and completed work cannot yet be assigned to a common chronology. The operator describes 20 rooms and the Society of Architectural Historians account gives 26. The room-count scope is unknown. [2–6, 8]
- The operator's contact page gives opening in 1968, while its detailed chronology says tours began in the 1970s. The 1968 acquisition is documented in the nomination. The exact first regular admission date and members' records remain open. The tour calendar controls current access over broader operator descriptions. [1–3, 5, 7]
