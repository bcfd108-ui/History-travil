# Springfield Route 66 — The address that meant a room

Missouri OP03 · Draft for independent Dash review · Quiet witness at the lost hotel

## 2. Story

**A few words in a travelers’ guide made a destination.** The 1955 *Negro Travelers’ Green Book*, p. 32, lists “Alberta’s Hotel” and “617 No. Benton” under Springfield. Those lines were read on the printed page image. The entry is an address for that edition’s readers. It does not tell us how many travelers came, how long they stayed or whether a room was free. In the context of segregated travel, it gave someone on the road the name of a place to seek lodging. [4–6]

**A woman’s business and a young bellhop.** Alberta Northcutt Ellis ran the hotel. Her grandson Irv Logan Jr. recalled living and working there. A 2015 interview transcribed by Teresa Bergen identifies his bellhop work and family help. In a separate interview later aired by KSMU he remembered, “you never knew who was going to come.” These are two later memories by someone who worked in the business. They belong alongside the printed address, which establishes the hotel was advertising to travelers by 1955. [5–7]

**Planning a trip was part of the labor.** The heritage trail describes a lodging and service business in a three-story building. Relatives helped keep the place going. Ellis also developed a different property known as The Farm outside Springfield, which should not be confused with the Benton Avenue hotel. A guest register from about 1956 is identified in the Logan Family Collection. It is evidence of a business recording guests, with specific entries and counts unverified here. [3, 5–7]

## 3. Then and now

The historical location is **Alberta’s Hotel, 617 North Benton**, as printed in 1955. The community trail and KSMU describe the former site as a parking lot between the Jordan Valley Community Health Center and Springfield Municipal Court. No lawful route onto the former parcel, parking lot or health-center property was established. Do not position a visitor at a former door inferred from the listing. [3–6]

The lawful anchor is **History Museum on the Square and its public Park Central Square frontage**, 154 Park Central Square. The museum gives a downtown Route 66 interpretation, distinct from the hotel building. Use a signed downtown public parking area and a short public sidewalk walk to the museum entrance. The dossier can be read from the open square or remotely when the museum is closed. [1–2]

## 5. A short visit

Start at the public square during suitable weather. If open, use the museum’s Route 66 interpretation. Finish from the public square or museum with **The address that meant a room**. Read the 1955 address and the bellhop’s recollection. This is where the listed address becomes the place a traveler might rely on. Stay with the off-site anchor, or read the source page remotely. ## 6. Gaps and conflicts

Logan’s 2015 interview places the business start around 1956 or 1957 while explicitly allowing his estimate could be off. The original 1955 issue establishes an earlier listing, and the heritage trail and state society report listings from 1954. The exact opening day and the first listed edition were not independently verified in this pass. KSMU and the heritage trail’s public search excerpts were usable, but the full trail page returned web-tool 403 and raw HTTP 403. The extended oral history was reviewed as a transcript with a research-use notice. Exact historic building footprint and a repeatable current photographer stance remain held. [3–7]

## 7. Historical photo records
