# Effigy Mounds — Whose history the park was entrusted to hold

## 2. Story

### Before the monument

The earthen works in this part of the Upper Mississippi Valley long predate the National Park Service. The register includes much more time than the Late Woodland effigy-building period described on the NPS interpretive page. Those different scopes matter. A landscape contains earthworks, places of burial and later history together. Interpretive labels cannot assign a single builder or date to the entire place. NPS lists multiple culturally associated Nations. [4–5]

### Preservation, and a breach of trust

The 2022 joint statement hosted by the Iowa Tribe of Kansas and Nebraska dates the monument’s founding to October 25, 1949. In 1990, according to the Park Service’s investigative record, the superintendent removed ancestral remains from the park’s collections and kept them outside the institution for more than two decades. The Park Service reports a July 2016 federal sentence. The court’s findings are a specific historical record of harm by an office entrusted with care. Discuss it quietly without reproducing burial information or converting loss into a visitor activity. [6–7]

### A Tribal government speaks for itself

On November 22, 2022, the Iowa Tribe of Kansas and Nebraska and the National Park Service announced a Tribal Sister Park agreement. Chairman Timothy Rhodd described educational work for the Tribe and public representatives. Lance Foster, the Tribe’s vice chairman and historic preservation officer, said, “We look forward to a period of healing.” In the Tribe-hosted original, he placed the agreement in a difficult history between the Park Service and Tribes. This statement is the source for his words. It expresses one Nation’s position on this agreement. [6,8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicting records

- The register’s archaeological period fields cover multiple eras. The NPS interpretive page describes a more specific effigy-building period. Neither provides a verified age for a specific unseen earthwork. [4–5]
- The date in the federal register is October 15, 1966. The Tribe-hosted announcement dates the monument’s establishment to October 25, 1949. Keep listing and creation as distinct actions. [4,6]
- The NPS sentencing release summarizes the case. The signed judicial documents and case-specific repatriation records were not independently retrieved, so the account stays at the documented agency actions and printed Tribe statements. [6–7]
- The attempted NPS history page at /learn/historyculture/tribes.htm was inaccessible to the retrieval tool. The published NPS moundbuilders page supplied the associated-Nations context. [5,9]
