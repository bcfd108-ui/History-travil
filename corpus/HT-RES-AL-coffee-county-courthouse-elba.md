# Elba’s Courthouse Square and the records that outlasted the floods

*On Old Ground · Alabama OP01 · Labeled research draft · Quiet reflection.*

## 2. Story
The 1903 courthouse on Elba’s square is the latest in a succession of county buildings. W. Warner Floyd’s original register nomination describes an 1852 vote won by Elba and a frame courthouse completed in 1853. That building burned during the Civil War. Another was replaced by a brick courthouse in the 1880s and the county built the present structure after its earlier accommodation fell short. The nomination gives the two-story walls, paired round towers and taller clock tower as the building’s architectural signatures. The original contractors and masons’ records remain open. [2, 3]

After the courthouse was built, the county established an eastern branch at Enterprise. The 1907 arrangement reduced a journey for residents of the eastern part of Coffee County while preserving Elba’s separate county work. The county’s current account describes how record books are split across the two divisions. The building gave a government a visible address. It also served as a place where clerks looked after accumulated deeds and records. [2, 3]

The county notes that “high water stains remain on the walls,” and says the present courthouse survived floods in 1929 and the 1990s. A 1929 Maxwell Field Air Corps photograph documents extensive inundation around Elba. The frame is landscape evidence. Its image alone cannot show which records remained dry, exactly how high water reached on the courthouse, or reliably identify the square. The county credits pre-flood microfilming with preserving legibility when books later became vulnerable to water and handling. [2, 4–6]

## 3. Then and now
The square remains the exterior setting for a working courthouse. Today the towers and clock can be read from ordinary town ground. The historic high-water evidence cited here belongs to the county’s record and the archival photograph, not to a public interior tour. No courthouse business, river stop or records-room trip is part of the entry. [1–6]

## 5. Public progression
Begin on the open public side of Courthouse Square. Look at the paired turrets and clock tower before reading the chronology of the changing buildings. End at **The Record Books Had a Second Life**. The county kept the damaged books, and its earlier microfilming made another copy available. From the square, think of a courthouse as both a visible landmark and a place that must preserve paper through emergencies. [1–6]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements
- The nomination and county put selection of Elba as county seat in 1852 and date the present courthouse to 1903. The city's historic walk summary compresses town movement and seat selection into its discussion of 1903. Treat the original seat vote, the location history of the settlement and building completion as separate questions. [1–3]
- The older nomination described the then-standing Enterprise courthouse as built in 1924; the county summary dates that building to 1925 and reports later replacement. This disagreement concerns the branch courthouse, not the Elba building. [2, 3]
- Ordinary indoor heritage access and building specialists’ names are unverified. The general state archive web item returned a JavaScript-only page in the text reader, the first image endpoint returned HTTP 501, and the official item API and corrected IIIF endpoint worked. [4–6]
