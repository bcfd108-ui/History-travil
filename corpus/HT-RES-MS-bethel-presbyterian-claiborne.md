# Bethel Presbyterian Church, rebuilt by a rural congregation

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP05 · November 20, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

The visitor can stand on the public sidewalk outside the parent congregation, First Presbyterian Church, 605 Church Street, Port Gibson. That downtown steeple carries the pointing hand. The subject of this record is Bethel, a separate rural building maintained by the congregation. It reports spring and fall worship there. No particular service date or ordinary access to the rural property was verified. [1–3]

## 2. Story

In 1824 the Rev. Samuel Hunter helped organize Bethel from members of the older Bayou Pierre congregation. Zebulon Butler later served Bethel and the congregation that moved into Port Gibson. A state charter of 1828 named William Young, Lewellin Price, John Magruder and Smith C. Daniell as Bethel trustees. Shared clergy and membership explain the lasting relationship between the churches. They do not place the buildings on the same site. [1,3]

## 3. Then and now

The congregation’s record places construction on Bethel’s present site during Joshua T. Russell’s pastorate, approximately 1837–1842. An independent preservation writer gives about 1829. The organization date and the state charter establish that the congregation existed earlier; neither establishes when the present-site structure was built. The difference remains open. [1,4]

## 4. Surviving features and access

A tornado on November 6, 1943, largely destroyed Bethel. Reconstruction took place between October 1944 and March 1945. Worshippers rededicated the church on Easter Sunday, April 1, 1945. Later maintenance depended on people who rarely get named in architectural histories. The church credits Mrs. W. R. Hammett with keeping records and accounts and serving as a local contact for visiting ministers, and deacon John H. Bare with substantial repair work in 1962. The same year Bethel’s members formally became an outpost of First Presbyterian. [1]

## 5. Public progression

From the downtown sidewalk, the gold hand is visible as an identifying feature of the parent church. Local craftsman Daniel Foley carved its early wooden predecessor. The church dates the present metal hand to about 1901. Bethel’s own story is the one preserved in the rural congregation’s records of reconstruction, reopening and care. [1,2]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

A 2013 account describes an interior visit to Bethel. It cannot establish present daily access. The operator’s posted seasonal worship is the narrower current evidence. An ordinary public interior being open downtown also would not grant entry at Bethel. [1,4]
