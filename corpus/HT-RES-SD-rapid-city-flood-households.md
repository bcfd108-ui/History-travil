## 2. The story
**First-person household source actually read:** Jim Kuehn's signed written memory, library item **549** [5]. The page is dated June 16, 2009, while the narrative explicitly speaks from the 25th anniversary in 1997. The historical event is June 1972; these are separate dates, not a contemporaneous diary.

Kuehn describes his household sheltering displaced people, including a mother and newborn, while his wife Yvonne continued hospital night work. He recalls ten unexpected guests, shared washing facilities and the strain of communicating with separated family. His newsroom perspective also makes him attentive to the decisions involved in reporting suffering. His account of Yvonne's work is a husband's testimony, not her independently recorded first-person voice. The narrative's positive judgment of redevelopment belongs to him, not every displaced household.

**Original interpretive voice:** Recovery is not only the clearing of a street. It enters a kitchen, a bathroom, a work shift and the effort to tell someone far away that a family is alive. The archive makes those ordinary spaces legible without turning the actual homes into attractions. Read for the work of caring, and leave room for the households whose losses cannot be folded into an uncomplicated success story.

## 3. Then / now
The city's Historic Preservation Commission explains that post-flood acquisition and open-space policy changed the urban landscape [6]. Its public account gives 238 deaths and connects present green space to recovery. This supplies civic context, not a license to visit a former residence or a claim that all survivors shared one experience.

The library has made later submitted memories, broadcasts and oral-history material available as distinct collections [7]. A later recollection can be firsthand evidence of the narrator's experience while still requiring attention to memory, editing and retrospective interpretation. The present reading room is a place to encounter that record, not the location of the events narrated.
