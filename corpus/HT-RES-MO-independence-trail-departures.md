# Independence — The wagon that left at four

Missouri OP01 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**Independence sold what travelers had missed.** Edwin Bryant reached town May 1, 1846 with R. T. Jacob and R. Ewing. After previous purchases in St. Louis, he still had, in his own words, a “long list of small articles necessary for the journey.” In his published text he identifies Wilson & Clarke as the store that supplied those items. Bryant gives no itemized receipt in this passage. He records paying on average $21.67 a yoke for three yokes of young oxen and explains that the party, which had intended to use mules, took cues from experienced travelers in the streets. Quotation checked against the contemporary printed edition, p. 14. [4]

**Brownell got the wagon moving.** May 4 had ended with duplicate axletrees, ox-bows and other fittings still promised. On May 5 Bryant found little done at the smith’s shop and stayed until the wagon was finished. He records an afternoon departure at four “under the guidance of Brownell the driver.” Bryant caught up after the wagon had traveled about a mile. The smith and the crew who readied the team made a continental journey possible one repair at a time. Brownell is named as driver in the printed diary. The same book describes hiring a driver and cook, but no independent employment record was retrieved. Quotation checked against the printed edition, pp. 18–19. [4]

**Several trails shared the business of departure.** Bryant describes wagon teams headed to Santa Fe while his own party planned for California. The city museum presents the Santa Fe, Oregon and California routes. That common supply story is documented by the traveler and operator. Treat Bryant's party as one particular itinerary rather than a template for all caravans. The author's racialized descriptions are evidence of the writer’s perspective. They do not speak for the New Mexican workers or Indigenous people he saw. [1, 4, 5]

## 3. Then and now

The historic location is the **1846 Independence outfitting town**, including the public square described by Bryant. The exact Wilson & Clarke storefront, the smith’s working address and Brownell’s first wheel track are unresolved. Modern buildings and street lines around the square cannot by themselves assign any of those historical positions. [4]

The lawful physical anchor is the **National Frontier Trails Museum in the Truman Memorial Building**, 416 W. Maple Ave. Use the signed Maple Avenue public entrance and ordinary municipal visitor access. The building is a later war memorial and community center. The museum is a present interpretation site separate from Bryant’s store or smithy. The city also documents the West Maple location in its municipal inventory. [1, 3]

## 5. A short visit

Walk from the public Maple Avenue approach to the museum. Read the portion of Bryant’s first chapter on oxen and small purchases alongside the museum’s open trail interpretation. Then read the opening of May 5. Finish with **The wagon that left at four**. The delay and Brownell’s departure make the work of the outfitting town concrete. Use the published scan remotely on a closed day or when the approach is unsafe. [1–4]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

NPS updates its museum address to 416 W. Maple yet still describes swales across from the museum and a quarter-mile paved path. A separate NPS place entry identifies preserved swales at 313 W. Pacific. The city confirms the Maple museum address. Use Maple for the museum and keep the swales off this itinerary until the land manager confirms a specific public approach. [1–3, 5]

Berkeley’s published scan returned HTTP 403 through web retrieval. A raw PDF request returned HTTP 200 with readable page images, including printed pp. 14 and 19. The checked copy is a contemporary printed edition, with the diary autograph, shop ledger and cadastral evidence still unlocated. A Nov. 3 city preservation bond includes the Truman Memorial Building and Bingham-Waggoner Estate. It establishes funding approval for work, with no basis here for a new public access schedule.
