## 2. The story
**Hook:** The enormous shovel is the surviving object. The work around it is the story.

Big Brutus is a Bucyrus Erie 1850B commissioned for Pittsburg & Midway. Its operator dates construction to 1962, retirement to 1974 and museum dedication to 1985. These are different milestones, not interchangeable opening dates. [2] Kansas Geological Survey places its operation in 1963–1974. It removed overlying earth in strip mining; it was not an underground coal-cutting machine. The survey describes southeastern Kansas's transition toward strip mining in the 1930s while underground work continued into 1960. [3]

A named regional worker helps prevent the machine from swallowing the human story. Kansas State's 1994 profile identifies John “Bud” Mackie Jr., raised in Scammon, as starting with Clemens Coal in 1940 and remembering descent into shaft mines. The profile later identifies him as company president. This is an institutionally published account of his recollection, **not a transcript or an eyewitness account of operating Big Brutus**. It supports a Cherokee County comparison between generations of coal work, not an invented assignment on this shovel. [4]

The state historical society describes volunteers restoring the donated machine as a museum. Preservation was another kind of work, undertaken after its commercial life ended. [5] Interpretive conclusion: the scale can draw a family in, but the people who worked, changed jobs and preserved the object should provide the ending.
