# San Antonio — land, water and the record at Mission San José
Research draft · Texas OP01 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

The English translation of the mission’s February 23, 1720 Writ of Possession begins with the lieutenant general’s account. He identified a place “where water can be drained from the San Antonio River to irrigate the land.” In the same record he named Juan of the Pampopas as governor, Nicolás of the Suliajame as judge and Alonzo of the Pastía as constable. These are names an official placed on paper. The translated document reports exchanges through the interpreter. Its first person is the lieutenant general and it cannot independently establish the consent or private views of all residents. [3]

The 1758 survey summarized by NPS counts 281 Indigenous residents, 84 stone homes and a set of working buildings, including a granary and mill. The 1794 record concerns a renewed effort to distribute farmland, livestock and tools. NPS’s site account dates complete secularization to February 29, 1824. The 1794 and 1824 dates describe different stages in the long transfer. The records show that water, maintenance and tools mattered as much as formal titles. [4–5]

The Tāp Pīlam Coahuiltecan Nation’s own public statement identifies contemporary descendants and their work of revitalizing language and culture. The institutional voice belongs with the colonial government records, which did not supply an unmediated community account. This visit is quiet witness at the NPS visitor center. [6]

## 3 · Then / now

Historic location: the Mission San José settlement, subsequent structures and working landscape. NPS describes substantial WPA restoration in the 1930s. Physical anchor: signed public parking and short walk into the NPS Visitor Center, with public orientation spaces only. The mission church, living religious space, burials and treaty or ceremonial sites are excluded. [1–2,5]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior: NPS visitor center exhibits and film when offered. Exterior structures can be discussed from authorized public orientation space where visible. NPS describes native family quarters, granary, mill and convento remains. Distinguish historic from restored fabric. No separate building entry claimed. [2,5]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

Check NPS alerts. Begin in the visitor center with the Nation’s published account. Read the Writ as an official’s narration, then compare the inventory of rooms and tools. **Strongest story moment — The first sentence about water.** Read the clause from the Writ before contemplating the named people and their work. [2–6]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

Original Spanish manuscript and Indigenous-authored first-person household accounts were not retrieved. The Writ was read in the NPS English translation. A 1936 HABS frame in LOC catalog returned an internal error in the direct item open. Subject claim at frame level and reuse remain held. [3,7]
