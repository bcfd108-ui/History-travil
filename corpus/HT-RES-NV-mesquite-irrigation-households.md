## 2. The story
**Francis Evans Hughes**, a Mesquite farmer, spoke with **Mark E. French on February 23, 1977**. In his household recollection he said, **“We packed the water in with a bucket”** for kitchen use. He also remembered his mother and girls in the family keeping flies away during meals. These passages distinguish his inclusive recollection of household effort from his observation of other family members' work. They do not establish an individual daily task allocation. [2]

The account gives irrigation history a domestic scale without turning the visitor toward a canal. A household needed more than a crop yield: someone had to make everyday meals possible. That is this draft's interpretive connection, not an engineering reconstruction or claim about current water service.

Hughes also described working within a family farming organization. His account is retrospective testimony, not a complete employment record. The inspected public transcript was minimally edited without the narrator participating in that editing; original audio was not heard. [2]

The strongest point is the labor between an available resource and an ordinary household routine. Do not romanticize that effort, perform it as a challenge, or repeat historical practices as present-day advice. This account is a settler family's perspective. It cannot stand in for Southern Paiute history or establish that the valley was empty before settlement. A Tribal-authored account would be needed to develop that dimension responsibly.
