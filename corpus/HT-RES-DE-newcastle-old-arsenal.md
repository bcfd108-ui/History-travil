# HT-RES-DE-newcastle-old-arsenal — The Old Arsenal on the Green: New Castle's 1809 federal arsenal turned school
Division DE seed-catalog • Research draft • Checked November 25, 2026

The brick building on New Castle's Green began in 1809–1811 as a one-story federal arsenal, storing munitions for the river batteries through the War of 1812. Around 1852 the town added a second story and cupola and made it a school; the building the 1936 HABS photograph shows is that enlarged form. It is now the headquarters of the New Castle Historical Society, inside the New Castle National Historic Landmark District.

## 1. Trip gate
The building stands on the historic Green in New Castle and is leased to the New Castle Historical Society. Exterior views are available from the public Green and streets. Interior access is typically during society hours, events, or by arrangement; confirm current status with the New Castle Historical Society or Delaware Division of Historical and Cultural Affairs before visiting.

## 2. The story
As tensions with Britain rose in the early 1800s, the U.S. government constructed coastal defenses and supply depots. The New Castle Arsenal was built in 1809–1811 on land associated with the town's Market Square/Green. It was a one-story brick structure with gable roof, attic, and wagon entrances at each end for loading munitions and equipment supplied from Philadelphia. It supported local river batteries during the War of 1812 and was used again in the Mexican-American War period. In 1831 it briefly housed the garrison from Fort Delaware after a fire there. A cholera (or yellow fever) epidemic in the early 1830s also saw temporary medical use.

By the 1840s the town restricted further military storage without consent. Around 1852 the Trustees of the New Castle Common added a second story and cupola and converted the building into the New Castle Institute (later high school), which operated until about 1930. Subsequent uses included WPA-related remodeling in the 1930s, offices, a restaurant, and finally restoration. It is now the headquarters of the New Castle Historical Society and part of the New Castle National Historic Landmark District.

## 3. Then / now
- 1809–1811: federal arsenal constructed.
- 1812–1840s: military storage, War of 1812 and Mexican War support, temporary barracks.
- 1852: second story and cupola added; converted to school.
- ca. 1930: school use ends.
- 1930s–2010s: various civic, office, and commercial uses; restorations.
- Today: New Castle Historical Society headquarters; exterior retains the enlarged 19th-century form.

## 4. What's still there
The brick building with its second story and cupola stands on the Green. The original one-story massing is no longer visible as built, but the site and later school-era appearance survive. The surrounding Green, historic houses, and New Castle streetscape remain. Interpretive materials from the historical society note the military and educational phases.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Park in or near historic New Castle and walk to the Green (Market Street area). View the Arsenal exterior from the public open space. If the historical society is open, enter for orientation and any exhibits on local history. Combine with a walk of the Green and adjacent colonial and early-national buildings. Return via Market or Delaware Streets.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
Precise interior layout of the original 1809 arsenal and exact dates of the second-story addition vary slightly across accounts (1852 vs. mid-1850s). Contemporary military storekeeper records and town minutes were not consulted for this entry. Population or inventory figures for munitions stored here are not quantified in the sources examined.
