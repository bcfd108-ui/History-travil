# Nebraska State Capitol — from clay to carved stone

Nebraska OP05 · Draft research for Dash · November 19, 2026

## 2. The story

### The architect and the work that went on

Bertram Goodhue designed the third Nebraska Capitol, built in four phases from 1922 through 1932. Construction had to proceed around the functions of an operating seat of government. William Younkin’s construction photographs, published by the Office of the Capitol Commission, show a temporary covered walkway threading between parts of the outer square. Crews used it to move between construction zones. Staff and public later used it as completed sections came into service. [4,5,8]

Goodhue died in April 1924. In the Commission’s minutes and telegram text reproduced in the Capitol’s first-phase display, the Commission called his death “an irreparable loss of the master mind in the creation of our new Capitol.” It was signed by Governor Charles W. Bryan as chairman and R. L. Cochran as secretary. The work that continued after this statement is visible in the same publication. The photographs follow masons, stonecarvers, installers and the people who arranged their work. [5]

### The model, the cast and the hand on the tool

The Commission exhibit gives the chain for sculptural work. Lee Lawrie modeled in clay in New York. After approval, plaster versions were sent to Nebraska. Stonecutters used them to work out full-scale ornament. The display shows the ammonite-inspired capitals being worked and identifies the temporary scaffolding used for relief carving in place. It also says the county names beneath the cornice were carved at an Indiana quarry. The job could move the design from New York in a cast, move stone already cut in Indiana, or bring people and tools right up to a facade in Lincoln. [5,6]

Hartley Burr Alexander was the thematic consultant. On the north entrance, the Capitol’s exterior guide transcribes his inscription, “The Salvation of the State is Watchfulness in the Citizen”. Read that as a public sentence designed to take a particular place in the building. Then look at the arch, integrated relief and separate panels. Alexander supplied a verbal program, Lawrie shaped the sculpture, and the cutting and setting involved additional trades. The photographed men at work have yet to be individually identified in the records read here. [5–8]

## 3. Then / now

The Capitol combines original stonework and an operating state building. Younkin’s 1924 views show the north arch, vestibule window and exterior stonework with scaffolding. The arch and facade remain a visual comparison from lawful public points. The contemporary work zone and winter stair restriction change the way a visitor can approach. An old image cannot confirm present access. [1–3,5,7,8]

## 5. Public trip-shaped progression

1. Start at the posted open public entrance in regular hours. Check the tour meeting point after the winter switch. Use street-level views or the digital exterior guide whenever the visitor approach closes. [1–3,7]
2. Compare the Capitol Commission’s plaster model and stoneworker photographs with one finished capital at a guide-authorized stopping point. If that room is unavailable, use the operator’s published interior and exterior views. [5,6]
3. **Strongest story moment — Where the sculptor’s model became a building.** From open public ground look at the north arch and put Younkin’s scaffold photograph beside it. Read Alexander’s sentence. The inset figures and letters still occupy the surface on which the photographs show people working. Use the archive view remotely if the forecourt or sidewalk is closed. ## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The standing hours page contains general weather-dependent observation-deck wording. Its current construction banner and the November events sheet state that the tower and decks are closed during HVAC work. Follow the dated access notice and construction closure. [1–3]
- The standing hours page gives a typical seasonal north-entry closure. The November 17 statement says the coming north stair closure will persist for the foreseeable future and calls the final pre-Thanksgiving weekend north entrance hours possible, subject to freezing. It specifies ground-floor opening for the weekend after Thanksgiving. Confirm the door and tour start before travel. [1–3]
- The Commission exhibit reproduces meeting/telegram language and images. Original full-resolution album leaves, worker name roster, named carver payroll and final subcontract documentation were not retrieved. The NPGallery nomination was read.
