# Rowan Oak and the household that made room for writing

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP14 · Labeled research draft*

## 2. Story

Begin with the parlor and with the people who used it. The operator identifies Estelle Faulkner's Chickering piano there and remembers the room as the site of family gatherings. The same room served for a viewing after Caroline Barr died in 1940. William Faulkner wrote a eulogy. A corrected two-leaf typescript survives in the University of Virginia's collection beside a possible later transcript. The collection finding aid secures the distinction. The manuscripts themselves were unavailable for inspection. [2, 3]

Barr was born into slavery and later worked in the Falkner household. A scholarly account quotes Faulkner's dedication of *Go Down, Moses* describing her as someone “who was born in slavery.” Those words are from the dedication as reproduced in the scholarship, without a first-edition facsimile check. They are the employer's words about a worker whose own account has yet to enter this dossier. Estelle and Barr's lives should be read beside the novelist's long occupancy. A house could serve at once as a workplace, a family home and the setting for literary labor. [2–4]

Rowan Oak embodied that overlap. The university identifies artworks by Maud Butler Falkner, including both portraits in the library and a portrait of Jill in her bedroom. It describes an upstairs room with strong light where Estelle painted, and family additions that changed the circulation of the building. The later writing room offered a separate place to work. A Martin J. Dain collection item records the room and the outline for *A Fable* on its walls. The public can see how a writer used a room, and how other people used rooms around him. The camera record and the current room guide establish different dates. [2, 5, 6]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The Sheegog house and the Faulkner household at present-day Rowan Oak. HABS identifies it as William Faulkner's residence from 1930 to 1962. The building and its working household long predate its museum life. [2, 5]

**Present public anchor.** University of Mississippi Rowan Oak, 916 Old Taylor Road, Oxford. Use signed visitor parking and the posted short walk to the house. Bailey's Woods is an optional named connection between the University Museum and grounds when open. Ordinary visitor access ends at marked grounds and the staffed house route. [1, 2]

## 5. Public progression

Cross the signed grounds to see the front approach and the house at the end of it. On the open house circuit, look first at how the family used the parlor. Next see the painting space and the writing room if these views are included. The story lands at **The outline on the wall**, a material trace of one person's labor in a house known through many people's work. On a closed interior day, continue through the open exterior boundary only and use the study collection remotely. [1–3, 6, 7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The university's current institutional page lists a $10 interior admission and summer closing at five. An older university test-host page shows $5 and a summer six o'clock close with some Monday openings. Follow the current operator page and confirm holiday exceptions. [1, 8]
- The operator has named materials and furnishings in particular rooms. Installation and conservation records for individual paint, instruments and objects were not retrieved. Barr's own account remains absent. The original corrected and possible later eulogy typescripts were identified but not downloaded, and a first edition of the dedication was not retrieved. [2–4]
- The opened HABS gallery request returned HTTP 403, though the parallel HABS item listing still identified its records. No HABS plate was adopted on catalog wording alone. [5]
- The operator tells a family story about a fictional Judith Sheegog attributed to Faulkner. It is family lore attached to a named teller by the current operator. Its earliest telling and original words were not verified. It supplies no event, grave or visitor destination. [2]
