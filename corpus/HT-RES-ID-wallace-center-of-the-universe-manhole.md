## 2. The story

On **September 12, 2002**, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency signed its Record of Decision (`EPA/ROD/R10-02/032`, CERCLIS `IDD048340921`) for **Bunker Hill Mining and Metallurgical Complex Operable Unit 3 (OU 3)**, extending Superfund cleanup authority well beyond the original 21-square-mile Bunker Hill smelter "Box" (OU 1 and OU 2) across floodplains and mining communities of the **1,500-square-mile Coeur d'Alene Basin watershed** — including Wallace along the South Fork of the Coeur d'Alene River. Crucially, the EPA's remediation modeling leaned on its technical memorandum **`USEPA 2001f: Probabilistic Analysis of Post-Remediation Metal Loading`**, applying probabilistic models to predict post-cleanup water quality and metal transport. [1]

Outraged that federal Superfund stigma threatened property values and civic identity across the Silver Valley, Wallace leaders turned the EPA's "probabilistic" terminology into civic satire. During the Silver Summit on **September 25, 2004** (stamped **`9:00 PM WUT — Wallace Universal Time`**), Mayor **Ron Garitone** issued a proclamation declaring Wallace the **"Center of the Universe."** [2, 3]

The proclamation invented a tongue-in-cheek scientific rule dubbed **"Probalism"** — attributed to the EPA and Idaho Department of Health and Welfare and supposedly peer-reviewed by "La Cosa Nostra and the Flat Earth Society" — holding that *"if a thing cannot be disproven, it is thereby proven."* Because contracted scientists had spent millions studying the Silver Valley without ever proving Wallace was *not* the Center of the Universe, Mayor Garitone declared it proven, pinpointing an off-center sewer access cover at **Bank and 6th Streets** as the exact epicenter. [2, 3] The proclamation further designated four regional mines — **Bunker Hill, Sunshine, Lucky Friday, and Galena** — as the **"Four Corners of the Universe"** (`BHM`, `SRLM`, `HL`, and `CDE` markers reported on the custom cover). [2, 3]

What began as an anti-Superfund protest stuck permanently as civic branding. The City of Wallace's official municipal homepage (`wallace.id.gov`) still displays **"WELCOME TO Wallace Idaho — The Center of The Universe"** in its official header, and local organizers turned the September anniversary into an annual Silver Summit / Center of the Universe celebration and scholarship nonprofit (**Center of the Universe, Inc.**, incorporated 2008). [4, 7]

### Haunted history / lore

No ghost or haunting lore attaches to the Bank & 6th Street manhole; its classification under Family 4 rests on its documented 2004 satirical civic proclamation and physical street-monument installation responding to the 2002 EPA Superfund Record of Decision. [1, 2, 3]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Original signed city document & foundry bill not retrieved:** The full text of Mayor Ron Garitone's **September 25, 2004** proclamation is quoted from Wallace local heritage archives (*Silver Valley Idaho* and *Historic Wallace Photo Museum*) and corroborated by *The Spokesman-Review* (2009, 2017); the signed original resolution at Wallace City Hall and the foundry/installation invoice for the inscribed manhole cover were not retrieved directly. [2, 3]
- **Close-up manhole inscription:** The 2020 Wikimedia Commons wide view (`File:Wallace Idaho downtown.jpg`) looks directly across Bank & 6th Streets, but a parked pickup truck obscures the exact pavement spot and close-up lettering (`BHM`, `SRLM`, `HL`, `CDE` and compass markings reported in local sources); close-up pixel verification of the cover's exact cast face awaits an unobstructed free-licensed photo. [3, 6]
- **"1,500 square miles" context:** Regional coverage frequently describes OU 3 as turning "1,500 square miles" into a Superfund site. The Sept. 2002 EPA ROD (`Part 3, p. 2-5`) actually cites **`1,500 square-mile watershed`** while clarifying that contamination exists primarily near historical mining operations, Upper Basin communities, and the river corridor rather than uniformly across all 1,500 square miles. [1]
- **No overlap with live catalog:** Verified `/sites/ID-wallace-brick-core` covers Wallace's post-1910 brick commercial buildings, not the 2004 manhole monument or EPA OU 3 satire. [5]
