# Sumner, where Mamie Till Bradley returned to testify

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP19*

## 2. Story

Emmett Louis Till went to Mississippi to visit family in August 1955. He was fourteen. His mother, Mamie Till Bradley, later known as Mamie Till-Mobley, had been born in Mississippi and raised largely in Illinois. The presidential proclamation puts their family in the larger history of the Great Migration and locates the independent roles of the Mississippi trial and the Chicago funeral. When Emmett was killed, his mother insisted that he come home and made decisions about allowing the public to see what had happened to him. This dossier treats him as a child and who he had been as part of the record. [4][5]

She came to Sumner for the September trial. The Civil Rights Cold Case Records Review Board records that she identified Emmett. Mose Wright testified about who came to his home and took the boy. Willie Reed offered other testimony about what he had witnessed. These were different witnesses speaking to different parts of the case. The federal review details the decision to exclude Carolyn Bryant’s testimony about the store from the jury, the acquittal in the murder trial and a later decision by another jury not to indict for kidnapping. A subsequent paid magazine account quoting Bryant and Milam is different evidence from sworn testimony and an acquittal. [5–7]

In the *Afro-American* of November 12, 1955, Mamie Bradley wrote about being “too busy with constructive ideas and actions” in the wake of the killing. The phrase is from a surviving clipping read in the released federal file, printed under her name. Her words turn the record toward the work she did after the funeral and court. The modern interpretive center, in partnership with NPS, gives people a public place to read that history without going to the family’s former home or the river. [1][4][5][8]

## 3. Then and now

The state nomination locates the 1955 murder trial in the Second District Courthouse. The present building dates from rebuilding after the 1908 fire. It was substantially similar in design to the earlier courthouse, an architectural association separate from the 1955 court record. The modern staffed visitor center is across the street. The courthouse has an operator-guided interpretive path with its own access on the day of a visit. [1–3][9]

## 5. Public progression and strongest story moment

Start inside the visitor center. Read the story of the trial and Mamie Bradley’s later statement. Join a courthouse program only when staff are offering the ordinary public tour. **Strongest story moment — Returning to be heard.** Pause with her decision to give evidence in Sumner and her own published words after the acquittal. ## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The state property summary compresses the story as a kidnapping trial followed by acquittal. The federal case record and the monument proclamation identify the Sumner trial as the murder trial. The later kidnapping case did not proceed after the grand jury returned no bill. Use the contemporaneous case distinction. [4][6][7][10]
- The federal investigation records material differences between the store allegation and the accounts of people with Emmett. It describes the evidentiary limits of later recantation and charging claims. A later interview or newspaper sentence cannot settle the details independently. The dossier does not reconstruct them. [4][6][7]
- The recovered FSU reconstruction PDF link returned HTTP 410. The archive catalog establishes that a transcription or reconstruction exists. The federal case records and the released period clipping were read separately. A full original trial transcript was unavailable according to DOJ. [6–8][11][12]
- NPS’s biography gives August 20 for boarding the train. The federal Review Board uses August 21 for travel, without in this file resolving the departure-versus-arrival scope. The chronology here keeps the initial trip at August 1955. [5][6]
