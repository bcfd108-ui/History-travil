## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** A newspaper office sold words, but also the ordinary paper a town needed to function. Look past the big headline and the print shop begins to appear.

**Primary evidence actually read:** the scanned **August 6, 1873 Bismarck Tribune, page 2**, carries its own large advertisement in the right-hand column. It names a **Taylor Cylinder Press**, a **quarter-medium Gordon**, and a paper-and-card cutter. The short original phrase **“Orders Taken for Ruling and Binding.”** ties the newspaper to commercial paper work. The advertisement promotes posters, cards, billheads and other printing, alongside subscriptions and news. This is the firm's claim about its equipment and services, not a payroll or an independently inspected workshop inventory. [3]

**Named editor and context:** the Library of Congress title history identifies **Clement A. Lounsberry** as the editor who established the paper. It describes a Washington hand-set press arriving by train in June 1873. That introductory account is later institutional synthesis, distinct from the contemporary advertisement. [4]

**Original interpretation:** Follow the work instead of making this another famous-editor monument. A page had to become an object people could hold. The commercial column makes printing visible as a business serving other businesses, not just as the production of news. Yet the advertisement speaks in the company name: the people composing, feeding, finishing and delivering particular jobs are not individually established by this source. Those tasks are interpretive avenues, not verified job assignments to named workers.

The most important absence belongs in the story. Lounsberry's recoverable name must not stand in for every person in the room. Do not invent an apprentice, woman compositor, wage, workday, union or incident to make the shop feel populated.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

1. **Opening-date ambiguity:** LOC says the first issue was dated July 11, 1873 but actually appeared July 6. Do not silently turn those into the same date. The selected primary issue is August 6 and does not settle the first-issue question. [4]
2. **Equipment sequence unresolved:** the introductory Washington-press account and August advertisement's Taylor/Gordon equipment references are not interchangeable. No acquisition, replacement or transfer date has been established. [3,4]
3. **Worker evidence limited:** a contemporary company advertisement and named editor context meet the primary-print starting point, not a complete labor history. No firsthand compositor diary or wage book was found in this pass.
4. **Editorial perspective:** the surrounding issue contains racialized and colonial representations. They are evidence of the newspaper's worldview, not neutral descriptions to reproduce as narrator truth. No Indigenous community voice has been secured for interpreting those representations; this dossier does not use them to narrate Tribal history. [3]
5. **Access:** winter holiday exceptions remain; no dated closure check, accessible entrance inspection or material reservation occurred.
