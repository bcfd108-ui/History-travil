# Denison — Eisenhower birthplace and the Katy railroad neighborhood
Research draft · Texas OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

**A neighbor went for the doctor.** Katy engineer James Redmon remembered the October night when several women had already gathered to help Ida Eisenhower. Redmon recalled, “I hustled out of my apartment and went for the physician.” This is his later recollection, quoted from Francis T. Miller’s 1944 book in the National Register nomination, §8. The nomination dates the birth to October 14, 1890. Read Redmon for the help he remembered the neighbors giving. His account comes more than half a century after the birth. [2]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then — housing within reach of the shop.** The nomination traces David J. Eisenhower’s 1888 move to Denison and employment as a wiper at the Missouri, Kansas and Texas Railroad roundhouse. It describes the Redmons and the Eisenhowers sharing the house in separate quarters. Families, shops and tracks were close enough here for the railroad to organize the workday and the neighborhood. The Eisenhowers returned to Kansas in 1892. **Now — the historic house on the museum lot.** Denison acquired the property in 1946. Later restoration and transfer put it in state care. The presidential chronology gives the span of the stay, and the nomination and operator explain how the building was preserved. [1–3]
