# Sedalia — The names that made it into print

Missouri OP05 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

The 1899 copyright deposit makes a town, a composer and a publisher legible on a single sheet. The printed cover inspected in the Library of Congress reproduction reads “COMPOSED BY SCOTT JOPLIN” and gives Sedalia and John Stark & Son. It has a 50-cent price. The Library describes two copies sent for copyright registration and reports one cent per sale for Joplin. The deposited music and its wrapper document a commercial collaboration. The wrapper carries a racial caricature of the era, discussed in the Library’s account. The document gives Joplin a visible credit and also reveals the terms on which a publisher presented his work to a mass market. [4–5]

There were other hands and ears on that music. The biography’s account of Arthur Marshall quotes his memory of Joplin asking him to “play that piece again” at the square piano. This is a brief quotation from the printed account of Marshall’s later recollection. The biography also reproduces a portion of the Dec. 23, 1898 club agreement and names Joplin and Marshall among the first members. Its transcription of a club business card calls Joplin “the entertainer” and prints 121 East Main for the club. The memorial’s present park address, 105 North Lamine, belongs to the visitor anchor. The same book transcribes 1899 newspaper coverage of local Black ministers who called for closing the club. A published rebuttal came from the president of the separate Black 400 club. The Maple Leaf Club’s own response is absent from this account. These records preserve disagreement within the town. The biography identifies Scott Hayden as a friend and collaborator of both men. The printed *Sunflower Slow Drag* cover names Joplin and Hayden together, whereas the Library’s item metadata names Joplin alone. The Mississippi State University catalog credits both. The cover’s two names are the basis for this dossier’s joint attribution. [6–8]

A St. Louis newspaper clipping as transcribed in that biography presents conductor Alfred Ernst as discovering Joplin in 1901. The article’s racialized hierarchy of musical skill and its promise of instruction are part of its frame. The score and the named Sedalia musicians show a working musical network already in place. The scores document a route from repeated practice and performance to a product people could carry home. [4, 6–8]

## 3. Then and now

**Historical locations.** The Maple Leaf Club was a Black social and musical venue, memorialized at the corner of Main and Lamine. The old club interior is absent. A photograph captioned as Carl Abbott’s Recreation Center in a later account concerns a subsequent use of the site. The city's preservation plan associates John Stark's music business and the published rag contract with the Pythias Building. The dossier keeps both businesses separate and makes no claims about surviving layouts or private interiors. The Katy Depot is a separate surviving 1890s rail building and an optional interpretation stop. [2–3, 6, 9–11]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive up to lawful downtown parking and use the open city-side steps or sidewalk approach to Maple Leaf Park, 105 N. Lamine Avenue. Remain on public paths and marked crossings. The park and its marker form a short walk from the public way. A separate drive-up fallback is Sedalia Katy Depot, 600 E. Third Street, during normal visitor hours. Closed businesses, residential addresses, former club fabric and rail rights of way stay out of the walk. [1–3, 9]

## 5. A short visit

Begin at the separate Katy Depot if open, or arrive directly at the park. At Main and Lamine, see the memorial from the open public approach. Finish with **Two names on the same sheet**. Read the park marker’s names, then compare the sole composer credit on the 1899 *Maple Leaf Rag* with the two composer names on *Sunflower Slow Drag*. The pair tells a larger story about working musicians than a single famous name on a monument. When sidewalks ice over, read the scores remotely or visit the open depot. [1–9, 12]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The biography places the Stark offices at 114 East Fifth in the summer of 1899. The city plan points to the Pythias Building as shop and contract setting. The exact business and signing room remain under review. The Library of Congress says the club is a possible inspiration for the title. The biography also offers a tree, popular waltz and railroad-associated possibilities. The club site marker commemorates the people and venue and cannot resolve the title’s origin. The 1899 Sedalia copyright deposit is distinct from the 1904 and 1911 printings in separate Library records. The Missouri Historical Society catalog describes a Stark St. Louis copy dedicated to the club and dated 1899. That catalog entry does not by itself establish the text of the Sedalia deposit. The original signed royalty contract and full club agreement were not retrieved. The 1898 Sanborn volume was identified at the Library of Congress. No individual sheet was adopted as a footprint match. NPS lists the separate Katy Depot as 79001388 at 600 E. Third, while the city’s preservation guidelines assign 79001350 to the depot. Use the federal depot identifier and keep its scope distinct from the Maple Leaf Park anchor. [4–11, 13–18]

## 7. Historical photo records

**Site photograph absent for adoption.** The Sedalia Ragtime Archive/Syncopated Times illustration captioned Carl Abbott’s Recreation Center is a candidate for a later building at the site. The inspected illustration shows the later recreation-center sign across a two-story street frontage, with parked vehicles in front. The photo’s exact item accession, maker, date and open reuse evidence were not established. Research-only credit as printed: Sedalia Ragtime Archive and the Syncopated Times, via the University Press of Florida online edition, photographer uncredited, undated. No adopted frame or repeat viewpoint. The Library of Congress reproduced Joplin’s period portrait from the *American Musician and Art Journal*, June 17, 1907. It is a person record and does not establish the Sedalia club room. The inspected 1899 score cover is documentary evidence, with the original printed item and a modern documentary reproduction kept distinct. No Photo Point. [4, 6]
