## 2. The story
The story is a question that was not allowed to become a conversation. Fourteen Black University of Wyoming football players were dismissed in October 1969 over their proposed armband protest concerning BYU and the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints' racial exclusion from priesthood. Their futures, not merely a team's winning record, are the center of this stop. [4–5]

**Mel Hamilton**, interviewed by **Phil White on August 27, 2013**, remembered that the players met in **Joe Williams's room**, considered the consequences, and chose solidarity. He said they wore armbands to the coach's office expecting discussion, not a completed demonstration on the playing field. His recollection of teammates' silence carries the emotional center: **“But nobody tried. I think that's what hurt.”** The published transcript was edited by the Casper College Western History Center and WyoHistory.org; it is retrospective firsthand testimony, not a verbatim contemporaneous record. [4]

At a **February 19, 2021 UMGC event**, **John Griffin** described remaining at Wyoming with help from two professors who found academic scholarships. He recalled refusing to surrender his beliefs as a condition of returning to football. **Anthony “Tony” McGee** described the stigma he believed followed him into professional football. These are different trajectories, not fourteen interchangeable stories. The university's published event account is the inspected source; its embedded videos were not independently listened to. [5]

The interpretive conclusion is not that later success made the dismissal harmless. Ask what institutions owe a student before they celebrate that student's resilience. This is a reflection prompt, not a reenactment, debate contest or instruction to impersonate the players.

## 3. Then / now
- **1969:** the confrontation concerned students' ability to express opposition to racial exclusion while belonging to a university team. Do not simplify it to a protest performed during a game. [4–5]
- **2013:** Hamilton was interviewed in Casper, not at this Laramie anchor. [4]
- **2021:** Hamilton, Griffin and McGee publicly discussed consequences across their lives. Their later careers do not erase their differing experiences. [5]
- **2022:** the AHC hosted a student-created Black 14 exhibition, developed through its Social Justice Summer Institute. The published online exhibit remains a research resource. [6]
- **Now:** the AHC's public visitor hours and online interpretation are independently documented. Its current reading-room restrictions remain a different gate. No inference is made that a 2022 exhibition is still installed. [1,3,6]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
1. **Before leaving:** check AHC hours, UW closures and current public-entry conditions. Do not turn an unavailable reading-room appointment into a walk-in request.
2. **Begin at the lawful public interpretation anchor:** distinguish where records are kept from where an event occurred. If the door is shut, use the online material elsewhere rather than searching adjacent university doors.
3. **Read the member's account first:** begin with Hamilton's explanation of the intended discussion, not a scoreboard or coaching biography.
4. **Add the institutional record layer:** use the online exhibition's dated captions to understand that the university community was not one voice. Do not claim to have read an unseen original.
5. **End on consequence:** consider Griffin's choice to retain his convictions. The strongest story moment is the difference between being permitted back into an institution and being required to abandon oneself to belong.

This progression is editorial, not an activated walking route. No armband posing, dismissal reenactment, crowd confrontation, scoring or photographic challenge is proposed.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Narrative precision:** Hamilton recalled being denied an opportunity to speak; the UMGC account compresses events into asking and being dismissed after the request. Preserve that difference rather than inventing exact dialogue or sequence. [4–5]

- **Participant totals:** the online exhibit introduction says six high-school students; the newspaper quotes a university release saying nine. These may count different participation stages, but that explanation is unverified. No total is used as settled fact. [6–7]
