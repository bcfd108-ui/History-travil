# Fort Worth Stockyards — cattle become a market report
Research draft · Texas OP02 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### From droving to selling

The early trail town and the later yard were stages of a long business. The district’s historical account dates Fort Worth’s rail connection to 1876 and the establishment of north-side Union Stockyards to 1887. The Livestock Exchange building dates to 1902. Its offices were designed for the transactions that followed the arrival and sorting of animals [2–4].

### The office that the newspaper sent readers to

A 1903 Fort Worth Telegram page carries a market-related advertisement and reporting with cattle-market office numbers. Its directive reads “ADDRESS ALL MAIL TO THE COMPANY.” That is period commercial copy from the Texas Tech newspaper scan, not an independent judgment on the company. Read it as evidence of routine commercial work: sales and quotations had to get from pens, buyers and commission firms to suppliers elsewhere. Several listings sit close together in the scan; avoid reassigning an officer or office based on column OCR alone [5].

### Three different kinds of work

A person driving animals into town, a clerk recording a sale, and a person working for the later packing plants left different records. The Exchange exterior is where the office assignment becomes visible. The museum places the exchange beside rail shipping and later Swift and Armour packing. The district nomination makes it possible to follow other named components without assuming the whole industrial system survives on the visitor itinerary [1–4].

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic place.** Livestock Exchange and its wider yards, shipping and industrial district [2–4].

**Lawful physical anchor.** 131 East Exchange Avenue, Suite 113 for the museum and signed public sidewalk on East Exchange Avenue for the exterior. Drive-up and short sidewalk walk. Remain out of managed animal areas, tracks, commercial work areas and private interiors [1–2].
