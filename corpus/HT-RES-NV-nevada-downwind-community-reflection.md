## 2. The story
**Linda Chase**, identified as a Nevada downwinder and author, contributed a named public statement to an April 18, 2024 Union of Concerned Scientists release. She challenged the exclusion of communities from proposed recognition and assistance. A brief excerpt is **“not to mention southern Nevada, New Mexico, and other affected areas?”** This is her attributed published advocacy statement, not an interview conducted for this project, a medical finding, or a current eligibility determination. [1]

Her question supplies the central story: a person can speak publicly about a community's experience while still having to argue that the community belongs within an official response. That is this dossier's interpretive reading of her statement, not an assertion that the 2024 legislative situation remains unchanged.

The University of Utah's institutional oral-history index separately documents **Claudia Peterson's June 12, 2017 interview with Justin Sorensen** and a different **November 18, 2012 interview by Rob DeBirk**. These are Utah community records connected to the wider Nevada-testing legacy, not evidence that Peterson is a Nevada resident. The index was read; the full recordings and transcripts were not reviewed here. No family medical detail is extracted from its summaries. [2]

A further Nevada repository lead demonstrates why availability and permission must remain separate. UNLV's item record for **Bennie Reilley, Sr.**, interviewed May 10, 2004, identifies a Western Shoshone narrator but expressly requires written permission before quotation in any form. This dossier does not quote, paraphrase his testimony, or claim permission. It uses only catalog metadata to document a rights barrier. [3]

The reflective landing is not spectacle or an attempt to relive an event. It is careful listening: a public statement, an archival summary and a restricted interview offer different kinds of access, and each person's choices still matter.
