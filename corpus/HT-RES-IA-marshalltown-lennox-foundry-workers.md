# Marshalltown — The shop that had to make the tools

Iowa OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**Before a furnace could be made, its tools had to be made.** The Biographical Dictionary of Iowa traces David Lennox's hands-on work from making barbs for a wire manufacturer to a machine shop that made repairs and products for other industries. Talbot Lennox joined the machine shop after the family moved to Marshalltown, the trade history reports. The biography says patent holders Ernest E. Bryan and Ezra Smith brought a furnace-manufacturing problem to Lennox in 1896. MIT's Lemelson account spells the first surname Bryant. Both describe a design brought to a machinist who could equip a shop to build it. A manufacturer’s origin date of 1895 appears in the centennial coverage and company account, while the university biography places the consultation in 1896. These claims refer to different milestones in the sources and leave the first factory run unsettled. [4–7, 12]

**When there was no roof above the production line.** Lennox's July 2018 statement records damage at the Marshalltown manufacturing plant after a tornado. In the firm's published account, plant manager Miguel Gutierrez recalled workers “working in makeshift operations”. He reported 510 employees working and earning wages or salaries in the phase he described. Radio Iowa reported a workforce of about 950 alongside a five-year contract ratification in 2021. The two figures describe different periods and different reporting purposes. They give perspective on the people behind the product and do not measure storm job losses. The manager speaks for his experience of the recovery inside the company. Rank-and-file testimony would add a voice missing from that account. [8–10]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic place.** The early Lennox Machine Company operated in Marshalltown. A lease, Sanborn map or deed that fixes the first shop, its foundry spaces and the furnace work to a specific block has yet to be verified. The tornado-era factory lies at the current address. The city’s 2025 plan distinguishes the factory's 200 S 12th Avenue frontage from the city trail access at 201 on the other side. [2, 4, 5, 8]

**Lawful public anchor.** Lennox Trail Access, 201 S 12th Avenue, Marshalltown. Use the designated municipal approach and remain on posted public path or access area. View only what is visible from there. Stop by vehicle only at a signed lawful parking location. Skip if posted closed, if a public approach is unclear, or if ice or roadway work obscures the boundary. [1, 2]

## 5. A short visit

From the municipal access read the sequence of work: wire barbs, machines and industrial repairs, then furnaces. Set that beside the 2018 recollection of restarting production. Land on **A shop makes its own tools** from the lawful public side of the street or with the same account at home if the approach is unsafe. [1, 2, 4, 9]
