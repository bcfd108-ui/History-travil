## 2. The story

Opened in **1952** along the US-190 pinewoods corridor east of Mandeville (directly north of Fontainebleau State Park), **Southeast Louisiana Hospital** was built as a modern mid-century regional state psychiatric hospital serving the Greater New Orleans and Northshore parishes. Its modernist sprawling pavilion plan—clearly laid out around radial drives and a central water tower on the 1968 USGS quadrangle—represented post-WWII decentralized mental-health care. [1, 4]

The hospital gained statewide political notoriety in **1959** when sitting Louisiana Governor **Earl K. Long** (`1895–1960`) was briefly confined there following erratic public behavior during an intense political and personal crisis before securing his release by firing the hospital board and state hospital director. [1]

In **2012–2013**, amid sweeping Louisiana Medicaid and state hospital restructuring under Governor Bobby Jindal's administration, the state closed **Southeast Louisiana Hospital** (`2013`), transferring operations to **Northlake Behavioral Health System**, which continues to provide inpatient psychiatric care on the Mandeville grounds (`23455 Sparrow Rd.`), though regional reporting in 2024 documented federal staffing and patient-supervision compliance challenges at the facility. [1, 2, 3]
