## 2. Story
**Noccalula Falls Park and Pioneer Village, Gadsden · Research draft · Fred**

The village belongs to the history of a city choosing what to save and how to show it. The Woman's Club of Gadsden traces its roots to Charlotte Chrisman Cox's organization in 1923. In its own history, the club records the purpose of having a body ready for a “progressive course that vitally affects the community.” Its members pushed for municipal acquisition in 1946. They understood that R. A. Mitchell had held the falls and adjoining property for a future public park. The surviving city park uses a broad admission area, with the village among the facilities named by the operator. The present buildings need individual provenance study before linking a particular structure to a particular household. [1, 3]

In 1968–69 the club took on a different job: raising money for a bronze figure by Suzanne Silvercruys. The Smithsonian inventory names Kay Smith Stanley of Glencoe as the model, and traces modeling to Tucson and casting to Norwalk. The dedication on September 21, 1969 is a dated event. The subject of the bronze comes from a regional legend, as distinct from the documented work of the artist, model, club and city. The sculpture and park histories document public commemoration and supply no independent record of a person corresponding to the character. This account leaves the narrative itself unretold. [3–4]

## 3. Then and now
The club's record explains the plan to bring the land into public use. Today the city lists the village with the garden and train under seasonal admission. The 2026 winter event has its own ticket and calendar. Regular village or interior access during the special event is unverified. [1–3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
- The club describes a campaign to have the city buy the land from Mitchell's estate. The later Library of Congress photographer's catalog note says Mitchell gave the site to the city. The city's acquisition deed and probate transfer were not retrieved. The two accounts of transfer are preserved, and no conveyance mechanism is asserted here. [3, 6]
- The Smithsonian includes an account of the legend in its object record. That part documents the telling and is not evidence of a historical event. A free scan of the cited September 21, 1969 newspaper and a building-by-building source for the village were not verified. [1, 4]
- Year-round grounds, the regular ticket season, and the special event are separate. The operator's general footer shows 9–5 in regular months while its detailed summer page gives a later weekend close. Verify the detailed calendar before travel. [1–2]
