## 2. The story
**Bunro Sakaino's March 26, 1943 diary entry** records preparing for transfer while still incarcerated at **Camp Livingston, Louisiana**, not yet writing from Santa Fe. He notes help with packing and correspondence, then writes: **“I am planning to write to people I know after I get settled down in Santa Fe.”** [4]

That sentence is the center of this entry: transfer interrupts not only someone's location but their ability to keep people informed. The English text, translated by **Naoko Tanabe in 2024–2025**, was directly read on the item page. The Japanese manuscript was not independently translated. This is distinct material from the earlier Fort Missoula dossier, not a recycled claim about his April 1942 departure. [4]

A second voice describes communication **inside Santa Fe**. In his **September 23, 2009 interview with Tom Ikeda and Barbara Takei**, **Frank Sumida** recalls receiving letters from his mother with much of their content removed. This account is retrospective testimony, not a surviving censored letter examined here. The relevant published transcript passage in segment 28 was directly read; the recording was not listened to. [5]

Interpretive landing: a letter can survive physically while the words a family needed have been taken away. Do not dramatize, reenact or gamify that loss.

## 3. Then / now
**Santa Fe was a Department of Justice/Immigration and Naturalization Service detention institution, not one of the War Relocation Authority's ten camps.** Transfers between systems do not make those systems identical. Densho's institutional history identifies successive phases of confinement and people arriving from different places. No blanket assumption about citizenship, nationality, individual guilt or reasons for detention is appropriate. [6]

The University of Idaho collection history says Sakaino's surviving diary ends **March 27, 1943**, as he prepares for transfer. It separately reports later records placing him at Santa Fe until release in **February 1946**. Those later records are the library's research summary, not entries in this diary. [3]

The museum/library is today's independent public research setting. It is not the former detention place, and no physical connection is implied. Reading a source in Santa Fe does not authorize tracing its historical addresses or approaching private property.
