# Baxter Springs: a filling station that looked like a cottage
KS OP05 · Draft for Dash · November 20, 2026

## 1. Trip gate
**Kansas Route 66 Visitor Center, 940 Military Avenue, Baxter Springs, KS 66713.** NPS identifies the historic station and its current public use. Plan on a short daylight exterior view from a clear public sidewalk; interior only if the operator confirms walk-in hours that day. No current station-specific winter hours verified; do not transfer the separate museum schedule. Use lawful parking and marked crossings, never the highway shoulder or pump apron as staging. [1–3]

## 2. The story
**The little house sold a recognizable service.** A surviving 1932 Phillips painting specification, reproduced in the National Register nomination, depicts the chain's cottage-style prototype. The nomination identifies it as the mirror image of the Baxter Springs station, not a site plan. It helps explain why a roadside business presented a small domestic-looking gable and shop window instead of a plain garage. [4]

Site evidence is separate. The nomination cites a December 1929 property transfer to Independent Oil and Gas and a July 1930 Sanborn sheet showing a new brick station and two tanks. Later, Phillips added a service wing for maintenance work. A locally held 1940s photograph records an attendant between pumps; the worker is not named in the nomination. The story is about making fuel stops recognizable and adding a place to do work, not about assigning an identity to an uncaptioned attendant. [4]

## 3. Then / now
The former station still shows the cottage-scale gables and later L-shaped footprint; NPS presents it today as a visitor center, not a working fuel stop. The local operator describes restoration to a 1940s appearance in 2007. Do not treat restored pumps, finish or signage as demonstrably original to 1930. [1,2,4]

## 4. What's still there
- **Exterior-only fallback:** northeast corner of 10th Street and Military Avenue, visible from the street. Approach on open public sidewalks, use a marked/legal crossing, and stand only where clear and safe. The historical nomination describes broad curb cuts; they are vehicle paths, not pedestrian stopping points. Today's pavement and access were not field-inspected. [1,4]
- **Conditional public interior:** the historical society operates the visitor center at this building. Use only its signed visitor entry if open. No parking permission inferred for its historic service apron, and no access to staff space or adjacent property. [1–3]
- **Historic location:** the 1930-era service station occupies the visitor-center parcel. The old alignment is background, not the visit approach. A historic road or district pin is not a public entrance. [1,4]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
One short town stop, not a highway itinerary. From a safe sidewalk, read the illustrated prototype against the visible cottage form without trying to recreate its angle. Finish with the documented addition: a station that began as a recognizable shop grew into a place to work on cars. If no safe public standing space is available, read remotely. [1,4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
The 2011 corridor plan calls it built in 1929; the nomination supports a December 1929 purchase and station present by July 1930. NPS and operator say 1930. NPS says society acquisition in 2003; the operator says 2005. The acquisition deed was not examined. The local station page's indexed copy is available but direct fetch returned no readable lines. The July 1930 map and deeds are cited through the nomination, not individually inspected here. No worker name, station-specific holiday hours or field access confirmed. [1,2,4,5]

## 7. Photo record
The nomination identifies 1940s **P1771**, Baxter Springs Heritage Center, and logs distinct 2002 photos by Elizabeth Rosin. NPS credits its current illustration to Rhys Martin. The draft does not claim direct inspection of P1771, a matched current frame, a safe Photo Point or reuse license. The 1932 drawing is a prototype, not an archival photograph of this site. [1,4]
