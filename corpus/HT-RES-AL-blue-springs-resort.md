# Blue Springs and the people who made a resort around the water

*On Old Ground · Alabama OP01 · Labeled research draft.*

## 2. Story
In the late nineteenth century, people made Blue Springs into a seasonal destination by improving access to the water and building places where travelers could stay and eat. The state encyclopedia identifies the Whigham family as investors in the spring area and dates their resort hotel to about 1900. A local history linked by the state park gives the proprietor as J. T. E. Whigham and identifies Frank O. Deese as an early manager. It also describes hired and family work that extended beyond the hotel rooms to bathing facilities and gatherings. These are researched accounts. The hotel ledger and primary employment records remain open. [1, 3, 4]

The local account also describes an earlier small hotel associated with Mr. Harrison. The Whigham hotel, later improvements to the pool and changing summer business belong to a sequence, not one construction event. Some guests attributed healing powers to the water. That was a reason people came, as the historians report it. No medical effect is established by those claims. [3, 4]

A later visitor left a personal record of a different kind. In a published 2020 recollection, Wendy English McGhee remembered “the home cooked meals my Grandmother cooked for us”. She places childhood swimming alongside meals in her grandparents’ home. Her recollection gives us a person’s account of a visit shaped by neighbors and family, after the era of the resort hotel. It does not establish any current hours or safe waterfront access. [4]

The state park developed after the state acquired land in 1963, according to the state encyclopedia. The modern operator describes clear groundwater feeding two public pools and gives distinct seasonal day-use hours. This public recreation phase helps a visitor understand why the place kept drawing people even after the earlier resort business faded. [1, 3, 5]

## 3. Then and now
The once-commercial resort and the current Alabama state park are different operating phases of the same springs landscape. The signed inland day-use space provides a public point for interpretation. This dossier does not establish surviving hotel fabric or a public boundary at its original footprint. The pool and spillway belong to the current water facilities, outside the stated ground scope. ## 6. Gaps and disagreements
- The encyclopedia describes the growth of tourism and the Whigham hotel. The local account distinguishes Harrison’s earlier place and tells a story of the spring appearing in different locations. No geologic or land survey verifying that movement was obtained. It is presented as a local recollection, without a historic spring locator. [3–5]
- Modern operator and independent encyclopedia counts represent different inventories. Modern hours, admission and facility decisions come from the operator. The original hotel register, land instruments and period newspaper reporting remain unread. [1, 3–5]
- A historic-looking Blue Springs picture in a Georgia repository has a different town and region; it does not verify the Alabama resort. The local account’s Alabama pool image did not yield the required item-level rights and record. [4]
