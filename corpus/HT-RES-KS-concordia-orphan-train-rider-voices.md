# Concordia — Paul Young and the depot that keeps his story

## 2. The story

**A train to Clyde, a car to Concordia.** On October 13, 1911, sixteen children arrived in Clyde with Children’s Aid Society agents B. W. Tice and Anna Laura Hill, according to the museum’s reconstruction. Boyd and Louise Clithero drove from Concordia for the placement and brought two-year-old Paul Young home by car. Their grocery business and Paul’s later schooling link him to Concordia. His birth parents and the circumstances that sent him west remain unknown. The locality matters. The placement occurred in Clyde, and the museum’s present Concordia depot dates to 1917. [2,3,6]

**A child's own request.** In November 1912 the family moved within Concordia. In the newspaper passage preserved by the museum, Paul was upset at leaving his friend Norman Lewis. Asked about gaining a sister, he reportedly answered, “No mama, but I would like a little Norman.” Those are Paul’s reported words via the museum’s quotation of the November 6 Blade-Empire item. The original issue was not inspected. Read it as a small, second separation visible in a child’s own language. [3]

**What the placement paperwork could see.** The January 13, 1911 Jefferson County Tribune item in the Kansas Collection transcription describes placement at Oskaloosa and the program’s claimed monitoring. The Kansas Collection also reproduces a Children’s Aid Society placement card. The Blue Rapids Times source and an arrival photograph from February 25, 1908 document a different placement setting. The museum says the Blue Rapids paper named the local committee and receiving families. These records explain the administrative machinery without supplying a placement agreement for Paul or showing what the Clitheros’ household felt like to him. [4,5,7]

## 3. Then / now

**Historic location.** Clyde for Paul’s selection and Concordia for the family years. The precise Clyde platform, grocery premises and both Concordia homes have not been established here as visitor ground. [3]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive up to the Morgan-Dowell Welcome Center and follow staff directions for the short public walk to the restored Union Pacific depot on the same Concordia museum campus. The NRHP entry identifies the depot property at 300 Washington Street. The visitor center is the museum entry; archival services are separate. [1,2,6]

The depot represents the regional passenger railroad era and is itself surviving fabric. It now holds exhibits about many riders, including placements that took place elsewhere and earlier. Its waiting-room scale can bring the story close. The 1917 building never served as Paul’s 1911 arrival platform. [2,3,6]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Start at Morgan-Dowell and take in the museum’s introductory film. Set apart an agent’s placement decision, a receiving household and the child who had to live with both. [2–5]
2. Walk only along the museum approach to the historic depot. Compare the exhibits’ personal stories with the placement card and the example from Oskaloosa. [2,4,7]
3. **Strongest story moment — A little Norman.** Read Paul’s reported words while sitting or standing quietly in the public exhibit space. The name belongs to a friend he knew after arriving, and it puts ordinary childhood back into the placement record. The operator’s virtual tour and the published story support the same reflection from home. [2,3]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The museum’s rider chronology and both quoted newspapers reach us through its published account. Original Clyde and Concordia issue images, Paul’s individual placement card, any formal adoption record and his birth family are outstanding. The Blue Rapids newspaper clip did not open in the web reader. The museum’s artifact page and photograph catalog were readable, so the article details are attributed to the museum. [3,5,8]
- The operator uses 1854–1929 for the movement. A Kansas Collection contributor uses 1854–1930 and estimates 150,000–200,000 children, while Cloud County Tourism gives an estimated 250,000 through 1929. These are published endpoints and estimates with their own scope and counting assumptions. None affects Paul’s dated placement. [1,9,10]
- The current operator front page supplies the dated holiday override and a reopening date. A complete operator-issued regular-hours grid and the railcar’s ordinary access were not verified. Confirmation during staffed hours is important before making a special trip. [1,2]
