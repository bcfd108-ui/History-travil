## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** a museum can do more than display a people's past. It can let that community decide how its history is framed, who speaks and what remains outside the visitor's entitlement.

In the Tribe's **June 14, 2025** release announcing *Section 14 The Untold Story*, **Reid D. Milanovich**, identified there as Tribal Chairman, said: **“This is our home, and it has been since time immemorial.”** [4] This is a named, Tribe-published public statement. The title is attributed to that dated release, not asserted as a verified current officeholder identification.

The release describes an exhibition centered on Tribal Elders' recollections and documentary evidence about efforts to retain land and sovereignty. [4] That account is the Tribe's framing of its history, not a substitute for inspecting every underlying document or for the distinct accounts of every other former resident. The release was read; its referenced film was not viewed, and no individual Elder's words were invented.

**Original interpretation:** the meaningful change in perspective is from treating Palm Springs as a backdrop to someone else's vacation to recognizing a continuing people speaking about home. The visitor's task is to listen within the invitation actually offered, not to use cultural knowledge as a clue to places that were never opened to them.

## 3. Then / now

The museum's current exhibitions page marks *Section 14 The Untold Story* **previously on view, June 14, 2025–May 31, 2026**. The presently advertised changing exhibition is **A Beauty that Endures: Native Painting, Ritual, and Resilience in the 20th Century**, **October 3, 2026–May 30, 2027**, curated by **Tony Abeyta (Diné/Navajo)**. [5] This is a material trip-planning correction: a retained homepage image or 2025 announcement cannot establish that the older exhibition remains installed.

The permanent gallery begins with **Our Home**. [2] The fuller operator description distinguishes the Agua Caliente Band from the other Cahuilla bands and presents both historical change and contemporary self-determination. [5] Neither the Cahuilla peoples nor the Native artists in the changing exhibition should be flattened into a single interchangeable identity. A Diné curator's work is not automatically an Agua Caliente community account.

The public museum can supply present interpretation even when a particular temporary exhibition has ended. It cannot promise access to every story, recording, document or object cited in this draft.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Choose the gate first:** distinguish an ordinary timed visit from the specific free-evening offer. Check holiday exceptions and any individual access needs before travel.
2. **Orient inside:** begin with the museum's public welcome and the Tribe's own introduction, rather than treating artifacts as a puzzle to solve.
3. **Listen for attribution:** notice whether a label identifies an Elder, artist, curator or government record. These voices do different work and need not be collapsed into one institutional narrator.
4. **Keep exhibition dates visible:** if interested in Section 14, use the dated public release as background. Do not ask the current art exhibition to supply an absent historical film or claim that an old show is still there.
5. **End with the strongest story moment:** return to the published declaration of home. Reflect on how a place can welcome visitors without surrendering authority over its own story or opening all its land.

One museum visit is enough for this narrative. No extension into cultural landscapes, photography expedition or search for historical remains is part of it. If the museum is closed, the Tribe's public writing is the fallback, not a nearby substitute site.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The visitor FAQ retains daytime-only summary hours and a 4 p.m. final-ticket statement alongside seasonal Thursday evening hours. The homepage independently specifies the evening walk-up arrangement. [1][2] Exact holiday-eve closing times and current paid-ticket prices remain unverified.

The Section 14 exhibition's closing date is explicit, so this draft does not leave its present status ambiguous. [5] Its release remains useful historical evidence, not current admission guidance.

Current leadership was not separately researched; dated quotations retain dated titles. A public event announcement is not a transcript or evidence that its named speakers attended. No testimony from that event is paraphrased. [6]

No complete film, original land document, current gallery condition, individual accommodation arrangement or rights clearance was obtained. This dossier does not adjudicate disputes involving Section 14 or conflate Tribal sovereignty with the experiences of all former tenants. More depth would require the relevant distinct primary accounts, not invented consensus.
