## 2. The story
**One-minute hook:** A historic photograph can be a visitor's curiosity, but it can also be a community's way of speaking to its own future. Those are not the same purpose.

**Named Jemez-published primary account:** In the Pueblo's *The Walatowan*, January 2020, page 2, **Cornell Magdalena**, Public Health Advocate, authored an account of a historic-photo display. He described community-selected images and their relationship to health and continuity. His published words include: **“The health of our ancestors is a model for a healthy future”**. The short quotation is from his written article, not an interview conducted or audio heard here. [3]

**Publishing and access distinction:** This is a named account in a Pueblo-published newsletter. Its community invitation concerned a different institutional display, not the visitor-center museum. It supplies a perspective for reading history, not a public-admission claim. No trip to its original display location is included. [3]

**Separate museum account:** The operator describes interpretation in the community's own words, including a comparison between historic black-and-white pottery and contemporary artists' work. [2] A visitor can therefore encounter continuity without treating people as exhibits. The named newsletter article is not represented as a current museum label or as proof that its photographs have moved into this museum.

**Separate sovereign government account:** The Pueblo's government identifies Jemez as an independent sovereign nation with its own government and court system. Its visit page emphasizes families, language and responsibility to the community. [4] Government statements establish who holds authority; the enterprise website supplies the narrower operating information. Neither turns a published account into blanket permission to explore.

**Historical context:** Jemez Enterprises' history account describes the harm of colonization, coercion and disease alongside preservation of language and culture. It also discusses the 1838 arrival of people from Pecos and the continuing distinct recognition of Pecos within Jemez. [5] This is attributed institutional history, not a claim that all Pueblo communities are interchangeable. No sensitive origin narrative or historical route is reproduced.

Interpretive synthesis: the strongest visit is not a hunt for an “untouched” past. It is an encounter with people selecting what to tell, for whom, and for what future purpose.

## 3. Then / now
The dated newsletter shows historical imagery being actively selected and interpreted for community use, rather than existing only as an outside archive. [3] That provides a method of comparison, not a list of picture locations to revisit.

At the public museum, compare what the installed interpretation actually says with the separate published account. Keep creator, date, purpose and audience distinct. Do not assume an older photograph captures an entire community, that every caption is complete, or that a present-day resident owes a visitor a reenactment.

No archival/current image pair, repeated camera position or physical landscape alignment was verified. “Then / now” here concerns the uses of history, not access to the pictured ground.

## 4. What's still there
The operator documents the visitor center's museum and current interpretive program. Its webpage also labels an illustrated fieldhouse as a **replica**. [2] That label matters: it must not become an “original surviving ancestral building” in the app. No close approach, interior access or current physical condition of that feature was independently checked.

The existence of the museum does not establish the present display of any particular artifact, photograph or textile. There was no onsite inventory. Collection provenance, rights and culturally appropriate use must not be inferred from a general exhibition description.

The government's public-facing account remains a readable source in its own right. [4] Use that resource if opening conditions change; the community itself is not a substitute attraction.
