## 2. The story

**One-minute hook:** A boom can offer a livelihood. A household still has to decide whether that livelihood can become a lasting home.

**Named firsthand published testimony actually read:** NPR reporter **Stacey Vanek Smith's June 26, 2020** report includes **Fallon and Chase Justice** discussing Williston. Fallon grew up there; Chase came from Montana in 2008 for oil work. His remembered earnings are his testimony, not a current wage promise. Fallon describes the strain as families faced layoffs and departure. Her short statement, **“There's been lots of phone calls with tears between a lot of us,”** places the downturn in a social network rather than only an economic chart. [3]

**Mediation:** the published NPR transcript was read through WFAE. The audio was not independently listened to. Reporter narration, interview excerpts and the couple's expectations are distinct; the report is not a complete oral-history interview or a representative household survey. Fallon's hope that Williston would recover is her 2020 perspective, not a verified forecast. [3]

**Original interpretation:** Hold opportunity and uncertainty together. The household is neither a prop in an industry success story nor proof that every newcomer had the same experience. The interesting question for interpretation is how people make durable commitments around work that may change faster than their lives can.

The end of this story is not an oilfield spectacle. It is the difference between a place counted as a labor market and a place somebody calls home.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

1. **Historical boundary:** a 2020 interview cannot establish current conditions or outcomes. No present-day update about the named people was sought.
2. **Scope:** one couple's published testimony is not the experience of all households, Tribal communities, long-term residents or transient workers.
3. **Publicity distinction:** chamber and industry collections may contain promotional material. Their existence is not independent confirmation that a boom benefited everyone. No promotional claim is adopted as household evidence.
4. **Periodization:** the archival guide uses several boom periods and a start point different from some later narratives. This draft does not merge “Williston Basin,” the city and every Bakken development into a single event with one agreed opening date. [4]
5. **Media limit:** published transcript read; audio, raw recordings and editorial cuts not reviewed.
6. **Museum content:** general energy interpretation is documented, but no household-focused display or object inventory was independently checked.
7. **Winter access:** ordinary hours and listed holidays verified; exact arrival, weather, road safety, accessible entrance and emergency closures unresolved.
8. **Private life:** publicly giving an interview does not authorize a visitor approach to the person's home or workplace.
