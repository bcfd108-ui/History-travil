# Silver City: the store that carried a mining community

Division NM • OP03 • Local research draft • Checked 19 November 2026

Museum and public documentary interpretation only. Reflection for economic hardship and discrimination. No mine, industrial, residential, cemetery or private-property route; no upload or contact.

## 1. Trip gate

**Independent public anchor.** Silver City Museum's visitor page gives **312 W Broadway, Silver City, NM 88061**, telephone **575-538-5921**. Published hours are **Tuesday–Friday 9 a.m.–4:30 p.m.; Saturday 10 a.m.–4 p.m.; closed Sunday and Monday**. Admission is free; a **$5 donation** is suggested, not mandatory. [1]

**Winter closures.** The operator lists Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and New Year's Day among closures. No separate winter daily schedule or weather guarantee was found. Treat listed hours as the published gate, not independently observed opening. [1]

**Accessibility boundary.** First-floor exhibits and facilities are accessible; the historic building's smaller second-floor exhibits and third-floor cupola are not accessible to all visitors. Do not promise an elevator or an implemented virtual alternative. [1]

**Only the museum entrance is the destination.** The family's historic store and homes are subjects of testimony, not visitor stops. No appointment, tour or research assistance was requested.

## 2. The story

**Amy Jaurequi's family account.** The museum publishes her Chihuahua Hill oral-history transcript. At **13:13**, she remembers her grandmother's kitchen: **“there was always food.”** The surrounding passage locates her own childhood memories in Silver City. [2]

Earlier, she recounts family migration connected with mining near Hurley and the family's later Silver City grocery business. She explains that some earlier information comes from cousin Rachel's written account. Her grandmother's extension of credit to miners during a strike appears as inherited family history, not Amy's eyewitness memory. [2]

**Original interpretation.** A mining household's story does not end at the workplace. A grocery ledger, meals and relatives' unpaid work can carry the consequences of an interrupted wage. The story's center here is care circulating through a household and neighborhood, not industrial production or strike tactics.

**Keep the voices separate.** This is one descendant's published account, containing personal recollection and stories received from others. It is neither a company record nor proof that every local family experienced the same circumstances.

## 3. Then / now

**Community memory is itself historical work.** The museum's oral-history project describes conversations held around kitchen tables and organizes accounts around neighborhood change, social life, traditions and challenges. Its online interpretation names residents including Margaret Ogas, Maria Arellano, Billy Maldonado and Consuelo Muñoz. Their recordings were not listened to here and are not treated as additional inspected testimony. [3]

**Not a generic mining-town story.** This entry concerns Silver City's neighborhood support systems and a specifically named family. Hurley appears in the family background; it is not collapsed into Silver City or added as a physical stop.

**A contemporary institution.** The museum offers regional interpretation in a historic building with added galleries. Its public status does not establish public access to the historic homes, businesses or district discussed by interviewees. [1]

## 4. What's still there

**Public museum:** established visitor gate, limited to areas actually open and permitted.

**Published oral-history transcript:** a durable digital means of engaging with family memory without approaching the family or its former properties. [2]

**Independent library research resources:** Silver City Public Library lists local newspapers, historical photographs, oral histories and directories. Rare or fragile items require check-in at the information desk; some microfilm requires advance preparation. This is a research lead, not a booked session or a promise that a particular ledger survives. [4]

**Excluded:** former stores and dwellings, mines, works, rail corridors, work yards and any location inferred from an interview. No residence addresses are reproduced.

**Permanent rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

**Start with the distinction between two kinds of memory.** Read Amy's kitchen recollection beside the earlier family history. Keep what she experienced separate from what she learned later.

**Enter the public institution, not the neighborhood's private life.** During an actual museum opening, begin with available first-floor interpretation. No particular textile, ledger, household object or mining exhibit is promised in this dossier.

**Let present labels determine identity.** A displayed kitchen object is not automatically the family's possession. A photograph of miners is not automatically evidence of the particular strike described. Record only provenance actually given.

**Finish with the household.** Return to the image of a kitchen where food remained available. The ending is the relationship between livelihood and care. No shop doorway, house or mine must be found to complete it.

**Fallback.** If the museum is closed or a desired exhibit absent, the digital transcript still supports the story. Do not replace missing interpretation with an improvised neighborhood walk.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

**Transcript problems are visible.** The PDF is labeled as an Otter transcription and contains garbled names. Its file title contains **20211108**, its header **November 16, 2021**, while the opening spoken introduction says **November 5, 2021**. The interviewer's name also varies in the rendered text. Those discrepancies are unresolved; no silently corrected interview date or spelling is adopted. [2]

**Evidence limits.** Selected transcript passages were directly read; audio was not heard. Family migration, shop credit and strike chronology have not been corroborated with business accounts or contemporary newspapers. No exact strike identity is assigned from inference.

**Physical survival gap.** This research does not establish whether a particular historic shop interior survives, who owns it or whether entry is allowed. The documentary connection is sufficient without claiming physical access.

**Current gate limits.** Winter holidays are published, but short-notice closures and same-day access were not checked by contact. No specific mining-household display was independently verified inside the museum.
