# Mississippi Civil Rights Museum and the Dahmer family’s truck

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP08 · November 21, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

Enter the Two Mississippi Museums at 222 North Street, Jackson. Select the Mississippi Civil Rights Museum galleries. Tuesday–Saturday 9 a.m.–5 p.m., Sunday 1–5 p.m.; the museums advertise free admission on Sundays. Reflection only. [1]

## 2. Story

Vernon Dahmer Sr. ran a farm and a shop near Hattiesburg and led the Forrest County NAACP. A store could do political work: people who could scarcely afford a poll tax could arrange to pay it there. On January 10, 1966, the Ku Klux Klan attacked the family’s home and store. Vernon helped his family escape and died of his injuries. His widow Ellie and their children survived. The museum places him among the local people who organized voter registration and endured the consequences. [2,3,4]

## 3. Then and now

The family kept pieces of their 1958 Ford pickup. Ellie recalled that Vernon’s brother Harold moved the truck and got family members away. A side panel still bore damage. When the museum was preparing to open, family members showed collections staff what remained. The steering wheel and dashboard survived as well. In a later museum interview Vernon Jr. identified pieces of the truck in their case. The people who had used the vehicle helped establish what the metal meant. [3–5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

Gallery 7, Black Empowerment, tells the Dahmer story. The burned household is in Forrest County; the staffed museum and its family testimony are in Jackson. The two museums share a lobby and admissions desk. The civil-rights galleries have their own narrative and exhibits. [1,2]

## 5. Public progression

Start with the family’s work before the attack. Then read the displayed material beside the family’s recollections. Ellie’s 2015 interview with Emilye Crosby is preserved in the national Civil Rights History Project. A visitor can read or listen without leaving the museum for the family’s former property. [2,3]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

Reporting on the transfer used both donation and loan. The named collections official described a loan, and the family’s participation is documented. Present legal title and the precise selection of parts currently in the case were not verified. A later conviction came in 1998. The interval belongs in the story of how long the family waited for accountability. [4,5]
