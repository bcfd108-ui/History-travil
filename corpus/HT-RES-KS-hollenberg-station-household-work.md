# Hollenberg Station — who kept the door open

Kansas OP01 · Draft research for Dash · November 19, 2026

## 2. The story

### The ford before the relay

C. M. Clark described reaching Cottonwood Creek in 1860. In the digitized 1861 edition of his account, “The Stage Company have a station here”. Clark described a winding, steep approach and stones in the ford. His description puts the building in a traveler’s world of water, terrain and care for a team. It directly observes a stage station. The Pony Express relay’s documentary identification rests on separate records and synthesis. [3,4,13]

The NPS historic resource study treats Cottonwood and Hollenberg as names of the same identified stage and Pony Express stop, and places it at the Kansas end of the relay list. The match draws on multiple trail sources, including historical route and station documentation. A relay service depended on a keeper, horses and a place to exchange one effort for the next. The same business also served people on longer, slower journeys with food, clothing, livestock and rest. No signed stationkeeper’s contract or exchange log for the Hollenbergs was retrieved. [3,4,8]

### A household on the road

The Kansas Historical Society credits Gerat and Sophia Hollenberg together. Its education sheet assigns wagon repairs and draft-animal sales to Gerat and describes Sophia caring for and feeding travelers. Those are the operator’s attributed accounts. The surviving business could bring people and their animals to a household’s door, and the house’s growth tells a story longer than the relay service of 1860–61. The state register account starts with a one-room log cabin and describes the evolution into a long building with five rooms, with barns and sheds helping to support the trade. The material history of the building and household is harder to date than the mail service. [4–6]

## 3. Then / now

The ranch building survives on its original site. It served as a farm home in the later nineteenth and twentieth centuries and was purchased by the state in 1941, according to the nomination and archive description. It is now an interpreted state historic site. The developing structure documented by preservation sources and the later photographic archive make the building a surviving piece of the story even as questions about construction phases remain. [3,5,8,9,12]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The site overview and historic resource study use 1857 for the establishment or ranch house. The state register page says the Hollenbergs moved the business to the present site in 1858. The state education sheet says they built the station in 1859. The records do not establish that these statements refer to a single construction event. A dated deed or building account may resolve the sequence. [4–7]
- The visitor and archive copy uses 1961 for “listed.” State register detail dates the landmark designation November 5, 1961 and National Register listing October 15, 1966. NPGallery confirms 66000352 for the National Register entry. [2,5,8,9]
- An NPS place article calls the building unaltered. The historic resource study qualifies that wording, the state register documents enlargement and Kansas Memory captions a view after restoration. Distinguish original location, historic alterations and preservation treatments. Exact fabric phase and restoration scopes are open. [3–5,10]
- Clark’s passage was checked in a digitized 1861 edition and against the NPS excerpt. The separate antiquarian book page returned HTTP 403 and supplied no text used here. A signed local contract and a first-person statement from Sophia were not retrieved. [3,4,11,13]

## 7. Historical photo record

Kansas Memory item 228955, DaRT 228955, dates its multi-view record to 1963. Its separate item 300 describes a view after restoration. With the site closed and no verified permitted standing point, the surviving building and published photos do not establish a repeatable shot here. No Photo Point. [1,9,10]
