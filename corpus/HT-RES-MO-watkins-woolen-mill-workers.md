# Watkins Woolen Mill — The workers between the belts

Missouri OP02 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**The work began before a loom moved.** The updated landmark nomination identifies immigrant carder Thomas McGerty, who had trained in Europe. Its company-record summary identifies eleven-year-old Mandy Dangley as a twister in 1866 and records women weaving in the 1860s. Sorting and carding prepared the fiber. Around the power train, placing looms face to face could let a weaver tend several ordinary machines, while a broad or fancy loom demanded closer attention. The nomination reconstructs these jobs from the equipment and archival sources. No personal account by McGerty or Dangley has been retrieved. [5]

**A sales notice described what the factory wanted a customer to know.** In the *Liberty Tribune* of Oct. 27, 1882, W. L. Watkins & Son announced that “OUR WOOLEN MILLS” were “in full operation.” The printed advertisement offers custom work including spinning and roll carding. It puts a public sales voice beside the quieter archive of wages, housing and tasks. The two quoted phrases were checked against the newspaper image. They announce operating status at the time of the advertisement and do not establish a year-round schedule. [6]

**Wages could mean several things.** The nomination discusses daily rates, cash and farm goods recorded in store ledgers, and piece rates for weavers. Its cited 1872 letter from John Watkins describes evaluating a prospective carder called John Rupp. A recommendation is evidence of hiring needs, not proof that Rupp worked here. Workers could shift to agricultural tasks in the off-season. Three people identified as enslaved by Watkins in census schedules cannot be assigned to individual mill jobs from those tabulations. The loss and coercion in the labor record calls for quiet witness. [5]

## 3. Then and now

The historical location is the Watkins family’s integrated milling and farm complex, including the woolen factory whose construction state interpretation dates to the Civil War era. Production equipment remains an important source for how work was arranged. The nomination describes worker housing and archaeological traces separately from the currently public mill and museum. [5, 7–8]

The lawful anchor is the **Watkins Woolen Mill State Historic Site Visitor Center/Museum**, 26600 Park Road North near Lawson. Use the signed historic-site driveway and visitor lot, then the designated visitor paths. The public mill interior requires the operator’s tour and available staffing. The state directs visitors to call for tour times. The visitor center is the ordinary interior alternative. Keep the campground road, east-dam day-use parking and closed recreation facilities outside this visit. [1–4]

## 5. A short visit

Start at the visitor center with McGerty’s and Dangley’s documented occupations. Follow open signs toward the mill. Inside only on a scheduled operator tour, read how belts and shafting distributed motion to the workstations. Finish with **The workers between the belts**. Pair the newspaper’s sales message with the difference between a pay rate and a worker’s experience. Read from the museum if tour access is unavailable, or use the newspaper and nomination remotely in severe weather. [1–8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The newspaper is a dated advertisement. Its claim of full operation coexists with evidence of seasonal variations, and says nothing about an individual pay packet. Piecework and daily-rate figures have different scopes. Company and family records named some workers but the original store ledger, Rupp letter and individual payroll evidence were not retrieved. The direct updated-nomination URL returned an HTML “404 – Page not found” page, while the indexed PDF text delivered the cited pages. The HAER west-view web item returned HTTP 403 and the initial raw item JSON request timed out. The oral-history collection was available as a catalog description about the preservation organization, rather than firsthand nineteenth-century worker evidence. Current holiday coverage remains to confirm because the operator labels the displayed list 2025–26. [1, 5–7, 9]
