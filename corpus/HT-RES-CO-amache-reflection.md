## 2. The story
**Min Tonai**, interviewed by **Tom Ikeda in Los Angeles on September 2, 2010**, remembers being a ninth-grade pupil at Amache when the principal asked him to teach his own algebra class during a teacher transition. He recalls receiving no lesson plans, preparing ahead and conducting the class for about a month. His emphasis is on his classmates' cooperation, not on making himself a hero: **“That's why I say I give 'em credit.”** The printed transcript also preserves his uncertainty about the departing teacher's name and destination. It should retain that uncertainty. [4]

The preceding segment complicates any easy school-success story. Tonai recalls varied teaching quality and girls' household responsibilities limiting after-school participation. He also remembers responding to news that people could leave camp with worries about food, housing and his parents' losses. His approximate date for that classroom exchange is late 1944 or early 1945, not an exact reconstructed day. These are his recollections of particular experiences, not a statistical description of every student or family. [5]

The contrast matters: people worked to learn and support one another while deprived of ordinary freedom. Their accomplishments do not justify the confinement that made such improvisation necessary. Nor did permission to leave automatically restore a household. This is the dossier's interpretive argument, not a quotation from the interview.

Both complete segment transcripts were retrieved and read after the web opener failed; no claim is made to have watched the recording or read the whole interview. Densho supplies the testimony; the present museum is a separate interpretive venue, not an asserted holder or exhibitor of these exact interview segments. [4,5]

## 3. Then / now
- APS dates Amache's operation from **August 27, 1942 to October 15, 1945**, identifies its relationship to the War Relocation Authority, and describes people detained there. This establishes confinement context rather than a voluntary school-community origin story. [6]
- **September 2, 2010:** Tonai's interview date, decades after the events. [4,5]
- **Today:** APS describes a student-volunteer preservation and educational organization established by **John Hopper**, including museum work and historical research. This is the organization's published account of its work, not a firsthand student interview collected for this draft. [7]

The recurring educational theme connects past testimony and present stewardship without equating today's volunteers with incarcerated children.
