## 2. The story

**Named firsthand family voice: Clifton Lewis**, speaking about the family's Wigwam Motel preservation and reopening in a September 29, 2026 Arizona's Family report, syndicated by Gray stations. The indexed article text identifies his daughter Karina alongside him. His father was Chester Lewis. Clifton says: **“Many people are grateful that we have preserved it, and that was our main reason in opening it.”** [4]

This is a short published interview quotation, not an unedited oral-history transcript or a contemporaneous 1950 account. Indexed text was read in the syndication; direct article opens failed and the video was not watched. The statement supports Clifton's own explanation of preservation, not a claim that he personally witnessed every earlier building decision.

The motel's own history names Clifton, Paul and Elinor as the children involved in its later restoration. That is family/business interpretation, not an independent payroll or restoration ledger. [5]

**Original interpretive voice:** The photograph makes a roadside business look effortless: a sign, a room, a car pulled up for the night. The family account points somewhere less visible. A place survives because people return to it, put work into it, and decide that welcoming another traveler is worth doing again. Preservation here is not simply keeping a building. It is keeping a working relationship between a household, a town and people passing through.

No housekeeper's firsthand account, wage book or complete staff roster was retrieved. This family-owner voice is a starting point, not a substitute for everyone who performed hospitality labor.

## 3. Then / now

The NPS identifies the motel as completed in 1950 and later reopened by the Lewis family in 1988. It describes removed gas pumps and a converted office museum. Those physical changes matter: today's nostalgic presentation is not an untouched 1950 workplace. [6]

The independent courthouse museum is the public interpretive anchor for this dossier, **not the historical motel workplace**. The City describes Route 66 material among its local collections. It does not promise a Clifton Lewis interview, Wigwam payroll, or particular motel object on display. [2]

The motel operator itself acknowledges that its commercial name uses “wigwam” for tipi-shaped accommodation. This entry treats the design as roadside marketing, not as an authoritative representation of any Indigenous nation's domestic life. No Tribal cultural history is inferred from commercial architecture. [5]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Chronology conflict:** the motel's history page says operation lasted into the late 1970s but also says it closed in 1974, an internal inconsistency. It gives reopening as 1988. A secondary summary of 2017 Arizona Republic reporting instead gives closure in 1980 and reopening in 1987. NPS uses 1988. These dates remain attributed and unresolved, not silently averaged. [5,6,7]
- The September 2026 report attributes a May 1950 opening to Clifton; the broader NPS year agrees, but an original opening advertisement was not inspected. [4,6]
- Hospitality ownership/family preservation is evidenced; the distribution of paid and unpaid work, staffing, pay and working conditions remains gapped.
- No complete interview transcript or recording review, exact museum display match, holiday calendar, detailed accessible entrance description or museum photo policy was established.
- The listed archive resources do not prove that the museum holds a particular person's papers. No research request was sent. [3]
- No evidence here settles Indigenous perspectives on the commercial imagery. Do not use the motel as a proxy for Tribal interpretation.
