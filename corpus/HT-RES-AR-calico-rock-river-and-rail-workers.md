# Calico Rock — A grocer and a newspaper on the same block

**HT-RES-AR-calico-rock-river-and-rail-workers · AR OP06 · Labeled research draft · November 20, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate
The museum operator, checked Nov. 20, 2026, lists free entry at 105 Main St. Monday–Saturday 10 a.m.–4 p.m., with Sundays and major holidays closed. Call ahead to confirm. Use open exhibits and public sidewalk where safe. The street rises through town. End a sidewalk visit at any closure, steep section beyond your ability, or unsafe crossing. ## 2. Story
Around 1906 Nick Rand built two stone buildings on the west side of Main Street, one known in the district inventory as the Calico Rock Grocery Store and the other as the Calico Rock Progressive Newspaper Building. The nomination traces changing uses over the following years. The two building numbers, CR-4 and CR-5, let a reader check the relationship without assuming that their present occupants reproduce those uses. A June 1924 issue held by the Library of Congress fixes the paper’s locally published identity on the page as The Calico Rock Progress. The newspaper is independent evidence for its named title and date. It gives no proof by itself of the issue’s printing address. [2, 3]

River shipping and ferries had already connected this hillside town to customers before the railroad brought construction work and a wider trading network. The 1985 nomination distinguishes farmers doing wage work from those paid more when they supplied teams. It names Frank Aiken as a ferry license applicant in 1896. The application and payroll have yet to be checked. By April 1923 the railroad’s proximity also mattered when a fire spread through commercial buildings. Comparing materials and dates on the block brings that transition down to one working street. [2, 4]

## 3. Then and now
The historic district remains centered on Main Street’s commercial buildings. The museum’s current four-building footprint includes a general store exhibit. Treat displays and the operator’s interpretive steamboat as interpretation. A Library of Congress Sanborn map catalog confirms two digitized sheets for 1913. Those sheets predate the 1923 rebuilding and need visual comparison before using them as an overlay. [1, 2, 5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

## 5. Visit progression
Start with the staffed museum. If the sidewalk and conditions allow, compare the two west-side storefronts on a single block. Finish with **a shop beside a newspaper office**, using the old issue’s masthead and the paired building inventory to read the street as a place where goods and news moved. No crossing or rear-yard access is needed for the reading. [1–3]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
The independent Arkansas encyclopedia dates arrival of freight and passenger service to 1903. The nomination refers to line completion in 1904. Those milestones may differ. A timetable or company engineering record is needed to resolve the sequence. LOC gives June 6, 1924 page metadata; the issue’s article text and the Sanborn pixels were not read successfully. Aikens’s license, Rand deed or notice, and a contemporary fire report remain open. [2–5]
