## 2. The story

In the fall of 1907, the **Reverend O. P. Christian** of Eau Claire, Wisconsin — representing the National Children's Home Finding Society — wrote to Idaho Governor **Frank R. Gooding** proposing a non-criminal home-finding society for Idaho's abandoned and neglected children. Gooding replied in support, noting Idaho already had custodial reform provisions at St. Anthony for delinquent youth but lacked structured care for non-delinquent homeless children. Christian arrived in Boise in April 1908, and on **April 14, 1908**, state and civic leaders organized the **Children's Home Finding and Aid Society** inside Governor Gooding's Statehouse office. Four days later, on **April 18, 1908**, Boise schoolteacher **Cynthia A. Mann** donated a full block of land along Warm Springs Avenue; the society opened temporary intake operations on **July 22, 1908**, and in 1909 the Idaho Legislature authorized charitable societies to receive and place neglected children for adoption while appropriating **$20,000** contingent on matching public donations. [2]

Boise architects **Tourtellotte & Hummel** (plans attributed to **Charles F. Hummel Sr.**) designed the permanent home on Cynthia Mann's block, with stonework contracted to the **Kinney Brothers** and carpentry to **H. A. Larson** at a total construction cost of **$42,700**. Built of thick sandstone quarried from nearby **Table Rock Quarry**, the two-story Western Colonial Revival building featured a low hipped roof with exposed rafters and dormers, a centered portico with a second-story loggia, separate boys' and girls' dormitories on the second floor, an on-site Boise public schoolroom, a nurse- and volunteer-physician-staffed hospital/operating space, and an exterior-access rear isolation room for flu, colds, and contagious illnesses. Dedicated on **December 28, 1910**, the Boise home served **326 children** in its first two years and handled statewide intake until a North Idaho branch opened in Lewiston in 1914. [1, 2]

In **1934**, Tourtellotte & Hummel returned to design a **two-story reinforced concrete infirmary** at the right rear of the sandstone home — a plain, square-planned clinical structure with an enclosed outset porch, balcony, and tall multi-light casement windows (later complemented by a 1940 north wing linked by an underground tunnel). After federal and state child-welfare shifts in **1966** replaced institutional orphanages with foster care, the campus operated as a residential treatment facility for troubled youth (**1970–1975**, following a 1970–71 interior remodel by Charles F. Hummel's grandson reducing capacity from over 100 beds to 37) and then transitioned in **1975** to outpatient mental-health care as the **Warm Springs Counseling Center**. A major **$1.4 million** interior renovation in **1997** (supported by 1996, 1997, and 1999 Idaho Heritage Trust preservation grants) modernized clinical suites while preserving the 1910 sandstone exterior. [1, 2, 3, 4]

### Haunted history / lore

No verified local haunting tradition or supernatural claim was identified for the Warm Springs Avenue facility in the historical or preservation sources reviewed, and no lore is fabricated. Note that this is an active children's counseling clinic; institutional hardships from the orphanage era are documented strictly as social and medical history.

## 4. What's still there

- **1910 Table Rock sandstone main building:** Raised foundation, thick quarried sandstone walls, overhanging eaves with exposed rafters, hipped-roof dormers, and central two-story portico facing Warm Springs Avenue (upper loggia enclosed). [1, 6]
- **Porch & institutional signage:** Carved/applied lettering visible on the street-facing portico reading `"CHILDREN'S HOME"` and `"WARM SPRINGS COUNSELING CENTER"`. [6]
- **1934 Tourtellotte & Hummel concrete infirmary (rear/right):** Documented on the historic-district inventory as part of Site #60 behind the 1910 building (visibility from the street is limited; do not trespass onto clinic grounds to view rear buildings). [1]
- **Roadside historical marker:** Publicly readable at the Warm Springs Avenue street edge. [7]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Sweep-list NRHP number correction:** The Idaho sweep list entered **`NRHP 78001084`** for this entry. Primary NPGallery verification shows `78001084` is actually the **Henry C. Kettenbach Residence in Lewiston, Idaho** (Nez Perce County). The Boise Children's Home at `740 E. Warm Springs Ave.` is **Site #60** inside the **Warm Springs Avenue Historic District (`NRHP 80001287`)**, listed in 1980. (A separate 2007 NRHP listing, `07000090`, covers the North Idaho Children's Home branch in Lewiston.) [1, 8, 9]
- **Cornerstone vs. dedication interval conflict (`May 14, 1908` vs. `Dec. 28, 1910`):** The 2008 Ada County Historic Preservation centennial brochure states the Masonic Lodge laid the cornerstone on **`May 14, 1908`**, and then writes *"The dedication of the Home took place seven months later on December 28, 1910."* Those two dates are **31 months apart**, not seven months — indicating either the cornerstone date was **May 14, 1910** (consistent with the 1909 legislative matching appropriation and the NRHP `1910` construction date) or "seven months" was a brochure error. Original 1908–1910 *Idaho Statesman* issues needed to resolve the cornerstone date were inaccessible behind LOC's Cloudflare block. [1, 2]
- **Rear-wing photography & marker text:** No verified public-license photograph of the 1934 concrete infirmary at rear or of the roadside marker plaque text was adopted this run (HMdb `267450` was consulted strictly as a lead finder; its text and photos were not copied). [7]
