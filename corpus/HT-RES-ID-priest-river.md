## 2. The story
Priest River offers a useful counterpoint to a logging story told only through saws and log drives: the timber resource also became a place where people tested how forests grow and how fire behaves. The Forest Service established the Experimental Forest in 1911. Its current account names researchers including **J. A. Larsen, Harry Gisborne, Ken Davis, Charles Wellner and Irvine Haig**, connecting their work with forest management and fire research. [1]

The most vivid primary record recovered is not a grand dedication speech. It is an office memo about snow. On **March 22, 1949**, **H. T. Gisborne, Chief, Division of Fire Research**, wrote to **Chas. L. Tebbe, Director**, under the subject **“RF-NRM, SUPERVISION, Quarters – Priest River.”** He explained that photographs of February 10 conditions illustrated **“the difficulties of research work due to snow-shoveling requirements.”** The memo image itself was downloaded and visually read; the date is visible, unlike the catalog's approximate-year field. [6]

Gisborne also described using the photographs to show architects the consequences of building, drainage and shrub-placement choices. His handwritten continuation complains about the state of the photographic record, but its full wording is not transcribed here because portions are difficult to read. [6]

Original interpretive line: *A research forest needs more than trees and instruments. Someone must clear the way to work, keep the building usable and preserve a record good enough for the next person to understand.* That connects forestry science to ordinary work without pretending Gisborne spoke those words.

The memo is evidence about working conditions and institutional communication, not a modern weather report. It does not prove that a present driveway is snowbound or that today's buildings are unsafe. The timber-museum component is kept separate: local logging interpretation should be supported by actual exhibits and operating records, not inferred from the fact that the surrounding country is forested.

## 4. What's still there

**Public access rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance. These access classifications are separate from the locator record.
- **Experimental Forest:** Forest Service research continues in forest ecology, genetics, fire, disease and hydrology. Facilities are for research/education, with residential accommodation described for users; these are not advertised public vacation rentals. [1]
- **Historic compound:** registration documentation and archival photographs identify surviving/replaced structures, but no current building-by-building visitor inspection was performed. Do not claim every 1914 cottage survives. [8,9]
- **Town museum:** a separate proposed public interpretive destination, subject to resolving its conflicting addresses and hours. Do not attach its public purpose to the Experimental Forest compound. [3–5]
- **Public digital archive:** readable period evidence available without field access. This is the ready component now, not a substitute counted as an in-person visit. [6–8]

Do not disturb weather instruments, sample plots, tagged trees, experimental treatments or access barriers. No evidence establishes a self-guided public circuit through these features.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
The memo catalog dates the record approximately 1949; the image gives **March 22, 1949**. Its referenced snow photographs concern **February 10,1949**, a different date. Keep memo date, event date and catalog approximation distinct. [6]

The Forest Service summary dates CCC headquarters buildings 1934–39; the University collection history says 1935–42. These may describe different sets of structures or phases. No single construction span is assigned to all buildings; individual nomination entries require separate reconciliation. [1,2,9]

The archive sometimes labels features “Priest Creek” although the institution and nomination use Priest River. Preserve source wording as a catalog variant, not a new destination. The nomination's Sandpoint vicinity/zip is a registration locator, not visitor directions. [6,9]

The museum address conflict is unresolved. Do not copy the Boundary County museum website's footer hours/fees from Bonners Ferry onto Priest River's museum. Likewise the Chamber page's visitor-center entry and footer differ in suite/hours; neither supplies a reliable substitute museum entrance. [3,5]

No local mill payroll, named logging-camp labor roster, shipping ledger or log-drive account was independently read. Named primary work evidence here is specifically **forestry research**, not a claim to have completed town logging history. The 1994 nomination was downloaded and its property/classification fields inspected; the full architectural chronology was not audited. No Tribal narrative specific to these parcels was established, and the 1911 station start is not presented as the beginning of human presence.
