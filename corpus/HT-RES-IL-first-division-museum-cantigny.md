# First Division Museum at Cantigny — the Big Red One's museum on Colonel McCormick's estate
HT-RES-IL-first-division-museum-cantigny · Illinois · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

The museum's ground and its subject were joined in France on May 28, 1918. The 1st Division — organized in June 1917 and, in the Association of the U.S. Army's phrase, the Army's longest continuously serving division — had been the earliest American combat division in France. Opposite the German-held village of Cantigny, northwest of Montdidier, the division made the first divisional attack by the American Expeditionary Forces in the First World War. The 28th Infantry Regiment led the assault, reinforced by companies of the 18th Infantry, behind American and French artillery, with French tanks and flamethrower teams in support. The village was taken by noon; the division then held it through three days of German counterattacks and bombardment. The American Battle Monuments Commission's tour account puts the cost at 1,603 casualties, including 199 killed. Small as the operation was beside the Aisne fighting then underway, ABMC records its effect plainly: it showed the Allies — and the German command — that large American units could attack, take ground, and hold it. [3, 5, 6, 7]

Commanding the 1st Battalion, 5th Field Artillery in support of that attack was a thirty-seven-year-old citizen-soldier who was also publisher of the *Chicago Tribune*: Robert R. McCormick. The battle marked him permanently. He renamed his family's 500-acre Wheaton estate — Red Oaks, his grandfather Joseph Medill's country place — Cantigny, after the village, and for years hosted 1st Division reunions there. When he died on April 1, 1955, his will left the estate in trust as a park for the people of Illinois, under what is now the Robert R. McCormick Foundation. Cantigny Park opened to the public in 1958. [3, 4, 8, 9]

The museum his trustees built to honor the division opened in 1960 as the Cantigny War Memorial Museum of the First Division, housed in the estate's renovated stables — the building that is now the park's visitors center. In 1992 it moved into its present, much larger building. A ten-month renovation reopened it on August 26, 2017, for the division's centennial, in two galleries that still structure the visit. "First in War" follows the division from 1917 through Vietnam — a walk-through World War I trench, the Omaha Beach landing experienced from wooden benches, and a Vietnam jungle. "Duty First," a 2,500-square-foot gallery in the former temporary-exhibit space, covers the division from the 1970s to today, with interactive and virtual-reality experiences including a Bradley Fighting Vehicle simulation of the 1991 "Fright Night" and a helicopter raid in Iraq. Outside stands the Tank Park: eleven tanks from World War I through Operation Desert Storm, with artillery pieces, on ground the Cantigny site invites visitors to explore up close. The museum also holds the Colonel Robert R. McCormick Research Center, its library and archives. The museum tells the division's whole century, but it stands where it stands because of one morning in 1918 and the artilleryman who brought the village's name home with him. [3, 4, 8, 9, 10]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** Two locations, on two continents. The battle ground is Cantigny, Somme, France, where the ABMC's Cantigny American Monument marks the village center the 28th Infantry took on May 28, 1918; McCormick spoke at that monument's dedication in 1937. The Illinois ground is the former Medill/McCormick estate at Wheaton — Red Oaks renamed Cantigny — which is commemorative ground, not battle ground: nothing of the division's fighting happened here, and the dossier keeps that distinction explicit. [5, 6, 8]

**Lawful physical anchor.** First Division Museum, Cantigny, 1S151 Winfield Road, Wheaton, IL 60189 — staffed museum interior plus the outdoor Tank Park, reached through Cantigny's public park entrance with per-vehicle admission. The wider park (gardens, McCormick House, picnic grounds, golf) is the same operator and the same gate, but the museum and Tank Park are this dossier's visit; the McCormick House opens only on select dates in the operator's current materials and is not promised here. [1, 2, 10]

## 5. Public progression

Pay the per-vehicle admission at the Cantigny gate and start at the visitors center — the 1960 museum's own first home — for orientation, then walk to the present museum. Take "First in War" in its own order: the trench first, so the name Cantigny lands where the museum intends it, at the start of the division's war and not as a label on a building. Move through Omaha Beach and the jungle, then cross into "Duty First" for the division's present tense, told in veterans' voices. Finish outside in the Tank Park, where the machines carry the same chronology in steel. **Strongest story moment · An artilleryman's address.** The museum exists at 1S151 Winfield Road because a battalion commander renamed his home for a French village his guns had helped take — the estate itself is the first exhibit, before any gallery door opens. End in the park's gardens if time allows; the contrast between the trench recreation and the grounds McCormick left "for the people of Illinois" is the site's own closing argument. Reflection posture throughout the galleries: these exhibits carry named soldiers' deaths, including a Medal of Honor donated by the soldier's parents. [1–4, 8, 10]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- Hours conflict printed in full: the First Division Museum homepage banner checked this run reads "Open 10am–5pm Wednesday through Monday," with no season stated. Cantigny's hours-and-fees page gives the controlling seasonal table: museum Wed.–Mon. 10–5 (Mar.–Nov. 15), Wed.–Sun. 10–3 (Nov. 18–Jan. 3), closed Dec. 29–Feb. 5, Fri.–Sun. 10–5 (Feb. 6–28), and closed Thanksgiving (Nov. 26), Christmas Eve and Day, and New Year's Eve and Day in the 2026 holiday list. Older third-party listings giving Tuesday–Sunday hours are superseded by both. Follow the seasonal table. [1, 2]
- Parking-fee drift: the operator's 2026 fees are $6 daytime parking January–March, $6 weekday / $16 weekend through November 15, $21 on concert and special-event nights, and $6 daily in November–December ($11 after 3 p.m. during Christmas at Cantigny), cashless, last admission 30 minutes before closing. Older museum coverage quoting $5 weekday / $10 weekend parking is out of date and is not used here. [1]
- Cantigny casualty figures conflict in the record: ABMC's battlefield-tour account gives 1,603 casualties including 199 killed; other published tallies of the same battle give a higher killed figure (318 appears in secondary summaries). This dossier follows ABMC, the federal monuments authority for the ground, and prints the variance rather than averaging it. German losses and prisoners (about 200–250 captured in ABMC-based accounts) likewise vary by source. [5, 6]
- Tank count framing: the museum's own and reopening accounts count 11 tanks in the Tank Park; some secondary coverage says "a dozen" vehicles. The park's display also includes an armored personnel carrier and artillery pieces in some accounts, which likely explains the drift. This dossier uses the operator's 11-tank figure. [2, 3, 4]
- McCormick's exact artillery role is stated differently across sources — battalion commander of 1st Battalion, 5th Field Artillery (Society for Military History and the 2005 Cantigny, France monument text) is the best-supported form; looser retellings that promote him to command of the attack itself exceed the record. The attack was the 28th Infantry's, with McCormick's battalion in artillery support. [6, 8]
- Research Center public access hours were not published on the pages checked this run; researchers should contact the museum before planning archival work.
- McCormick House tour availability is by select dates only in current operator materials; no house interior is promised in this dossier.
