## 2. The story

Founded in **1736** through a bequest by French sailor **Jean Louis** as a hospital for New Orleans' indigent poor, **Charity Hospital** occupied six successive buildings across nearly three centuries. Its sixth and largest monument—affectionately known statewide as **"Big Charity"**—was constructed with Public Works Administration (**PWA**) backing in **1938** and opened for patients in **1939** at `1532 Tulane Avenue` (`NRHP 10001173`, listed January 24, 2011). [1]

Designed by leading New Orleans architectural firm **Weiss, Dreyfous & Seiferth** in stepped **Modern Classic / Art Deco** style, the massive steel-frame limestone-clad tower quickly anchored the downtown New Orleans skyline. Two detached **Streamlined Modern waiting pavilions** flank the front court along Tulane Avenue, linked to the main entrance by flat-roofed breezeways. Above the curved Tulane Avenue main portal, sculptor **Enrique Alférez** created an intricate cast-aluminum Art Deco grille, reverse-incised lettering (`CHARITY HOSPITAL OF LOUISIANA`), and a horizontal frieze of small **bas-relief panels** depicting stylized Louisiana flora, fauna, and medical instruments (later supplemented by sans-serif lettering honoring Louisiana civil rights leader **Rev. Avery C. Alexander**). [1]

For sixty-six years (`1939–2005`), Big Charity served as Louisiana's premier public safety-net hospital and clinical training ground for LSU and Tulane medical schools—handling generations of emergency trauma, public health, and indigent care across the Gulf South until floodwaters and infrastructure failure following **Hurricane Katrina in August 2005** forced its evacuation and permanent closure as a hospital (succeeded later by University Medical Center New Orleans nearby). [1, 3]

After two decades of vacancy and stalled proposals, **Tulane University** announced on **June 23, 2026**, that it had signed a purchase-and-sale agreement with original developer `1532 Tulane Holdco, LLC` to assume ownership and lead a **$500 million** (part of a $700M downtown investment) adaptive reuse—dedicating >650,000 square feet of the 1-million-square-foot landmark to Tulane's School of Public Health and Tropical Medicine, Innovation Institute, School of Medicine, and labs for ~700 researchers, alongside housing, food hall, and community spaces, with completion targeted for **2029** under NPS/SHPO historic preservation review. [3, 4]

### Haunted history / lore

No haunted-place folklore is claimed or carried here. Charity Hospital's weight rests squarely on its 1736–2005 public-health mission, PWA architecture, Enrique Alférez's artwork, and post-Katrina resilience. [1]

## 4. What's still there

- **Visible from public city sidewalks (outside fencing):** Full limestone stepped **Modern Classic / Art Deco tower**, two Streamlined Modern front waiting pavilions and breezeways along Tulane Avenue, **Enrique Alférez** aluminum doorway grille and medical bas-reliefs, and Tulane/LaSalle/Gravier street elevations. [1, 5]
- **Closed / non-public:** Entire building interior and fenced superblock plaza (entering active construction/redevelopment). [3, 5]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Foundation settling & fire stairs:** `NRHP 10001173` notes the heavy steel-frame building settled over **18 inches** shortly after 1938–39 completion and gained exterior fire exits later; exact engineering remediation details were outside this run's scope. [1]
- **Exact fall 2026 groundbreaking date:** Tulane's June 23, 2026 release targeted fall 2026 financial closing/groundbreaking (and an Oct. 21, 2026 energy panel discussed ongoing NPS/SHPO preservation design), without posting a final closing date as of late November 2026. [3, 4]
