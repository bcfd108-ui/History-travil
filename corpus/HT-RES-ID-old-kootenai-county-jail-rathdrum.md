# Old Kootenai County Jail, Rathdrum — the last building of a lost county seat

## 2. The story

Kootenai County was created by the territorial legislature in 1864 but did not organize a government until July 1881, after the Northern Pacific Railroad brought settlers across the panhandle. By October 1881 the commissioners had chosen **Westwood (soon renamed Rathdrum)** as the county seat. One of their first acts was a jail: a **$540** frame building, bid in October 1881 and inspected in January 1882. [4]

Rathdrum boomed as the closest railhead to the 1883 Coeur d'Alene mining rush, passing 1,000 residents by 1884, but a Northern Pacific branch to Coeur d'Alene in 1886 began shifting the county's weight south. The jail was a running embarrassment. A planned 1889 brick jail was stopped by a district-court restraining order. In **March 1891** a grand jury condemned the existing jail as "insecure and a disgrace to the county." The commissioners ordered a four-cell steel cage from a Helena, Montana firm; A. D. Robinson laid its foundation and T. E. Anderson put a minimal building around it for **$185** (August 1891). A September 1891 grand jury demanded "a good substantial brick building around the steel cells," and after a November 1891 taxpayer petition the board agreed in January 1892. [4]

The county paid the Missoula firm of **Galbraith & Fuller** $200 for plans. **Isaac J. Galbraith** (trained in Pittsburgh; later known for Montana's building at the 1893 Chicago fair and for Lewiston work 1901–04) was the senior partner; which Fuller was his partner is unresolved (see §6). **O'Sullivan Brothers** of Coeur d'Alene won the contract at **$5,500** in March 1892. Building changes — thicker second-story walls, a cut-granite sill course in place of brick, a corrugated-iron roof in place of shingles, a concrete floor under the cells, and a ceiling over them — added nearly **$1,800**; final payment was **August 17, 1892**. The rear cell room held a free-standing, two-tier block of eight steel cells reached by a catwalk; **A. J. Hasom** added a riveted steel lining to the cell room for **$3,990** (finished January 1893), which an independent inspector valued at over $4,000. Upstairs, five small front cells served as holding cells and, per the nomination, confinement for "mentally unstable prisoners." [4]

The prisoners themselves are almost absent from the record reviewed. The nomination's sources are the commissioners' journals, grand-jury reports and newspapers about money and politics; no inmate register, named prisoner, escape, or death in custody was found in the sources read this run. That silence is printed as a gap rather than filled with stories. [4]

The county-seat fight ended the building's jail life. After a 1905 division law was struck down by the Idaho Supreme Court, the 1907 legislature split off Bonner County, and in **November 1908** Kootenai voters moved the seat to Coeur d'Alene, **6,064 to 2,020**. Rathdrum's boosters had argued the jail (capacity **thirty-two prisoners**) and courthouse were adequate; within days records went to Coeur d'Alene. In **March 1910** the county sold the old courthouse and jail to W. H. Edelblute for **$450**; the courthouse became a National Guard armory, and the jail passed to town use. [4]

Over the next ninety years the jail was Rathdrum's town hall, library and city maintenance shop, vacated by early 1999. It was listed in the National Register in **2001** (nomination by Nancy F. Renk, Flume Creek Historical Services, March 12, 2001). R/WHS (incorporated 1999) bought it from the city in **2006** to prevent demolition, restored it with private funds, and opened it as a museum in **2012**. Summer sign-in visitors rose from 178 (2021) to 446 (2024). [2, 4, 5]

## 4. What's still there

- **Public (museum season / private tour):** the 1892 brick jail with granite sill course, round-arched entry, and the second-floor cells described as surviving in 2001; plaster walls, wood floors and trim described as original. Museum exhibits on Rathdrum/Westwood history. [2, 4, 5]
- **Visible from the public sidewalk:** the northwest-facing 2nd Street facade. A 2014 photograph (§7) shows a **bell tower now present** above the center bay, which the 2001 nomination recorded as removed before the 1960s — a reconstruction whose date and documentation were not found (see §6). [4, 7]
- **Gone:** the 1881 frame jail, the 1885 frame courthouse (replaced by a brick Moose Lodge hall by 1932, per the nomination), the women's ward, and the steel cell block. [4]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Arrive at 7940 W. 2nd St.** and use legal street or museum parking. Out of season, view the facade from the sidewalk only. [1]
2. **Read the facade:** three bays, granite sill course at the second story, barred small upper windows, the round-arched entry. [4]
3. **In season (from June 5, 2027) or by arranged private tour,** go inside for the cell room and the five upstairs cells, and the museum's county-seat story. [1, 2]
4. **Close on the county-seat loss:** the 1908 vote that ended Rathdrum's role, the reason this is the only county building left from the period. [4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Street address:** The 2001 nomination gives **802 Second Street**; the operator and current geocoders give **7940 W 2nd St** (Census and ArcGIS PointAddress agree within ~10 m, and the nomination's UTM falls within ~20 m). The renumbering date was not found. [1, 4, 8]
- **Why the brick jail was built:** The nomination (citing commissioners' journals and the *Kootenai Herald*, Sept. 19, 1891) attributes it to grand-jury condemnations and taxpayer petitions. The operator's page says "the city sued the jail" over a hole in the floor. No lawsuit appears in the nomination; the operator's account is printed, not adopted. [4, 5]
- **Cell supplier:** operator — "Mason and Curtis of Helena, Montana"; nomination — an unnamed Helena firm. Whether the eight cells installed in 1892 include the four from 1891 is unresolved in the nomination itself. [4, 5]
- **Upper-floor use:** the nomination describes two rooms, a bathroom, and five holding/confinement cells upstairs, and lists a separate "county hospital" among Rathdrum's 1908 assets. The society's 2025 letter says the jail's multi-purpose rooms served "as a temporary hospital and housing for the poor"; the operator's page says "temporary accommodations for the community." Not reconciled against a primary record. [2, 4, 5]
- **Fuller's identity:** an 1890 Missoula directory lists Irwin Fuller and J. R. Fuller; the nomination does not resolve which was Galbraith's partner. [4]
- **Belfry:** recorded as removed (probably before the 1960s) in 2001; a tower is present in a 2014 photograph. Reconstruction date, funding, and documentary basis not found.
- **Inmates:** no inmate records, named prisoners, or custodial deaths located this run. Kootenai County Commissioners Journals (Books A–B) and court records at the Kootenai County Recorder / Museum of North Idaho were not consulted directly.
- **Summer hours:** not posted; only "every weekend in the summer" (2025 letter).
- **Newspapers not retrieved directly:** LOC Chronicling America returned a Cloudflare challenge from the research box; newspaper citations come from the nomination's endnotes.
