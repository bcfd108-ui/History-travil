# HT-RES-CT-woodstock-roseland-cottage — Woodstock: the pink cottage
Division CT seed-catalog • Research draft • Checked November 25, 2026

Roseland Cottage is the pink one — a Gothic Revival summer house built in 1846 for Henry and Lucy Bowen, with ornamental vergeboards, a boxwood parterre garden, and, in its carriage barn, what its operator identifies as the oldest surviving indoor bowling alley in the United States. Four presidents came to visit. The money that built it tells a harder story alongside the pretty one.

Roseland Cottage, 556 Route 169, Woodstock, Windham County, Connecticut

**Public access:** Roseland Cottage is a seasonal museum operated by Historic New England. Tours run Thursday through Sunday, June through October, with paid interior admission; designated visitor parking is provided behind the barn. Unrestricted off-season grounds access has not been established — this is not a year-round freely accessible site. Confirm the season and hours with Historic New England before visiting.

## The story

Henry Chandler Bowen established a successful commercial career in New York before returning to his hometown of Woodstock to build this summer residence in 1846. Roseland became a gathering place for political and social figures, including four American presidents.

Its history also exposes the contradictions of nineteenth-century wealth. Bowen supported abolition, yet some of the money that funded the estate came indirectly from commerce dependent upon enslaved labor. Both facts belong to the house; the entry keeps them together.

The architecture and formal gardens illustrate the picturesque residential design that reshaped American domestic taste in the Victorian period — steep gables, decorative vergeboards, and a garden laid out as part of the composition. The interiors are remarkably preserved, and the estate's carriage barn holds its private bowling alley, identified by Historic New England as the oldest surviving indoor bowling alley in the country.

## Then / now

- 1846: Roseland Cottage built as the Bowens' summer retreat.
- Later 1800s: a social and political gathering place; four presidents among its visitors.
- Today: a National Historic Landmark museum of Historic New England, open seasonally.

## What's still there

The cottage itself — pink board-and-batten, Gothic Revival detailing, the parterre garden in front — and the estate buildings behind it, including the carriage barn with the bowling alley. What you see in season is substantially what the Bowens built.

## The stop, step by step

Come in season: Thursday through Sunday, June through October, parking behind the barn. From the authorized visitor grounds, find the distinctive Gothic Revival façade and its ornamental vergeboards. The house tour carries the interiors and the bowling alley from there.
