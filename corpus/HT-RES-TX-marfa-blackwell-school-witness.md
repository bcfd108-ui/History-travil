# Marfa Blackwell School — ‘No one’s going to take away my Spanish’
Research draft · Texas OP07 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

**Maggie Marquez answered back.** She remembered replying, “Nadie me va quitar mi español. No pueden hacernos esto.” The National Register nomination prints those words and an English translation, citing Sterry Butcher’s 2007 Big Bend Sentinel interview. Marquez remembered being overheard, sent to the principal and physically punished. Read the words as a child’s refusal recorded later in her life. The original newspaper and its interview notes were not retrieved. [4, p. 16]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then — a school district imposed a separation.** Marfa assigned its Hispanic children to separate schooling, first elsewhere and from 1909 in a two-room adobe building on South Abbot. The nomination recounts the variation over time: in the 1930s the principal organized competition for Spanish-speaking students, while in 1954 Evelyn Davis organized a schoolwide exercise requiring students to pledge to stop speaking Spanish. Marquez’s later account reports what the exercise cost her. The nomination places the period of significance through 1965, when desegregation closed Blackwell’s separate schooling. [3–4]

**Now — surviving adobe and the record alumni saved.** The foundation document describes former students organizing when demolition was considered in 2006, obtaining a lease and gathering photographs, objects and memories. The federal site was established in July 2024. The loss and the work of keeping its memory belong to the same place. Along with the schoolhouse, a later band hall survives. Parts of the former campus have changed and the nomination boundary is smaller than the historical school landscape. [3–6]

## 4 · What’s still there

- **Public interior only when open.** NPS school interpretation at the signed entrance. Staff and labels govern indoor limits. [1–3]
- **Exterior only on open NPS paths.** Schoolhouse front and surviving band hall exterior. The nomination records alterations to the schoolhouse entry, window replacements and later additions. [3–5]
- **Not accessible under this dossier.** Neighboring parcels, closed grounds, and any attempted location of the 1954 activity. [1,4]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The April 2025 Basic Information page describes possible weekday appointments. The newer superintendent’s compendium gives ordinary public hours as weekends only with holiday and storm closure terms, so the trip gate follows it. The nomination’s Figure 6 date is circa 1930 while its discussion of the entry refers readers to Figure 6 as evidence for an alteration by 1920. Both claims are visible in the same file. The exact alteration date remains unresolved. Nomination quotes from the archived May 2007 article and 1954 Davis memo were read as reproduced in the nomination. Neither underlying item was obtained. [1–2,4]
