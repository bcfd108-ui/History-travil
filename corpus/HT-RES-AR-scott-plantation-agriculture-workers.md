# Plantation Agriculture Museum and Steele–Dortch General Store, Scott
HT-RES-AR-scott-plantation-agriculture-workers · Arkansas OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

Johnnie Tillmon grew up in Arkansas. As an adult she set down some numbers about work that equipment catalogs rarely show. She reported earning about $20–30 a week for fifteen years of laundry work and wrote that she was “picking cotton on the side for carfare.” The words are from her 1972 essay in a preservation transcription attributed to Liberation News Service issue 415. The archive’s text is a first-person source reproduced later. The state encyclopedia traces Tillmon’s birth to Scott in 1926, in a sharecropping family. Her essay does not identify the field, town, employer or gin for her own cotton work. [3, 4]

The storefront in Scott dates from 1912. A post office added in 1929 occupies adjoining space, and the public state museum interprets the work of growing, gathering and processing cotton across generations. The gin and seed warehouse help make sense of where a crop went after harvest. Separate rooms in the surviving complex show different stages of the business. A shopper or postal customer, a person handling seed and a hand in the field would have seen different parts of it. [1, 2, 5]

The state encyclopedia’s Scott history connects Robert Dortch to experimentation with soybeans and the marketing of a cotton seed variety. A separate state account traces his private museum of equipment and the eventual state museum. Those institutional histories preserve a grower’s perspective and a collection of machines. Tillmon’s line gives the visit a different scale. The work she did on the side helped pay for a trip to her other job even when it left little trace on the machinery or in a farm’s records. [1, 3–6]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The historic store and attached post office are part of the museum. The gin and seed warehouse are nearby buildings on the current park campus. The location of the fields Tillmon worked and any connection to the Steele–Dortch business are unresolved. [1–6]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Plantation Agriculture Museum, 4815 AR Hwy 161 South, Scott. Drive up, park where signed and take the short walk to the general-store museum entrance. Use the signed public building approaches during the visit. Separate gates and staff directions govern the gin and warehouse interiors. Nearby plantations, occupied properties, fields and service grounds are outside this visit. [1, 2]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The Scott history links the private museum to a 1964 opening and describes later closure and acquisition. The museum entry’s timeline concerns the state institution and its 1989 opening. The current operator presents the 1912 store as surviving, where the town history also mentions a 2017 fire without clearly specifying its relationship to the museum in that paragraph. Use the extant building and the operator’s scope, with the fire’s exact property held. [1, 5, 6, 7]
- Tillmon’s Liberation News Service version was read from a PDF transcription. The original issue page image, local work rolls, farm identity and named Scott oral-history testimony were not retrieved. A separate newspaper domain search was denied by robots.txt. [3–5]
- The operator confirms both register listings but uses the museum’s property name rather than supplying item numbers. Official records and boundary treatment for the two buildings remain open. [1]
