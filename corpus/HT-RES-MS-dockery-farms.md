# Cotton work and the musicians who learned from each other at Dockery

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP19*

## 2. Story

Will Dockery established the cotton enterprise in 1895. The historic district nomination records a community with a railroad connection, businesses, farm buildings and housing. Those places relate to the work that brought particular people into earshot of one another. Charley Patton learned from Henry Sloan and later influenced other players. In Pete Welding’s edited first-person interview with Chester Arthur Burnett, Burnett remembers hearing Patton and says, “It was he who got me interested.” The statement is evidence of what Patton meant to Burnett. It does not independently locate that lesson, first meeting, or every performance at a precise place within Dockery. [5–7]

Bill Lester gave the Southern Foodways Alliance a description of work and of preserving Dockery. Asked about its past arrangements, he said “some people wanted to sharecrop.” The appendix to the historic district nomination instead emphasizes wages and says Dockery “did not rely on sharecropping.” The evidence available does not specify which workers, parcels and periods each statement covers. Both sources report people working in several parts of a large enterprise. Individual wage, tenancy and commissary records would better establish the risks workers actually carried. Lester’s testimony is a later interview that includes his own observations, interpretation and stories relayed by others. [5][8]

The music reached places beyond the farm as musicians moved and recorded it. The register nomination identifies Sloan, Patton and later musicians and explains the work of the farm and rail connection. MDAH describes eight surviving original buildings at its designation, including the gin and seed house, and attributes their design and construction to Dockery and farm workers. The individual workers who framed or repaired each of those buildings are still largely unnamed in the records read. [3][5]

## 3. Then and now

The historic district covers a small core of a much wider historic agricultural enterprise. The listed gin, seed house, barn and commissary evidence show part of that core. The heritage area funded historic gin restoration for interpretation. A restoration grant and a visitor marketing listing cannot establish today’s hours or its lawful public approach. Preserved fabric and documented visitor access are distinct facts. [1–5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

- **Documented historic fabric.** Gin, seed house, cotton shed and other core farm structures are recorded in the nomination and landmark files. Present interior access remains unverified. [3][5]
- **Boundary held.** The historic working ground, occupied buildings, burial areas and the water edge have no visitor direction or location pin in this draft. [1–5]
- **Remote evidence.** Read the original district nomination, the oral histories and the heritage grant account from home. Archive and repositories act as sources. [2][5][7][8]

## 5. Public progression and strongest story moment

Research reading only. Compare the nomination’s description of the seed house and rail connection with Burnett’s plain account of how he became interested. **Strongest story moment — The distance a song traveled.** The enterprise organized where people worked. Musicians chose what to teach and what to carry with them. Read their decisions into the same record as the buildings, where the evidence permits. [5–8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The nomination appendix emphasizes paid work over sharecropping, whereas Lester remembers mixed arrangements. This draft keeps the distinction open until worker-level records and dates can be read. [5][8]
- Visit Mississippi prints a 25,600-acre figure. The nomination appendix describes more than 6,000 acres in row crops with additional acreage elsewhere. These figures may measure different holdings, dates and uses. The nomination and state register also give conflicting approximate birth years for Charley Patton. No exact birth date is assigned here.
