# Hope Visitor Information Center and Missouri Pacific Railroad Depot
HT-RES-AR-hope-railroad-and-farm-market · Arkansas OP02 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

In December 1956, the trade journal Mid-Continent Banker devoted a brief illustrated report to a challenge from a banker in Hope. The November issue had printed a Texas claim to being “watermelon capital of the world.” Hope banker C. C. Spragins sent a picture of O. D. Middlebrooks, kneeling behind a melon identified as weighing 161 pounds. The pictured melon carries the number 161. The paper reported a prize of $1,000 and recalled Middlebrooks’s 195-pound melon of 1935. The story is a contemporary printed account, while the banker and journal were interested participants in the reputation being contested. [3]

That reputation had been built by other growers too. The Encyclopedia of Arkansas names Hugh and Edgar Laseter, who developed a seed line for the local contest. It attributes a 136-pound melon in 1925 to Hugh and describes the first festival in 1926. It also records two trains halting in 1928 so passengers could be served cold watermelon. The stoppage turns a civic boast into a scene of actual work. A festival needed people to raise the fruit, carry it, cool it and get it to passengers while a train waited. No matched waybill, dispatch or loading record yet puts a named grower or specific train in the depot. [4]

The present building binds the story to a physical place without resolving that shipment question. The register nomination identifies a combined depot with spaces for freight and passengers. The city says the freight portion now holds a waiting area for passenger rail service that returned in 2013. Look for the difference in size and function of the building portions from the public side or use the nomination’s description while inside the visitor center. The depot is evidence of how a town moved people and goods. The 1956 image is evidence of someone competing for the town’s name, and the two records have different origins. [1, 3, 5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The historical precinct is Hope’s railroad and town-center market. Middlebrooks’s field is placed near Patmos by the state encyclopedia, but the trade-journal photograph supplies no exact setting. The present combined depot stands at Main and Division. The town’s visitor interior and the active boarding area use different entrances. [1–5]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Drive to the city’s Hope Visitor Information Center downtown and use signed lawful parking and the short public street-side approach to 100 E. Division. The Clinton interpretation inside is a local visitor amenity; verification of any particular railroad or watermelon exhibit remains open. Exclude the west Amtrak entrance, operating platform, tracks, private grower properties and field locations. [1, 2]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The city calls 1912 the building date. The nomination’s historic chronology states circa 1917 after the Missouri Pacific acquisition. The state encyclopedia’s image description also says 1912. The surviving depot’s firm construction year remains in dispute until building and railroad records are compared. [1, 5, 6]
- The 1956 magazine’s 161 pounds belongs to that campaign, while 195 pounds refers to 1935. The earlier 136-pound Laseter entry is a different grower and year. The large-fruit claims in the paper are promotions and the printed photograph has no proof of a weighing procedure. Search for an original 1925–28 local newspaper page yielded secondary results only; freight bills, the actual stop program and weights are open. [3, 4]
