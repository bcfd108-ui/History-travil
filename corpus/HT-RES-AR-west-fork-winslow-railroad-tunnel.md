# Winslow Tunnel and Winslow Museum
HT-RES-AR-west-fork-winslow-railroad-tunnel · Arkansas OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

Jim Elliott had been the Frisco’s manager of automotive equipment in 1967, when the railway began enlarging the Winslow Tunnel. The opening had to accommodate the taller and wider loads of a later railroad. Both portals were rebuilt, and the date stones came down. The Frisco Railroad Museum’s 1997 account says one was destroyed. Elliott received the other. “Where it landed when we unloaded it” is how he began his recollection of the stone’s long stay on his property. His contribution belongs to a named period of railroad work and preservation. [2, printed p. 4]

The stone reads 1882. By then crews had been advancing from both ends under a Boston Mountains ridge. The state encyclopedia traces a change of contractor in March 1882, then the convergence of the two headings in July and an opening to traffic in August. A temporary switchback had a separate job while the bore was underway. It got rail and provisions to crews working farther south before the permanent opening was available. Above ground, railroad work produced its own town, roadbed and supply demands. [3, 4]

The history is far less complete for the workmen who opened the tunnel. The state encyclopedia reports serious disease, racially divided work at the two ends, and segregated burial. Those accounts are part of the story of labor, and the individual names and burial claims need independent support. The later report of the stone’s return to downtown Winslow in 2007 places a surviving work object back in the town. It leaves the former construction grounds and people who could speak for them as distinct research questions. [2–4]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The bore and two portals lie on an operating railroad in the Boston Mountains. The city museum and town frontage are the public setting for reading the story. The surviving south date stone was removed and later reported back downtown. Its precise modern public approach has not been established here. [1–4]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Winslow Museum and City Hall, 108 N. Winslow Blvd. Drive up and use the short signed public entrance while open. ## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The Frisco museum newsletter gives 1,726 feet for the early tunnel, sandstone for the stones and 18 feet for the widened bore. The state encyclopedia gives 1,693 feet at completion, later about 1,702 feet, limestone and a widened bore of 19 feet. Do not combine the measurements or assert a stone type. [2, 3]
- The newsletter associates a complete brick lining with company records and two and a half years of work, without giving that work’s dates. The encyclopedia distinguishes early timber support from 1898–99 brick lining and gives a 1967–69 enlargement range. The newsletter dates the enlargement’s start to August 1967 and calls it a nine-month job. Original specifications are needed to explain the time and width differences. [2, 3]
- The encyclopedia’s original Thomas Burns 1931 employee-magazine link redirected to www.thelibrary.org and returned HTTP 404. The library’s older issue directory loaded, but both the relevant page range and whole issue PDF returned HTTP 404. An alternate legacy-host request for the page range returned HTTP 503. The separate Rogers museum document download returned HTTP 403. No early worker quotation is attributed to Burns. Survivor names, labor rolls and the original early railroad annual report remain open. [3, 5–10]
