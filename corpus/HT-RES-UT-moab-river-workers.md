## 2. The story
**Named river worker, mediated primary voice:** in its April 28, 2023 article, the museum reproduces Mart Fish's recollection from a 1953 interview by Austin and Alta Fife. His account connects river travel with paid provisioning. His succinct statement is: **“I ran several rafts down the river after that, taking contracts for bringing provisions in for people.”** The museum dates his arrival to 1888. This is retrospective testimony quoted by a community institution, not an independently heard recording or a retrieved freight contract. [4]

**Named tourism worker and household:** the museum-hosted *Mitchell Marvin “Mitch” Williams* transcript bears “Mitch Williams 2004” and the Eastern Utah Human History Library heading. Pages 28–29 and 34–36 connect family business to tourism: Williams credits Mary with financial administration and promotion, and describes guides, office employees and repair workers. These are his retrospective descriptions, not Mary's own testimony or independently audited employment totals. His account gives labor a broader meaning than the person visibly guiding a boat. No historic operating method is adopted. [5]

**Original interpretive angle:** a river can appear to a traveler as scenery and to a worker as an obligation to somebody else. Fish's contracts put customers and provisions into that landscape. A later visitor business makes another kind of promise, sustained by people whose names are often missing from the holiday photograph. This entry asks visitors to notice the work surrounding the view without reenacting it.

The two accounts do not prove an uninterrupted family, company or technological lineage. They are separate windows into making a livelihood. Neither narrator stands for every worker or the whole valley. No invented dialogue, reconstructed employee payroll or nostalgic claim that earlier work was safer or simpler is supplied. Indigenous land history is not reconstructed from these settlers' accounts; that broader subject requires Tribal-published evidence and its own scope.
