# Norton — The people who carried the mail before the rails

## 2. The story

**The town paid for the service. The government supplied the sacks.** William G. Cutler’s 1883 county history names B. W. Rawlins as the person who brought the first mail from Republican City and N. H. Billings as the person keeping the post office. In the published transcription it says, “The service was paid for by the people,” adding that the government furnished the mail sacks. The same passage says the first office stood about a mile and a half from the office of the writer’s day. It supplies no direction or parcel. The sentence describes an early service arrangement. [1]

**A named worker and a precarious contract.** F. M. Lockard’s 1894 local history says Morrison L. Atkinson farmed east of Norton, sold his farm and cattle in 1882, and “sub-contracted the route from Norton to Shibboleth from Simpson & Lockard.” It says he carried mail until 1886. The writer shared the Lockard surname with a contractor named in the passage, but the text does not identify that person by given name. This is a near-period account, not Atkinson’s own testimony, and no contract was recovered. The account tells us the route, his work, and its reported end year. It does not identify his vehicle or a particular surviving stage stop. [2,5]

**Stages, and the office at the other end.** A later local synthesis reports regular stage connections with North Platte, Hays and Trego. A separate Lockard biography calls W. B. Rogers the predecessor of the postmaster appointed February 11, 1878. A stage network and a succession of postmasters appear in the record while the exact boarding place and handover point stay open. The story is the work that connected a young county seat to other places before rail service changed its reach. [1,6,7,11]

## 3. Then / now

**Historic location.** The Norton end of routes to Republican City and Shibboleth and wider scheduled stage services, with more than one early office. No site-level tie to a specific surviving building or public strip was established. Lockard describes the post-office building put up by Captain Jarvis as later destroyed in the 1886 fire. Cutler’s distance is measured relative to a historical office reference and does not locate a modern parcel. [1,2,6]

**Lawful physical anchor.** 

The listed downtown district contains later commercial fabric. The 1899 Sanborn map describes the town after the fire and Atkinson’s reported contract. The county museum occupies the former 1938 library. These are sources and contexts for later Norton, with no verified identity as a stage or mail-work anchor. [3,4,8,9]
