## 2. The story
In 1894, Black Helena residents were not simply subjects appearing in someone else's description of the West. A Black-edited newspaper addressed them as voters who could shape its capital.

**James Presley Ball Jr.** edited **The Colored Citizen**, launched September 3, 1894. His father, photographer James Presley Ball Sr., is a different person. The Montana Historical Society's newspaper history identifies the younger Ball's editorial role and the paper's support for Helena against Anaconda in the capital contest. William Andrews Clark financially backed that campaign and the newspaper; this was an interested political publication, not a neutral survey. [4]

The Society publishes an actual clipping from the first issue headed **“Colored Citizens! Vote for Helena!”** The image was opened and visually read. It describes business ownership, institutions and residents' investment in the city's future, declaring **“We are all proud of our lovely city”** before appealing for support in the November 6 vote. The clipping is unsigned; the evidence supports attribution to Ball's newspaper, not a guaranteed individual byline. [5–6]

It also paints a sweeping picture of equal opportunity. Read that as a campaign argument. It does not erase discrimination documented elsewhere. Historian **Anthony Wood's** published political-history essay describes Black Montanans using voting, litigation, candidacy and the press while confronting an increasingly restrictive color line. That is historical analysis, separate from the newspaper's contemporary persuasion. [7]

A later family record gives civic life a different scale. In **Alan M. Thompson's July 2016 summary of his June 20 interview with Dr. Jules Harrell**, Harrell remembers Helena's Pleasant Hour Club supporting educational scholarships and causes beyond Montana, family meals, and his aunt **Octavia Bridgewater** preparing Christmas dinner before working a hospital shift. The four-page summary was read, **not the complete audio**, so these are paraphrases of the published interview summary rather than a fresh transcript. [8–9]

The Society also catalogs the **1929 Helena Montana Federation of Colored Women's Clubs ribbon, 1979.39.04**, and a **1956 convention badge identifying Maxine Howard of Helena, 1999.33.10**. Their catalog records attest to organized participation beyond one newspaper campaign. Neither object is confirmed on display. [10]

**Interpretive conclusion:** the strongest story is the contrast between a public demand to be recognized and the ordinary work that sustained a community. A newspaper advocated; clubs organized; families fed people and carried professional responsibilities. Loss and exclusion belong in the account without reducing Black Helena to disappearance alone.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- Sunday museum hours differ between the older PDF and current HTML. Current published operator pages take priority; no day-of confirmation was obtained. [1–3]
- The newspaper history calls November 5, 1894 its final issue; the search-indexed extract of the Society's newspaper union list says it ceased December 15 but lists holdings ending November 5. Direct PDF retrieval failed, so that catalog discrepancy remains a lead for verification. Publication end and surviving holdings may differ. Do not collapse them or claim a complete run was inspected. [4, 12]
- Wood's essay says the paper ceased weeks before the election, which conflicts with the November 5 issue described elsewhere and the November 6 election appeal. That statement is not adopted. [4–5, 7]
- The clipping speaks of five hundred local Black residents, while the institutional newspaper history supplies 279 in discussing the launch. No population total is settled here; source purposes and reference dates need reconciliation. [4–5]
- The LOC issue metadata was retrieved, but its PDF download returned HTML instead of a usable scan. The independently inspected state-hosted clipping is the actual primary-image evidence, not a claimed reading of the full LOC issue.
- Harrell's summary is selective and not verbatim throughout. His full audio remains unreviewed. No quotation is fabricated from the summary.
- Specific exhibition placement, original-object availability, reproduction licensing and a verified photographic station remain open.
