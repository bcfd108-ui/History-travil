## 2. The story

**A directly examined worker voice.** Railroad worker **Alva F. Lyons** was interviewed by **Robert W. Delaney on June 29, 1982, in Durango**, for Fort Lewis College's Center of Southwest Studies. The downloadable transcript was retrieved and its Farmington passages read. Audio was not heard. [2]

On printed page **5**, Lyons connects Farmington's oil economy to the railway's changing business. His short voice is **“a considerable increase in business over the years.”** This is a worker's retrospective economic observation, not a production statistic. Technical descriptions elsewhere in the interview are intentionally not reproduced. [2]

**A family's later perspective.** In *Extraction Point*, **Christian Marquez** writes about returning to his hometown and remembers his grandparents **Bonna and Dean Bond**. He describes Dean's electrical work in the gas industry supporting the family and a pension helping Bonna afterward. Marquez also recalls resisting a school counselor's narrow career expectations and leaving for university. This is a directly read first-person family essay, not a recorded oral history. [3]

**Original interpretation.** An industry can sustain one generation while feeling like a constraint to another. The two accounts should not be forced into agreement: Lyons describes expanding business; Marquez describes a descendant's changing expectations. Together they make energy history about choices and household continuity rather than machines alone.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

**Start with two generations.** Read the short worker passage and the family essay before arriving. The first establishes a work connection; the second supplies household consequences and a younger person's choice.

**Enter only the independent public museum.** During a confirmed opening, use the authorized visitor entrance and public interior. Do not seek an outside view of energy infrastructure or the river as an extension of the visit.

**Let actual interpretation set the comparison.** If regional economic history is displayed, record only the labels and provenance actually present. No unmarked machinery or generic family photograph becomes evidence of Lyons or the Bonds.

**End with the decision, not a machine.** Return to the contrast between a livelihood that supported a family and a descendant who wanted a different future. This is the strongest story moment. It can be understood without locating a former employer or a family residence.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

**Source scope.** Relevant transcript passages were directly inspected; the entire oral-history recording was not reviewed. Lyons is a transport worker discussing Farmington's energy connection, not a gas-plant employee or a named member of the Bond household. [2]

**Family evidence is an essay.** Marquez's recollection is personal published testimony, not a pension ledger, household interview recording or independent verification of every family detail. Do not merge it with Lyons into an imagined composite speaker. [3]

**Hours precedence.** Older official brochures show an 8 a.m. opening; the current operator page states 10 a.m. The current page governs this draft. Holiday exceptions remain unresolved rather than inherited from a 2020 leaflet. [1][4]
