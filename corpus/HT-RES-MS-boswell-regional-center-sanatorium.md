## 2. The story

Created by the **1916 Mississippi Legislature** under the State Board of Health to combat tuberculosis, the **Mississippi Tuberculosis Sanatorium** began with **200 acres** and **$2,000** donated by citizens of Magee and the Gulf & Ship Island Railroad, plus **$25,000** in legislative startup funds. After first superintendent Dr. Walter Rowan's health failed, **Dr. Henry Boswell** took charge in **1917**, admitting the **first 12 tuberculosis patients on February 4, 1918**. [2]

A subsequent **$1 million** legislative appropriation financed a modern **75-bed** sanatorium hospital, followed in **1930** by Dr. Boswell's **Preventorium** for children infected with or at risk of tuberculosis. Known statewide simply as **"The San,"** the 623-acre campus functioned as a self-contained town with its own post office (`Sanatorium, MS`), water tower, bakery, butcher shop, laundry, **underground laundry tunnels** beneath the rolling hills, movie theater, fire department, dairy and poultry farms, lake, and golf course. [2]

As antibiotic therapies dramatically reduced long-term sanatorium admissions, the state transferred the campus to **Ellisville State School** in **1972** and formally established **Boswell Regional Center** on **July 1976** (beginning with 35 clients and growing across multiple regional sites) in honor of Dr. Henry Boswell. [1, 2]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Surviving 1918–1930 fabric & tunnels:** Boswell Regional Center's centennial bulletin (`Feb. 2018`) describes the historic 1918 hospital, 1930 Preventorium, depot entrance, and underground laundry tunnels, but does not inventory which original structures remain standing versus demolished. [2]
