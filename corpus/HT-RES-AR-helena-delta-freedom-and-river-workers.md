# Helena — A wage promised at the river
HT-RES-AR-helena-delta-freedom-and-river-workers · Arkansas OP01 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

On January 2, 1863, Chaplain Samuel Sawyer wrote from Helena about people who had come within Union lines. In his reply to a survey on freedpeople, he counted workers at the fort, asserted a daily wage rate in cents, and then wrote that they “have never received a dollar.” His letter estimates that about $20,000 was owed. It records one officer’s estimate and describes work already claimed by the Union. It cannot show the sum owed to any one worker or whether an individual was later paid. [3]

Read the section as a person writing from within his own era’s assumptions. Sawyer alternates between criticizing unpaid wages and assuming authority to judge and supervise the people he observed. He also describes learning to read and write, preaching and a preference to remain in the South if freedom were secured. His report lets a reader hear the terms an officer used. The missing payroll and petition leave room for the people working there to speak in their own words. [3]

By 1864 Quaker relief efforts helped establish the institution that became Southland College. The University of Arkansas finding aid describes the initial work with displaced and orphaned children and preserves the later institutional records. The Smithsonian’s reproduction of Freedmen’s Bureau records points separately to relief, labor and property evidence. Formation of a school and an earlier dispute over wartime pay belong to overlapping lives, with continuity for individual people still to be established. [4, 5]
