## 2. The story
**One-minute hook:** Organizing can look like speeches when viewed from a distance. A worker’s memory can move the story back into family conversations, choices about earning a living, and what parents teach children.

**Named firsthand local voice:** California Humanities’ *Pictures from the Field* publishes Robert Garcia’s account of Salinas Valley organizing. He recalls initially remaining a foreman while his parents joined the 1970 strike, then changing his position after witnessing the treatment of his father. He credits Guadalupe, his mother: “My mom was really the inspiration.” This is Garcia’s remembered account, including his recollection of her views, not a separately recorded interview with Guadalupe. [2]

**A household’s longer view:** CSUMB’s catalog describes Simon Salinas’s interview by Guadalupe Casco, dated 11 October 2012. He recalls growing up as one of twelve children in a migrant-farmworker family and connects education with later advocacy. The record covers Salinas and seasonal family life in Texas and California. It identifies him as district supervisor at the time; that is not a current-office claim. The catalog is directly read, but its transcript download returned 403 and the video was not played. No verbatim words are attributed to him here. [3]

**Interpretive synthesis:** A family can be part of a movement without every member making the same decision at the same time. Reading these sources beside a literary museum creates a useful question: who describes labor from outside, and who remembers doing it? That comparison must not turn a novel into a transcript or make one family stand for every worker.
