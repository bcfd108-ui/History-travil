# Fort Towson — what it took to supply a post
Research draft · Oklahoma OP05 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

Choctaw Nation’s history describes repeated removals and the U.S. government’s inadequate provisions and uncoordinated planning. The Nation describes the loss of elders and children as a loss of knowledge as well as people, and records remaking government afterward. Its account distinguishes the removals from the creation of the Chickasaw District negotiated nearby in 1837. These are quiet readings in the regular public center. [2]

Inside the Army files is a different kind of provisioning record. Researcher Kevin Crisman located Commissary General George Gibson’s June 29, 1837 call for supply bids. For Fort Towson it called for 240 barrels of pork, 500 barrels of flour, 220 barrels of beans, and soap, candles and salt. Gibson required strong boxes “of convenient size for transportation” for soap and candles. The short quotation comes through Crisman’s reproduction, with the underlying circular still to be retrieved. For an Army post the ability to request a barrel of food meant a whole network arranging its contents, manufacture, transportation and inspection. [3]

In May 1838 the *Heroine*, a steamboat hauling provisions toward Fort Towson, sank. Crisman’s archaeological account identifies a flour barrel head stenciled for the U.S. Army among recovered material. The find links the contracting record to something that was loaded, handled, carried upstream and lost. Colonel J. H. Vose’s May 14, 1838 letter to Gibson is separately located by Crisman in NARA RG 92, entry 225, box 1145. It remains an uninspected manuscript lead. [3]

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic ground.** Fort Towson’s post, its shipping network and neighboring places differ from one another. No river, wreck, grave, treaty or community destination is assigned. **Lawful physical anchor.** Staffed Fort Towson Historic Site visitor center via the operator’s signed public driveway, then a short walk to its entrance. [1–4]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior — ordinary visitor-center exhibits. OHS identifies the sutler store as reconstructed and names an interpreted teamster’s cabin. Those structures and fort foundations remain interpretive context in this visitor-center-only draft. Original structures’ foundations are described by HALS. [1,4–5]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

Enter the staffed center. Start with the Choctaw Nation’s account and the difference between relocation under pressure and the Army’s procurement system. Read the requisition figures. **Strongest story moment — The marks on a barrel head.** Read the two supply accounts side by side and pause with the people affected. [2–3]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates
