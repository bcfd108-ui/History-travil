## 1. Trip gate
**Public interpretation is the visit; the historic cutoff is not a cleared driving route.** The NPS lists Sheep Rock/Soda Point Interpretive Site at **1564 Soda Point Power Plant Rd**, with auto/bus parking and exhibits, managed by BLM and Idaho Transportation Department. Its approach leaves US-30 at the signed historical-site entrance and passes the highway facility. Follow public signs, not utility access. No opening hours, winter maintenance guarantee or date-specific gate status was published in the page read. November access is therefore conditional, not verified open today. [1]

The separate **Oregon Trail Park and Marina**, on US-30 west of Soda Springs, is described by NPS as county-managed and open year-round. That statement does not guarantee snow-free paths, functioning winter restrooms or a usable boat ramp. [2] Both stops are outdoors. Use Mountain Time and daylight; no sunset time or live road condition was checked. If signs close either entrance, do not find a substitute through the highway yard, utility property, rail corridor, private land or riverbank.

A BLM Soda Hills map dated **2013**, although newly indexed online, distinguishes roads, primitive roads, trails and several seasonal closures, including a November 16–May 14 class. Its legend is not evidence that every nearby road closes on those dates or that the public interpretive approach does. No route-specific current closure order was recovered. Backcountry cutoff travel remains **HOLD**, not an add-on to this visit. [3]

## 2. The story
Start with the people whose homeland the wagon roads crossed. The Shoshone-Bannock Tribes' own published account describes ancestral lands across today's state boundaries and seasonal movement for food and resources. Its present-day account also describes sovereign government and continuing stewardship. A wagon-route beginning is not the beginning of human movement here. This source supports regional context, not a specific campsite or permission to enter culturally sensitive ground. [4]

The interpretive question is **how travelers chose a route when a shorter line did not mean an easier journey**. NPS dates the Hudspeth/Meyers party's departure from Sheep Rock to July 19, 1849. Its dedicated cutoff history describes a 113-mile alternative that saved roughly 25 miles but crossed four mountain ranges. Wood, grazing and accessible water also influenced the choice. [5]

**Short primary voice, mediated:** NPS quotes Elijah Farnham at the turnoff on July 20, 1849, describing the previous day's track as **“look[ed] like an old road of a good deal of travel.”** The bracketed adaptation belongs to the NPS quotation. No original diary manuscript, edition page or scan was independently read here. This is evidence of what one traveler reportedly observed, not an objective traffic count. [5]

Original visitor narration: *Imagine deciding with everyone else already moving. A busy track can look like proof that somebody knows the way. Here, popularity and certainty were not the same thing.* The narration is interpretation, not a diary quote or a claim about each emigrant's motives.

The names Soda Springs, Sheep Rock and Hudspeth Cutoff overlap in travel storytelling but refer to different scales. This dossier joins two public interpretive stops; it does not map the full historic corridor.

## 4. What's still there

**Public access rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance. Access classifications here are separate from the locator record.
- **Oregon Trail Park:** interpretive signs and a short original wagon-swale segment near the entrance road. Observe from permitted visitor ground; do not drive into the grass or cut a new path along a depression. [2]
- **Sheep Rock/Soda Point:** interpretive displays and a viewpoint toward the Bear River/bluff. The NPS describes an overlook walk but gives no verified accessible grade, surface or winter condition. Do not substitute a river descent. [1]
- **Historic alignment:** mixed land ownership and modern travel classifications appear in the BLM map. Historical alignment alone conveys neither right-of-way nor an open motor route. [3]
- **Additional highway sign:** NPS's auto-tour guide locates a separate cutoff interpretation pullout around US-30 milepost 376.2, about 17 miles west of Soda Springs. It is not the Sheep Rock public site or historical junction. Optional future stop only; safe turn, snow clearance and present signing unverified. [6]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
**Small westbound module, not a forced through-route.** Proposed total 60–90 minutes from Soda Springs and back, including reading and stops, is an editorial allowance, not a measured ETA. Stop durations below are also estimates. Weather may make the entire module inappropriate.

1. **Before leaving town:** read the Tribal regional account and check current road/site notices. This avoids telling the story as if the landscape awaited the emigrants. [4]
2. **Oregon Trail Park, 15–25 minutes:** begin with physical evidence. Identify the marked swale from lawful visitor space; read the sign before deciding what kind of trail it represents. [2]
3. **Continue west to Sheep Rock, approximately 2.5 miles:** this distance comes from the NPS auto-tour guide, not a new routing calculation. Allow roughly 5–10 minutes for the short transfer, signs and parking in good conditions; no live ETA is claimed. Use the signed entrance. [6]
4. **Sheep Rock interpretation, 20–30 minutes:** separate the public viewing point from the historic junction and the three historical route choices. Read Farnham's observation after orienting to the landscape. [1,5]
5. **Strongest story moment:** end at the authorized viewpoint, if safely accessible, with the choice itself: a shorter route could still cost more effort. Return by public highway, not along the historic trace. If the viewpoint is closed or icy, finish with the exhibits in the accessible public area, or skip the stop rather than extending access by guesswork.

Open-season mode may permit more comfortable walking, but does not automatically authorize backcountry travel. A separate vehicle-appropriate route review would be required. No Route Group is activated by this draft.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
There is a material **official-source endpoint conflict**. The dedicated NPS Hudspeth page says the cutoff rejoined the California Trail west of Malta and explicitly says the travelers were still east of City of Rocks. The NPS Sheep Rock page instead says the cutoff rejoined at City of Rocks; the HMdb-transcribed local panel also uses City of Rocks. Do not collapse these into a precise shared endpoint. Neither description is treated as a settled navigable endpoint; the western geometry remains unapproved. [1,5,7]

The historical-junction coordinate and visitor-site coordinate are separately printed by NPS. They must not be snapped together or substituted for one another. Historic directional descriptions are not driveway instructions. [1,5]

No attack/grave location is part of the proposed visit or hunt. Original Farnham diary verification and manuscript locator remain open. No Tribal endorsement or consultation is implied.
