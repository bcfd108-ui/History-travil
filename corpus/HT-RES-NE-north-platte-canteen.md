# North Platte Canteen — what the volunteers brought to the train

## 2. The story

### The wrong train

On December 17, 1941, people in North Platte went to meet a troop train they thought would be carrying local Nebraska men. It carried Kansas men. In her letter published the next day in the *North Platte Daily Bulletin*, Rae Wilson wrote, “we very willingly gave those things to the Kansas boys.” Read the line in the state education project's newspaper facsimile and typed transcription. The snacks and gifts had started out with particular recipients. That platform encounter gave Wilson a case for organizing to meet people no one locally knew. The first troop train met by the organized canteen, according to the county museum, came on Christmas Day. [2–4]

### The work between arrivals

An organizational appeal signed by secretary Mrs. Chas. Hutchens and treasurer R. L. Self said, “Everything dispensed from this good will builder is FREE.” The UNL edition identifies her as Jessie Hutchens. On the same letterhead sit the active board and the officers of the day. The appeal describes a system for bringing donated time, supplies and cash into a depot and putting food into the hands of people who had a few minutes. Read its claim of service regardless of rank, color or creed as what the canteen's officials told donors. It does not independently document the treatment of every traveler. [5]

Nebraska Studies describes head officials being told about troop movements and volunteers being summoned. When passengers could not leave the train, the service could reach the car windows and aisles. The museum credits local cooperation and the Union Pacific's permission to use depot space. A wartime fundraising letter counts seven thousand women contributing time or supplies and above 50,000 people fed per month. A later education account gives approximately 125 participating communities and reports 3,000–5,000 people at the wartime peak. These figures count different things. The funding appeal is not a final tally. [4–6]

### The depot is gone

The last service date in the text of the 2003 Senate concurrent resolution as introduced is April 1, 1946. Nebraska Studies says the depot was torn down in 1973 and refers to a marker made from its bricks. Start at the museum and follow the work through letters, food, photographs and the train schedule. The historic platform is a subject to read about. Its current public boundaries were not established for this itinerary. [1,2,6,7]

## 3. Then / now

The wartime operation worked in the Union Pacific railroad depot. The old station was demolished. The Lincoln County Historical Museum comes into the story as the modern public interpretation point, with a digital exhibit useful during winter appointment closures. [1,2,6]

1. Arrange the winter appointment directly with the museum. If unavailable, start online with the first-train story. [1,2]
2. Read Wilson's letter and then the board's fundraising appeal. Look for the shift from meeting one train to finding provisions and staffing for the next one. [3,5]
3. **Strongest story moment — The gifts changed hands.** Read Wilson's words aloud in the museum's public interpretation space or from home while looking at a canteen photograph. Read the directors' appeal immediately afterward. [2,3,5,6]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- December 17 is the spur in the county museum and newspaper sources. December 25 is the first organized canteen service in the county museum and the operating start in the introduced Senate resolution. Keep both dates. [2–4,7]
- The appeal's monthly and volunteer estimates, Nebraska Studies' peak and community figures, and the introduced Senate resolution's approximately six million cumulative figure have different scopes and provenance. A ledger that reconciles them was not retrieved. [5–7]
- Confirm appointment scope and the status of individual objects in person. The period news facsimile was read via its typed transcription and described crop. No verified standing point for the former depot or marker. [1,3,6]

## 7. Historical photo records

Nebraska Studies reproduces a 1943 scene of canteen officers displaying sandwiches, and credits other canteen imagery to repositories including the Union Pacific Railroad Archives. The state page links several period frames. The depot is demolished and no frame with a surviving, accessible alignment was established. No Photo Point. [6]
