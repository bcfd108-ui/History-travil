## 2. The story

Owyhee County's diamond fever struck twice, twenty-seven years apart — first as a territorial governor's salted-ground fiasco in 1865, and again as a Snake River "Diamond Basin" promotion undone on a New York diamond dealer's bench in 1893.

### Act I: Caleb Lyon's 1865 Ruby City diamond rush

In the fall of **1865**, Idaho Territorial Governor **Caleb Lyon of Lyonsdale** returned to Idaho after spending months away in the East (following Secretary C. DeWitt Smith's death in August 1865). [1] According to the early Idaho historical accounts compiled by Hubert Howe Bancroft and Frances Fuller Victor from pioneer manuscripts, a man named **Davis** had shown Lyon rough gems in New York, claiming he had washed them from a gulch in the Owyhees and promising to guide Lyon back to the spot. When Davis drowned in the wreck of the steamship *Brother Jonathan* (July 30, 1865), Lyon believed himself sole possessor of the secret. Reaching **Ruby City** (above Silver City), Lyon traded one specimen "diamond" to prominent local mining figure **D. H. Fogus** for a **$500** silver bar, then slipped out with Fogus under cover of darkness to locate the bonanza. Disappointed miners trailed them into the hills, triggering a furious stampede across the gulches. [2]

Contemporary regional newspapers confirm the mania in real time. On **December 15, 1865**, *The Daily Mountaineer* of The Dalles, Oregon, reported: **"The diamond excitement at Owyhee is raging violently. It is reported that several have been found."** [3] In reality, prospectors hauled back barrels of bright quartz crystals and shiny pebbles — no genuine diamonds were ever recovered, and Lyon's public standing collapsed before he departed Idaho permanently in **April 1866**. [1, 2]

### Act II: The 1892–1893 "Diamond Basin" and Kimberley bortz exposure

Twenty-seven years later, in **December 1892**, diamond excitement flared anew on the Snake River desert below Owyhee's foothills. A Boise dispatch printed in *The Dalles Weekly Chronicle* on **December 16, 1892**, reported that **Charles Vandorn** (claiming prior experience in Kimberley, South Africa), mining man H. Branoben, and ferryman **Mr. Waters** (associated with Walters Ferry) had formed a company holding claims across a **"Diamond Basin"** near the Snake River, sending stones east to **Tiffany & Co.** and escorting Engineer Wellington and a party of experts to inspect the ground. [4]

Within seven weeks, the boom was exposed in New York. A dispatch in the *Omaha Daily Bee* on **February 6, 1893** (quoting the *New York Sun*), described how **J. D. Yerrington** of Broadway had just submitted four more Boise-sent "Diamond Basin" stones to veteran rough-diamond dealer **Herman Levy** of 58 John Street / Maiden Lane. Levy examined the specimens and pronounced them **South African "bortz"** (crushed industrial diamond fragments from Kimberley worth roughly **$1.50 per carat**), pointing out sharp **fractures caused by dynamite blasting** in African mines rather than any natural river-worn alluvial surfaces. Meanwhile, the *Bee* noted, Boise newspapers had already dropped the "great Owyhee diamond find" because stones actually recovered locally turned out to be ordinary **quartz crystals**. [5] Even so, promoters lingered: on **March 1, 1893**, *The Dalles Daily Chronicle* carried a *New York World* item citing Amsterdam-trained diamond cleaver Herman Vreislander's defense of three small Idaho stones as genuine — illustrating how claims and counterclaims echoed long after the hoax was exposed. [6]

### Haunted history / lore

No ghost or haunting lore is attached to Diamond Gulch or ITD Marker #454; the site's oddity is strictly its two documented mining hoaxes (quartz pebbles in 1865 and blasted Kimberley bortz in 1892–93).

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Geography of 1865 ("Diamond Gulch") vs. 1892 ("Diamond Basin"):** Contemporary 1892–93 dispatches place Vandorn's "Diamond Basin" near the **Snake River** and **Walters Ferry** (roughly 10–15 miles north/northeast of Murphy), whereas ITD Marker #454 stands along ID-78 at Silver City Road near Sinker Creek and connects the site to both the 1865 Ruby City episode and the 1892 episode. Original 1865 *Owyhee Avalanche* issues and Owyhee County claim books needed to pinpoint exact claim boundaries for the 1865 gulch vs. 1892 Diamond Basin were not retrieved this run (LOC Chronicling America blocked by Cloudflare).
- **1893 expert disagreement:** While Herman Levy exposed four Boise-sent stones as blasted South African Kimberley bortz (*Omaha Daily Bee*, Feb. 6, 1893), Herman Vreislander subsequently argued in the *New York World* (*The Dalles Daily Chronicle*, Mar. 1, 1893) that three tiny stones he inspected were genuine. Both primary press accounts are printed side by side without smoothing over the promoters' rebuttal. [5, 6]
- **No adopted photograph:** Neither a Commons photograph of ITD Marker #454 nor a Commons photograph of Diamond Gulch was found this run (existing Owyhee Commons files cover Murphy's Idaho Street and Silver City, not the marker). **Photo Point stays blank.**
