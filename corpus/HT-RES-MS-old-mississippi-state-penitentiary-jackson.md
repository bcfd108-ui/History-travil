# Old Mississippi State Penitentiary Site, Jackson — 1836–1901 William Nichols prison replaced by New State Capitol

**Research draft · Labeled THEME SWEEP (Family 1 — Prisons, penitentiaries, jails · NO-ANCHOR / demolished-site reading on Capitol grounds) · Jackson, Hinds County, Mississippi · November 21, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate

**SUPPLEMENT CORRECTION & SITE STATUS:** The institutional sweep supplement listed the **Old Mississippi State Penitentiary site** in Jackson as a demolished *"1840–1930s"* penitentiary. Primary Mississippi legislative and MDAH architectural records establish that the walled brick penitentiary—designed by state architect **William Nichols** and built **1836–1840**—was **demolished in 1901** (not the 1930s) to clear the four-block square for construction of the **Mississippi New State Capitol** (`400 High St., Jackson, MS 39201`), built **1901–1903** (`NHL 2016`). [1, 2, 3]

**Zero physical fabric of the 1836–1901 penitentiary survives.** While the standing **Mississippi State Capitol** (`400 High St.`) is an active public civic landmark and museum-toured building, the penitentiary itself is strictly a **demolished-site historical reading** grounded on the public Capitol Square perimeter. [1, 2]

**Anchor ruling: NO-ANCHOR / DEMOLISHED-SITE READING (1836–1901 penitentiary completely demolished in 1901; public New State Capitol grounds occupy the four-block site at 400 High St.; supplement's "1930s" demolition span corrected).**

## 2. The story

Alongside the Old Capitol and Governor's Mansion, the Mississippi Legislature commissioned state architect **William Nichols** in **1836** to design a walled brick **Mississippi State Penitentiary** on a four-block square north of downtown Jackson (`High Street` / `North West` / `North President` corridor), completed in **1840**. [1, 3]

Prisoners inside the Jackson walls operated brickyards and workshops—even supplying bricks used to build the original **Mississippi State Lunatic Asylum** (`1855`) on Asylum Hill to the north—before Civil War destruction prompted the state in **January 1867** to lease the repaired 130-bed facility to **J. W. Young and Company** under a 14-year convict-lease arrangement. [4, 5]

As Mississippi purchased state penal plantations in **1894** (Rankin, Oakley, Belmont) and **Parchman** in **1900**—moving state prisoners onto Delta farm stockades in **1901**—Governor **Andrew Houston Longino** and the Legislature selected the four-block old penitentiary square for a monumental **New State Capitol**. The **1836–1840 penitentiary was demolished in 1901**, and St. Louis architect **Theodore Link's** Beaux-Arts Capitol (`171,000 sq ft`, funded by >$1 million in Illinois Central Railroad back-tax settlement funds) was erected over 28 months and dedicated in **1903** (`Mississippi Landmark 1986`; `National Historic Landmark 2016`). [1, 2, 4]

## 4. What's still there

- **Public civic grounds (`400 High St.`):** The **1903 Mississippi State Capitol** and landscaped four-block square (`Liberty Bell` replica, `1912` women's monument, `1966` Mississippi Freedom Trail "Capitol Rally" marker, `2016` NHL plaque). [1]
- **Gone:** All **1836–1901 William Nichols penitentiary** walls, cellblocks, and brickworks (razed 1901). [1, 3]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Supplement date correction (`1840–1930s` vs. `1836–1901`):** Official Mississippi Legislature and MDAH sources establish demolition occurred in **1901** ahead of New Capitol construction (1901–1903), not in the 1930s. [1, 2]
- **Surviving underground masonry or marker:** Whether any subterranean 1836–40 foundation remnants survive beneath the Capitol lawn, or whether a dedicated penitentiary plaque stands on Capitol Square, was not verified in `legislature.ms.gov` summaries checked this run. [1]
