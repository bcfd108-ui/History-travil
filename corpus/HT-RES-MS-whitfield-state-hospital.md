## 2. The story

Founded in 1848 and opened in **1855** as the Mississippi State Lunatic Asylum on north State Street in Jackson (now the **Asylum Hill / UMMC** site), Mississippi's original psychiatric hospital suffered chronic overcrowding—reaching 1,350 beds after 1900 and **2,000 patients** by **1926**. Superintendent Dr. Charles Mitchell championed relocating to an open rural campus. [1, 2]

In **1926**, the Mississippi Legislature dropped "Insane" from the title—renaming it **Mississippi State Hospital**—and appropriated **$2.5 million** to build a new cottage-plan campus on **3,333 acres** of state-owned Rankin County land (formerly the Howell state penal colony, renamed **Whitfield** after Governor **Henry L. Whitfield**, 1924–1927). Depression-era revenue collapse delayed construction (`1927–1935`), eventually bringing total cost to roughly **$5 million**. [1, 2, 3]

Designed by prominent Jackson architect **N. W. Overstreet**, the **350-acre** core campus comprised more than **70–75 red-brick Colonial Revival buildings** accented with white columns, trim, and cupolas (the surviving cupola atop the administration building remains the hospital's emblem), surrounded by ~3,000 acres of dairy, orchards, and truck farms. In keeping with Jim Crow segregation, Overstreet's plan divided the facility into two parallel sub-campuses—the **western side for African American patients** and the **eastern side for white patients**—which remained segregated until Superintendent **Dr. William L. Jaquith** desegregated the hospital following passage of the **Civil Rights Act of 1964**. [1, 2]

On **March 4, 1935**, as recorded in Superintendent's **Fortieth Biennial Report (`July 1, 1933–June 30, 1935`)**, staff began moving **2,464 patients** from the old Jackson asylum to Whitfield, completing the entire transfer in **nine actual working days** with zero casualties and zero escapes—though the superintendent warned immediately that the new campus still held only ~2,600 usable beds while operating fuel and utility costs tripled. [4]

## 4. What's still there

- **Active institutional campus (NON-PUBLIC):** Historic red-brick **Colonial Revival cottage-plan buildings (`1927–1935`)** designed by N. W. Overstreet, including the cupola-crowned administration building on the 350-acre core, operating inside an active state mental-health hospital complex. (MDAH noted a 2013 Mississippi Landmarks Significance Report was prepared, though the campus was not designated a Mississippi Landmark.) [1, 3]

- **Building count (`70+` vs. `75+` vs. `100+`):** Hospital history cites 70 original 1926–35 buildings out of >100 current campus structures; *Mississippi Encyclopedia* cites "more than seventy-five" original Overstreet buildings. [1, 2]
- **Whitfield campus burials:** Whether an institutional cemetery operated on the 3,333-acre Whitfield grounds after the 1935 move—and its location or size—was not documented in the MDAH/MSH sources reviewed this run.
- **Public exterior visibility:** Public traffic along MS Highway 468 passes the campus frontage, but due to active inpatient care and privacy rules, no roadside pullout or exterior viewing stop is routed.
