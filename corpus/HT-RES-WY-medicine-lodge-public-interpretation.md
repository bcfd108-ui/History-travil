## 2. The story
The central change is who gets to explain what the place means. A visitor can learn archaeological dates and still leave with the mistaken impression that the people belong only to the past. This stop instead starts with publicly shared voices of living people.

**Robyn Rofkar**, identified as an enrolled Eastern Shoshone member working at the Eastern Shoshone Cultural Center, consulted on the installation and contributed voice work. In Hannah Habermann's July 2024 report, Rofkar encouraged learning as a form of respect: **“come visit and come learn about our culture.”** The same account identifies **Shane Doyle**, an enrolled Apsáalooke member, among the recorded speakers. Their public contributions are the basis for this dossier, not a claim that they authorized this project or every possible interpretation. The report was read; the full exhibit recordings were not independently heard. [2]

**Ivan Posey**, identified in Andrew Rossi's May 2024 reporting as Central Wyoming College's tribal education coordinator and a participant in developing the experience, discussed the importance of retaining knowledge across generations. His published words include **“When older people move on, we lose a whole library of knowledge.”** That is a firsthand published account of why cultural continuity matters, not permission to extract stories, reproduce ceremonies or decode protected imagery. [3]

**Original interpretive frame:** the ending should change the visitor's tense from “they were” to “people are.” The point is not to master somebody else's cultural knowledge during a short visit. It is to recognize both ongoing presence and the limits of what has been shared publicly.

## 3. Then / now
- The park interprets a long human history, but this dossier does not turn archaeological sequences into an asserted single Tribal identity or a reconstructed ceremony.
- **April 27, 2024:** local reporting and the Washakie Museum's autumn newsletter identify the Cultural Experience opening. The latter describes access when an attendant is present, another reason not to assume automated installation means unattended public admission. [3–4]
- **2025:** a different facility, the Interpretive History Cabin, was announced for reopening June 7 after repairs for flooding and snowmelt damage. [5]
- **Now:** the operator still distinguishes park/headquarters hours and seasonal cabin closure. Those categories must remain separate in any trip listing. [1]

A technology-mediated presentation is a modern interpretation, not surviving footage of ancient life or a direct substitute for community authority. This dossier does not reproduce the installation's animated scenes, music, cultural designs or ceremonial narratives.

## 4. What's still there
**Public interpretation candidate:** the Cultural Experience and its recorded contemporary voices are documented, but this winter's functioning screens, accessible admission and full content inventory are not field verified. A historical opening announcement does not establish that every component works today. [2–4]

**Interpretive History Cabin:** the operator's May 21, 2025 release names superintendent **Heather Jolley** and construction supervisor **Cole Horton**. It describes raising/replacing the foundation, replacing lower sill logs and flooring, and reinstalling interpretation. This is a concrete record of preservation labor, distinct from the Tribal voices and distinct from current admission. [5]

**Protected cultural context:** archaeology is not a visitor search area. No rock panel, archaeological deposit, burial location, ceremonial place, private property or surrounding habitat land is included as a stop. No artifact handling or ground disturbance is proposed.

**Permanent rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
1. **Planning gate:** confirm the particular public interior is open for the intended date. A park-open message alone does not pass this gate.
2. **At authorized admission only:** follow staff direction into the public interpretation. Do not test locked cabins or use an unmarked entrance.
3. **Listen before comparing:** attend to how named living speakers describe continuity. Keep a speaker's words attributed rather than presenting them as a universal Indigenous perspective.
4. **Separate mediums:** distinguish modern animations, archaeological explanation and firsthand testimony. None licenses a self-directed cultural exploration outside.
5. **Strongest final moment:** leave with Posey's point about knowledge held by people. Ask what respectful learning requires of a visitor, rather than which symbol the visitor has “solved.”

If the interior gate fails, end with the published account remotely. Do not add a cliff, trail or habitat-area loop as consolation. This is an editorial sequence, not a field-cleared route.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Cabin versus Cultural Experience:** they are different facilities. Their dates and seasonal gates must not be merged. [1,5]
- **Opening language:** Rossi's May 5, 2024 article reports an April 27 opening but ends with future-tense wording about later spring public access. The Washakie newsletter supports the ceremonial opening date; neither is current winter admission proof. [3–4]
- **Older field-trip advice:** WyoHistory's page says grounds mostly open 24 hours, a May–September visitor-center season, and a 1.5-mile walk. This conflicts with current operator hours and does not resolve access to the newer installation. No distance or route from that page is adopted. [6]
- **Namesakes:** Medicine Lodge, Kansas, and Medicine Wheel/Medicine Mountain are not this site. Neither supplies this site's history or visitor instructions.
- **Adjacent management:** a separately indexed Wyoming Game and Fish notice lists a Medicine Lodge habitat-area vehicle closure January–June. The full page returned 403; it is not used as permission for any alternative approach. [7]
- No direct Tribal-published full transcript or project-wide cultural reuse approval was verified. The named statements here are published through reporters, with that mediation made explicit.
- No artwork meanings, precise cultural locations or claimed “safe” photography locations are inferred from photographs.
