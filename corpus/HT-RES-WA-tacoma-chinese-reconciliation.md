## 2. The story
On November 3, 1885, Tacoma's Chinese residents were forcibly expelled by an organized white mob that included civic leaders. The park's own history describes people driven out and buildings subsequently burned, but its framing of a supposedly orderly method must not be mistaken for absence of harm. Families, livelihoods and community were attacked. [6]

**Listen first to an expelled resident:** Lum May's sworn account, dated June 3, 1886, describes ten years of Tacoma business selling provisions, medicine and general merchandise. His short sentence **“I asked General Sprague and other Citizens of Tacoma for protection”** restores a person's request for safety, rather than centering the perpetrators' self-description. The published transcription also records forced removal of his wife. This is testimony, not a modern medical diagnosis. [7]

Kwok Sue's account describes merchandise and labor contracting, destroyed records and losses he could not fully document. Sing Lee explicitly distinguishes what he learned from others from what he personally witnessed: he was away when his family was expelled. Their differing vantage points must remain visible. [7]

The university project's source index identifies the originals as **Miscellaneous Letters of the Department of State, 1789–1906, NARA M-179, Roll 707**. Six scanned affidavits and a transcription are published. The transcript was read; the Lum May scan was located but not fully visually collated. Do not describe this as an independently checked diplomatic original. [8–9]

Reconciliation is also labor: the City's chronology names Dr. and Mrs. David Murdock's 1991 citizen request, a 1992 advisory committee, and Resolution 32415 of November 30, 1993. These are modern actions to confront the record, not a claim that the harm has been undone. [10]

## 3. Then / now
**Then:** Chinese households and businesses occupied several parts of Tacoma; the expulsion and destruction were not confined to a single modern park parcel. [6–7]

**Now:** This is a deliberately created landscape for historical interpretation and reconciliation. The City describes the former National Guard property as near the early Chinese settlement, not as a preserved cluster of original Chinese homes. The Fuzhou Ting is a modern gift from Tacoma's sister city, not a surviving 1885 structure. [5,11]

The Foundation continues public anti-racist commemoration. Its October 24, 2026 walk announcement describes a symbolic reversal of the expulsion. That dated event is not a standing invitation to retrace an unverified historical route, cross rail ground, or join an event after it has ended. [12]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Reflection sequence within an open public park, not a mapped route:
1. At the authorized entrance, read the welcome and understand why this landscape exists. Do not begin with a photo challenge.
2. Use open interpretive paths to encounter the community's work and ordinary lives before reading about their forced removal.
3. Read the short Lum May testimony excerpt with its source label. Allow silence; do not reenact an expulsion or ask visitors to role-play victims.
4. Compare the modern city's acknowledgment with the older failure of protection.
5. **Strongest ending:** At a permitted quiet public place, consider the request for protection and what responsibility the present community accepts. This is an editorial reflection prompt, not a claimed authentic standing location for Lum May.

If closed or unsafe, read the published testimony remotely. Nothing is collected, scored, unlocked or hunted.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Numbers:** The Foundation synopsis says roughly 200 people remained to be driven out. Testimony gives larger estimates of Chinese residents in and around Tacoma. Different estimates and scopes must not become a falsely precise single count. [6–7]
- **Fire dates:** The synopsis places burning the next day; the affidavit transcription contains November 5 and November 6 references. Preserve this discrepancy pending original-document collation; do not conflate distinct fires. [6–7]
- **Transcription quality:** The compilation contains omissions and apparent typographical/date errors. It is a research entry point, not a critical edition. No complete property-loss inventory reconstructed. [7]
- **Construction dates:** City 2015 fact sheet says Phase III finished September 2015; current project archive gives 2016. These may refer to different completion stages, but that explanation is unverified. [2,11]
- **Hours:** General code verified; current site-specific posted exceptions and same-day conditions remain unverified. [3]
- **Location:** HMdb's marker-address approximation differs from the operator park address. Do not substitute a marker geocode for the visitor entrance. [1,13]
- **Tribal context:** This dossier does not supply a researched Puyallup first-person account or site-specific Tribal history. Do not invent one or frame Chinese community history as the beginning of habitation here.
