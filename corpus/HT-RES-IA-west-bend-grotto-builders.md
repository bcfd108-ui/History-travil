# West Bend — The hands behind the stone

Iowa OP05 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**The first stone was only the beginning.** The National Register nomination dates the beginning of the work to 1912 and describes the connected stone and concrete structures. The operator says Father Paul Dobberstein collected and stockpiled material for years before that. Its list of materials includes petrified wood, agate, calcite, quartz and geodes. This was a building and sorting job as much as a search for striking stone. In the workshop, Dobberstein and assistant Matt Szerensce could make components such as rosette panels in advance. SPACES Archives documents how that method helped them carry components to other sites. That broader workshop practice supplies context for West Bend. Individual portable panels at West Bend still need documented identification. [2, 5, 6]

**A coworker finished what a single artist could not.** The nomination calls Szerensce the person who carried out work after Dobberstein’s death in 1954 under Father Louis Greving’s direction. It says Dobberstein called Szerensce “his good right hand”. The present shrine account gives the wording “my good right arm”. Both are retrospective attributions; no independently dated statement in Dobberstein’s hand was found. The significance of Szerensce is supported by both accounts. The shrine puts him to work full time in 1912 and gives his retirement as 1959, while it also describes a 52-year association. Those dates and the duration do not reconcile as a single full-time term. [2, 5]

**What a museum can put in your hand through a case.** The operator identifies stone specimens, early news reproductions and tools used by Dobberstein in the museum. Think of each tool beside the repeated seams and changes in texture on the outdoor walls. This was a working Catholic shrine as well as a building project. The public visit leaves worship and private activity undisturbed. [1–5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic place.** The listed Grotto complex adjacent to Saints Peter and Paul Catholic Church. The federal listing and walking guide use 300 North Broadway. The homepage gives 208 1st Avenue NW in the footer. These are published address contexts; the nominated structure and current visitor parking should be found by the operator’s on-site signs. [1, 4, 5, 7]

**Lawful public anchor.** Shrine of the Grotto of the Redemption, West Bend. Drive up to public visitor parking indicated by the shrine and use signed lower paths in safe daylight. Operator map and posted barriers control. Office, gift shop, church, upper passages and the museum have separate use or schedules. [1, 3, 4]

## 5. A short visit

Start at the lower outdoor entrance and use the operator map before moving between alcoves. From a clear open path, look for contrasting textures where individual stones meet the concrete. Read the two accounts of Szerensce's work, then finish with **A wall two people could build**, recognizing the named assistant and the continuation after 1954. If winter conditions prevent the walk, use the operator’s virtual tour. [2, 4–6, 8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The nomination and operator differ in the reported nickname for Szerensce. The owner’s published dates and labor-duration statements differ internally in scope or chronology. Direct reading of a work ledger, dated construction account from Szerensce or Dobberstein’s original undated account was not achieved. The operator’s January inventory closure describes the gift shop, not the outdoor shrine. Current museum entry hours were not separately established. The LOC landing page for a distinct Margolies candidate was inaccessible and its frame and rights were held. [1–3, 5, 9]
