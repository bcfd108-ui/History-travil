# Atchison: the house Amelia Earhart kept calling home
KS OP05 · Draft for Dash · November 20, 2026

## 1. Trip gate

Walk-in self-guided tickets need no reservation; operator admission is separate. Date-specific winter holiday closures were not verified. ## 2. The story
**Four places claimed her. She named Atchison.** In her 1928 book *20 hrs., 40 min.*, Earhart remembers that after the Atlantic crossing, cablegrams arrived from at least four communities claiming her. She answered with a small distinction: “I spent more time in Atchison, Kansas, than anywhere else.” She describes her father’s traveling work as a railroad claims agent and attorney, and her own attendance at high schools in six communities. Her words explain the geography of a mobile childhood without making Atchison her only home. [2]

The museum identifies this as her maternal grandparents’ home, where she was born July 24, 1897, and lived on and off through eighth grade. Purdue's family-paper guide distinguishes the sisters' stay with their grandparents from their parents' move to Des Moines in 1907 and records that the girls stayed in Atchison through September 1909. The household helped make continuity possible when work moved the parents. [3,4]

## 3. Then / now
The museum dates the start of the Otis cottage to 1861. Subsequent owners made changes; the Ninety-Nines' museum preserves and interprets it rather than presenting every object as unchanged Otis household material. [3,5]

## 4. What's still there
- **Public museum interior during opening hours:** use operator-listed street parking on Santa Fe or N. Terrace and the marked north entrance (four steps). High curbs are flagged. An east ramp entrance is available by ringing the bell or calling staff for the door. [1,6]
- **Limited interior access:** two downstairs rooms have steps; second floor requires 20 stairs. A subtitled upstairs video is offered in the Butler's Pantry. Use signed museum areas only. [6]
- **Historical location:** the Otis house is the museum itself, not the separately operated airport museum, a residence nearby, or the bluff beyond the grounds. A historic road or district pin is not a public entrance. [3,5]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
One drive-up, short-walk stop. Begin in the entry hall at the scale of a family house. Use the open rooms or the ground-floor video according to access; end by reading Earhart's line about Atchison. It lands better here as a statement about where a child spent her days than as another aviation milestone. [1–3,6]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
The operator's on-and-off school-age account and the archive's date for the girls leaving Atchison serve different purposes; neither is a continuous residence calendar. No current room-level provenance or holiday-hours confirmation. No airport or private property route. [1,3,4]
