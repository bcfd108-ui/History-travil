## 1. Trip gate

**NO-ANCHOR RESEARCH READING — NO VISITOR ROUTING.** **Pinecrest Supports & Services Center** (`100 Pinecrest Dr., Pineville, LA 71360`) is an **active state-operated residential Intermediate Care Facility (ICF/IID)** administered by the Louisiana Department of Health (**LDH**) for individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities. [1, 2, 3]

Although a 163-acre edge parcel of the original state tract was leased separately to the Ward 9 recreational facility, the **784-acre active residential and clinical care campus** houses vulnerable residents (`~430` supported in 2021 reporting across residential cottages, clinics, and day-service buildings) and carries **no public museum or heritage tour anchor**. **No visitor pin or campus routing is issued.** [2, 3]

## 2. The story

After 129 individuals with intellectual disabilities were transferred from a New Orleans asylum into **East Louisiana State Hospital** at Jackson in **1884**, Jackson's superintendent recommended in **1914** creating a specialized institution apart from psychiatric wards. The Louisiana Legislature responded with **Act No. 141 of 1918**, establishing the **State Colony and Training School** (later Pinecrest State School and today **Pinecrest Supports & Services Center**). [1, 2, 3]

In **1921**, the state purchased **1,000 acres** from the Beauregard Development Company—incorporating former **World War I Camp Beauregard** acreage north of Pineville—and officially opened on **December 21, 1921**, admitting **37 initial residents** on a **$50,000** annual budget. In its early decades, the campus operated as a nearly self-sufficient farm colony with vegetable gardens, a dairy, hogs, chickens, bakery, and laundry. [2]

Resident census peaked at **more than 2,100 individuals during the 1970s**. As Louisiana shifted toward community-based care and closed or privatized seven of its nine state-run ICFs under Governor Bobby Jindal (after Hurricane Katrina destroyed another in 2005), **Pinecrest remained Louisiana's sole surviving state-run Intermediate Care Facility**—supporting roughly **430 residents** across **40 residential home buildings** and **12 day-service buildings** (out of 335 campus structures on 784 active acres) at its centennial in **2021**, while three former Pinecrest buildings were renovated in 2024–25 for relocated Central Louisiana State Hospital operations nearby. [1, 2, 4]
