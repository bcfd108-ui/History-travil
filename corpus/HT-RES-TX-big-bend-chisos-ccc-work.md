# Chisos Basin — the men who made a road into Big Bend
Research draft · Texas OP05 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### The road first had to be made

The road into today’s visitor basin was once work ground. NPS describes a 1934 Civilian Conservation Corps camp and credits its crews with cutting an all-weather road, moving 10,000 truckloads of earth and rock and building 17 stone culverts. A later camp, established in 1940, worked on trails, facilities and boundary surveys. Its March 1942 closing predates the national park’s 1944 establishment. The period photograph makes the scale legible: hand tools, workers spread across the grade and a truck waiting on the cut [4–5].

### An enrollee left a different kind of record

In his August 23, 1940 statement, enrollee Austin Lawson described a reprimand from camp commander Walter Scoggins. In the park-history study’s reproduction, Lawson remembered that “everything went black before him.” That report traces his subsequent time in the infirmary. Scoggins told investigator James Reddoch he had questioned Lawson for three minutes, while Lawson described approximately twenty. These are separate statements with separate accounts of duration and circumstances. Their disagreement shows why a camp history needs both the people who administered it and the people who lived under their authority [6].

### The visitor entry is part of the story

The road lowered the work of reaching the Basin for later generations. The ordinary visitor center makes it possible to consider the landscape of that work without walking onto road structures or locating camp debris. Read the wage figures in NPS’s program overview alongside the personnel record. A program valuable to families could also put its enrollees far from home and under the authority of an institution whose files seldom preserve each man’s voice [4, 6].

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic place.** Crews worked on the Chisos Basin road and in camp areas, including the area of the modern campground identified by NPS [4].

**Lawful physical anchor.** NPS Chisos Basin Visitor Center, reached by signed open park road into the Basin and a short walk from the signed visitor lot. The historical road cut can be experienced as part of normal driving. Do not stop on its curves, walk to culverts or search for camp remains [1–4].

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Check NPS alerts, then use only the signed open road to the public lot. If Basin access is restricted, use online reading or the open visitor center at Panther Junction for orientation [1–3].
2. At the Basin visitor area view the historical road-work image and read the record of hand labor and culverts [4–5].
3. **Strongest story moment — The distance someone else cut.** Look out from the designated visitor approach and put Lawson’s statement alongside the road photograph. End with the workers whose names the picture leaves undocumented [5–6].
