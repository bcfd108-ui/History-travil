## 2. Story
**Churchill Downs and the Kentucky Derby Museum · Research draft · Fred**

When the Louisville Jockey Club opened its meeting on May 17, 1875, the newspaper account emphasized the crowd. In the free Library of Congress transcription from the following day's Nashville Union and American, attendance was “upwards of 12,000.” The grandstand that journalist saw was an earlier stand, on the opposite side from today's historic grandstand. The landmark nomination describes how a local club, subscriptions and leased Churchill land brought the track into operation. It names Meriwether Lewis Clark Jr., landowners John and Henry Churchill, financier W. H. Thomas, and clubhouse architect John Andewartha. [3–5]

The workers and riders made the event. Black jockey Oliver Lewis rode Aristides to victory in the original mile and a half race. The race distance changed in 1896. Another Black rider, Isaac Murphy, became a three-time Derby winner. The Register account preserves both riders' names in a narrative that might otherwise be dominated by organizers and later construction. The 1895 grandstand's Twin Spires became the visual shorthand for the track. The museum gives today's visitors a controlled way to read that history without entering racing or stable operations. [2–5]

## 3. Then and now
1875 founding site, 1895 grandstand era and modern exhibition are three layers. The Kentucky Historical Society marker gives the use of the Twin Spires grandstand as 1895 and the first Derby as 1875. The museum stands at the public Central Avenue entrance. The racecourse remains a working venue with staff-operated public visits. [1–4]

## 4. Surviving features and access
- **Public interior with ticket** — Museum galleries when open.
- **Public approach** — Signed museum entrance on Central Avenue. Race days may affect parking.
- **Controlled exterior** — Track or grandstand viewpoints only when the museum includes them on a staff-guided tour.
- **Operations areas** — Racing surfaces, barns, stable and staff ground outside this itinerary. [1–2]

## 5. Public trip progression
Begin in the Kentucky Derby Museum galleries. If the operator's regular guided track viewpoint is available, take only its permitted route. End at **The first winner and the later grandstand**, connecting the accounts of Oliver Lewis's 1875 win and the date of the spires visible today. The indoor display can carry that comparison when the exterior tour is unavailable. [1–5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
- The visitor guide's winter Sunday display reads “11 PM - 4 PM,” which cannot be an ordinary daytime schedule. The winter Sunday opening is held pending operator confirmation. The museum guide lists various year-end and spring closure dates, so use its date-specific calendar. [1–2]
- The newspaper's crowd figure describes its estimate for the 1875 opening, not a measured admission tally. The first two readable LOC resource views returned an inaccessible URL in retrieval. The archive's one retrieved full page did not contain the cited story. The quoted line is identified as LOC's later newspaper transcription. [5–6]
- Direct 1875 jockey papers, stable labor records and early race payroll remain unverified. The 1895 grandstand and 1875 venue refer to different building phases. [3–4]
