# Greensburg Big Well — Who lined the shaft

## 2. The story

**The town needed more than drinking water.** The original registration statement describes a waterworks to serve households, fire protection and the railroad. It records the first Santa Fe train arriving June 23, 1887 and a July 18 franchise to the Greensburg Water Supply and Hydraulic Company. The well opened a larger question in a fast-growing town: who could finance a reliable supply, and who would undertake the labor? The original ordinance was cited in the nomination’s narrative. Its signed text was not inspected here. [4,5]

**A date for the shovel and a date for the system.** The nomination dates the first work to August 9, 1887 and official city acceptance to March 14, 1888. Its reading of contemporary newspapers reports 25 to 30 men at the site. Chief engineer J. H. Rhodes supervised the project. J. W. Wheeler directed digging and walling, and railroad foreman Harry F. Hall supervised carpentry. The period articles listed by the nomination were not read as scans. The credential belongs to the nomination’s attributed evidence. [4]

**The carpenter returned.** The nomination says Hall revisited Greensburg on August 22, 1960, when he was in his nineties. A local story said that workers constructed the casing at ground level and lowered it as a unit. In the nomination’s summary, Hall recalled temporary timber cribbing during excavation and the stone wall being laid “from the bottom up.” Those words are the nomination writer’s phrasing. Hall’s original interview or contemporaneous notes were not retrieved. The difference changes what the visible masonry can teach about the construction sequence. [4]

**A shaft through two civic lives.** The shaft supplied public water. The city says it was used for that purpose until 1932 and opened as a visitor attraction in 1939. The shaft survived the 2007 tornado. Local visitor information dates opening of the present museum to May 26, 2012. The modern building frames both the well story and later rebuilding. The masonry and the museum envelope have different dates and evidentiary roles. [2,3,5,6]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- Local visitor information compresses the hand digging to 1888; the register gives both 1887 and 1888 and names the August start and March acceptance. Use the dates for their different milestones. [2,4]
- The lowering story and Hall’s 1960 account are competing construction explanations within the same nomination. The signed franchise, crew payroll, supporting photographs, Hall’s original record and the newspaper issues listed by the nomination remain uninspected. [4]
- The city’s claim about the world’s largest hand-dug well is promotional. The register describes it as billed that way and supplies dimensions. The comparative global claim was not independently verified. [4,5]
- The museum homepage returned zero readable lines in the web reader. The city lists the park, and local visitor information confirms the replacement museum, but it supplied no current Big Well opening grid. Holiday hours, threshold accessibility, upper viewing access and stair availability are unconfirmed. The hours lower on that visitor page are for the separate Kiowa County Historical Museum. [1–3]
