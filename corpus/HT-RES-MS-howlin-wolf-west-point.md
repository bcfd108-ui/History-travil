# Howlin’ Wolf and the work of making a song carry a story

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP16*

## 2. Story

Before the Chicago records, Chester Arthur Burnett was a boy moving between households in Mississippi. He remembered starting to pay attention to music when he heard Charlie Patton. That is his recollection in Pete Welding’s interview, published under a December 14, 1967 date. Welding explains that he cut his own questions and rearranged the replies in rough time order. Burnett remembers West Point as the place of his birth and gives June 10, 1910. The Mississippi Encyclopedia specifies White Station near West Point. The West Point memorial presents his regional roots without identifying a birth parcel. [2][4][5]

In the interview Burnett stops at the work of making words fit what his band played. “I just watch people, their ways,” he said. A song grew from how people moved and lived, and a singer needed to make a story fit the music. That is his edited first-person account of the craft, rather than a lyric or a claim that every verse is autobiography. The Mississippi Encyclopedia traces the separate chapters from Patton and Delta performance to recording in Memphis and later Chicago. He had to become a musician on each of those stages, working with other players and people who recorded, sold and broadcast the records. [4][5]

West Point’s commemoration also involved labor by named people. The city page identifies Mayor Kenny Dill, Joe K. Stephens and Albert Mattix among the organizers of the Blues Society in 1995. The organizers report continued participation by Burnett’s family, including Lillie Handley Burnett and their daughters. Lillie’s memorial seat makes visible a second person and the family’s role in the history this town decided to remember. [1]

## 3. Then and now

Burnett’s formative landscape extended across a region and beyond West Point. The present Broad Street installation is the town’s public memorial to him. The bank building on Commerce Street is the separately operated Black Prairie Blues Museum. Burnett’s actual childhood parcel and the museum's present exhibit inventory remain open research questions. [1–5]

## 4. Surviving features and access

- **Public exterior.** The granite memorial, interpretive marker and associated memorial bench on the public display side of East Broad Street. Observe within the immediate memorial space and obey any local signs. [1][2]
- **Separate interior, access requires confirmation.** The museum lists Wednesday and Friday noon–4 alongside appointment language. This draft directs a visit to the memorial. The museum’s website supplies interior contact details. [3]
- **Audio archive.** The University of North Texas catalogs a distinct interview in John Gilliland’s Pop Chronicles. It is a source for further listening. This article quotes Welding’s published interview only. [4][6]

## 5. Public progression and strongest story moment

Use the memorial’s public exterior, read the names and pause at the bench if it is available. Bring Burnett’s short comment on watching people to that place. **Strongest story moment — The second name on Broad Street.** The granite figure records the performer a record buyer would know. The dedication to Lillie Handley Burnett puts a family name back in view at the same stop. [1][2][4]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- Burnett told Welding “West Point, near Tupelo, in Monroe County.” The state encyclopedia uses White Station near West Point, while the city calls Burnett a native of Clay County. The exact birth ground and how the geographic descriptions relate remain open. The memorial is a modern public anchor with its own documented address. [1][2][4][5]
- An older city Blues Society page still speaks of promoting the establishment of a museum. The current Black Prairie Blues Museum publishes its own Commerce Street visitor information. Its appointment wording and recurring hours do not by themselves establish reliable drop-in entry. [1][3]
- The UNT oral history was retrieved at catalog level. Audio contents and timestamps were not verified here. The LOC recording-registry listing returned HTTP 403 at retrieval and is not used for the narrative. [6][7]
