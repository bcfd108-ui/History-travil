## 2. The story

**Named firsthand worker:** Cleophas (“Bill”) Williams, in an edited oral-history selection published in *The Dispatcher*, August 1998, edited by Harvey Schwartz. The union archive’s introduction places his Local 10 career in 1944–1981 and election as its first Black president in 1967. Williams recalls arriving from the segregated South, finding both opportunity and racial indifference, and learning from other workers. He describes the California Labor School as his first integrated school. His concluding distinction is concise: **“It wasn't arrogance. It was pride.”** [4]

This is retrospective testimony, selected and edited by a union historian for the union’s newspaper. It is not an unedited recording or independent comprehensive history. The introduction identifies interviews early in 1998 and acknowledges a separate 1995 interview; the precise source of each excerpt is not specified. His wife’s encouragement appears through his recollection, not as her own interview. [4]

**Original interpretive voice:** A waterfront is often pictured as ships and cargo. Here the center of the picture is a person deciding whether he counts. A job can offer a wage without offering full recognition; belonging can grow through learning, friendship and disagreement. Williams’s pride should not erase the exclusions he describes. It gives us a way to read labor history as more than machinery or a heroic leader’s biography.

**Loss and earlier history:** ILWU’s commemoration identifies Howard Sperry and Nick Bordoise as the two men killed in San Francisco on July 5, 1934, and remembers the subsequent funeral and general strike. Use this as an explicitly union-authored remembrance. No violent sequence, tactic, death-site route or reenactment is included. Williams’s later waterfront career must not be recast as eyewitness testimony to 1934. [5]

5. **Strongest ending:** return to the distinction between pride and arrogance. What would a historical record need to preserve so that workers are remembered as thinkers and citizens rather than anonymous labor?
