## 2. The story
**Lorina Antonio**, a former Intermountain student, told KUER reporter Jon Reed that she arrived in 1967 at age twelve, frightened and far from home. Her published words include **“I didn't know when I was going to go home.”** She also described activities, supportive adults and opportunities she valued. Both parts of her account belong together. The written 2021 article was read; no unedited recording is claimed. Her experience must not become a verdict on every student. [3]

A different named family voice asks what descendants need from the record. **Monica Tsinagini**, in a publicly filed letter dated May 13, 2022, identifies her late mother **Annie Sam Tsinagini** as an Intermountain student in 1951–55 and 1957. She describes difficulties reconstructing family history from incomplete institutional records and asks for access that would help families document their ties. The letter's phrase **“properly document our family ties”** is the central point used here. This is the daughter's published account; underlying private student files have not been accessed or independently authenticated. Sensitive family and health details in the submission are not reproduced. [4]

These are not opposing sides in a debate over whether a school was good or bad. They reveal different relationships to the institution: a former student's memories of separation and opportunity, and a daughter's effort to recover a family record. Neither requires visitors to judge which family is more representative.

USU's collection overview identifies assimilation into mainstream American society as an educational goal and points to student publications, classroom material, publicity photographs and congressional correspondence. Those categories have different purposes and power relationships. An official publicity image is not a student's private account; a student publication is not automatically an unrestricted statement of everything a student experienced. [5]

The interpretive landing is the person beyond the institutional label. A preserved record can matter deeply, while still being partial, biased or silent about what a family needs to know.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Choice is not presumed:** AASLH’s earlier award summary broadly describes attendance as chosen, whereas Monica Tsinagini describes removal of her mother and siblings by BIA personnel. The broad institutional characterization is not adopted as a universal fact. [4,7]
- **Different experiences are not a factual contradiction:** positive recollections do not erase forced-assimilation policy or other people's harm. Institutional summaries cannot replace students' own voices. [3–5]
- **Date caution:** the congressional file path includes May 12, 2022, while the letter itself is dated May 13. Cite the document date, without inventing a submission chronology. [4]
- **Institutional versus public name:** the library describes Intermountain School and later Intermountain Inter-Tribal School; familiar historical usage varies. Keep the source's label where quoted. [6]
- **Current display:** older award/exhibition reports are not current gallery verification. The museum's generic interactive features are not adopted as an activity for this subject. [1,7]
- **Winter:** regular operator hours and Thanksgiving closure verified; Christmas/New Year's exceptions, weather status and step-free public entry remain open.
