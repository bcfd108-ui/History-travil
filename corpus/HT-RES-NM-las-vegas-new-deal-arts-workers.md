## 2. The story

**Named arts worker: Lloyd Moylan.** The community arts organization gallupARTS documents his Federal Art Project commissions, including **The Dissemination of Education at Highlands University's Rogers Hall in Las Vegas, New Mexico**, dated 1938 in its entry. This establishes a specific local connection, not merely a statewide artist list. [4]

The same source reproduces a statement from his Federal Art Project essay: public art **“will bring to the surface much that is growth-provoking in the spirit of humanity.”** Its link identifies the Holger Cahill Papers, reel 5292, frames 0224–0226, at the Smithsonian Archives of American Art. The linked archival images failed retrieval. Thus this is **a named artist's statement reproduced by a community research publisher**, not a manuscript independently read here. [4,5]

**Original interpretive voice:** Public art is often introduced as something beautiful left on a wall. A labor history starts earlier: an artist needed paid work, a public program commissioned it, and a community became its audience. That changes the meaning of the finished image. It is evidence of a decision that making art counted as work, and that people outside private collecting circles deserved to encounter it.

No assertion is made that Moylan's pictures provide an authoritative account of the cultures or historical peoples represented in them.
