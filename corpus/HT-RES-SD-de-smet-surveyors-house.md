# De Smet Surveyors’ House and railroad families

## 2. The story

### A railroad camp after its crews left

In a manuscript version of *By the Shores of Silver Lake*, Laura Ingalls Wilder called the surveyors’ quarters “a real house.” That short phrase comes from Nancy S. Cleaveland’s published transcription of the manuscript. Wilder describes a camp of specialized buildings arranged around the lake, with a boarding shanty, bunkhouse, stable, feed storage and her father's office and store. Each stands for a job. The term “real house” has force because a child accustomed to moving could compare a room set aside for living with places meant to feed men, house teams or hold supplies. [3]

The Chicago and Northwestern Railway built the shanty in 1878 according to South Dakota SHPO. Railroad workers left for winter and the Ingalls family occupied the building in 1879–80 according to SHPO and the De Smet teaching resource. The latter also describes the family offering room and board to incoming settlers as spring approached. Read that as an episode in household work. Rooms, fuel, food and time had to be made available to paying strangers while the family was deciding where it would live next. The record here gives the arrangement without a surviving account book telling us who cooked each meal or what guests paid. [4,5]

### Which winter belongs to which house?

The Society’s current tour covers the household’s 1879 arrival and the 1880–81 Long Winter. Those tour subjects can merge in a visitor’s memory. The Society’s teaching resource dates the family’s move into the surveyors’ quarters to December 1879 and discusses the later winter separately. Author Susan Wittig Albert’s reader’s companion reports that handwritten and typed versions of *Pioneer Girl* place the family in the surveyors’ house during 1880–81 and name a boarder, Walter Ogden. Carry both sources into the visit. Wilder’s retrospective manuscript is a record of what she wrote decades later. The exhibition’s chronology is a separate claim. [2,5,6]

### What moved

The house now visited in town is a preserved railroad building, moved from its original setting. The nomination describes a move of about three blocks, provisionally dated to the 1880s or early 1890s, and records that the Memorial Society purchased the building in 1967. [11] A photograph of the house today cannot show the lakeside relationships Wilder wrote about. Stand inside on a guided tour and use the manuscript’s list of buildings to picture a camp that ran on paid work, animal power and domestic labor together. [1–5]

## 3. Then / now

The railway built quarters on the Silver Lake side of the camp. The visitor encounters the relocated building in De Smet, on a tour with a school and a separately built Ingalls home. The National Register’s Railroad Camp Shanty record concerns the shanty. The 210 West Third Street Ingalls Home has a separate nomination. [2–5,8,10]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- Albert’s report of the later manuscript versions and the 1879–80 interpretation differ on the winter in the Surveyors’ House. Preserve that disagreement without locating the 1880–81 boarder from the fiction. [4–6]
- The nomination and SHPO image caption call the interior unaltered. An indexed architectural history by Cleaveland describes removal of a wall and a staircase reset during restoration. The direct article returned an internal retrieval error. The degree and chronology of alterations need physical or archival confirmation. [4,7,11]
- SHPO caption says February 1973 for listing and NPS metadata prints March 20, 1973 as its published date. The documents checked do not resolve the difference in month or date label. [4,8]
- The nomination is readable. Wilder’s original manuscript and a period view tied to this exact shanty were not retrieved. [3,8,9,11]

## 7. Photo record

The 2014 SHPO exterior image is a modern comparison view. The annotated manuscript guide reproduces a Frank Jay Haynes 1880 stereoview of railroad grading activity. It does not establish a De Smet Surveyors’ House view. An NPS companion photo file was located with only six text lines rendered.
