# Decorah — The Egge household made room

Iowa OP03 · Research draft for Dash

## 2. Story

**A room for the newcomers.** The Egges’ 1852 log house originally stood at Washington Prairie. Erik and Helene Egge were housing another newly arrived couple, Ulrik Vilhelm and Elisabeth Koren, in the winter of 1853–54. Elisabeth's Dec. 31 diary, as translated and reproduced on the park sign, gives the household a voice: “I sat thinking of Father and all the dear ones in our distant native land”. The house tells two stories at once. One household had put up a building in northeast Iowa. Another had just found shelter there and was still thinking about the people it had left behind. The diary sentence is checked against the published sign transcription. The handwritten diary was unavailable for comparison. [3, 4, 5]

**What a trunk can carry.** Vesterheim's object record 1973.008.062 supplies a separate family history worth reading beside the house. According to the museum’s summary of B.T. Pedersen’s 1934 family account, the painted trunk had belonged to his father, Peder Tollisen Ryggsaas. The son emigrated in 1890. The catalog dates the trunk to 1837 and leaves its painter between two named candidates, Ivar and Kjetil Urdal. The story and the uncertainty both belong with the object. This is a separate household and an online collection record. No current gallery display is established. [6]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** Washington Prairie. The museum’s institutional history records Paul Egge’s 1913 donation, the house's 1921 move to the Luther campus and its later placement in the museum’s outdoor division. The house has a surviving building story and a changed geographic setting. Present-day Decorah paths cannot establish the original farm layout or the direction of Elisabeth’s view. [3, 5]

**Lawful public anchor.** Vesterheim Heritage Park at the 520 W Water Street campus, Decorah. A drive-up and short walk through posted park paths. Follow the open walk to the labeled house exterior. Guided interiors require the operator’s public seasonal offering. On icy or blocked days use the Main Building entrance marked on the operator’s 520 W Water Street campus map during museum hours or read remotely. The map identifies the Egge-Koren House as building 4. [1, 2, 8, 9]

## 4. Surviving features and access

- **Public exterior** — the relocated log house and interpretation along posted park paths. Confirm path conditions at the entrance. [1, 3, 4]
- **Seasonal guided access** — the heritage buildings are interpreted on the operator’s public summer program. Individual building opening remains subject to the actual tour. [1]
- **Source only** — original prairie site, diary manuscript, recipes and trunk catalog. Luther’s publication identifies material in the museum collection. Those references give context and no itinerary entries. [4–7]

## 5. A short visit

Read the dated diary line on the public path, then take in the house as a dwelling that could shelter two households. In winter, the main museum supplies the indoor portion where open. Land on **New Year’s Eve, far from home** at the house exterior or in the remote reading. Give Elisabeth's one sentence the last word. [1–4]
