# HT-RES-IN-mansfield-covered-bridge — Mansfield Covered Bridge: Daniels's 1867 Burr-arch bridge over Big Raccoon Creek
Division IN seed-catalog • Research draft • Checked November 25, 2026

Parke County, Indiana, keeps the densest surviving stand of timber covered bridges in America, and the Mansfield Bridge is its second-longest: a double-span Burr-arch truss built by Joseph J. Daniels in 1867 for $12,200, still carrying light traffic over Big Raccoon Creek beside the Mansfield Roller Mill.

## 1. Trip gate
The bridge spans Big Raccoon Creek on Mansfield Road (historic alignment) near the village of Mansfield, southeast of Rockville. It is open to pedestrian and limited vehicular traffic under a posted load limit (historically 10 tons). The nearby Mansfield Roller Mill is a state historic site with seasonal tours. Confirm current road status, load limits, and mill hours with Parke County or the Indiana Department of Natural Resources before travel. Parke County holds an annual Covered Bridge Festival.

## 2. The story
Parke County became known for its concentration of timber covered bridges in the second half of the 19th century. The Mansfield Bridge was contracted in late 1866 and completed in 1867 by Joseph J. Daniels of Rockville for $12,200. It is a double-span Burr arch truss (timber arch combined with multiple king-post framing), with a clear span of roughly 245 feet between abutments and an overall length exceeding 270 feet including portals. The site was chosen in part because a large water-powered roller mill operated just upstream on the west bank of Big Raccoon Creek; an earlier mill had stood in the area since about 1820.

The bridge survived major floods in 1868, 1913, and 1957. A flood-control dam completed upstream in the early 1960s further reduced flood risk. It was closed for abutment, roof, and deck repairs in 1980 and again received a new roof and deck in 1990. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1978 as part of the Parke County Covered Bridges thematic resource. At the time of the HAER survey it was closed for abutment work but scheduled for reopening; it remains in use today.

## 4. What's still there
The red-painted timber Burr-arch covered bridge with its portals, interior trusswork, and wooden deck remains in place. The abutments and approaches are intact. The historic Mansfield Roller Mill stands nearby on the creek. The surrounding rural setting and Big Raccoon Creek corridor match the 19th-century context.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Reach Mansfield via county roads southeast of Rockville. Park near the bridge approaches or mill. Walk or carefully drive the bridge (observe the load limit and "cross at a walk" signage). View the exterior portals and, from the interior, the Burr arches and truss framing. Optional visit to the Mansfield Roller Mill for milling history. Return by the same roads.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
Exact overall length figures vary slightly across sources (approximately 277–279 feet including portals). Secondary accounts differ on precise mill ownership sequences and the exact year the concrete dam replaced the earlier wooden one. Primary county contracts and Daniels's papers were not examined for this entry.
