# Emporia Red Rocks — Sallie White and the work behind the byline

## 2. The story

**The kitchen was at one address. The presses were elsewhere.** William Allen White acquired the Emporia Gazette in 1895. He and Sallie Lindsay White moved into the sandstone and brick residence later called Red Rocks in 1899. The nomination follows their move from a rental and documents the adjoining houses. It identifies Bertha Colglazier as the longtime family housekeeper and records the 1947 transfer of 913 Exchange to her. Her words and the terms and conditions of her work are missing from the sources read. [2,3]

**Sallie and the proof.** After their daughter Mary Katherine died in 1921, White remembered that he and Sallie walked to the Gazette office together. In the state historian’s quotation of his autobiography, he describes the pair as having “revised it three times in the proof.” The passage gives a specific task to someone the printed byline can obscure. He describes shared revision in the newspaper office. He does not give us a draft in Sallie’s hand, or tell us which changes she proposed. The same state study credits her with directing aspects of the household renovation, including French doors from the dining room and a change to the entrance hall. [3,4]

**A sign in a newspaper window.** In July 1922, during a railroad strike, White publicly challenged Governor Henry Allen’s anti-picketing enforcement after displaying a sign sympathetic to strikers at the Gazette office. The July 21 Eugene Daily Guard reported White preparing for arrest. In the University of Kansas’s transcription of “To an Anxious Friend,” the July 27 Gazette editorial, White wrote that “only in time of stress is freedom of utterance in danger.” Read the sign as a newspaper proprietor’s intervention. To recover a railroad worker’s perspective would require a separate record of a worker or union. [5–7]

## 3. Then / now

**Historic location.** The White residence is 927 Exchange Street. The editorial work in the obituary account and the placard incident is associated with the separate Gazette office. This dossier offers a home visit within the state operator’s public boundaries. The Gazette property and neighboring former family and staff houses are research context. [1–4,6,7]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Kansas Historical Society’s Red Rocks State Historic Site at 927 Exchange, in its staffed season. The approach is a drive-up followed by a short designated walk. The renovation moved the house’s formal entrance to Tenth Avenue. Use the visitor operator’s open entrance instructions. The grounds have no verified ordinary winter access in the operator record. [1–4]

The Whites retained and remodeled the house after the 1920 fire. The resulting Tudor Revival work, garden and porches are the later form of the residence. The early Queen Anne frame visually records a different arrangement. This is where a newspaper family lived, worked and entertained. [2–4]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The KU journalism page says White wrote the July 27 editorial after charges were dropped. The 2018 legal history reports that the charges stood after he took down the sign, a December 8 trial was set, and the governor subsequently had charges dropped. The contemporary July 21 report confirms the arrest proceedings were then beginning. The original Gazette issue and dated charging and dismissal records were not retrieved. This dossier does not resolve the sequence by adopting either retrospective account. [5–7]
- White’s autobiography is read here through a Kansas Historical Society publication’s quotation. The original manuscript, the three marked proofs of Mary’s obituary, Bertha’s own account and the site’s precise reopening date remain outstanding. Special appointment language for winter groups does not supply ordinary public access. [1–4,8]
