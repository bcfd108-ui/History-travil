# Langston — making the case for a school
Research draft · Oklahoma OP06 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

The *Langston City Herald* helped Black settlers find Langston and described practical work already underway there. An editorial cited in Nudie Williams’s study says, “The warrants have been cashed at a good figure.” The surrounding account connects a school-board census to access to school funds, the purchase of lumber and an order for furniture. The words are from the newspaper through the published transcription. The underlying issue page was not recovered here. William Eagleson handled daily management, and Edward McCabe edited and contributed, according to the archive. [2–4]

Langston University says people in the town petitioned for a higher-education institution in 1892 and that Territorial House Bill 151 formally established the Colored Agricultural and Normal University in 1897. The local school-building editorial is evidence of one effort. The later institution required a different legislative step. The university names Inman Page as its first president. Read the petition and the paper’s school-board details as two examples of families and institutions arranging education. [2–3,5]
