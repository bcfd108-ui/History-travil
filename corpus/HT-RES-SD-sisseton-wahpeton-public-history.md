## 2. The story

### The history the Nation chooses to tell

The Sisseton Wahpeton Oyate’s government page describes transitions from traditional government to an advisory committee in 1913 and a constitutional council in 1946. Set those dates beside the charter. The institution that holds authority today publishes its account of the changes in governance. A visitor starts with those words rather than a building or a marker. [1–2]

### The charter puts education into writing

The 2024 college charter identifies a purpose of “promoting economic development and self-sufficiency.” The same document sets out postsecondary education as a primary responsibility and gives the institution a board drawn from the Oyate’s districts. A documentary reader can follow the passage from a public account of self-government to the practical decision to organize and sustain a college. The college’s institutional history places its founding in 1979. The charter records a 2007 board action and an amendment in 2024. These dates refer to different acts. [1–2,4]

### A record can be shared under its own rules

The Tribal Historic Preservation Office says its work includes preserving cultural resources through documentation as well as physical care. Its public page identifies an archive function. The Tribal Research Code is an official statement of the Oyate’s authority over research activities within its scope. The archive and code are evidence of active stewardship. Contents, handling and reuse depend on the terms of the relevant record and office. The public pages themselves are sufficient for this encounter. [3,5]
