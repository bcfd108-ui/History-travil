## 2. The story
A dinosaur mount can make the human work behind it disappear. Ekalaka offers a different question: **who keeps the knowledge, and what makes a town willing to keep doing that work?**

The museum's own account dates its establishment to **1936**, by the Carter County Geological Society. It identifies **Walter Peck**, founder and first director, with the legislation that made county museum funding possible. It places the first museum in the local high school and the move to a former automotive garage in the late 1970s. These are the institution's published historical claims, not a legislative bill independently retrieved here. [3]

A named contemporary community voice brings the story into the present. In **Charlie Ban's County News report**, curator **Nate Carroll**, who grew up in Carter County, describes family members returning to see material associated with their relatives. His succinct description is **“a true community-built museum.”** In that same interview he explains that relationships and permission from landowners are part of his work, alongside archival research. His testimony is a reason to respect boundaries, not an invitation to follow his field routes. [4]

Executive director **Sabre Moore** describes the institution as useful to residents through community activities, not solely as a display for outsiders. These are published firsthand accounts of the speakers' work and priorities; they do not stand in for every local family's view. [4]

There is a paper trail behind the public story. The museum reports that a 2019 digitization effort covered 6,000 letters, documents and scrapbooks spanning 1930–1990. **Curtis Durham** curated a 72-document virtual selection, including Peck correspondence and exchanges involving **Barnum Brown** at the American Museum of Natural History. The linked Montana Memory exhibit returned a 403 access error in this research session, so no individual letter has been read or quoted. [5–6]

**Interpretive conclusion:** this is not merely a remote place that happens to have fossils. It is a community that built an institution so local knowledge could have continuity. The most important change of scale is from the spectacular specimen to the people who preserve a label, a relationship and a record. That interpretation does not endorse every historical collecting practice or imply that scientific collections and cultural belongings have identical histories or obligations.

- **2019:** a digitization project made a selected institutional paper trail available online, subject to the retrieval gap below. [5]

## 4. What's still there
**Public display setting:** an operating county museum with natural-history and community-history interpretation. Its website describes mounts and casts; do not label every displayed element an original fossil or assume every advertised exhibit is presently accessible. [3]

**Institutional records:** a museum-described digital collection and virtual exhibit. Catalog descriptions establish that correspondence exists, but are not substitutes for inspecting the individual documents. [5]

**Community knowledge:** the published accounts name working museum professionals and explain how local relationships enter museum practice. Their stories can be encountered without tracking families or locating private land. [4]

**Excluded:** fossil-site coordinates, dig directions, tools or collecting methods, private ranches, laboratory/preparation spaces, storage, construction areas and any cultural or archaeological location. No collecting and no artifact handling are proposed. If interpretation concerns Indigenous loss or contested collecting, use reflection only, with no scoring or reenactment. The museum's land acknowledgment is its own statement, not a substitute for a Tribal-authored voice. [7]

**Permanent rule:** a historic road or district pin is not a public entrance.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
1. **Before travel:** establish the day's public opening and any construction-related change. Read the named community account first, so the museum does not become a checklist of specimens.
2. **At the signed public entrance:** use ordinary admission. Do not infer an alternate door from the map pin or enter barriers to photograph the building.
3. **Within admitted galleries:** choose an openly displayed, nonsensitive interpretive label, if available. Read what it actually says about how knowledge was preserved. Do not assume it names a finder or gives an original accession history.
4. **End with the people:** return to Carroll's community-built description. Ask what a display would lose if the town's records and relationships disappeared, even if the specimen remained.

The strongest moment is the realization that a museum object can connect generations of residents, while its scientific meaning depends on more than its size. This is a visitor-reading sequence, not a guaranteed exhibit order or an activated route. If no suitable label is available, the published account alone supports reflection.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- The homepage calls the former garage the museum's original building, while the detailed history explicitly gives a high-school beginning and later move. Use the more specific sequence, and keep the conflict visible. [3, 7]
- The current expansion page gives approximately **17,000 square feet** of added space; County News reports **15,000**. They are planning/reporting figures, not reconciled as-built dimensions. No new gallery is claimed open. [2, 4]
- Website counts of Dinosaur Trail institutions differ among sources. No count is needed to establish this stop, so none is treated as definitive. [7–8]
- Original founding minutes, funding legislation and individual Peck letters were not inspected. The virtual exhibit's 403 response is an evidence gap, not proof the collection is unavailable to everyone. [6]
- The published winter schedule is verified; exceptional weather closure, holiday-week variations, entrance relocation and safe sidewalk conditions remain day-of-travel gates.
- No photo point, field visit, complete specimen provenance, Tribal-authored historical account or commercial image permission was established. The dossier deliberately does not expand into those unsupported claims.
