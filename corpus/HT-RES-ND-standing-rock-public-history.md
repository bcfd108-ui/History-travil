## 2. The story
**One-minute hook:** A Nation's history is not finished when the visitor reaches a famous nineteenth-century name. It continues when a leader speaks about children, public service and obligations in the present.

**Named Nation-published primary voice actually read:** **Janet Alkire's January 7, 2025 North Dakota State Tribal Address**, published on the Tribe's website in a page dated **January 18, 2025**, speaks in her capacity as **Chairwoman at the time of the address**. She describes remembrance alongside a forward-looking responsibility: **“our determination to remember our past while building a bridge to the future.”** [2]

The speech places her leadership among family roles and military service, and connects education and public services with shared responsibility. These are her stated priorities in that dated address, not a claim that every proposed measure had been achieved or that she holds the same office today. [2]

**Source discipline:** This is a text published by the sovereign Nation, with an identified speaker and occasion. It was read as text; no recording was heard and delivery has not been word-for-word compared against audio. A speech is primary evidence of its speaker's public account, not an independently audited report of every policy outcome.

**Distinct community contexts:** The Tribe's own history identifies Standing Rock people as Dakota and Lakota and distinguishes Upper and Lower Yanktonai contexts from Hunkpapa and Sihasapa. It explicitly cautions that Sihasapa, sometimes rendered Blackfeet, is not the same people as the Blackfeet of Montana and Canada. [3] Preserve those distinctions rather than simplifying everyone into one generic Plains identity.

**Institutional history, not anonymous narration:** The same Nation-published account emphasizes self-government and a continuing government-to-government relationship. [3] That framing matters: a public-history reader is encountering the present Nation's account of itself, not merely a historical population described by outsiders.

**Interpretive synthesis:** The strongest movement in this entry is from a historical subject to a speaking political community. Listen to the named address as a dated act of governance. Neither a tourist itinerary nor an outsider's summary can replace that voice.
