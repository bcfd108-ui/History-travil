# St. Joseph Pony Express Stables — A horse saddled, a pouch still waiting

Missouri OP01 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**A stable already stood here before the service started.** The federal building survey names Benjamin Holladay as the builder of the 1858 timber structure. The NPS trail page describes its first use serving a freight and stage business, with the express company acquiring the stable in 1860. Horses had to be housed and saddled. A former hotel down Penn Street housed the office and riders. The NPS account for Patee House describes staff sorting, cancelling and packing letters into a leather saddle cover with secure pockets. A fast dispatch took two workplaces. [2, 3, 8]

**Salt Lake City counted the hours differently.** On April 11, 1860, Elias Smith’s *Deseret News* reported the westbound express leaving St. Joseph “at 6:30 p.m.” on April 3 and arriving in Salt Lake City at 6:25 p.m. April 9. The article calculated a local time difference and described the speed as a striking change for a community accustomed to much older news. That is a newspaper’s report from a receiving city, with no reporter identified as a witness at the stable. The departure time was checked against the printed page scan. [4]

**The later accounts disagree about who carried the first dispatch.** The museum names Johnny Fry and puts the start at 7:15 p.m., after a late rail delivery and a cannon. The NPS stable page names Billie Richardson, who it says rode from this stable to Patee House to collect the pouch. The NPS trail FAQ presents Fry as the usual attribution and Richardson as a competing one. The NPS Patee House page gives a first-departure association to the hotel, where the mail work took place. Read the building roles and the differences separately. No single departure minute or rider is adopted here. [2, 4–8]

## 3. Then and now

The historic location is the Pike’s Peak Stable parcel at present 914 Penn Street. The HABS survey reports a 60-by-125-foot 1858 frame building, with brick walls put inside the timber walls in 1888 and later removal of frame walls. The NPS interpretation says the brick building replaced the wood structure and reused some posts and beams. The 1950 Goetz Foundation restoration sought the late nineteenth-century appearance. The museum retains the site and later stable fabric. The precise extent of original timbers accessible to visitors remains unresolved. [2–3]

The lawful physical anchor is the **Pony Express National Museum’s signed visitor entrance and open Penn Street sidewalk**, a drive-up/short walk. The Patee House public street frontage at 1202 Penn is a separate anchor in the route draft. It has no claim here to a cleared joint admission or entry schedule. [1–3, 8]

## 5. A short visit

Start on the open sidewalk at the stable facade. If the museum is open, read its mail and stable interpretation. At the public front, read the receiving city's newspaper report next to the two later rider attributions. Finish with **A horse saddled, a pouch still waiting**, the transition between ready animal and sorted mail. Use the museum’s published virtual tour and digitized newspaper when local weather cancels the exterior. [1–8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

Retain Fry at 7:15 p.m. in the museum timeline, Richardson in the NPS stable account, and the April 11 *Deseret News* 6:30 p.m. dispatch report. NPS FAQ explicitly keeps rider attribution unsettled. Descriptions of the 1888 work differ in emphasis. HABS describes brick walls built inside older ones and removal of frame walls. NPS describes replacement with reuse of some wood. Neither authorizes identification of every visible timber. [2–7]

The HABS main item returned 403 in the web tool, while the LOC JSON, HABS data PDF and color JPEG succeeded directly. The Patee House operator website returned web-tool 400 timeout and raw HTTP 503. The supported stop is the public exterior at Penn and 12th, with ordinary interior access held. An original April 3 St. Joseph newspaper and stable employment ledger were not retrieved. [3, 8–11]

## Connected ground — The Pony Express Eastern Divisions, 1860–1861

This stable was the eastern terminus of the Pony Express in 1860–61; westbound mail sorted at the Patee House office passed next through the Kansas stations at Marysville and Hollenberg and the Nebraska crossing at Rock Creek.
