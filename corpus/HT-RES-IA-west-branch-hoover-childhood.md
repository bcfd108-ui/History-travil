# West Branch — The house the Hoovers came back to

*Iowa OP01 research draft · Dash review pending.*

## 2. Story

### A house and a shop

In 1871 Jesse Hoover and his father Eli built a small board-and-batten home. The federal Historic Structures Report describes fieldstone brought from the prairie, wood transported through Muscatine, and auger holes visible in the rafters that the report relates to timber rafting. It describes a small bedroom, a room for cooking, eating and living, and an enclosed end of the back porch that could serve as a summer kitchen or sleeping room. Across the street Jesse repaired wagons and worked as a blacksmith. His original shop was moved in 1888 and subsequently lost. The park’s present shop dates to a 1957 reconstruction on the lot behind the original footprint. [4–6]

### Hulda’s working household

Herbert was born here in 1874. The family later moved to the House of the Maples, a larger home down Downey Street. Jesse died in 1880. Recalling his mother’s work after the death, Hoover wrote, “She took in sewing to add to the family resources.” He says he learned only years later that she had saved a $1,000 life-insurance payment for their education. Read that care as work spread across places and years. The cottage visit introduces the household. The sewing recollection belongs to the later years, after the family moved. Hulda died in 1884. Herbert lived with his uncle Allan before leaving for Oregon in 1885. [4,7–8]

### The cottage became somebody else’s kitchen

The 1890s owners enlarged the dwelling, repositioned it and used the earlier building as an annex. Visitors drawn to Hoover’s birthplace encountered Jennie Scellers’s house in this altered form. In the 1930s the Hoovers recovered the property and removed the addition. Lou Henry Hoover used family memories to guide the restoration and furnish the place with period objects. The old building survives, with layers of repair and an interpretation shaped by the family’s later decisions. [4,6,9]

## 4. Surviving features

- **Public interior during posted hours.** Restored cottage rooms and period furnishings. The NPS describes three steps at the front and an accessible approach at the rear. [1–4]
- **Public exterior.** Cottage elevations, view from open sidewalks and boardwalks. Grounds access is separate from building hours. [1–4]
- **View from public ground.** Original shop site and adjacent reconstructed shop, from the historic-core path. Stay out of banks, service areas and closed interiors. [2,5]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Park at the NPS visitor center and walk the designated historic core. [3]
2. Pause where the operator interprets the original shop and its reconstruction. Read the difference between Jesse’s business address and the building visitors see. [5]
3. **Story landing — The room that did three jobs.** At the cottage, take in the scale of the living, cooking and dining room. Read Hoover’s recollection of his mother’s later sewing. Use the open exterior with the NPS cottage audio transcript when the rooms are closed. [4,7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicting records

- NPS visitor interpretation and the early federal survey count two rooms. The Historic Structures Report and the memoir editorial footnote count the enclosed shed room as a third. The sources distinguish the main rooms from the porch space. [4,6–7,10]
- The memoir’s editorial footnote dates the sons’ purchase to 1934. The NPS cottage page says 1935. The Historic Structures Report traces an intermediary transaction and separate restoration. Exact deed sequence is held for instrument verification. The NPS FAQ places the sale in 1878, while the structural report puts improvements and the Hills’ move in spring 1879. [4,6–9]
- The winter 5 p.m. schedule label and the earlier close are reconciled by the Superintendent’s Compendium, which says 4:30 unless staffing permits later. Weather and posted holiday closures govern. [1–2]
- Lou Henry Hoover’s restoration manuscript is identified by the operator. Its full original and the Maud Stratton scrapbook pages were not retrieved for quotation. [4,11]
