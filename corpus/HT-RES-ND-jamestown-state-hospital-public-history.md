## 2. The story
**Hook:** The institutional record can explain who held authority. It cannot, by itself, explain how that authority felt to each person receiving care.

**Read historical institutional material:** the North Dakota Legislative Council memorandum **17.9129.01000**, *History of Behavioral Health Services in North Dakota*, identifies **Dr. O. Wellington Archibald** as the first superintendent and dates the hospital opening to **May 1, 1885**. It also summarizes **Dr. John M. Gillette's** 1915 proposals concerning admission and professional preparation. This is a later government historical synthesis, not Archibald's diary, Gillette's original text or patient testimony. Historical diagnostic language in the source is not adopted as present-day terminology. [2]

**Actually read contemporary primary institutional record:** HHS's posted agenda for **June 8, 2026** names **Aaron Olson, superintendent**, against the item **“Quarterly and Annual Report Templates”**. The same agenda includes institutional reporting and a staff/public-comment period. This establishes a published governance process and a named accountable official, not proof that any discussion occurred, that a report passed, or that outcomes were independently verified. No meeting recording or minutes were read. [3]

**Original interpretive voice:** Follow the difference between an institution describing itself and a person choosing to describe their own care. Administrative records are essential for tracing responsibility, but they must not be mistaken for consent or for a complete account of lived experience. The respectful public-history experience leaves that gap visible rather than extracting a story from confidential files.

## 3. Then / now
The State Archives' agency history distinguishes territorial authorization in **1883**, opening in **1885**, and state control after **1889**. These are different milestones, not competing founding dates. [4]

The current public agenda shows institutional governance continuing, but does not turn the institution into a heritage visitor campus. A past open meeting was limited to its stated occasion; it is not standing permission for a later visit. [3]

This draft is historical interpretation, not a clinical assessment, a review of current patient care, or a claim that administrative change necessarily improved every individual's experience.
