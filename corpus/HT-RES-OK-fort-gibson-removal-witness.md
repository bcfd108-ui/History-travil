# Fort Gibson — A destination one detachment could not reach

## 2. The story

**Before the route was imposed.** The Cherokee Nation’s history places forced removal after a long struggle against Georgia and the United States. Principal Chief John Ross’s September 28, 1836 protest told Congress, “We are denationalized; we are disfranchised.” The words are read in NPS’s published transcription, with the Smithsonian’s separate transcription as a cross-check. The Cherokee Nation gives the decision its own political context. The protest makes clear that a fort’s supply records tell only one side of the history. [3–5]

**The fort in a register of movement.** The National Archives describes Fort Gibson as the western military staging point and identifies records of letters received there. John Drew led the final removal detachment, for which Fort Gibson had been assigned as the place to disband. NPS records that low water stopped its steamboat short; people continued overland and disbanded at Tahlequah. The planned destination and actual end of travel are distinct. The agency account is a named-detachment source, while original travel logs and lists remain separate archival work. No alternative embarkation, settlement or burial location becomes a destination here. [6,7]

**What the fort can show.** The fort’s present public interpretation contains multiple phases of occupation and reconstruction. The reconstruction helps explain a garrison’s organization. It does not put a named Cherokee detachment at a precise modern doorway. HABS documents surviving military buildings, including the headquarters and barracks. The reflection is on the public interpretation threshold and begins with the Cherokee political voice. [1,2,8,9]

## 3. Then / now

Historic location: Fort Gibson’s military station and correspondence system; Drew’s assigned destination, with his actual disbandment elsewhere. Lawful anchor: OHS staffed site entrance and open visitor interpretation rooms at 907 N Garrison. Keep movements inside designated public interpretation only. No cemetery, landing ground, tribal gathering place or private grounds. [1,2,6–9]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicts

The original Drew itinerary, survivor list and complete western records remain uninspected. The Federal Archives inventory is a locator. The visitor-center display specific to Drew and individual interior offerings need onsite confirmation. November 26–27 closure is dated. [1,6,7]
