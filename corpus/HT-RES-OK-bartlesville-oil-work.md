# Bartlesville — Merchant, leaseholder and the name above the well

## 2. The story

**What the newspaper can place.** The December 3, 1904 Weekly Examiner reported that a buyer had purchased George B. Keeler’s “general merchandise business.” The short phrase comes from the searchable OCR of a digitized period issue. The story places Keeler in retail work and measures the news by how familiar his name had become to customers. That document does not make him the person who operated a drilling rig. [2]

**Who did what at the well.** Oklahoma Historical Society credits Keeler, William Johnstone and Frank Overlees with securing a Cherokee Nation lease and hiring Cudahy Oil Company to drill. The city describes the same men as the ones who drilled the well. The sources support a role distinction between arranging capital and contracting, and employees carrying out the drilling. A payroll, daily drilling log or contractor’s crew sheet is still needed to identify the hired workers. [3,4]

**Whose name carried forward.** The well was named for Nellie Johnstone, later Nellie Johnstone Cannon. OHS places land allotment with her and says she sold land to the city in 1917. The city dates allotment and production to 1903 and its park land purchase to that year. The distinction could involve parcels, dates or legal steps, none of which can be reconciled from the summaries alone. OHS says completion in 1897 preceded railway service in 1899; the city's later production date is a separate claim. [3,4]

The well site is represented by a reconstructed derrick rather than the operating 1897 fabric. Keeler’s store notice offers one early, independently dated glimpse of the commercial work that supported the town alongside oil. [2–5]
