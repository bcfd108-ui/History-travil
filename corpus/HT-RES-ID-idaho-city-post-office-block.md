# Idaho City · Pinney’s Post Office Block

History Travel · Idaho · Draft for Dash QC · Desk review 18 November 2026

## 2. The story

There were four weeks between losing the post office and opening its replacement. Fire destroyed the Main Street office on May 17, 1867. The Historic American Buildings Survey (HABS) quotes the Idaho World of June 5 reporting that the new building was “rapidly progressing.” The survey dates the opening to June 15. At Wall and Montgomery, look at how many businesses had reason to welcome the mail. When James A. Pinney moved in, Yale and Company offered books and musical instruments, Blake and Company ran a news depot, and the Idaho Lemhi Express had an office. Pinney supplied stationery and ran a circulating library. A letter, a newspaper and a borrowed book could all pass through this block. [2, 3]

The arrangement of the room mattered enough to make the paper again. On March 11, 1869, the Idaho World described Pinney moving a partition so that the boxes stood in one line with “more light from the front windows.” The west end became his living quarters. The builder’s name is uncertain. The survey found no contractor record and offers Pinney as a probable general contractor. It does identify the local low-fired brick. The office kept serving the town after Pinney moved to Boise. His assistant S. C. Silsby eventually bought the block. [2, 3]

That continuity took work. By about 1953 the brickwork at the top of the front was ragged and partly gone. The historic photograph makes the damage legible. Residents secured the property and the building opened as a museum in June 1958. HABS records a concrete floor over the wooden one, removal of rear domestic rooms and alterations to door openings. The Foundation says photographic evidence guided later roof and façade work. What visitors see is also the record of residents deciding that this particular building was worth saving. [2, 3, 4, 5]

## 3. Then / now

In the mining town, postal counter, shops and living quarters shared a block. Today the Foundation operates a seasonal museum here and interprets mining in the park behind it. The gold industry also supported businesses run by Chinese residents. The Foundation’s separately operated Pon Yam Museum interprets the merchant and his customers. Its occasional openings should be arranged independently. [1, 2, 3]

## 4. What’s still there

- **View from public ground** — the Montgomery Street front, the Wall Street side and the corner where they meet. The brick cornice and shapes of the openings can be compared to survey photographs. [2, 3, 4]
- **Public interior, seasonal** — the Boise Basin Museum in the former post office. Some former residential space was removed. [1, 3]
- **Public interior, special occasions only** — Pon Yam Museum and Resource Center, an optional separate visit. [1]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. On an open museum day begin inside with the town photographs, then come out to the street. In November begin at the public street corner with the circa 1953 image. [1, 2, 4]
2. Compare the stepped parapet and the lower openings to Duane Garrett’s 1976 front elevation. Use the permanent corner for orientation. [3, 4, 5]
3. **Strongest story moment** — read Pinney’s 1869 newspaper notice facing the front windows. After a fire, a new brick building warranted newspaper coverage. Two years later, moving the mailboxes into the light did too. Stand where the light came from. [3]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The Foundation credits Pinney with building the office in 29 days. The HABS citation dates bound the interval from fire to reopening, but the survey could not name the contractor. The June and March newspaper text used here was read in the HABS transcription. The original newspaper issue images were not independently read. [2, 3]

HABS and its cited 1871 deed place the property at the northwest corner. The 1974 district photo form describes the post office as on the east side of Montgomery. That conflicts with the corner description and HABS’s east-facing front. Use the identifiable façade and corner for the photograph. Do not generate a road waypoint from that conflicting caption. Register number below belongs to the district. [3, 6, 7]

## 7. Photo record
