# HT-RES-ME-south-portland-breakwater-lighthouse — South Portland: Bug Light, the cast-iron tower
Division ME seed-catalog • Research draft • Checked November 25, 2026

Portland Harbor’s first breakwater light was a small wooden tower whose size earned it the name Bug Light. The 1875 replacement is anything but plain: a cast-iron tower whose seams are hidden behind six Corinthian columns, modeled on an Athenian monument. It went dark in 1943 and was relit in 2002, standing now at the center of Bug Light Park.

Northeast end of Portland Breakwater, South Portland, Cumberland County, Maine

**Public access:** The lighthouse stands at the end of the historic breakwater in Bug Light Park, South Portland. The park is open to the public; visitors can walk the breakwater or shoreline paths to view the structure closely. The light operates as a private aid to navigation. Confirm park hours and any seasonal restrictions with the City of South Portland before visiting. Do not climb the tower.

## The story

A severe storm in November 1831 damaged Portland Harbor wharves and buildings. A protective breakwater was planned and construction began in the 1830s, eventually extending roughly 1,800–2,000 feet. An initial small octagonal wooden lighthouse was placed at its end and first lighted on 1 August 1855; its modest size earned the local nickname "Bug Light."

After the breakwater was extended, a new cast-iron lighthouse was erected in 1875 on a granite foundation. Built of curved cast-iron plates whose seams are disguised by six decorative Corinthian columns, it is inspired by the Choragic Monument of Lysicrates in Athens and is associated with the architectural circle of Thomas Ustick Walter (designer of the U.S. Capitol dome). A keeper's house was later added. The station was electrified in the 1930s, the keeper's house removed, and the light discontinued in 1943 amid wartime shipyard expansion that altered the breakwater. Restored in the late 20th century, it was reactivated in 2002 as a private aid and is the centerpiece of Bug Light Park, which also commemorates nearby World War II shipbuilding.

## Then / now

- 1830s–1850s: breakwater constructed.
- 1855: first wooden lighthouse lighted.
- 1875: present cast-iron tower with Corinthian columns erected.
- 1930s–1943: electrified, house removed, discontinued.
- 1989 / 2002: restoration and reactivation.
- Today: preserved structure in Bug Light Park; private aid to navigation.

## What's still there

The white cast-iron tower with black lantern, Corinthian columns, and classical detailing stands on its granite base at the breakwater end. The surrounding park includes walkways, memorials to the WWII shipyards, and views of Portland Harbor. The original wooden tower and keeper's house are gone.

## The stop, step by step

Reach Bug Light Park in South Portland. Walk the paths or breakwater toward the lighthouse. View the exterior, columns, and lantern from public areas. Interpretive markers note the breakwater history and shipbuilding. Optional views across the harbor to Portland Head Light. Return via park roads.

## Honest gaps & disagreements

Attribution of the 1875 design specifically to Thomas Ustick Walter is traditional but not uniformly documented in primary sources examined. Exact breakwater lengths and the precise date the keeper's house was removed vary slightly across accounts. Primary Lighthouse Board correspondence was not consulted for this entry.
