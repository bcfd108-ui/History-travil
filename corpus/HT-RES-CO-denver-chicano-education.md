## 2. The story
**Carlos Santistevan**, a community participant and founding member of the Crusade for Justice, recounts Denver's 1969 West High events in Matt Cortina's published 2015 interview. He describes students, parents and community members challenging how students' identity and history were treated in the classroom. His account makes parental involvement part of the story rather than presenting students as acting without a community. His wider statement is concise: **“As a Chicano we’re a slice of Americana.”** [5]

Santistevan discusses **Jeannie Perez**, but this is his recollection about her, not a directly retrieved interview with Perez. That distinction matters. The source includes eyewitness recollection, reported speech and the journalist's narrative; they are not identical evidence. Only the short excerpt and attributed educational concerns are used here, not tactical sequences or graphic confrontation. [5]

The central question is who gets to recognize their experience in what a school teaches. This dossier treats that as a historical question about dignity and educational representation, not a demand that visitors reproduce an argument or a protest.

The Chicano & Latino History Project separately catalogs a contemporary *West Side Recorder* item about curriculum demands and an *El Gallo* account titled *West High Blowout*. Their item descriptions concern Mexican history, culture and literature within education. **The catalog records were read; the linked newspaper pages returned 403 errors and were not read.** Thus they are corroborating research leads, not a substitute for original-text inspection. [6,7]

## 3. Then / now
- **March 1969, Denver:** the historical events under discussion. They are not the East Los Angeles walkouts of 1968, and no participant or chronology is transferred between cities. [5]
- **2015:** the interview records a community participant's retrospective account, decades after the events. [5]
- **Cataloged contemporary material:** the *Recorder* record prints March 1, 1969; the *El Gallo* record prints April 1, 1969. Those publication labels are not dates independently verified from scans. [6,7]
- **Today:** DPL publishes its own historical interpretation of the Crusade's relationship with student organizers and later community education. That is institutional interpretation, not an eyewitness account or proof of current physical access to former locations. [8]

## 4. What's still there
**A public reading institution:** Central Library has a documented specialist research service. It is the chosen venue for this dossier, not asserted to be the owner of every linked source. [1,4]

**Publicly available testimony:** a named community participant's published recollection can be read remotely. Formal interview releases, raw audio and unpublished papers were not examined. [5]

**Documentary leads:** the two history-project item records give newspaper references and source links. Their descriptions make the educational demands researchable, but do not resolve every claim or date. [6,7]

**Excluded:** active school grounds and buildings, former homes, historical organization properties, private archives, classrooms and sites of confrontation. No plaque, mural, classroom or original object is promised on display.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
1. **Start with the named account:** identify the speaker and why a recollection is different from a contemporary record. [5]
2. **Use the public library only if its gate works:** enter the authorized public reading area during posted hours; request materials through staff rather than entering restricted space. [4]
3. **Compare evidence types:** place the interview beside the two catalog descriptions, explicitly keeping their unread originals in the gaps column. [6,7]
4. **End with educational belonging:** consider what a curriculum communicates about whose lives count as history.

The strongest moment is not the confrontation. It is the ordinary expectation that a student's identity belongs in education. The full sequence works digitally without approaching a school, reconstructing a march or seeking personal accounts from present students.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- No direct Perez interview was retrieved. Do not turn someone else's recollection into her first-person testimony. [5]
- The *Recorder* catalog date precedes the commonly cited March 20 event. Without the original page, the date cannot be “corrected” or treated as proof of a post-event report. [6]
- The history-project import timestamps in 2022 are not original publication dates. [6,7]
- The 2015 interview's museum-exhibit notice is historical, not evidence that the exhibition remains open in 2026. [5]
- No count of participants, arrests, injuries, or causal claim about later appointments is adopted from a single retrospective account.
- The library's online narrative is useful context, but its claims are not presented as independently inspected school-board or administrative records. [8]
- Winter access, exact collection holdings, item availability, accommodation needs and media permissions remain separate checks. No outreach was performed.
