## 2. The story

Persistent overcrowding at Mississippi's original 1855 asylum in Jackson prompted the Mississippi Legislature on **March 8, 1882**, to establish a second state psychiatric institution—originally named the **East Mississippi Insane Asylum**—at **Meridian** in Lauderdale County, which formally opened to patients in **January 1885**. [3, 4]

Its original late-nineteenth-century main building—captured on an early-twentieth-century MDAH-held postcard postmarked **1908**—featured an imposing Second Empire / Victorian brick **central administrative tower capped with a mansard-and-cupola dome** flanked by symmetrical **three-story bayed ward wings** behind a circular carriage drive. [5]

Across the twentieth century, the institution expanded along the Highland Park / College Drive ridge north of downtown Meridian, evolving into **East Mississippi State Hospital** under the Mississippi Department of Mental Health—operating psychiatric wards, addiction recovery units, and skilled nursing care (`31`-county service area). [1, 2]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Founding vs. opening (`1882` vs. `1885`):** Legislative authorization passed **March 8, 1882**; patient admissions opened **January 1885** (*Mississippi Encyclopedia* / *Meridian Star*). [3, 4]
- **Survival of 1885 Victorian main building:** Whether any nineteenth-century masonry wings from the 1908 postcard survive inside the modern campus—or were wholly replaced—was not verified from MDAH Historic Resources Inventory sheets this run.
- **Address variants (`1818 College Dr.` vs. `4555 Highland Park Dr.`):** Current EMSH official homepage lists **`1818 College Dr., Meridian, MS 39307`**, while state directories and Highland Park entrances also reference **`4555 Highland Park Dr.`** across the same campus. [1, 7]
