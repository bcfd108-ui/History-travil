## 2. Story
When historian Sue Eakin asked Clementine Hunter about washing clothes, Hunter remembered, “if it was sunshine, you see we washed under a tree.” She went on to describe work she remembered from women in her family: pounding the washing, boiling it and using a rubboard. This passage comes from a 1974 interview transcript credited to Meredith Melançon and the Sue Eakin Papers at LSU Alexandria. The published transcript flags inaudible stretches and editorial reconstruction from Eakin’s unpublished writing. Hunter’s attributed words carry the story, with that qualification visible. [2]

The Northwest Louisiana History Museum says it displays twelve original works by Hunter, including ten about life at Melrose Plantation. Hunter lived and worked for much of her life at Melrose. National Museum of Women in the Arts describes her shift into painting later in life and gives material details about the surfaces and paints she used. A painting preserves what Hunter selected and remembered. Its choices of color, scale and placement can carry as much meaning as an inventory of the work she did. The interview provides a way to read those choices from the artist’s side without turning a painting into a measured diagram or a photograph. [1–3]

The museum is a later collecting and exhibition site. Hunter’s workplace and the place she remembered in the recorded conversation are separate from these galleries. Her words let a visitor think about everyday work and who gets to represent it. The opportunity here is to spend time with originals that the operator says are on view, wherever the museum has currently installed them. [1–3]
