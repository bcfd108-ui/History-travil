# Gulfport’s Union Depot and the passenger railroad that returned

*On Old Ground · Mississippi OP24 · Labeled research draft.*

## 2. Story
Two railroads gave Gulfport its defining junction. The Louisville & Nashville route ran east-west along the Gulf Coast. The Gulf & Ship Island Railroad carried traffic between Gulfport and the Mississippi interior. The original district nomination traces a struggling effort that gained capital under Joseph T. Jones and his Bradford Construction Company in 1895. Its account puts the completed line to Jackson in 1900 and the developed port’s official opening in 1902. The union station that opened in 1904 served passengers traveling on both lines. [2, 3]

A junction also made architecture. Amtrak’s station account describes two wings and a curved junction where passengers waited. Deep eaves gave shelter along the old platform sides. The district record distinguishes the depot from a surviving train shed moved from its original place. This is a building that had to answer for two directions of travel. It shaped the business blocks that grew around it, beyond the physical room where travelers waited. [1–3, 8]

Katrina damaged the historic commercial district. The later district boundary and inventory reflect the surviving buildings. For passenger rail, a different public record makes the next date precise: Amtrak’s new schedule gave August 18, 2025 as the effective date of Gulf Coast passenger service. In its first-anniversary report, Amtrak executive Jennifer Mitchell credited “Amtrak train, engine, on-board and maintenance teams.” This contemporary operating voice identifies the people needed to provide service. The date does not reopen the historical-society museum. [2–5]

## 3. Then and now
The former waiting-room building and the railroad operator’s active station are associated with the same address. The rail operator lists passenger service with a platform and no station shelter. The state inventory and historical society identify a museum in the depot. The exterior can be read from the ordinary signed public approach. Keep the route there. The historical society interior’s current walk-up access is unresolved. [1–4, 6, 7]
