# Hot Springs — The people behind the baths
HT-RES-AR-hot-springs-bathhouse-workers · Arkansas OP01 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

Elbert Payton told Ranger Toni Cooper in March 1993, “I was employed at the Fordyce from ’38 to ’43.” The operator’s transcript has his working life continue at the Arlington from 1945 to 1965. He remembered Jim Ferguson offering him work after military service and described how he became an attendant. The interview reveals how a bathhouse visit depended on workers who knew the rooms and moved throughout their shifts with the guests. It also records training, regular physicals and repeat visitors who asked for the attendant they knew. [3, 4]

In the interview’s wage discussion, Payton estimates that he started at about twenty-five cents per bath, and he remembers a higher rate and tips later. The exchange comes after his account of two jobs and refers explicitly to the Arlington in the tip discussion. Keep those amounts as his remembered rates, with employer, year and payroll confirmation still open. His description of return customers opens a piece of labor history visitors can walk through: people returned to the resort and asked for someone by name. [4]

The HABS photo index dates the Fordyce’s construction to 1914 and 1915, identifies Mann and Stern as architects, and describes the stained glass and crafted rooms. Set the building survey alongside Payton’s work history. The survey can show an intended room or visible doorway, while his interview supplies a human shift inside the building. When they agree only in broad terms, keep the historical work details tied to Payton and the physical details tied to the survey. [4, 5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The Fordyce is the actual historic bathhouse where Payton remembered working in 1938–43. Today NPS interprets it as a museum and visitor center. His later Arlington employment has a separate site history and is not part of this visitor progression. [1–5]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Fordyce Bathhouse Visitor Center, on Central Avenue between the Quapaw and Maurice Bathhouses. Use signed legal parking and the short public Bathhouse Row sidewalk walk. Enter the ordinary visitor doorway when open and follow posted room barriers. Separately operated bathhouses, private staff doors and spring infrastructure are outside the visit. [1, 2, 5]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

Payton corrected the interviewer from Albert to Elbert in the transcript. His dates for each employer are oral recollections and the wage figures are qualified by him. This dossier used the NPS transcript and did not authenticate the tape against it. Personnel sheets, the exact Fordyce room for a particular task, and day-of museum access remain open. [3–5]
