# Missouri Mines — The mill ran on more than ore

Missouri OP01 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**From four shafts to one mill.** The 1915 U.S. Geological Survey chapter reported that the Federal Lead Company “operated 4 shafts during 1915,” with 1,355,000 tons of crude ore mined and milled. The report lists the mill’s jigs, tables and vanners. Those machines separated material mechanically so the company could ship concentrate. The published figure is company operations at this complex for one year, distinct from the equipment rating of 4,600 tons per day. The short quotation is from the readable federal report text, printed p. 115. [4]

**One surviving company voice.** The State Historical Society identifies mining engineer Harry Adelbert Guess as general manager at Flat River and keeper of 17 outgoing letterbooks for 1907–1913. The catalog names shipping, production, labor relations and pollution as subjects. The indexed items are management correspondence. The finding aid gives access to the outline and the dates. The letters themselves were unavailable in the online record consulted. A second collection identifies monthly statistical reports prepared by company officers in 1918–1921. Neither source can stand in for a worker’s own words. [5–7]

**A district worker has a name.** The *Lead Belt News* of Oct. 15, 1948 reported the death of Earl Limbaugh, a 27-year-old employee who operated a rotary dump at St. Joseph Lead Company’s No. 12 mine at Elvins. Its local historical transcription says he had been “in continuous service with the lead mining company since May, 1941.” The newspaper named Delbert Dalton as the locomotive engineer working nearby. The two jobs put underground rail transport and unloading in the same account. This incident was in nearby Elvins, a separate mine. It is regional evidence and gives no basis to put either man in the Federal mill or to visit any accident ground. The wording is from a transcription, with the original issue and employment records held. [8]

## 3. Then and now

The historic location is the **Federal mine and mill complex at former Flat River**, now at Missouri Mines State Historic Site in Park Hills. An industrial-history report to Missouri officials dates the first shaft on the Federal property to the Central Mining Company in 1891 and the Federal Lead Company’s operation to 1902. St. Joe purchased Federal in 1923. Milling ended in 1972. The former powerhouse now interprets the work, and the operator shows a film about mining and milling from earlier footage in the historic miners’ shower room. [2–4, 9]

The lawful anchor is the **Missouri Mines State Historic Site Powerhouse Museum**, 4000 State Highway 32, Park Hills. Use the signed visitor drive, parking, short approach and public galleries. Exterior interpretation is limited to designated visitor areas or a staff-guided exterior visit in suitable weather. Industrial buildings, mine openings, tailings and Elvins accident ground remain outside this visit. [1–3, 9–10]

## 5. A short visit

Use the museum entrance from signed visitor parking. Begin with the underground equipment display. Follow it with the public milling interpretation and the film if the operator is showing it. Finish at **Between the rail car and the mill** in the museum. Read the federal mill account, then the named Elvins jobs as separate district evidence. The strongest moment is recognizing the handoff between underground carriage and surface processing. If the museum is closed, use the reports remotely. [1–9]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The operator dates donation of the complex and adjoining land to late 1975, while the technical report dates an approximately 8,000-acre donation for St. Joe State Park to 1976. The records may describe separate tracts or stages. The relevant deed records were not read. The operator says the mine-mill area was separately designated a state historic site in 1980. Do not treat designation, cessation and donation as one date. [2, 9]

The USGS PDF opened in the web retrieval with readable report text. A separate raw file request did not complete within 12 seconds, so the passage was not image-checked. A 49,647,523-byte historical economic-history scan returned web-tool 400, content length too large. The letters in R0520 and statistical reports in R0540 were reachable as catalog records only in this pass. The name of a Federal rank-and-file mill employee, an original issue for Limbaugh, and the claimed dates of specific equipment on museum display remain open. The operator’s 2025–26 holiday heading does not establish Thanksgiving 2026 hours. [1, 4–8, 11]
