## 2. The story

Challis grew in the early 1880s as the Custer County seat and supply center for the Yankee Fork mines, leaving behind a cluster of masonry commercial and civic buildings along Main Avenue. Local masons generally favored volcanic **tuff** — softer and easier to dress with hand tools — for Challis's stone structures. [1]

The **Custer County Jail**, standing on Lots 3 and 5 of Block 30 directly behind the county courthouse on Main Avenue, broke that local habit. Builders laid all four exterior walls out of **rhyolitic stone** — rock-faced, coursed rhyolite on the three elevations open to view, and random-rubble rhyolite brought to irregular courses on the rear wall. A concrete cap tops stepped side parapets designed to conceal a shallow, tarred lean-to roof. Even the rear elevation received careful coursed work rather than cheap unexposed rubble, and heavy stone lintels frame the original barred window openings along the sides. [1]

Regional press reports in the 2020s note that the small stone lockup historically held roughly six inmates before later county facilities replaced its custodial role, leaving the century-old building standing in place behind the courthouse as county storage. Between 2023 and 2026, Custer County officials explored and engineered a modern replacement jail and sheriff's complex elsewhere, keeping the old stone structure behind `801 E. Main Ave.` intact and unused for detention. [3, 4]

### Haunted history / lore

No documented ghost lore or commercial haunt narrative was found for the Custer County Jail in Challis during this run, and none is invented. No custodial deaths or execution records were identified in the sources retrieved.

## 4. What's still there

- **All-rhyolite masonry shell:** Coursed, rock-faced rhyolitic stone on the front and side walls, with random-rubble rhyolite at the rear — identified in the 1980 National Register survey as the only all-rhyolite stone building in Challis. [1, 6]
- **Stepped parapet and concrete cap:** Side walls step downward toward the rear under a concrete coping layer that masks the shallow pitched roof. [1, 6]
- **Original barred side windows:** Side bays retain steel/iron window bars recessed beneath heavy stone lintels. [1, 6]
- **Setting behind the courthouse:** Still occupies Block 30 behind the Custer County Courthouse on East Main Avenue. [1, 3, 6]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Construction date conflict (`1911` vs. `ca. 1922`):** The 1980 National Register inventory form (`80001309`) enters the construction date as **`ca. 1922`** and explicitly characterizes the stonework as "this 1922 example." By contrast, a 2023 *Challis Messenger* / *Post Register* photo caption for the July 1980 survey image states the stone jail was **"built in 1911."** Neither county commissioners' minutes nor the original construction contract from 1911–1922 were retrieved this run, so the conflict is left unresolved. [1, 3]
- **Custodial & architectural records missing:** No inmate register, named prisoner accounts, builder/mason name, or Sanborn Fire Insurance map sheet for Challis was retrieved (LOC search/API returned a Cloudflare challenge from the research box).
- **Interior condition and roof state:** Current interior cell preservation (whether steel cells remain inside during storage use) is unverified without county custodian permission.
- **No overlap with live catalog:** Verified against `onoldground.com` (`/sites/ID-challis` covers the town and Yankee Fork heritage center; `/sites/custer-yankee-fork` covers the separate ghost-town Custer cabin jail burned/gone in Custer, upstream on Yankee Fork). [5]
