# Windsor Ruins, the people who raised the columns and the men who left

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March · OP05 · November 20, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

The state maintains a public walking path around the ruin outside a barrier. Posted hours run from dawn to dusk. MDAH warns that flooding can make roads impassable and that there are no visitor amenities. Use the marked perimeter when open. [1]

## 2. Story

Windsor was finished in 1861 for Smith Coffee Daniell II. David Shroder designed the mansion. White tradesmen and enslaved laborers built it on a cotton plantation of about 2,500 acres. MDAH counts more than 300 people enslaved there. That figure describes the plantation workforce, not a crew of 300 building the house. The records cited here credit the owner and designer by name while identifying few of the people who formed the bricks, raised the columns or did the finish work. [1,2]

## 3. Then and now

Union soldier Henry Otis Dwight drew a residence near Bruinsburg in his diary on May 1, 1863. Architectural historians identify the house as Windsor. His drawing, which re-emerged in 1991, preserves the roofline and cupola missing from the ruins. Other men connected to the property left a different record that year and afterward. At least seventeen men formerly enslaved at Windsor joined the United States Colored Troops. [2,3]

## 4. Surviving features and access

Fire destroyed the house in 1890. Brick columns with stucco surfaces and cast-iron details survived the timber construction. The state’s current count is 23 full columns and five partial ones. One surviving iron stair was moved to Oakland Memorial Chapel at Alcorn. Windsor’s public path and interpretation allow people to see the material the columns were built from and how much has been lost. [1–3]

## 5. Public progression

On the outside path, look at one column from base to capital. The shafts that read as stone at a distance are masonry under applied stucco. Recent conservators reattached loose material, repaired missing brickwork and repointed joints. The visible ruin owes its survival to successive labor crews. [2,3]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

MDAH’s visitor page says 29 columns were left after the fire; its project account says 27 of the original 29. Both agree on today's count. The visitor page gives 2023 for stabilization and the detailed project account gives 2024. The records do not establish whether these dates describe different contracts. The cause of the fire is left open. [1,2]
