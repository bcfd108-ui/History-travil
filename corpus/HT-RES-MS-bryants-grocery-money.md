# Bryant’s Grocery, a private ruin and a disputed record

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP21*

## 2. Story

Emmett Till and other young people went to a store in Money in August 1955. The grocery occupied part of a two-story building with a living area above it. The National Park Service’s special resource study documents this organization and the damaged fabric that later survived. The physical form can establish where a business and household met. It cannot resolve what happened in the few minutes people have described so differently. [1]

The later federal case review presents a store employee’s allegations and the sharply different accounts by people who accompanied Emmett. In the Justice Department’s words, “Bryant suffered no physical harm.” That limited statement is from the 2021 memorandum, not from a witness transcript. Carolyn Bryant alleged physical aggression. The federal review records that the companions disputed her version, and the presiding judge excluded her court testimony from the jury’s consideration. Wheeler Parker and Simeon Wright each had a different field of view, and a later account is not an exact reconstruction of everything another person saw. What followed was the abduction and killing of a child, for which the store interaction supplied a pretext. [3–5]

The marked place has its own later history. The Mississippi Development Authority’s 2017 release records a repaired interpretive marker rededicated outside the former store after damage. NPS examined the property and former service station as potential preservation resources. Its appraisal of possible exterior interpretation is conditional on plans, physical work and permissions. The signed commemorative record that can be read remotely and the private parcel that still needs protection are different things. [1][2]

## 3. Then and now

The grocery once had a street entrance, a retail interior and rooms above. The study describes only part of its brick shell surviving, with missing structural parts. The adjacent service station is a separate property resource. It does not become a visitor facility because a federal study considered how it could serve that purpose. No current ownership change or public facility opening was verified. [1]

## 4. Surviving features and access

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- Bryant’s allegations and the companions’ accounts differ materially. The Department of Justice reports the limits of attempts to verify subsequent reported accounts. The dossier does not reproduce a purported definitive sequence. [3][4]
- The press release establishes the 2017 repair and rededication. The study’s management alternatives are prospective. A verified present ordinary public viewpoint and safe stopping boundary are absent. [1][2]
- Parker’s LOC index and federal summaries were readable. Direct transcript views returned HTTP 403, so no wording from Parker’s interview is treated as independently read. [4][5]
- The subject photo in the study lists an originating archive. Item-level U.S. public-domain rights and original file verification are still open. [1]
