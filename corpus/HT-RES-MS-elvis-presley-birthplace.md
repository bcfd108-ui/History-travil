# Elvis Presley Birthplace and the two-room house East Tupelo kept

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP14 · Labeled research draft*

## 2. Story

On the east side of Tupelo, Vernon Presley borrowed money for lumber and put up a two-room house with family help. The museum names his father Jessie and his brother Vester and gives $180 for the construction loan. Elvis was born there to Vernon and Gladys on January 8, 1935. His twin Jessie Garon was stillborn. The operator describes the family losing the house after roughly three years when the loan could not be repaid. That was the end of their time at the birth house, although their life in and around Tupelo continued. The names and loan amount are curatorial accounts pending original payroll and loan records. [1, 2, 3]

By the time Presley returned in 1956, the fairgrounds brought him back in a different role. A period trade-journal report in the October 6, 1956 *Billboard* describes the successful fair and notes that “he was born in East Tupelo.” Those words come from the original issue scan's extracted text. The fair was something the town staged, and a number of people made the performance possible. The article documents the public homecoming and a business measure of its crowds. It does not document any later deed or endorsement of a check. [4]

Accounts of Presley's gift divide over when the park money came and how much land the city bought. The operator newsletter places the home purchase funding with the 1956 appearance and later facilities with the 1957 performance. Other operator pages give the park gift as proceeds from the 1957 show. What survives for the visitor is the small original-location house and the result of later civic decisions to preserve it. A 2024 legislative resolution describes the distinction between a gift toward acquiring land and a later gift toward a youth recreation center. The original municipal records would connect the checks, purchase and improvements. [1, 3, 5, 6]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The state landmark and operator identify the modest shotgun birth house in East Tupelo, now within the museum grounds. The operator says the house is at its original location and has period furnishings. The Assembly of God building that the family attended in Tupelo comes from Berry Street, was used as a residence after 1959, and was moved to the park in 2008. The newer museum, landscape and memorial chapel belong to the later interpretation. [1–3, 7]

**Present public anchor.** Elvis Presley Birthplace Welcome Center, 306 Elvis Presley Drive, Tupelo. Drive to the signed visitor parking and follow the operator's marked path. Consult staff for ticketing and any accessible entrance. All viewing stays within signed park and staffed building access. [1]

## 5. Public progression

Start at the staffed Welcome Center and read the site map as a chronology. On the marked walk take in the house exterior, then look inside when open. See the separately marked church as a relocated piece of material history. Return to **Two rooms and a porch**, the strongest physical account the site gives of a household's means and the distance between the scale of a home and the scale of a later public career. On a closed interior day the exterior tells that part of the story from the public path. [1–4, 7, 8]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The state summary implies residence at this house until 1948. The operator distinguishes about three years in the house from later East Tupelo residences and a move to Memphis in 1948. Census, parcel and foreclosure documentation for each address was not retrieved. [1–3]
- The museum's 2023 newsletter gives 1956 funding for 13 acres and house and 1957 funding for the recreation center. The 2024 resolution gives 1956 support for 15 acres and house and describes about $14,000 in 1957 for a youth recreation center. The general operator pages instead tie the park gift to the 1957 concert. The original deed, check images and council vouchers were unavailable. [1, 3, 5, 6]
- The LOC notes for later Highsmith items 2017879175 and 2017879173 say the cabin was relocated. The operator states this house stands in its original location and separately documents moving the church. The exact house lot and any physical repositioning require parcel and conservation documentation. The adopted frame establishes visual appearance only. [1, 7–10]
- The Library of Congress catalog identified the local daily newspaper. Its direct page returned HTTP 403, and relevant original *Tupelo Daily Journal* pages and Presley family interviews were not retrieved. The *Billboard* coverage establishes the 1956 encounter at the fair without supplying the original household voice. [4, 12]
