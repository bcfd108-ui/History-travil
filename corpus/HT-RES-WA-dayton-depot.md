# Dayton Historic Depot · The stationmaster’s temporary office

Washington · OP01 · Research draft for Dash · November 18, 2026, Pacific

## 2. The story
A depot is a place where movement depends on paperwork, weighing, communication and labor. Dayton's preserved building makes that system legible at human scale. The operator traces its origin to an 1881 rail connection serving an agricultural town, initially at a different site beside the Touchet River. [1]

The strongest named work evidence recovered is in the state-hosted federal nomination's transcriptions and citations to the **Columbia Chronicle**. It identifies **C. A. Hayden** as the first stationmaster and describes his upstairs living quarters. In 1899, **Agent Hindle** reportedly continued business from a furniture car while the depot was relocated. The latter account cites the newspaper's **May 20, 1899** issue. The issue itself has not been collated; the claim is primary newspaper evidence mediated through nomination research, not a directly read newspaper facsimile. [4]

The short source phrase **“occupied by agent Hayden”** ties the working building to a household. Do not invent Hayden's family composition or quote an unlocated employee diary. The same nomination records Chinese and white workers preparing the yard, but does not supply individual Chinese workers' names in the checked passage. Their labor belongs in the story; identities must not be fabricated. [4]

The museum's digital archive lists **Gladys's Christmas travel plans, 1938**, and **Blanche's travel and plane-ride correspondence, 1938**. These are promising named traveler records, but their contents were not accessible in the retrieved page. No journey, fare or depot connection is inferred from the titles alone. [5]

Only part of the station came downtown. According to the nomination, a two-horse capstan hauled the two-story portion on rollers in May 1899. The narrative cites the July 3 *Columbia Chronicle* for arrival by July 1. A smaller baggage room received a Fairbanks platform scale; the old freight room went separately a block west. The nomination traces the consequence upstairs, where what it describes as a three-bedroom home became two bedrooms. Moving the depot changed a household as well as the price and convenience of sending freight. [4, pp. 3, 11]

## 3. Then / now
**Then:** Passenger waiting, freight handling and staff accommodation met in one building. Competition between railway companies reshaped where travelers and businesses met the trains. [1]

**Now:** A museum with interpreted rooms and changing exhibits. A 2023 regional museum booklet describes a freight scale on the main floor and a gallery upstairs in former stationmaster quarters. That dated description does not establish current scale operation, exhibit placement or accessible circulation. [6]

The interpretive focus should be work continuing during disruption: the business could move temporarily into a railcar while the station itself changed location. The building's survival does not make today's tracks or yard a visitor attraction.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates
- **Move date:** Detailed nomination narrative supports **1899**, while the NPS 2017 evaluation summary says **1889**. Retain the inconsistency; do not silently copy the summary. [4,7]
- **Register history:** The 2017 approval is additional documentation augmenting a 1974 listing, not the building's first recognition. [7]
- **Hours:** Chamber material puts October in summer hours; current operator puts it in winter. Use operator evidence. Christmas Eve wording still conflicts internally. [1–2,8]
- **Primary source gap:** Chronicle citations are located, but original issue scans and employee payroll/rosters remain unread. Transport correspondence titles do not establish contents. [4–5]
- No employee oral recording, contemporary visitor photograph, exact historical camera station or current rail access authorization obtained.
- The surrounding Indigenous history requires appropriate Tribal-published evidence before a local cultural narrative is added. The railway account does not establish empty land or erase earlier use.
