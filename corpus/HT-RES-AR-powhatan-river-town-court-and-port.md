# Powhatan Historic State Park, Lawrence County Courthouse
HT-RES-AR-powhatan-river-town-court-and-port · Arkansas OP03 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

Stand high enough to see why people brought records up a hill. At river level people reached a place for crossing, unloading and starting overland. The state preservation submission reproduces W. E. McLeod’s description of the ferry as “the most accessible point at all seasons of the year from Jacksonport to Pocahontas.” It is a specific claim about an older transport system and a quoted description reprinted in the nomination. It is no assurance of modern riverbank access. The same source records the 1829 arrival of the Laurel at Ficklin Ferry. [2]

Powhatan’s county-seat records show another sort of connection. A 19th-century printed biography summarized and quoted by the nomination depicts John A. Lindsay as the town’s founder. Elsewhere in the same Goodspeed volume, John S. Ficklin is associated with founding it. The nomination distinguishes an early ferry settlement and the later formal layout, and it cites a particular transaction it attributes to Lindsay. In 1889 he deeded Powhatan Block 38 to Public School District No. 9. That small piece of land paperwork gives an identifiable person a more secure role in the town’s life than a disputed honorary title. The deed itself was not retrieved. [2]

The courthouse of 1873 burned in 1885. The University of Arkansas’s project describes a fire-resistant record vault that preserved the records and a replacement courthouse completed in 1888. Losing a building and retaining deeds, cases and records made it possible to keep referring to an earlier town whose river trade later receded in importance. The railroad bypass, shared county-seat arrangements and eventual transfer of county functions were developments over time. They are not a single day on which a river town vanished. [1–4]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The historic ferry is a lower-river feature, the Lindsay school deed is a separate town parcel, and the 1888 courthouse survives uphill. The park manages the modern visitor approach. None of the historical references fixes the lawful location of a present landing or wandering route. [1–4]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Powhatan Historic State Park courthouse museum, 4414 Hwy 25. Drive to the signed park visitor area and take the short operator-designated route to the courthouse. Follow posted path and building controls. Keep the shore, former ferry footprint, private parcels and archive reading rooms outside the visit. [1]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The nomination reports Goodspeed crediting both Ficklin and Lindsay in different passages. It assigns early effective settlement to Ficklin and identifies 1849 as the plat date, with earlier land title still uncertain. The original Goodspeed pages and the 1889 deed were not retrieved. The university’s browser application loaded its Browse Map, Media and People shell, with no individual deed page confirmed. [2, 3]
- The architecture essay says the courthouse closed in 1966; the park’s archive profile describes its closure in 1968. The final operation/records-transfer dates remain unresolved. This distinction has no bearing on the present museum hours. [1, 5]
- The nomination says ferry approach traces were apparent when recorded. Neither it nor the public park description establishes current safe use of the bank or a former launch. [1, 2]
