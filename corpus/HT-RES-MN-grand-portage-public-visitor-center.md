# Grand Portage — The work at the carrying place

*History Travel research draft · Minnesota OP06 · Independent Dash review pending.*

## 2 · Story — The work at the carrying place

Gichi Onigaming, the Great Carrying Place in NPS interpretation, was a portage between Lake Superior and the Pigeon River. Its geography made a major trade route possible but goods did not move by geography alone. The Grand Portage Band’s public heritage account says Ojibwe people “taught the traders how to make and repair canoes” and supplied food, naming game, wild rice and maple sugar. Put canoe knowledge, food and carrying work beside the traders’ paperwork. The trade required each of them. [4–5]

North West Company papers cataloged at McGill include a copy of the preamble and signatures from the 1790 articles of agreement signed at the Grand Portage depot. The copy is evidence of a company’s formal arrangements. It does not give a census of the people manufacturing, repairing or handling supplies. An NPS study identifies correspondence between Alexander Mackenzie and Roderic McKenzie, including a July 16, 1790 letter from Grand Portage. That correspondence is a source lead in this draft, with no direct quotation from an unverified scan. [6–7]

The Band’s heritage history describes the 1854 treaty and subsequent pressures on reservation land. NPS records that the 1958 monument authorization included a donation of about half of the resulting monument land by the Band and the Minnesota Chippewa Tribe. The two accounts connect the modern interpretive center to a history after the fur trade. The dated winter notice identifies the Band and NPS as co-managers. The Heritage Center’s public displays include Grand Portage Stories, which the operator describes as drawing on oral histories. [2,4–5,8]

## 3 · Then / now

The trade complex was a meeting point for supplies and cargo on a much older travel corridor. Today's visitor anchor is the modern public Heritage Center at the lake end of the monument. The center’s indoor interpretation is separate from seasonal reconstructed depot buildings, the portage interior, community spaces and living cultural activity. [2,4–5,8–9]

## 5 · Trip progression

Start indoors with the public overview of the carrying place. Locate public exhibits about canoe materials and trade, subject to current display availability. Read the Band’s published account of making and repairing canoes. **Story landing — The skills that got cargo across.** End inside the visitor-facing gallery by reading the Band’s account alongside the company archive description. If the facility or signed approach is unavailable, read remotely. [1–8]

## 6 · Gaps and conflicts

- The October dated operator release closes the depot October 13. The current operator page specifies reopening Memorial Day weekend 2027. The general Heritage Center description refers more broadly to May–October. The dated and current operating notices govern. [1–3,8]
- The Band heritage narrative refers to a complex known as Fort Charlotte in its condensed account. NPS separates the lake depot from the inland end of the portage. Do not conflate these references or derive directions from them. The detailed original 1790 agreement and correspondence were located by description and not read in scan. [4–7,9]
- HABS documents the Buildings Complex; this draft does not treat survey frames as evidence of an eighteenth-century building fabric or matching modern visitor stance. [10–11]
