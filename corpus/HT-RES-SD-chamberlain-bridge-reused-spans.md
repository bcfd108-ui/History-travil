# Chamberlain Bridge and the reused Missouri River spans

DRAFTED · South Dakota OP05 · Pending independent Dash QC

## 2. The story

### A bridge with pieces from two crossings

The U.S. 16 crossing that opened in 1925 was part of South Dakota’s five-bridge program across the Missouri. The National Register nomination connects the program to the State Highway Commission’s attempt to create through road corridors. Chamberlain’s bridge helped give an east–west road a dependable link. The listing records the crossing on the later I-90 business loop. [4,5]

Look for two different histories in one steel silhouette. The river impoundment would raise the water enough to require the replacement of several old crossings. Contractors built new supports at Chamberlain and moved the old trusses into a different configuration. Six former Wheeler spans were dismantled and floated up the Missouri. The nomination gives roughly 70 river miles for that move and says all six were in place by September 1953. Four spans from the older Chamberlain bridge were set in place after the Wheeler spans. [5]

### Opening day and dedication day

The nomination dates the resumption of traffic to the morning of October 30, 1953. Officials formally dedicated the bridge on December 7. A later transportation history emphasizes the December ceremony. It does not provide the same distinction between the ceremony’s ribbon and the first day of traffic. The October date belongs to the actual traffic opening in the nomination. [5,6]

South Dakota DOT reported that “Chamberlain’s truss bridge re-opened in 2011” after being closed to traffic for most of the previous two years. Its annual report is the agency’s own published record of the rehabilitation and reopening. The 1953 reuse and 2011 return to traffic happened under different projects. The same report’s short description calls the bridge a 1953 creation, while the detailed nomination and architectural inventory explain the pieces that came from the 1920s. [5,7,8]

### The view you can actually use

Today’s business-loop bridge and the newer Interstate crossing are separate structures. Read the bridge story at established public ground in Chamberlain. The nomination describes former piers and the inundated low ground near the west approach. The supplied vantage is for reading the river landscape, with bridge visibility conditional. It never depends on standing in traffic or locating remnants on shore or in the water. [1,4,5]

## 3. Then / now

A 1925 crossing linked two banks of the Missouri. The 1953 structure used truss material from Chamberlain and Wheeler across the newly impounded reach. Interstate 90 gained a separate bridge downstream in 1974. The business-loop crossing remains the listed site. [4,5]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- Some sources date the structure to 1925, 1953 or both. The 1925 crossing, 1953 recombination and 2011 rehabilitation have distinct scopes. Formal dedication on December 7 followed the October 30 traffic opening. [4–8]
- The nomination reports five remaining older piers at its survey date. Present visibility, a reliable park sightline, winter path maintenance, and any usable pedestrian bridge access remain unverified. [1,5]
- State archive record H2001-030 was located in the county guide. The historical frame, original 1925 and 1953 opening coverage and any named ironworker account were not retrieved. [5,9]

## 7. Photo record

State Archives Brule County guide lists a 1925 bridge photograph with H2001-030 and locator 8535A. Frame not retrieved. SAH Archipedia offers a modern comparison image, and the secondary dedication article uses a photograph taken in 2007. Neither fixes the place where the 1925 photographer stood, and the bridge has moved. No Photo Point. [6,8,9]
