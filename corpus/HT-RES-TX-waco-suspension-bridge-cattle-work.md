# Waco Suspension Bridge — a river crossing becomes a business
Research draft · Texas OP02 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

### A charter wrote a crossing into existence

On a crowded river the right to cross is worth money. A printed legislative act directed the Waco Bridge Company to build a “good, safe and substantial bridge across the Brazos river.” The line is from the Portal to Texas History transcription of H. P. N. Gammel’s collected laws, page 1456, reproducing the 1866 act. The legal instrument gives the corporation a way to recover its investment through a toll crossing. Earlier visitors depended on a ferry [2–4].

### The span and the books

The engineering job involved founding masonry at the river, making towers and importing wire and steelwork from the Roebling firm. Thomas M. Griffith was the civil engineer engaged for the project. The published Register nomination records the bridge as a historic structure in its own right and the City’s account connects the crossing to cotton and cattle traffic. The handbook’s report of toll receipts before the formal opening is especially revealing: the commercial crossing and its public ceremony did not begin at the same instant [2, 4–5].

### The price of a free crossing

Waco’s history page records a $75,000 county purchase in 1889 and transfer to the city for $1 with a maintenance and toll-removal condition. Public ownership still carried an obligation to maintain the bridge. A traveler can see the scale of that obligation from the bank, where the tower and hanging span fill the view rather than appearing as a line on a map [1–2].

## 3 · Then / now

**Historic place.** The Brazos crossing at Bridge Street and the historic toll business at the ends [2, 5].

**Lawful physical anchor.** Indian Spring Park, 101 N. University Parks Drive, on the signed city park path near the river. Drive-up and short paved walk with viewing from the park. Skip any closed path or bridge. The tower machinery and riverbanks below signed paths are outside the visit [1–2].

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The American Society of Civil Engineers article dates completion to 1869 [6]. Waco’s page dates the celebration January 6, 1870, with formal traffic the next day [2]. The Handbook of Texas puts the official opening January 7, describes the parade, and reports evidence of earlier traffic. It gives $135,000 and $141,000 as commonly quoted construction totals [4]. Keep those figures separate from the 1889 purchase and read 1869 completion separately from formal 1870 opening. The direct Baylor company record and LOC survey item could not be authenticated in the retrieval; rely on the statute and register as independently retrievable sources [3, 5, 7–8].
