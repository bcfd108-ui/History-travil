# Boise City — Two kinds of Dust Bowl house

## 2. The story

**The home Donna remembered.** In a recorded 2000 interview with Shelly Lemons, Donna Craighead recalled, “In Boise City I lived in a sod house.” Her father had worked in a bank before the Depression pushed him into day work and then state highway equipment work. Road jobs moved the household through several towns. In her account, making a home meant renting where work had taken them and dealing with dust under whatever roof they found. These are Craighead’s words from the university transcript, not a reading of the audio. [3]

**The house at the museum.** The operator traces the exhibit building to Liberty School House between Felt and Wheeless. Clarence Roberts acquired the former school for his brother-in-law’s household, which divided it into three rooms. Troy Baker offered it to the center in 2014. A moved building that sheltered a family is valuable evidence. Craighead’s sod house is a separate documented home, with no verified footprint. The museum building demonstrates adaptation by a different household. [2,3]

**The town in a dust storm.** The Library of Congress file identifies a 1936 federal photograph of dust at Boise City. It provides a separate contemporaneous record of visibility in the town. Neither the pictured town scene nor Craighead’s later oral testimony supplies an exact parcel for the sod house. [3,4]
