# Clifton–Morenci: the household was part of the story

Division AZ • OP06 • Local research draft • Checked 19 November 2026

Museum-led history reference with a digital testimony fallback. Reflection-only for household disruption and loss. No mine, industrial ground, private residence, clinic, union office or historic-worksite route. No upload or contact.

## 1. Trip gate

**A small public museum, not a mine visit.** Greenlee County Historical Society publishes a free museum at **299 Chase Creek, Clifton, AZ 85533**, open **Saturdays noon–4 p.m.** Other visits are by appointment. Its general telephone is **928-865-3115**. No appointment was requested. [1]

**Winter is not independently cleared.** The operator gives a regular weekly schedule, not a separate winter or holiday calendar. Thanksgiving-weekend availability, weather interruptions, step-free access, restrooms and precise entrance conditions remain unverified. Do not translate an undated Saturday schedule into a guaranteed opening on a particular travel date. Use the published oral-history excerpts if the museum cannot be confirmed open.

**Research has a separate practical gate.** The museum advertises a dedicated computer with the Morenci Mine Historical Preservation Project. Terminal availability, assistance, appointment requirements and accessible equipment were not independently checked. Public availability of historical records grants no access to the places those records depict. [2]

## 2. The story

**Anna Ochoa O’Leary speaks for herself.** The university-hosted Mapping Chicana/Mexicana Voices project publishes excerpts and transcripts from her interview with **Jennifer Nájera, October 27, 2023, Tucson**. Its biography places her upbringing in Clifton and her father's employment at Phelps Dodge. [3]

Her household recollections challenge the idea that women merely supported someone else's labor history. She describes becoming an auxiliary spokesperson, traveling, writing and encountering disagreement at home. Her brief recorded voice, read in transcript here, is **“We're writing statements. We're writing letters. We're doing the outreach.”** Audio was not listened to. [3]

**A retrospective account, not a verdict on every relative.** O’Leary acknowledges uncertain chronology in one excerpt and explicitly notes that her husband's recollection differs from hers. Preserve that distinction rather than adjudicating a family disagreement. [3]

**Original interpretation.** The most useful shift in attention is from the industrial skyline to the work that made a community's claims audible. A household can be a place of solidarity and disagreement at the same time. This is a story about whose labor becomes visible, not a reenactment of a confrontation.

## 3. Then / now

**The 1983–1986 frame.** University of Arizona Libraries' announcement for O’Leary's **March 6, 2012** lecture identifies her as the auxiliary's president in **1985–1986**. It describes a three-year strike ending with the decertification of thirteen unions in 1986 and emphasizes altered family relationships and community connections. This is an institutional account of a historical presentation, not the lecture transcript. [4]

**Do not revive an expired exhibition.** The same announcement says its related *Company Town* display ended **March 9, 2012**. It is useful evidence of historical interpretation, not a current Tucson exhibition or an exhibit promised at Clifton. [4]

**A living collection, not a frozen town.** The Greenlee museum says its project computer includes **1,614 digitized photographs**, descriptive spreadsheet data, historian **Don Lunt's** recorded commentary and transcripts. Those materials offer another perspective to compare with worker and household testimony. His recordings were not heard, and individual images were not retrieved. [2]

## 4. What's still there

**Public interpretive institution.** The museum occupies the former **Eagle Hall**, which its operator attributes to **Antonio and Ambrose Spezia in 1913**. It reports surviving original form and detailing and describes former community uses of the upper level. This institutional history is not permission to enter upper floors or staff spaces. [5]

**Access classes.**

- Upstairs, stores and archives: no access established.
- Historic household, clinic, employment and gathering places: documentary subjects only.
- Mines, industrial facilities, rail corridors and private property: excluded entirely.

The building's presence does not prove that a particular worker, household or auxiliary story is currently exhibited inside. No such exhibit claim is made.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

**Excerpt versus complete interview.** The project says full audio or transcription requires a separate access request. No request was submitted. This dossier uses only the publicly posted excerpts and does not imply that the complete interview was reviewed. [6]

**Related archival leads are not inspected records.** ASU's search-indexed listings identify *Anna Marie and Jorge O'Leary Papers* and *United Steel Workers of America, Local 616 Records*. Direct retrieval hit a JavaScript verification wall. They remain research leads, not evidence of a specific letter, folder or public walk-in arrangement. [7][8]

**Not interchangeable histories.** The focus is the later twentieth-century auxiliary and household experience, not Bisbee's 1917 expulsion or a generalized mining-town chronology. Earlier and later disputes should not be merged because their names or geography overlap.

**Remaining trip gaps.** Particular-date opening, holiday exceptions, accessibility, terminal availability and current photo rules need operator confirmation before relying on a physical visit. No contact was made, and no public interior was independently inspected.
