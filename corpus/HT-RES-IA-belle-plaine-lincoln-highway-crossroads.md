# Belle Plaine — The filling station followed the road

Iowa OP04 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

**A building turned into a way to follow traffic.** In fourth-generation owner Mary Preston's written family account, Frank Fiene built the station in 1912 and George W. Preston bought it for $100 in 1923 so four sons could run it. She puts the former site near Seventh Avenue and Nineteenth Street and the move to Fourth Avenue and Thirteenth Street around 1927, responding to the highway reroute. A history article preserved by the State Historical Society names 21st Street for the original alignment. Preservation Iowa places the move between 1928 and 1930. Each tells a plausible part of the business story. The original use, street address and exact year of relocation still need independent records. [2, 5, 6]

**A mechanic's walls became a history collection.** Mary names her grandfather George H. Preston as the collector who covered the station with signs, then filled the nearby garage. The garage and three-room motel extended service beyond the filling pump. As the family passed down the collection, her father Ronald added to it. She attributes this line to George H.: “If you don’t believe me, go ask Blanche”. The words come through a descendant, with the family page describing some recollections as urban legends. In a later item citing the Cedar Rapids Gazette, George H. recalls being treated “just like the President of the United States” when he appeared on the Tonight Show. He sat for an interview as a storyteller. The source chain calls for separate evidence for the dates or claims inside those stories. [2, 7]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic place.** The station's earliest location and its relationship to the original alignment have competing accounts. Its documented registered setting is near Fourth Avenue at 402 13th Street. The 12th Street museum is a separate downtown business-district building and an interpretation stop. [2–6, 8]

**Lawful public anchors.** Belle Plaine Area Museum, 901 12th Street, a drive-up and short walk from legal downtown parking using posted public entrance and sidewalk. At Preston's Corner use only an open, clearly public sidewalk with a safe view. Its exact field stance has yet to be checked. The byway guide lists the station and an interpretive panel; it establishes no public access through restoration fencing, doors, drives or the forecourt. If the sidewalk approach is unclear or obstructed, keep this a museum visit. [1–4, 9]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

The family puts the first site at Seventh and Nineteenth and the move around 1927. The state-hosted article associates the old station with the alignment on 21st Street, and Preservation Iowa supplies a relocation window of 1928–1930. Both streets and both date ranges are retained. No conveyance, Sanborn series or moving invoice was obtained. NPS weekly list verifies the district's September 2020 listing. The separately retrieved NPS Lincoln Hotel nomination belongs to Lowden and supplies no evidence about Preston’s property. The Tonight Show broadcast and the original Cedar Rapids Gazette item were not retrieved. The association blog preserves an attributed excerpt. Exact current ordinary sidewalk stance and any station interior hours remain open. [2, 5–9, 11]
