## 2. The story

**Named firsthand household voices: Cathy Lane and Eddie Estes**, in a StoryCorps *Brightness in Black* conversation, broadcast by NPR August 15, 2025. The published transcript was read through public-radio distributor VPM. They remember growing up in Nathaniel Estes’s plumbing family: Eddie recalls assisting his father, while Cathy remembers answering the business telephone and her mother’s supervisory role. Cathy’s compact statement of pride is **“I never was ashamed that my father was a plumber.”** This is firsthand memory of their own household experience, not their father’s own interview. [4]

Denver Public Library independently describes a separate Nathaniel Estes oral-history excerpt as concerning his licensing and work in Five Points. That catalog/video-description evidence ties the subject to the neighborhood, but its recording was not watched or heard. Do not blend the DPL interview with the later children’s conversation. [5]

**Original interpretive voice:** Five Points should not become only a list of famous stages and famous visitors. A community also depended on people who answered phones, learned trades, kept businesses going and raised children inside that work. This family’s memory invites the visitor to notice recognition: who gets called an important historical figure, and who quietly made everyday life possible?

The family’s humorous recollections are their own, not a universal model for childhood work or a claim about all Black Denver households. They should remain a named, limited account. No technical plumbing detail, customer identity, private address or active business recommendation is included.
