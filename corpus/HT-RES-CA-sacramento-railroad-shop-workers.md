## 2. The story
**Named firsthand account, mediated through a documented historical report:** the Library of Congress-hosted Historic American Engineering Record **HAER No.CA-303**, *Southern Pacific Sacramento Shops*, printed page139, cites **Laura Walton's interview, page2**, in the California State Railroad Museum's Sacramento Shops Oral History Project. Walton recalled seeking work in the early1950s and encountering a job **“wasn't open to women”**; the report says her railroad-employed husband intervened on her behalf. [3]

The report also notes her earlier wartime shipyard experience. This is a worker's retrospective testimony quoted and summarized by a historian, not the original interview transcript or audio independently retrieved here. No exact job title, interview date or pay rate is supplied. Technical material elsewhere in the large report is excluded.

**Original interpretation:** a workshop's history is partly a history of permission: who could enter employment, whose prior skill counted, and who needed someone already inside to advocate for them. That question makes labor visible without turning a museum object into an engineering lesson or treating a single experience as representative of every worker.

**One-minute hook, original:** The hard part of a railroad job could begin before the first workday. A woman who already had industrial experience remembered a door that was not supposed to open for her.
