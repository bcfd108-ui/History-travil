# Alma native stone — The Feidens’ corner

## 2. The story

**Two masons have names.** The National Register district nomination identifies John and Rudolph Feiden as the stone masons who built the 1906 Meyer Block. It places O. A. Meyer laying its cornerstone in the week before May 25, 1906 and credits August and Otto Meyer with constructing or commissioning the store building. A masonry crew and the people financing the work are distinct roles. The period newspaper issue and the builders’ accounts were not directly retrieved. [3]

**One wall, several kinds of daily work.** The nomination describes separate storefront uses for the post office and Dr. August Meyer’s Post Office Drug Store. The postal counter later moved to the Third Street side. It identifies C. O. Kinne as an earlier postmaster and I. A. Robertson with the later location. The Farmers National Bank occupied part of the street frontage after 1912. Later changes removed an interior partition and the historical society eventually acquired the building. The side entry lets a visitor track the public functions without guessing what date each block was laid. [1,3]

**Why build at this scale?** Two blocks along Missouri, Joseph and Christena Brandt’s 1887 hotel served a town newly linked by rail. The district nomination quotes the Alma Enterprise calling it a “first class hostelry.” The claim is local period promotional language, reproduced by the nomination, and the original issue was not inspected. The hotel’s tooled stone and pressed-metal details speak to a separate commission and date. The Feidens’ names are attached to the Meyer Block, not automatically to every limestone facade on the street. [3–5]

## 3. Then / now

**Historic location.** Meyer Block at 227 Missouri. The nomination identifies the earlier county office building formerly on its lot. The 1906 building is distinct from that courthouse, the adjoining former cooperative building and the later county courthouse. [1,3]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Wabaunsee County Museum’s posted public entrance at 227 Missouri, with exterior views from the public sidewalk at Missouri and Third. Use only the marked entrance and rooms opened by staff. The historic Brandt Hotel at 400 Missouri is a separate documentary comparison. No interior access there is assumed. [1–5]
