## 2. The story
**One-minute hook:** A finished textile can hide the hours that made it possible. One woman's account places artistic work after the rest of the household has gone to sleep.

**Named firsthand testimony:** The National Endowment for the Arts identifies **Eppie Archuleta** as a Hispanic weaver and 1985 National Heritage Fellow associated with the San Luis Valley. Its biography quotes her own recollections of learning within a weaving family in northern New Mexico and later continuing her work in Colorado. The short household passage is: **“Everybody's asleep so I can weave. All by myself. Nobody bothers me.”** This is a directly read published quotation, not an audio recording heard here. The source places her move with her husband Frank and children to the Valley in the late 1940s and describes her teaching younger makers. Do not describe her New Mexico childhood as having occurred in Colorado. [3]

**Separate, attributed cultural context:** The **San Luis Valley Colcha Embroidery Project** describes colcha as a textile practice carried from northern New Mexico into the Valley in the nineteenth century and subsequently shaped by revivals and individual artists. Its account identifies **Tiva Trujillo**, born in San Acacio in 1917, as a founding member of La Costura de Saguache and names her *Map of the San Luis Valley* (1979), held by History Colorado. This is a separate regional art-history account, not Archuleta's testimony. Weaving and colcha embroidery are related through community histories but are not the same process or interchangeable art forms. [4]

The project also identifies photographer **Kathryn Nelson's** 1978–81 documentation and the 1980 slide-show documentary *Los Testamentos*, featuring Archuleta and Trujillo. That establishes a documented family-and-art research thread; the complete documentary was not watched in this pass. [4]

**Interpretive synthesis:** Household care, earning, making and teaching occupy the same life. The night-time sentence makes the cost of finding creative time visible without reducing Archuleta to hardship. Her experience is one person's testimony, not proof that every Hispano household followed the same pattern. Nor does a regional Hispano account erase distinct Indigenous communities or turn shared artistic influences into interchangeable identities.

## 3. Then / now
**Then:** the published recollection connects art to family obligations and the limited quiet time available to one maker. [3]

**Now:** a community project documents continuing artists and different approaches, while a public regional museum offers a lawful setting to consider family history. [2,4]

This is an interpretive comparison, not a claim that the museum is the original household or that a current display reconstructs Archuleta's room. No home address, loom location or photographic alignment has been verified or supplied.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
Suggested museum-and-reading sequence, not a neighborhood route:
1. **Before arrival:** check the public museum gate and bring the short quoted passage with its attribution. If closed, remain with the digital reading.
2. **Begin with the maker:** identify Eppie Archuleta by name and distinguish New Mexico childhood from later Valley life.
3. **Within open public exhibits:** look for an ordinary household or art-history interpretation, if present. Ask whether its label names the maker, household, date and source. Do not invent a connection to Archuleta.
4. **Add the separate context:** read about colcha and Tiva Trujillo without calling her an interchangeable example of Archuleta's weaving.
5. **Strongest ending:** return to the night-time sentence and consider what labor disappears when a beautiful object is displayed without its maker's account. End with that human question rather than a private-property search.

Optional ordinary public-desk question, if staffed: “Do you have a published family or maker account to read alongside these household displays?” No contact has been made. If interpretation turns to loss or coercion, the activity becomes quiet reflection, not scoring.
