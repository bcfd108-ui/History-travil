## 2. The story

### A festival becomes a permanent job

In May 1923 the trade paper *The Billboard* published a statement by W. H. King, the incoming manager: “The first Corn Palace was held in Mitchell in 1892.” King recalled a small frame structure and then a succession of buildings and additions. He describes the 1921 building and a program for which acts were booked through national entertainment networks. His statement is a period manager’s account in a trade paper. Its attendance boasts and construction cost are his claims. The paper shows how a palace decorated with crops also became a venue where somebody had to book performers, seat patrons and prepare the building for a season. [2]

### A design becomes a wall

The operator describes a partnership with Dakota Wesleyan University dating from the 2019 designs. The published 2026 design sheet credits the university’s students and the Corn Palace Entertainment Board. The city’s description of its ordinary production year adds a different kind of labor: growers provide colored corn and other plant material, and crews replace the exterior scene. Across the city’s historical list, drought in 2007 and 2023 kept the previous themes in place. In 2021 the published record says only five murals were replaced. A changed mural records agriculture as well as an artistic decision. [3–5]

### Oscar Howe’s name in the record

South Dakota’s state profile dates Yanktonai Dakota artist Oscar Howe’s Corn Palace design work from 1948 through 1971. A later photographic survey gives 1948 through 1972. At this stage the sources identify his design role and its long duration. Year-by-year drawings and installation credits are needed to distinguish a design date from the display season and to recover more crew names. Howe’s career gives the design work a person. King’s account gives the venue a manager. The installer on a ladder still deserves a name. [2,6,8]

## 3. Then / now

The frame building King recalled belonged to the first 1892 event. The photographic survey and architectural history identify the present building as the 1921 one, with domes and minarets added in 1937 according to SAH Archipedia. The facade carries changing art on a surviving venue. Read each image’s date before comparing it to the current facade. [2,6,9]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The operator page’s all-week banner conflicts with its November–December and January–April blocks. Use the detailed Mon–Sat schedule. Thanksgiving is November 26, 2026. The posted policy also closes Christmas and New Year’s Day. [1]
- The tourism listing describes approximately 20 workers and seven murals as a general pattern. A September 2025 news account reports a nine-mural contract for the 2026 theme. These differ in scope and date. The announcement does not establish final completion or the display present on this visit. [10,11]
- State biography ends Howe’s run in 1971, while the photographic survey ends it in 1972. No individual installation payroll or complete run of signed designs was inspected. [6,8]
