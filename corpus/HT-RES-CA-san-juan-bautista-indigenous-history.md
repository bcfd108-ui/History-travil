## 2. The story
**One-minute hook:** A restored building can make colonization appear orderly. Begin instead with a living descendant community’s account of what was lost and what continues.

**Named Tribal-published voice:** In *An Introduction from Chairman Valentin Lopez*, published on the Amah Mutsun Tribal Band’s website, Lopez identifies the community through descendants of Indigenous people taken to missions San Juan Bautista and Santa Cruz. He describes colonization, historical trauma and a return to stewardship. Recounting a 2005 appeal by Tribal elders, he says they insisted: “we must find a way to return to this path.” This is his published account of the elders’ direction, not a separately recorded quotation from a named elder. The page is not visibly dated in the inspected text; do not present its office titles or administrative descriptions as a fresh 2026 statement. [3]

The statement makes a visit’s ethical center clear: the history did not end with the mission era. Present-day people choose how to restore relationships and remember their ancestors. That is an interpretive reading of Lopez’s account, not permission to reproduce ceremonies or enter stewardship ground.

**Historical context from the Tribe’s own history:** The Band’s history distinguishes Mutsun connections with San Juan Bautista and Awaswas connections with Santa Cruz, describes forced removal and family separation, and emphasizes continuing descendant lineages. These are the publisher’s own framing and identifications. “Ohlone” must not be used to flatten distinct languages, communities or contemporary organizations into a single undifferentiated group. [4]

No graphic description, cultural ritual, burial location or reconstruction of violence is needed for reflection. No demographic death total or disputed precise population estimate is reproduced here without additional corroboration.

In 1902 Barbara Solorsano worked with C. Hart Merriam to record Mutsun words for places, plants, animals and baskets. Her daughter Ascencion later worked with John P. Harrington. The Band’s own language history says Harrington worked with her daily from August 1929 to January 1930, recording pronunciation and grammar. A word list and the sound of someone speaking answer different questions. A written name might survive after an outsider copied it, while the knowledge of how to say it depended on the speaker who supplied it. [6]

The Band describes language lessons, sound files and classes in its own account of revitalization. The Land Trust describes transcription work with the Band on the notebooks left by Harrington. Read those public accounts in the state museum or remotely. They give the historical record a present use chosen by descendants. The programs described by the Band are not visitor activities. [6,7]

## 3. Then / now

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression
One state-museum reflection sequence, conditional on open public areas. No directions between buildings or to excluded ground.

1. **Establish scope at entry.** Use the current state visitor information and open public interpretation only. Keep all unrelated property outside the visit.
2. **Read the named voice first.** Identify Lopez and the publisher before looking at the restored setting. Distinguish personal testimony, institutional history and operator interpretation.
3. **Examine the narrative presented.** In an open exhibit, ask whether Indigenous people appear as named people, labor, a generalized category or present-day communities. Record only what is actually visible.
4. **Notice evidence and silence.** A missing credit or perspective is a question for interpretation, not a license to fill the gap with an invented story.
5. **End quietly with continuity:** the important contrast is not old buildings against modern streets. It is a colonial story told as finished against a descendant community explaining obligations that continue.

Optional unsent question for a public interpreter: **Which current exhibits were developed with named descendant communities, and where can visitors read those communities’ own published accounts?** Do not ask for restricted knowledge or directions to cultural ground.
