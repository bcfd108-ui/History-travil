## 2. The story
**Named household evidence: Dean Hamblin.** The Preserve's archaeology page summarizes his childhood recollection of a household chore while living in the caretaker's home. It associates the home and related domestic remains with the railroad era, specifically the late 1940s. This is a named memory **mediated by the operator's interpretation**, not a transcript directly read or an audio interview heard. No exact words are placed in Hamblin's mouth. [4]

**Original interpretation:** a caretaker's workplace was also somebody's home. Seeing that overlap changes what a place of public infrastructure can mean: behind the institutional history were household routines, children and ordinary obligations. A childhood recollection gives a different scale from a citywide history, without making the child a paid worker or assigning unverified duties to the parent.

The operator describes combining oral memories with historical records and archaeological evidence. Those evidence types answer different questions. A remembered chore is not proof of an exact building date, and an excavated object is not automatically proof of its owner's identity. [4]

**One-minute hook, original:** Before this was a place to visit, it was a place where someone lived beside someone else's work. Read the household into the story, but leave room for what the records do not tell us.

**Primary verbal voice remains incomplete:** the original Hamblin interview, date, interviewer and release were not retrieved. A separate UNLV project, *Voices from the Past*, preserves interviews about creating the Preserve. Its finding aid is useful context, but it is not a substitute Hamblin recording. [5]
