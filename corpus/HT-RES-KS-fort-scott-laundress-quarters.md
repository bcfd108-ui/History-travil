## 2. The story
In July 1846, the post Council of Administration set the basic laundry charge at **50 cents per soldier per month**, with buttons, mending and alterations priced separately. Its schedule lists ten cents to mend a shirt and twenty-five cents to mend a coat. This is an NPS-rendered schedule, not an inspected original image or a record of what any specific worker earned. [4]

NPS cites the usual local workload as fifteen soldiers per laundress, making $7.50 for that routine service at the set rate before other pay or circumstances. The interpretation describes rations and medical services as part of the arrangement. No named Fort Scott laundress or first-person voice is established here. Thomas Swords's plan, quoted by the Historic Structures Report, names a place for laundresses alongside other support rooms in the barracks basements. Swords is a planning witness, not the worker. [5–6]

**Strongest story moment:** The Army could price every button; the records read here do not name the woman attaching it.

## 3. Then / now
**Then:** Swords's basement arrangement is evidence of intended use; it is not proof of a particular woman's occupancy or of a single room's exact furnishing.  
**Now:** NPS describes a laundress's room interpreted in a **reconstructed dragoon barracks**, distinct from the historic Post Hospital admissions doorway. The furnished room illustrates the historical arrangement, not an unaltered original. [5–6] No verified matched photo pair or original quarter photograph.
