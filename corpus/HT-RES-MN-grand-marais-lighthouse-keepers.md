# Grand Marais — A light to come in by

## 2. The story

### A place to turn toward shore

The federal nomination pairs two navigational purposes. Split Rock warned mariners off a reef. At Grand Marais the light marked a harbor where a crew could come in. Read the small keeper’s house in that second sense. It is the domestic part of a system whose working end stood out in weather. The nomination calls Grand Marais and Two Harbors “places of refuge.” [4]

### A keeper became a builder

The historical society’s site plan traces Henry Mayhew through an appointment in February 1886, purchase of building lots for $300 and a call for bids in February 1896. That ten-year interval matters. The nomination mentions a keeper dwelling as early as 1886 and separately dates the standing house to 1896. A person researching the earlier household should start with the appointment and records of the earlier dwelling before assigning any room in this house to it. [4–5]

### The harbor at work

In a 1938 Forest Service photograph a vessel lies by loose pulpwood corralled in the harbor. The original agency caption reads “Boat landing at harbor for load of pulpwood. Grand Marais, Minn.” The caption gives the sheltered water a job. This was working infrastructure for boats and freight as well as an entrance mariners needed to find. The Coast Guard dates the light in today’s steel tower to 1922 and identifies its original lens as fifth order. [6–8]

### The house gets another job

The historical society traces the dwelling’s use by the Coast Guard station commander after 1946. The historical society bought it in 1966. Today the old dwelling interprets the county. The house, harbor structures and tower have distinct histories even where the same manager once looked after them. [6,9]

## 4. What's still there

- **Public interior on posted hours.** Historic keeper’s residence and county exhibits. The current interior serves as a museum. [1,9]
- **Exterior only.** Keeper’s house from South Broadway public frontage. [1,4]
- **Not accessible on this itinerary.** Pier, breakwater, tower and operating navigation equipment. The Coast Guard publishes the tower description. That record establishes the asset, and public winter access to its footing remains unverified. [6]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. Start at the 1896 house on South Broadway. Read the ten-year interval between Mayhew’s appointment and his call for construction bids. [4–5]
2. If the museum is open, use its public county-history rooms to set the household in its maritime setting. In a posted closure, use the public street frontage when passable. [1–3,9]
3. **Strongest story moment — When a light meant shelter.** At the keeper’s house, look at the Forest Service image alongside the contemporary harbor image. Follow the pulpwood out to the vessel, then return to the house where somebody’s work was to keep that harbor findable. [4,7–8,10]

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- The nomination dates the first lighthouse to 1884. The Coast Guard dates establishment of the station to 1885. Lighthouse Friends gives first lighting in 1886. Those sources may be describing different milestones. An original light list or dated keeper entry is still needed to assign an exact first-light date. The NARA guide was retrieved. Individual Grand Marais log pages were unavailable in this pass. [4,6,11–12]
- NPS registration metadata gives 12 S. Broadway and a published date of November 28, 1978. The society’s visit page gives 8 S. Broadway, which is the public address used here. The society’s 2018 site plan gives 1971 for its placement on the register, its current museum history agrees with 1978, and the older hours release calls it a National Historic Landmark. The verified federal classification is National Register listing 78001528. Neither a separate NHL designation nor a 1971 listing action was found. [1,4–5,9,13–14]
- The dedicated operator visit page shows summer hours. The society’s 2026 schedule gives winter Wed–Sat 10–4 and holiday specifics, while its dated calendar independently confirms a public November 28 evening opening 5–7. Posted exceptions govern. [1–3,14]
- The NARA catalog page exposed a Google Tag Manager placeholder in text retrieval. The 1938 scan and catalog description were inspected through the Commons mirror. [7–8]
