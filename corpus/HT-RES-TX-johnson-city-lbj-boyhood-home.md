# Johnson City — Anita Winters remembers the boy who made speeches
Research draft · Texas OP07 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

**An upside-down tub.** On March 18, 1987, Anita P. Winters spoke to Ted Gittinger about visiting the Johnson children. In the LBJ Presidential Library’s transcript, she remembered that they gathered outside and “he would get on that tub, and we had to sit around and listen to him.” She grew up in Johnson City, where her father was a blacksmith. This is her recollection of how the neighborhood played and how Lyndon expected people to listen. Nobody has identified the tub or its exact place in the yard. [4, pp. 1, 7]

## 3 · Then / now

**Then — a place to grow up and try out being heard.** NPS describes the family moving to Johnson City from near Stonewall in September 1913 and records the 1901 house built by sheriff W. C. Russell. Sam Ealy Johnson Jr. paid $2,925 for the property. NPS places Lyndon Johnson’s 1937 congressional announcement on the porch. Winters also remembered a little outbuilding and thought it had been removed by the time of her interview. Her story describes the people around the house well before its public duties. [4–5]

**Now — a house and a layered yard.** The landscape inventory records significant work in 1934 and 1964 and identifies a period-type barn moved from Albert in 1972 to interpret the type of building that once stood there. The water tower is reconstructed. The visitor center gives context, and the park staffs the house as a separate stop from the LBJ Ranch near Stonewall. [1–3,5–6]

## 4 · What’s still there

- **Public interior on park program.** The Boyhood Home interior is accessed through the NPS guided program when operating. Visitor Center galleries are available during their posted hours. [1–3]
- **Exterior only from open paths and porches.** Restored house and yard features. The relocated barn and reconstructed water tower are interpretation features. Consult posted limits for any outbuilding. [1–3,6]
- **Not accessible.** No surviving tub or verified site of the outbuilding from Winters’s story is presented as a stop. [4,6]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Historic location.** The Johnson family’s home in Johnson City, separate from birthplace and ranch ground near Stonewall. [5–6]
2. **Lawful anchor.** Begin at the NPS Johnson City Visitor Center and use the signed path to the Boyhood Home. Take an open tour if offered. Use the open porches or exterior path otherwise. [1–3]
3. **Strongest story moment — A backyard audience.** End on the permitted exterior approach with a view of the yard. Read Winters’s description aloud. The remembered tub can stay in the story even though its exact location cannot be shown. [4]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

The current NPS alert and Boyhood Home visit pages give three times, 10:30, 11:30 and 1:30 [1–2]. The compendium lists 10, 11, 1 and 2 [3]. Use the current operating pages and visitor center confirmation for tour departures, and observe all applicable closures. The park’s district National Register metadata gives 1914 as a significant year [7]. The NPS house history places the family move in 1913 [5]. The metadata does not identify the event its 1914 label refers to. Winters’s recollection was read in transcript. The original audio and precise tub or outbuilding location were not retrieved. [4–7]
