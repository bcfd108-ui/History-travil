# Guthrie — the words at the top of the paper
Research draft · Oklahoma OP05 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

On June 20, 1894, the *Daily Oklahoma State Capital* claimed in its masthead, “The First Paper Published in Oklahoma.” Printing that sentence each issue helped turn a business claim into an account of the town’s origins. The Gateway archive describes Frank Hilton Greer arranging printing in Winfield, Kansas, before carrying copies into the Territory, followed by production in Guthrie. First distributed, first locally printed and first continuously published are separate claims. The masthead is the paper’s own language and survives in the archived issue. [4–5]

The 1902 State Capital Publishing Building was the company’s fourth home. The growing concern moved beyond the first tent and early workplaces. OHS reports that the building carried the enterprise’s printing and publishing years and transferred to Guthrie Tomorrow Coalition in 2024. A 2019 survey and HABS data provide physical records of the town and its buildings. Typesetting, feeding paper and moving the finished issue took workers for whom no individual logs have yet been retrieved. [2–3,6–7]

The Gateway notes the paper’s Republican alignment and its sale to the *Daily Leader* in 1911. The masthead and the owner’s politics both shaped what the reader could see. The original newspaper is an account of local work and also a participant in the town’s contests for influence. [4–5]
