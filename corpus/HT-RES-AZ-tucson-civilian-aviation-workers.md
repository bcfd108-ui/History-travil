## 2. The story
**Danette Bewley's published first-person career recollections** provide a specific Tucson civilian-work connection. In **Jay Gonzales's April 17, 2023 Q&A**, she describes choosing airport management after exploring other aviation careers. The article dates her arrival at Tucson Airport Authority to 2012; that is a historical career statement, not a claim about today's leadership. [4]

Her concise explanation is **“It's not just being a pilot.”** She describes learning through career moves and mentors, and says Tucson's people and collaborative character mattered in her decision to work there. The interview text was directly read; no underlying recording or employment file was examined. [4]

Interpretive thesis: a display airplane can dominate the eye while organizational and service work disappears from the story. Start with a named person's choice to work on the ground, then view civilian interpretation with that missing labor in mind. This is a managerial firsthand perspective, not a substitute for airline cabin, maintenance, cleaning, baggage or clerical workers speaking for themselves.
