# Beaumont — the day the drill crew could no longer work
Research draft · Texas OP01 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

Lamar University dates the drilling start on the McFaddin–Wiess and Kyle land to October 27, 1900, with the gusher on January 10, 1901. Al and Curt Hamill were brought in by the backers of Anthony Lucas. William A. Owens’s 1958 narrative draws on interviews with both Hamills and Pattillo Higgins. It also names Will “Peck” Byrd on the crew, and places carpenters working nearby. Ordinary wage work surrounds the moment the natural resource became national news. [2–4]

Owens’s reconstructed account gives Al the line “Let’s get some of this off the floor,” after the crew returned to mud and damaged machinery. This wording belongs to Owens’s later interview-based reconstruction. The archive’s Al Hamill interview record was identified, but direct retrieval returned an internal error. Use the published narrative as mediated testimony, with Lamar’s institutional chronology alongside. [2–4]

In the same account a rig that had worked for months had to be reckoned with as damaged equipment. Lamar distinguishes the original oil work from the public interpretation. Land records, workers’ oral histories and equipment interpretation therefore have different evidentiary roles. [1–4]

## 3 · Then / now

Historic location: early drilling and the Lucas discovery well site. Public anchor: Lamar University’s Spindletop Boomtown Museum ordinary entrance at 5550 Jimmy Simmons Boulevard, drive-up and a short walk. The separate original field has no visitor approach in this dossier. [1–2]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior and exterior: museum exhibits, period interpretation and buildings where the operator opens them. A reconstruction or exhibit is identified by the operator, without claiming it is the actual 1901 drill platform. No field, working rig or fenced premises. [1–2]

## 5 · Trip-shaped stop progression

Begin in the public interpretation area with the local drilling chronology. Read the account from a worker’s perspective, before oil arrives. **Strongest story moment — The repair job that disappeared.** Read the reconstructed line about clearing the floor and picture the crew going from routine work to an incident that halted it. Keep to the museum even when reading about the separate well. [1–3]

## 6 · Honest gaps and conflicting dates

Al Hamill’s original recording could not be opened in this run. The 1958 reconstructive account and 1901 record chronology are different sources. No first-person sworn contemporary account for Peck Byrd or daily equipment log was verified. Specific weather and major-holiday exceptions on a date need direct operator confirmation. [1–4]
