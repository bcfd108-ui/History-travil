## 2. The story

**Directly examined first-person evidence.** **Alejandro Hernández**, interviewed in Spanish by **Hugo Camacho**, **January 12, 2008, Phoenix, Arizona**, is UTEP interview **1347**. The downloadable PDF was retrieved and relevant Spanish transcript passages read, not merely its catalog summary. Audio was not heard. [4][5]

On printed page **13 of 55**, Hernández directly identifies **Las Cruces, New Mexico**, as where he worked for ten years, describing cotton and alfalfa labor. The New Mexico connection is in his testimony; Phoenix is the interview location, not the agricultural setting. [5]

**Before daylight.** On page **14**, he recalls rising early to prepare food: **“Antes de que amaneciera, nos levantábamos a hacer nuestros frijolitos.”** My translation: before dawn, we got up to make our beans. Later, a family provided paid meals, while workers still prepared their own evening food. [5]

**Original interpretation.** The field shift was only part of the labor needed to sustain a worker. Cooking, paying for meals and keeping relationships alive belong in agricultural history alongside acreage and machinery. This entry's strongest moment is the household task before the paid workday begins.

## 3. Then / now

**Relationships crossed the work schedule.** Other directly read passages recall sending money to his mother and traveling from Las Cruces to see the woman he later married. These are his retrospective recollections, not independently audited payroll or household accounts. [5]

**A record kept outside the county.** UTEP identifies a **55-page Spanish transcript** and a **74-minute interview**. The Bracero History Archive gives **1:13:43**, file identifier **Hernandez_AZ030**, and rights holder **UTEP Institute of Oral History**. The durations are consistent with rounding rather than a substantive conflict. [4][6]

**Program context.** The Bracero History Archive describes the guest-worker program as spanning **1942–1964**. This historical framework is not contemporary immigration information and does not establish that every worker's contract, treatment or experience matched Hernández's. [7]

**Present institution versus historical workplace.** The museum is a current public interpretive institution in Las Cruces. It is not identified as Hernández's employer, former camp, home or original work ground.
