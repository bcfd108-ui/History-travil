# Palo Duro Canyon — building the first park by hand
Research draft · Texas OP01 · Fred → Dash

## 2 · Story

Texas Parks and Wildlife dates the land purchase and arrival of Civilian Conservation Corps workers to 1933 and public opening to 1934, while construction was still underway. The workers built a road down from the rim and walked into and out of the canyon before the road was complete. They also built a lodge, cabins and trails and made furniture and decorative metalwork. The finished building shows the work of multiple skills. The operator’s history describes the CCC’s standard pay of $30 per month and remittances to families, without naming a particular enrollee’s earnings. [2]

The state’s CCC publication names George Nason as a chief federal inspector in Texas and Herbert Maier as a regional supervisor. It documents the woodshops, designs intended to fit the local terrain, and the mix of field and workshop projects. Those names describe the supervisory record. A local enrollee’s original words remain missing. The public park actually opened while its people were still fabricating the place visitors came to use. [2–3]

## 3 · Then / now

Historic location: the wider original CCC park, rim road, quarrying and lodge work. Public physical anchor is El Coronado Lodge, now the rim visitor center, via the signed TPWD park entrance and the normal visitor parking and walkway. No descent, worksite or battlefield route is assigned. [1–3]

## 4 · What’s still there

Public interior when staffed: former lodge visitor center. Exterior only in open designated rim arrival spaces. The historic road and other CCC resources survive as interpreted features; no assumption that every segment is original or open. [1–3]
