## 2. The story

**The work behind a saved place.** Preservation includes persuasion, records, money and the people willing to keep answering questions long after a campaign ends.

**Elisabeth Ruffner's own account.** Historical League publishes a transcript of her interview with **Pam Stevenson**, filmed by **Bill Leverton**, **July 21, 2007, Prescott**. On printed page 47, Ruffner describes continuing as a volunteer adviser and helping people locate references. Her short voice is **“And I'm happy to share whatever I know.”** [3]

On page 85 she recalls prolonged efforts concerning the Hassayampa Hotel and explaining its National Register eligibility to an owner. That is her retrospective account of preservation work, not independent verification of every transaction or a current visitor invitation to the hotel. The transcript was read; video was not watched. [3]

**An earlier instruction to continue.** A museum article quotes **Sharlot Hall's will**, written ten years before her 1943 death. She directed successors to maintain the collection, organize it and provide public educational use, recognizing continuing financial needs. [4] Her legacy was an ongoing obligation, not merely an admired building.

**Original interpretation.** The useful connection is between an institution's founding purpose and the patient everyday work that keeps historical evidence usable. A preservation story becomes stronger when it includes the adviser, interviewer and cataloger as well as the celebrated founder.

## 3. Then / now

**Beyond the famous name.** The museum's account of **Sylvia Neely**, its 2014 Sharlot Hall Award recipient, describes oral-history work with **Mona McCroskey**. It credits Neely with broadening interviews beyond an initial education-history focus to include Hispanic and African American community members whose stories had been overlooked. This is an institutional account of her work, not a first-person interview with Neely. [5]

**Primary leads remain distinct.** Museum records list Ruffner's preservation interview with McCroskey on **February 18, 1994**, audio **1002A–1002B**, and another on **May 2, 2001**, **1361–1362**. These cataloged recordings are not the 2007 Historical League interview used above. They were not heard here. [6]

The museum is a current public institution. Mentioned buildings and districts elsewhere in Prescott remain historical subjects, not authorized destinations. A saved structure can continue as a private or working place.

## 4. What's still there

**Public museum campus:** admission and seasonal hours are published. Individual structures or rooms may have separate limits; use operator directions rather than treating every historic door as open. [1]

**Public research service:** the institution holds manuscripts, oral histories and photographic collections, with archival requests handled separately. Its materials are not available for home checkout. [7]

**Published transcript:** Ruffner's 2007 account is accessible digitally. Its introductory page says original tapes are held by Arizona Heritage Center Archives in Tempe, not the Prescott museum. [3]

**Local archival labor:** Neely's interview work is documented by the museum, while its catalog supplies named interview leads. [5][6]

**Excluded:** original residences, active businesses, staff workspaces and burial locations. No preservation designation is converted into permission to enter.

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

**Start with the institution.** Enter the museum during its published winter opening, following the current visitor areas and signs.

**Read the worker's voice.** Carry the short Ruffner passage as a compact reference. Do not promise that it appears on a physical panel.

**Notice the continuing work.** Use the Hall will excerpt and Neely's documented interviewing to connect physical preservation with stewardship of records and voices.

**End with a person helping another person find evidence.** The strongest moment is Ruffner's volunteer-adviser recollection. It changes preservation from a finished rescue into a relationship maintained over time.

This is a proposed interpretive sequence, not a field-verified room map. An archive visit is optional and requires its own arrangements. No street circuit or historic-house extension is included.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

**Dates of creation versus publication.** The Historical League transcript identifies the interview as 2007, the Historymaker honor as 2008 and the transcript copyright as 2018. Its 2025 upload path is not the interview date. [3]

**Uneven institutional text.** The Neely profile carries a 2016 date but says more than forty years of museum volunteering while also placing its start in the early 1980s. It gives inconsistent Lincoln School anniversary years. Those durations and event dates are not adopted here; the documented interview-work description is retained with its source. [5]

**Appointment conflict.** The operator's main hours and FAQ use different appointment language. This is unresolved, not a reason to choose the more permissive version. [1][2]

**Collection scope is not content read.** The Hall papers finding aid identifies **SHM MS-12** and museum correspondence. It does not establish that every original item was examined. A “Restrictions: None” summary coexists with reproduction restrictions and some specifically restricted listings; no blanket reuse permission follows. [8]
