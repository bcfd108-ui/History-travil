# Oakland Memorial Chapel and the school Hiram Revels came home to lead

Labeled research draft · On Old Ground · Mississippi Regional March OP06 · November 20, 2026 Pacific

## 1. Trip gate

**Drive-up and short exterior walk.** Alcorn’s 2026–27 visitor parking policy provides a 24-hour permit from the Welcome Center. Use permitted visitor parking and open campus walks at 1000 ASU Drive. Plan an exterior visit in daylight and fair weather. Campus closures and event controls take precedence. The chapel hosts worship. Ordinary visitor interior hours remain unverified. [1–3]

## 2. Story

The large room above the ground-floor classrooms once belonged to Oakland College, a Presbyterian school whose work included preparation for the ministry. Its brickwork still gives that arrangement away. The public face uses Flemish bond, with headers and stretchers alternating in the same course. The plainer side and rear walls use common bond. The surveyors could record what the masons built. They left the architect’s name unknown, and the records reviewed here leave the building crew unnamed. The chapel needed a new roof and renewed interior finishes after an 1858 tornado. [2,4–5]

In March 1870, Hiram Rhodes Revels stood in the U.S. Senate against a proposal that could keep Black citizens from holding office in Georgia. The Senate’s published transcript records his appeal for “the rights which are theirs by God’s universal law.” He spoke in Washington. His next work here carried the question of equal citizenship into a campus with rooms, teachers and students. Mississippi bought the Oakland property in 1871 and established Alcorn University. Revels, a minister and educator, became its first president. Alcorn’s religious life office records that he taught moral philosophy. [2,5–7]

The June 7, 1871 *Hinds County Gazette* announced the new school and named Revels in the same notice that attacked the appropriation. Its hostility is evidence of the political environment in which the school opened. The classrooms and chapel adapted from Oakland gave the new institution an immediate place to work. Alcorn’s catalog records John H. Burrus’s election as president in 1882. The HABS record dates the first degree granted by Alcorn to a Black Mississippian to that same year, without naming the recipient. [4,6,8]

## 3. Then and now

Oakland’s brick chapel, its meeting room and teaching spaces served a church-affiliated college before the Civil War. Alcorn used the existing campus for Reconstruction-era Black higher education. The university still uses the chapel for spiritual life, memorials and gatherings. Campus services are listed by the operator. A listing for a service has a different purpose from a general history visit. [2,4–6]

The cast-iron stair brought another former estate into this landscape. HABS and MDAH identify the stair and balustrade as pieces transferred from Windsor after its mansion burned in 1890. The retrieved records establish the donor structure and destination, with the transfer year and installers unverified. [4,9–10]

## 5. Public progression

Get the permit at the Welcome Center, park where directed and take the open pedestrian walk toward the chapel front. From paving, read the raised portico above the former teaching level, then look at how the cast-iron stair meets the masonry. **Strongest story moment — One threshold, two schools.** The former Oakland entrance remained while the institution, students and purpose changed. [1–5,9]

## 6. Gaps and disagreements

Alcorn’s 2023–24 catalog dates the chapel to 1830 and records a first Oakland degree in 1833. HABS’s written survey and the National Register date the building to 1838. HABS written narrative says 1833 for Oakland’s first degree, while the LOC catalog summary says 1838. HABS’s institutional history gives 1878 for the establishment of a Black land-grant college here. Alcorn distinguishes the 1871 act and purchase, the 1878 renaming and the 1890 land-grant designation in its printed chronology. Treat each milestone as its source defines it and leave the chapel’s construction year disputed. [4–6]

The original acquisition and transfer receipts, named building workers, a dated modern matched field frame and an ordinary interior visitor schedule remain open.
