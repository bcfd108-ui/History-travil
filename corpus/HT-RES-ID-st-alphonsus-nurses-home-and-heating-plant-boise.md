# St. Alphonsus' Hospital Nurses' Home and Heating Plant, Boise — surviving Catholic hospital block

**Research draft · Labeled THEME SWEEP (Family 3 — Hospitals) · Boise, Ada County, Idaho · November 20, 2026 Pacific**

## 1. Trip gate

The lawful public anchor is the **public sidewalk and street-front exterior** along West Washington Street, North 4th Street, and West State Street in downtown Boise (`341 W. Washington St.` and the northeast corner of `N. 4th St. & W. State St.`, Boise, ID 83702). [1, 2, 5]

**Interior exclusion:** Both buildings are active **State of Idaho** facilities. The four-story Nurses' Home (`341 W. Washington St.`) houses the **Idaho Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired (ICBVI)** — an active state agency, low-vision clinic, and client residential rehabilitation center — and the Heating Plant/Laundry at 4th & State serves as **Idaho Division of Public Works** shop/office space. Neither building offers historic public tours; visitor observation stays on the public sidewalks. [1, 2, 5]

**Anchor ruling: ANCHORED (public downtown sidewalks along W. Washington St., N. 4th St., and W. State St. only; active state agency, clinic/residence, and shop interiors excluded).**

## 2. The story

These two red-brick buildings on Lots 1, 7, and 8 of Block 105 (Boise City Original Townsite) are the surviving fragments of Boise's original downtown Catholic medical and educational complex. Just one block south stood **St. Teresa's Academy** (founded 1889), and one block west stood **St. Alphonsus' Hospital** (founded 1894), both expanded earlier by Boise architects **Tourtellotte & Company** (1902) and **Tourtellotte & Hummel** (1912). When St. Alphonsus later moved out to Curtis Road and the original downtown hospital was demolished (removed from the National Register in 1977), the Nurses' Home and its matching power plant remained standing a block east. [1]

In **August and November 1920**, Boise City issued building permits for Tourtellotte & Hummel's **Nurses' Home** (`job 227E`) at `341 W. Washington St.`: **Charles A. Clark** took the **$10,805** foundation contract (Aug. 25, 1920) and **R. H. Posser** held the **$150,000** superstructure contract (Nov. 18, 1920). Because the corner lots left less horizontal room than Tourtellotte & Hummel's sprawling 1910s Western Colonial campuses (such as the Gooding school and Boise Children's Home), the architects stacked the nurses' residence vertically: four stories over a raised coursed rock-faced stone basement, using an `88'8" × 106'2"` minimal U-plan with a front square-columned porch and double curving parapet stairs. Stylized smooth-stone flat-arched window lintels and geometric end-set brick parapet panels signaled a shift toward their 1920s commercial aesthetic. Construction finished in phases: **C. N. Walkins** completed the fourth floor for **$9,655** in **1936**, **Jess Heller** built a **$1,047** sunporch in **1937**, and **G. M. Reese** installed an elevator shaft for **$2,320** in **1941**. [1]

In **1921**, the matching one-story **Heating Plant and Laundry** rose behind the Nurses' Home at the northeast corner of **4th and State Streets**. Its tall battered, smooth-sided brick smokestack and corbelled cornice echoed the Nurses' Home's parapet rectangles, while surviving Tourtellotte & Hummel heating drawings show **five- and six-inch steam mains** radiating outward in three directions from this single small boiler plant to heat the hospital, the academy, and the nurses' quarters. [1]

After St. Alphonsus departed downtown, the **State of Idaho** acquired both properties. The **Idaho Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired** moved into `341 W. Washington St.` (reported by secondary preservation summaries as a 1967 purchase, followed by a state Permanent Building Fund remodel funded around 2005–06) and continues operating there today, while the Heating Plant/Laundry remains under state ownership with **Division of Public Works** shop signage. [1, 2, 3, 5]

### Haunted history / lore

Secondary compilation *Idaho Architecture Project* repeats an unsourced local story claiming the building began in 1904 for St. Luke's, later became a Boise State fraternity house, and had "cadavers... dissected in the basement while nuns lived upstairs." [3] **That story is contradicted by the primary building record:** Boise City building permits (`Aug. 25` and `Nov. 18, 1920`) and Tourtellotte & Hummel's signed drawings prove `341 W. Washington St.` was built starting in **1920** specifically as the **St. Alphonsus' Hospital Nurses' Home**, not in 1904 for St. Luke's. [1] The basement-cadaver story is printed here strictly as a rejected secondary rumor, not verified history.

## 4. What's still there

- **Nurses' Home (`341 W. Washington St.`):** Four-story red-brick facade over a raised rock-faced stone basement, outset square-columned entrance porch with double curved stone parapet stairs, smooth-stone flat-arched lintels, denticulated upper cornice, and geometric brick/cast-stone parapet rectangles. [1, 5]
- **Heating Plant & Laundry (`NE corner N. 4th & W. State`):** One-story red-brick shop block over a high concrete base matching the Nurses' Home's lintels, corbelled parapet, and rectangular masonry panels. [1, 5]
- **Battered brick smokestack:** Tall, smooth-tapered brick chimney rising at the northeast corner of the Heating Plant, topped by brick corbelling — prominently visible along State and 4th Streets. [1, 5]

## 5. Trip-shaped stop progression

1. **Corner of W. Washington St. & N. 4th St. (`341 W. Washington St.`):** Stand on the public sidewalk facing the Nurses' Home's main north elevation. Observe the coursed stone basement, double curved entrance stairs, square-columned porch, flat-arched lintels, and patterned top parapet.
2. **Walk south along N. 4th St. toward W. State St.:** Follow the public sidewalk south past the Nurses' Home's west flank and the narrow alley/service gap separating it from the Heating Plant.
3. **Corner of N. 4th St. & W. State St.:** From the corner sidewalk, take in the nine-bay west and four-bay south elevations of the 1921 Heating Plant/Laundry and the tall battered brick smokestack rising above the State Street roofline, with both historic buildings visible in a single view.
4. **Boundary stop:** Do not enter the ICBVI client lobby/residence or the Division of Public Works shop yard.

## 6. Honest gaps and conflicting dates

- **Rejected secondary date & origin error:** *Idaho Architecture Project* claims the building dates to **1904** for St. Luke's Hospital (`and St. Luke's Nurses' Home`), later housing a fraternity and basement cadaver dissections. That conflicts directly with the **1920 Boise City building permits**, **Tourtellotte & Hummel drawings (`227E`)**, and **1982 NRHP nomination (`82000244`)**, and appears to conflate another site or local legend. [1, 3]
- **1967 state transfer & 2005–06 remodel specifics:** The 1967 ICBVI acquisition date and 2005–06 `$2M` Permanent Building Fund remodel figure come from secondary architectural summary notes (`Idaho Architecture Project`) and were not cross-checked against Ada County deed records or 2005–06 legislative session budgets this run. [3]
- **Steam tunnel / boiler survival:** Whether original 1921 boiler equipment or sub-street steam main segments remain beneath 4th and State Streets is undocumented in retrieved exterior records.
- **No overlap with live catalog:** Verified absent from `onoldground.com` (`/desk/state-id`) and `HT-US-INDEX.csv`.
