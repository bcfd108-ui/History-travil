## 2 · Story — A town was a place to bring things

A 1924 *Rock County Herald* article reports the memories of George DeForce, who came to the county as a child and returned decades later. In the local historical society president’s 2016 newspaper transcription of the article, Deborah Estey and her family arrived in stages from 1867 to 1870. In 1873, DeForce remembered being called to sing at a Fourth of July gathering in Amos Estey’s grove. The newspaper gives his song title as “Alcohol.” It estimates fifty to sixty people there. That is one participant’s memory transmitted by a reporter, and the attendance estimate is neither a census nor evidence of everyone living in the county. [2–3]

DeForce recalled Wold and McKay’s store, a blacksmith shop, a drug store, and banking and real-estate offices. The article uses the phrase “the only store in town” for Wold and McKay’s in 1873. These details are from the 1924 account, not recent testimony about where to stand. A blacksmith, a druggist and a merchant could support widely separated households without their buildings remaining today. The 1883 bird’s-eye view cataloged by the Library of Congress offers a second period document for the town that later grew around such businesses. It is a drawn view. [2,4]

Rock County’s farm families did not all arrive and stay in the same way. Arthur P. Rose’s 1911 county history describes Ole L. Tostenrud purchasing a Kanaranzi Township farm in 1885, cultivating it in season and returning to Osage, Iowa, in winter. Mary Isaacs Tostenrud and the family moved to Minnesota in 1887. The family lived temporarily in Ellsworth when improvements were being made on a later holding. This is Rose’s published biographical account, with a parcel description that could be tested against the historic plats. The 1911 text describes people through household heads and institutional offices, giving less detail on the work done within households. [5–7]

## 3 · Then / now

Then, households were scattered around a service town and could move between rented ground, acquired farms, seasonal homes and nearby towns. Today the history center interprets that network from a public building on Main Street. Its early-life exhibits are the interpretive anchor. Early Estey and Tostenrud lands are document locations for this entry. Their title chains, surviving structures and lawful visitor boundaries remain open. [1–2,4–7]

## 6 · Gaps and conflicts

- The 1924 transcription calls Collin Estey’s the first pre-emption along the Rock River valley in the county. The state place-name card calls Philo Hawes a first settler at Luverne. These are different claims and neither establishes first occupation of the region. A contemporary land-office entry for Collin’s claim was not verified. [2,8]
- The 1924 newspaper is available here through the historical society president’s 2016 transcription. No original clipping or taped voice was available. Rose was read through its web transcription, with typographical artifacts. Southwest Minnesota Historical Center catalogs the bound volume. The Borchert inventory lists an 1886 atlas and 1916 state plats. Individual plat sheets and a title-chain match were not verified. [2,5–8]
- The exact grove location and present farm or riverbank access remain unresolved. Field checks apply to the public museum entrance only. [1–2]
