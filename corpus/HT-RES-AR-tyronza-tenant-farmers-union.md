# Southern Tenant Farmers Museum, Tyronza
HT-RES-AR-tyronza-tenant-farmers-union · Arkansas OP03 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

Before he had an office in Tyronza, Harry Leland Mitchell knew the difference between growing cotton and getting paid for it. In an undated first-person account titled The Southern Tenant Farmer’s Union, Mitchell recalled starting field work as a child. He described arranging his own small sharecrop in Ruleville, Mississippi, and watching the cotton price fall. “All I got out of my year’s work was a suit,” he wrote in the published typescript. This was his recollection of early work in Mississippi. It explains what Mitchell brought to Tyronza without making his childhood a Tyronza event. [2, 3]

At Tyronza he ran a cleaning business beside Clay East’s filling station. The businesses gave the two men a way to make a living and, later, rooms from which to do union work. Workers already had a reason to organize. Federal payments intended to reduce acreage went through landowners, and tenants faced eviction or a struggle over what should reach them. A-State traces the union’s formation to eighteen men, seven Black and eleven white, who met in a schoolhouse near the town. The Mitchell–East buildings served as an early headquarters. The county histories of farmwork and the addresses of the actual first meeting and the office deserve separate labels. [3–5]

An archival collection guide points to a 1935 Brooks Hays memorandum addressing farm tenants and federal farm policy, correspondence in the Henry Clay East Papers, and the early STFU record series on microfilm. These are paths to the contemporary organizing papers. Mitchell’s recollection is the first-person voice read here. It is an organizer’s account of his own work and does not substitute for a distinct member’s record or prove what any landlord paid. [2, 6, 7]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The museum occupies Mitchell’s and East’s former businesses and an adjoining former bank. The country schoolhouse cited for the founding meeting is a separate historic location. This reading stop is in the surviving storefront complex. [1, 4, 5]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Southern Tenant Farmers Museum, 117 North Main Street, Tyronza. Drive up via the named street address and take the short open public walk to the signed museum entrance. Use the museum side of the frontage without entering closed buildings, adjoining lots or any former meeting or farm site. [1]

## 5. Public progression

Use the staffed museum to read the public interpretation of tenant contracts and look at the relationship between the two business spaces. On the way out, if the public frontage is open, read Mitchell’s personal account separately from the founding narrative. **Strongest story moment · A year’s work, one suit.** The recollection gives the question of compensation a human scale. ## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The A-State detailed history gives July 18, 1934, for the organizing meeting. The NEH application gives July 13 and identifies Sunnyside/Fairview School. A later union banner catalog description also gives July 13. The A-State visit page gives only the year. The event stays dated July 1934 here until meeting minutes or dated first-hand documentation are read. [1, 4, 8, 9]
- The undated Mitchell typescript was readable through the web document index; direct shell retrieval returned HTTP 406. The precise date and preparation of the typescript, an original meeting record on reel 1, and additional members’ own accounts remain open. The Hays memo and East papers were located at the archive-guide level. [2, 6, 7]
- The operator’s street address uses North Main. The district nomination references the wider district and South Main in parts of its description. The visitor address comes from the current operator, not from a nomination location line. [1, 5]
