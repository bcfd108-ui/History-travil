# Crater of Diamonds State Park, Prairie Creek diamond field
HT-RES-AR-murfreesboro-diamond-field-workers · Arkansas OP03 · Draft for independent Dash review

## 2. Story

When John W. Huddleston picked up two stones in August 1906, the largest uncertainty was what he had found. The Arkansas Diamond Company’s 1908 pamphlet places the discovery on his property and describes samples reaching jeweler Charles S. Stifft. The later USGS account gives August 1, names Horace Bemis as an early intermediary and identifies St. Louis jewelers and Stifft in the testing chain. Slow down over the distances in that account. A find on a farm only became a commercial claim after people carried material to people who had a way to identify it. [3, 4]

By 1908 the company wanted to show that it could recover stones from material in quantity. Its printed statement names John C. Peay and calls him “Our manager, Mr. John C. Peay”. It credits him with recovering fourteen carats from twenty loads, then extrapolates profits at that and another yield. In context the pamphlet was soliciting capital. The statement is a source for how the company described Peay’s work and the numbers it offered readers. An isolated test says much less about annual production than the promoters wanted readers to think. [3]

The later USGS investigation describes a first small wash plant erected in 1908, remodeling in 1909 and a larger plant in 1920. It distinguishes stones found by chance on the ground from those recovered in washing plants and notes the incomplete production figures available to investigators. Peay’s name persists in a company report, while the workers who moved material and operated the equipment are often represented only by loads and output. The visitor center can make that change in scale visible without assigning a precise modern footprint to the old mill. [1–5]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic location.** The historic Arkansas mine and its processing areas occupied part of the Prairie Creek diamond-bearing ground. The park occupies this historic landscape, and its contemporary public field and visitor buildings have separate signed boundaries. The old wash-plant footprints are documentary for this dossier. [1, 3–5]

**Lawful physical anchor.** Crater of Diamonds State Park visitor center, 209 State Park Road, Murfreesboro. Drive up, use signed public parking and walk via the normal visitor route to the exhibits. If continuing outdoors, remain on open maintained public paths and follow the separate field gate and operator rules. Former pits, plant locations, service ground and off-trail areas are outside the itinerary. [1, 2]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts

- The 1908 company text gives August 1906 and credits the Stifft identification. The USGS history gives August 1 and identifies an additional early intermediary and St. Louis jeweler. No contemporaneous day-of-find statement from Huddleston was retrieved. [3, 4]
- The company printed sample yields and capital projections, while the USGS describes an incomplete output record. Treat the company’s numbers as published claims with no payroll or complete production corroboration. [3, 4]
- The USGS publication was available as web-indexed federal text and mapped plates. Direct PDF requests returned HTTP 403. Local PDF pages of the 1908 pamphlet were read, including the investors’ statement and the map. No identified field worker’s payroll or a georeferenced plant match was recovered. [3–5]

## 7. Historical photo records

No adopted site photograph. The inspected 1908 company pamphlet is mainly text and includes a map rather than a usable historical camera frame. The USGS mapped historical plant and mine features, but no independently inspected photo of the actual surviving site with item-level provenance was established. A verified then-and-now frame and Photo Point are absent. [3, 4]
