# Ida B. Wells-Barnett, Holly Springs and the work of putting facts in print

*On Old Ground · Regional March · Mississippi OP15 · Labeled research draft · NO-ANCHOR · Reflection only*

## 2. Story

Ida B. Wells was born into slavery at Holly Springs in 1862. Her parents, James and Elizabeth Wells, and the school she attended belong near the start of the story. The house museum describes James, also called Jim, as apprenticed in carpentry during construction of the Spires Boling house, and locates Ida's birth on the property where her mother cooked. This supplies a claim about the property and the parents’ labor, while leaving the particular room or building open. The state inventory independently identifies the present historic house and describes its four octagonal porch columns, a feature visible in a dated exterior photograph. [1, 2, 4, 5, 9]

The institution now called Rust College started as a school founded by the Freedmen's Aid Society in 1866 at Asbury Methodist Episcopal Church. Rust's institutional account includes James Wells among the local African American craftsmen who helped build the permanent campus. Other records place the young Ida in the school known as Shaw University before the name Rust. Her later turn to teaching after the deaths of her parents gave responsibility for other people a practical meaning before her career in the press. No original James Wells apprenticeship or trusteeship papers were inspected in this run. [2, 5, 6, 7]

Wells later used newspapers and pamphlets to confront racial murder and the stories used to justify it. In *Southern Horrors*, she writes, “The people must know before they can act.” That line was checked in a published transcription of the pamphlet, with the 1892 first edition uninspected. She distinguishes gathering facts from repeating news accounts that she identified as unreliable. The historical geography of the pamphlet and her later career extends beyond Holly Springs. The same insistence applies here: a precise claim about where her mother labored or where she was born needs its own record. [5–7, 10]

## 3. Then and now

**Historic locations.** Spires Boling property, Holly Springs, associated by the current museum with the Wells family. The founding site and later Rust campus are separate places, and the exact room or outbuilding of Ida's birth is unresolved. [1, 2, 4–7]

**Present public anchor.** Blank. Museum and college publish appointment arrangements. Mississippi's state tourism office identifies the later Oak Palace mural in Holly Springs, but the specific lawful viewing boundary and any scheduled public access at that business were unverified. [1–3, 8]
