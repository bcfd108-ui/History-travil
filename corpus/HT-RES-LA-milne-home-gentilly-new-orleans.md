## 2. The story

The campus traces its endowment to Scottish-born New Orleans philanthropist and real-estate financier **Alexander Milne** (`1742–1838`), whose 1838 will left land and funds to establish asylums for destitute orphan boys and orphan girls in New Orleans. Nearly a century later—after an earlier boys' home model and growing city juvenile-welfare demand—the City of New Orleans constructed the modern **Milne Boys' Home** complex on a large Gentilly parcel at **5420 Franklin Avenue**, opening in **1933** (`City Archives records span 1926–1974`). [1, 2, 3]

For more than fifty years (`1933–1986`), the campus housed orphaned, destitute, and court-committed boys in brick institutional buildings arranged around a formal courtyard (paired alongside the city's historic Colored Waifs' Home legacy in juvenile care history). Milne Boys' Home closed as a residential youth facility in **1986**, then hosted intermittent after-school and summer youth programs until flood damage from **Hurricane Katrina in 2005** closed the complex for nearly a decade. [1, 2]

In **2013–2014**, the City of New Orleans launched an adaptive-reuse project converting the historic Gentilly campus into the centralized headquarters and youth athletic complex of **NORDC** (linked with city youth violence-prevention initiatives), preserving the main campus footprint in active municipal service. [1]
