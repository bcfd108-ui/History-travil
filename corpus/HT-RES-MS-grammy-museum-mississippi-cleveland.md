# The Cleveland museum and the work behind a recorded song

*On Old Ground · Labeled research draft · Mississippi OP18*

## 2. Story

A museum about a national music award took shape on a piece of a Mississippi university golf course. A contemporary Delta State account identifies Lucy Richardson Janoush as president of the Cleveland Music Foundation’s board from 2011. It places the building at the former southeast corner of the course. An archived Recording Academy groundbreaking announcement describes the Foundation’s long lease with Delta State and names museum deputy director Rita George as part of the development. Both institutions had concrete work to do before a record could play inside the building: finding land, planning exhibits, building spaces and financing a public museum. [3][4]

“I think the partnership is perfect,” Janoush told Delta State in November 2015. It is her assessment of the university and museum working together, published while the project was approaching opening. A Mississippi legislative resolution introduced in 2017 described her involvement from fundraising through planning and records that the museum opened on campus in 2016. The dedication itself went through earlier schedules. The Academy’s March 5 announcement and the later legislative account establish the public opening year more reliably than the early projections. [3–7]

The present galleries make labor audible. The operator lists a Mississippi Music Bar that lets visitors listen across artists and periods, a timeline for the awards, and exhibits about creating and recording music. A music award database would answer who won. The museum’s own floor, through an example track and a recording exhibit, can help a listener hear the contribution of performance, recording and mixing. Cleveland is the place where this account of musical work is exhibited. Earlier sessions and recordings represented there retain their own locations. [1][2]

## 3. Then and now

The foundation’s project used former university golf-course ground. The modern building opened in 2016 and interprets records, performance and awards across decades. The institution’s benches, studios and programmed galleries are present-day exhibits. Their relationship to past performance depends on the named objects, recordings and labels on view. [1–6]
