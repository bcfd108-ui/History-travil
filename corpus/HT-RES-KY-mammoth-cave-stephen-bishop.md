## 2. Story
**Mammoth Cave National Park, visitor center and historic cave interpretation · Research draft · Fred**

Early visitors depended on knowledge made by people whose legal freedom was denied. The NPS records enslaved workers producing saltpeter, used for gunpowder, in demanding underground conditions. That industrial history predates the guided travel that made the cave famous. Stephen Bishop was enslaved when he became a guide. He learned the passages, led visitors and compiled a map. The credit printed on the 1845 map reads “STEPHEN BISHOP ONE OF THE GUIDES.” A named Black worker appears here as the maker of geographic knowledge. [4–6]

The anonymous 1845 guidebook now associated with Alexander Clark Bullitt promotes the hotel's comforts and calls the visitor's reassurance “Stephen his guide,” in the digitized book transcription. It gives a paying customer's expectations and the reliance those expectations placed on Bishop. NPS says he drew the map from memory during an 1842 visit to the Croghan estate. The endpoint of the story isn't the work alone: Bishop gained freedom and guided for pay. The public entry gives no route to his grave or to archaeological material. [4–7]

## 3. Then and now
Belowground, former mining and visitor use left traces interpreted by NPS. The visitor center and ranger program now set the boundary. The 1845 printed Bishop map represents explorations and vocabulary at the time of publication. Today's tours have their own scope and are controlled by the operator. [1–6]

## 4. Surviving features and access
- **Public interior** — Visitor center and staff-open historical displays.
- **Controlled interior** — Designated cave route only, with a valid tour ticket or approved program, keeping to guide instructions.
- **Protected or nonpublic** — Other cave passages, burials, archaeological areas, rivers and closed ground. No independent entry or geographic pin. [1–5]

## 5. Public trip progression
Begin in the visitor center and learn who worked as miners and guides. With a confirmed tour that covers relevant history, proceed only as staff direct. Conclude at **The guide who made the map**, reading Bishop's printed credit with the park's history of his work. The visitor center and the free printed source carry the story when cave entry is unsuitable or unavailable. [1–7]

## 6. Gaps and conflicts
- The NPS biographical page says Bishop drew from memory in 1842. One NPS teaching-resource label says drawn in 1845, but the map imprint printed in the same resource gives 1845 as publication. Distinguish the documented print date from the park's different descriptions of drawing date. [5–6]
- The period book is anonymous on the title page. The Library of Congress copy cataloged via Internet Archive attributes it to Bullitt. The traveler's line is from a digitized transcription. No first-person manuscript testimony from Bishop about the map was established. The full free scan is listed with the transcription. [7–8]
- The locator of the ordinary public entrance requires agreement from a second source. The conditions page's displayed weather point does not identify it. [2]
