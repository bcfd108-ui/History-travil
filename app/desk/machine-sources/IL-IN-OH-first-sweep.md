# Illinois · Indiana · Ohio First Sweep — On Old Ground lead list

**Date:** November 20, 2026
**Purpose:** First checklist of significant visitable historic ground in Illinois, Indiana, and Ohio. The live catalog (550 entries, 21 states) was checked before drafting — none of the three states has an entry yet (verified: no IL/IN/OH slugs or state values in sites.json), so there are no HAVE rows; every row below is new ground for the catalog.
**Counts:** 179 candidates — 162 LEAD, 17 NO-ANCHOR? (Illinois 62: 53 LEAD / 9 NO-ANCHOR? · Indiana 51: 48 LEAD / 3 NO-ANCHOR? · Ohio 66: 61 LEAD / 5 NO-ANCHOR?) Counts verified against the tables below by row count.
**Lead sources used (leads only):** Illinois Historic Preservation Division state historic site list (20 ILCS 3405) and Enjoy Illinois heritage itineraries; Indiana State Museum and Historic Sites system list, Indiana Archives state hospital and prison records pages, Visit Indiana historic-site lists; Ohio History Connection site network and partner list, NPS Ohio unit and National Heritage Area resource inventories, Erie County / Firelands historical society museum listings; National Register and National Historic Landmark state lists; NPS Network to Freedom / Underground Railroad site lists.

**LEADS ONLY — nothing here is researched copy.** Names, locations, and topics are a checklist so no ground is missed. Every entry that advances gets researched from primary and independent sources and written as an original dossier. No wording or photographs are taken from any lead source.

**Marks:** LEAD = a lawful public anchor likely exists (anchor type named). NO-ANCHOR? = significant documented ground but no known lawful public anchor — flagged, not dropped; the researcher confirms or converts.
**⚑ = theme family (prisons / asylums / hospitals / military)** — these count double in the standing sweep weighting and are flagged wherever they appear.

## Sequencing note

Ohio opens most naturally at the Firelands cluster (Norwalk–Milan–Sandusky–Marblehead sit within a half-hour of each other and carry the owner's standing interest) and at the earthworks belt (Newark–Fort Ancient–Hopewell–Serpent Mound, now a single UNESCO story). Illinois opens at Springfield/Lincoln country and Cahokia; Indiana at the Coffin House–Richmond corridor and Indianapolis. Theme-family density is highest in Ohio (reformatory and asylum ground statewide) and in the Joliet–Pullman Illinois corridor — the sweep lane can work those ahead of the regional march without collision, per the standing lane split.
