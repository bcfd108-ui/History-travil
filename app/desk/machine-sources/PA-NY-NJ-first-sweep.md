# Pennsylvania / New York / New Jersey First Sweep — On Old Ground lead list

**Date:** November 20, 2026
**Purpose:** First checklist of significant visitable historic ground in Pennsylvania, New York, and New Jersey. No dossiers exist yet for any of the three states — this is the list the research lanes work down when each state opens.
**Catalog check:** Live catalog read November 20, 2026 — 550 entries across 21 states; **zero entries in PA, NY, or NJ. HAVE = 0 in all three states.**
**Counts:** **179 candidates — 170 LEAD, 9 NO-ANCHOR?, 0 HAVE.** By state: Pennsylvania 61 (58 LEAD, 3 NO-ANCHOR?) · New York 62 (59 LEAD, 3 NO-ANCHOR?) · New Jersey 56 (53 LEAD, 3 NO-ANCHOR?).
**Lead sources used (leads only):** National Park Service National Historic Landmarks lists for PA (169 NHLs), NY, and NJ (58 NHLs); National Register state listings; NPS unit lists for the three states; Pennsylvania Historical & Museum Commission state historic sites; New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation State Historic Sites and the Path Through History highlights; New Jersey State Historic Sites and Journey Through Jersey listings; state park, county park, and municipal museum listings.

**LEADS ONLY — nothing here is researched copy.** Names, locations, and topics are a checklist so no ground is missed. Every entry that advances gets researched from primary and independent sources and written as an original dossier. No wording or photographs are taken from any lead source.

**Marks:** LEAD = a lawful public anchor likely exists (anchor type named). NO-ANCHOR? = significant documented ground but no known lawful public anchor — flagged, not dropped; the researcher confirms or converts. **★ = theme family — prisons, asylums, hospitals, military** (these count double in the sweep; the researcher flags them first). Indigenous, labor, civil-rights, women's, immigration, and canal/industry weight is named in the note column.

## Sequencing notes for the lanes

- **Pennsylvania** opens most naturally at Philadelphia (a dozen LEADs within walking or short-transit distance) and the anthracite cluster; Eastern State and Carlisle are the two theme-family dossiers to file early — both are national stories, not state stories.
- **New York** opens at the harbor and runs up the Hudson on the NPS/state-site spine; the equal-rights triangle (Seneca Falls–Auburn–Peterboro) files as one research run. The three NO-ANCHOR? rows are all asylums or controlled-access ground — they convert only if a lawful public program verifies.
- **New Jersey** opens at Paterson and the Trenton–Princeton corridor; Burlington County Prison and Paulsdale are the state's theme and women's anchors and should not wait for the shore batches.
- All three states are dense with small museums on limited hours — the dossier standard (verify hours and lawful access at research time, print gaps) applies with extra force here.
