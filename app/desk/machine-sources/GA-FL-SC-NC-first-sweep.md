# Southeast First Sweep (GA · FL · SC · NC) — On Old Ground lead list

**Date:** November 20, 2026
**Purpose:** First checklist of significant visitable historic ground in Georgia, Florida, South Carolina, and North Carolina. The live catalog was checked November 20, 2026 — it holds 550 entries across 20 states and ZERO in these four states, so no candidate below is a HAVE; every row is new ground for the research lanes to work down as each state opens.
**Counts:** 126 candidates — 109 LEAD, 17 NO-ANCHOR? (Georgia 32: 28 LEAD / 4 NO-ANCHOR? · Florida 30: 26 / 4 · South Carolina 32: 26 / 6 · North Carolina 32: 29 / 3)
**Lead sources used (leads only):** National Register of Historic Places and National Historic Landmark listings for GA, FL, SC, NC (NPS); NPS unit lists for each state; Georgia State Parks & Historic Sites, Florida State Parks / Florida Memory / Division of Historical Resources, SC State Parks State Historic Sites and SC Department of Archives and History records, NC Division of State Historic Sites and NCpedia; Wikipedia state history and site lists used as a finder only.

**LEADS ONLY — nothing here is researched copy.** Names, locations, and topics are a checklist so no ground is missed. Every entry that advances gets researched from primary and independent sources and written as an original dossier. No wording or photographs are taken from any lead source.

**Marks:** LEAD = a lawful public anchor likely exists (anchor type named). NO-ANCHOR? = significant documented ground but no known lawful public anchor — flagged, not dropped; the researcher confirms or converts. Theme families (prisons, asylums, hospitals, military installations, weird spots) are flagged in the note column with [THEME: …] and count double in lane priority.

## Sequencing notes

- **Georgia** opens most naturally on the Indigenous spine — Etowah, Ocmulgee, Kolomoki, New Echota — with Andersonville and the MLK / Sweet Auburn ground as the heavy national anchors, and the coast (Pulaski, Frederica, Sapelo, Pin Point) as one Gullah/Geechee and colonial cluster.
- **Florida** opens at St. Augustine (Castillo, Fort Mose, Lincolnville — one walkable cluster carrying Spanish colonial, free-Black, and civil-rights ground at once), then Pensacola and the Keys forts for the military layer.
- **South Carolina** opens at Charleston and Beaufort — the deepest single cluster in this sweep (Fort Sumter, Gadsden's Wharf / IAAM, Penn Center, Mitchelville, Reconstruction Era NHP) — then the Revolutionary backcountry chain: Camden, Cowpens, Kings Mountain, Ninety Six.
- **North Carolina** opens on the state-historic-site spine (Town Creek, Bentonville, Fort Fisher, Reed Gold Mine, Bennett Place) with the Greensboro sit-in museum and Princeville as civil-rights anchors, and Dix Park as the rare asylum ground that converted to a LEAD through a public park.
- The 17 NO-ANCHOR? rows concentrate where the ground is heaviest: active prisons and asylums (Reidsville, Raiford, Central Prison, Chattahoochee, Broughton, Central State Hospital GA), massacre and lynching ground with no site left (Rosewood, Moore's Ford), and restricted or private NHL ground (Snow's Island, the Vesey and Rainey houses, Windover). They stay on the list, flagged, per the standing method.
