# New England First Sweep — On Old Ground lead list

**Date:** November 20, 2026
**Purpose:** First checklist of significant visitable historic ground across the six New England states (Maine, Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island). No New England dossiers exist yet — verified against the live catalog (550 entries, 21 states, none in New England; zero HAVE). This is the list the research lane works down as the region opens.
**Counts:** 213 candidates — 193 LEAD, 20 NO-ANCHOR? — Maine 35 (29 LEAD, 6 NO-ANCHOR?), Vermont 26 (21 LEAD, 5 NO-ANCHOR?), New Hampshire 29 (28 LEAD, 1 NO-ANCHOR?), Massachusetts 61 (58 LEAD, 3 NO-ANCHOR?), Connecticut 32 (30 LEAD, 2 NO-ANCHOR?), Rhode Island 30 (27 LEAD, 3 NO-ANCHOR?). 61 candidates carry the ⚑ theme-family flag. Counts verified by script against the tables below.
**Lead sources used (leads only):** National Park Service unit lists for the six states (Acadia gateway and Maine state sites, Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller, Saint-Gaudens, and the dense Massachusetts NPS cluster — Minute Man, Lowell, Salem Maritime, Boston NHP, Boston African American, Adams, New Bedford Whaling, Saugus Iron Works, Springfield Armory, Blackstone River Valley; Roger Williams NM and Touro Synagogue in Rhode Island); state historic site rosters (Maine Bureau of Parks & Lands 17 state historic sites; Vermont Division for Historic Preservation state-owned sites; New Hampshire Division of Parks & Recreation state historic sites; Connecticut SHPO state museums; Rhode Island state parks); National Register / National Historic Landmark state lists and Wikipedia state site lists as finders; tribal museum listings (Mashantucket Pequot, Tantaquidgeon, Tomaquag, Mashpee Wampanoag, Aquinnah).

**LEADS ONLY — nothing here is researched copy.** Names, locations, and topics are a checklist so no ground is missed. Every entry that advances gets researched from primary and independent sources and written as an original dossier. No wording or photographs are taken from any lead source.

**Marks:** LEAD = a lawful public anchor likely exists (anchor type named). NO-ANCHOR? = significant documented ground but no known lawful public anchor — flagged, not dropped; the researcher confirms or converts. **⚑ = theme-family ground** (prisons, asylums, hospitals, military installations — the institutional/haunted sweep families): these count double in lane priority and are flagged in the Mark column.

**Research-lane notes:** New England is the densest opening yet — six small states, most candidates within day-trip clusters. Open Massachusetts first at the Boston–Minute Man–Lowell triangle and the Plymouth/Patuxet ground; Rhode Island can be worked nearly whole in a few passes (Slater Mill and the Blackstone corridor also unlock the Massachusetts mill valley). The Champlain corridor (Chimney Point → Mount Independence → Hubbardton → Bennington) is Vermont's spine; Maine's coast is a fort ladder from Kittery to Lubec; New Hampshire's seacoast cluster (Portsmouth–New Castle) is one compact batch. The theme families are unusually rich here: Old New-Gate Prison (CT) and Slater Mill (RI) are national-flagship entries, and the asylum ground (Brattleboro Retreat, Danvers, Northampton, Ladd School) files mostly as NO-ANCHOR? — documented, flagged, and unrouted until a lawful public anchor verifies. Tribal ground is handled as in Mississippi: Sipayik, the Missisquoi cemeteries, the Mystic Fort site, and the Narragansett Reservation stay flagged and unrouted unless and until the tribal voice leads; tribal museums (Mashantucket Pequot, Tantaquidgeon, Tomaquag, Mashpee Wampanoag, Aquinnah, Penobscot) are LEADs whose visitor protocol the researcher verifies from the tribe's own sources. Several grounds carry the reflection posture from the start — Great Swamp, Deer Island, Plymouth's Cole's Hill, Hannah Duston, Salem's witch-trials memorial. State-site seasons are short (many Maine and Vermont sites run Memorial Day to Columbus Day); the researcher verifies the current gate from primary sources for every LEAD.
